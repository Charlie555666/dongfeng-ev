Why Limpopo mining suits an EV truck

Mine haul roads climb with ore and descend empty, so a diesel tipper burns the most fuel on the loaded climb and wastes the descent as heat in the brakes. The TZ3Z captures that descent as regenerated energy, often returning 15-30 percent of the climb energy to the pack. Fixed ramps and predictable cycles let the battery be sized tightly, and the absence of a diesel engine removes heat, exhaust and coolant failure risk from abrasive mine air. For South African mines with grid or solar at the workshop, the saving is immediate.

Downhill regeneration and energy use

On a 5-12 percent mine ramp, the TZ3Z recovers a meaningful share of descent energy through its motor acting as a generator, which extends effective range and cools the brakes. At 1.4-1.6 kWh per km loaded on the climb and far less on the empty return, the 282 kWh pack covers 200-280 km of mixed duty before the workshop charge. The liquid-cooled battery holds the 25-35 C operating window in Limpopo's heat, and the 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty matches the working life of the truck body.

Charging at the mine workshop

  • Place a 240 kW DC charger at the workshop and opportunity-charge during loader queues.
  • Fill from 20 to 80 percent in 60-90 minutes between production shifts.
  • Add a solar canopy so daytime charging draws from site generation.
  • Monitor state of charge remotely from the dispatch office.
  • Keep the brake inspection light because regeneration reduces but does not end wear.

Durability in dust and heat

Platinum mine dust is fine and abrasive, so the TZ3Z seals the battery enclosure to IP67 and routes intake air through dual filters. The liquid cooling loop keeps cells in the 25-35 C band even when ambient heat exceeds 35 C at the bench, protecting cycle life. The CATL LFP chemistry resists thermal runaway better than nickel cells, an important point where vehicles work close together. The 8-year warranty gives the mine a fixed battery cost for the full depreciation period.

Battery recycling and compliance

After 4,500 cycles or eight years the LFP pack still holds useful capacity and can serve as stationary storage at the mine, buffering the solar canopy or the workshop load. CATL supports a take-back path that recovers lithium, iron and phosphate for reuse, so the pack is never hazardous waste. This second-life value lowers the true cost of ownership and helps South African sites meet tightening rules on battery disposal. We document the end-of-life plan in the supply contract so the mine has a clear, funded route when the warranty period ends and the first packs rotate out.

Workshop safety and training

Although the pack is sealed and isolates on fault, the workshop needs basic electric-vehicle precautions: a marked charge bay, a lithium-rated fire blanket and a trained isolation officer. The TZ3Z training covers these and the high-voltage isolation drill, and telematics flag any pack anomaly before the truck returns. Limpopo sites already running electric shovels will find the same rules apply, so adding the TZ3Z needs no new safety regime, only a short refresher for the haulage crew and the night-shift supervisor before the first truck enters the ramp.
