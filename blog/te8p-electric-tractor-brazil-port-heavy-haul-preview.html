Brazil's port heavy haul is dominated by the Santos drayage corridor, where 120-tonne gross combination weight loads move between terminal and inland yard, and the TE8P 120 t heavy-haul electric tractor is a preview of how that duty electrifies. Why heavy port haul suits an EV truck

A 120 t GCW tractor does not need highway speed; it needs relentless low-speed torque and a battery that survives a hard cyclic day. The Santos drayage loop is short and fixed: gate, terminal, weighbridge, inland yard, repeat. That is the same ideal profile as a quarry or a port shunt, where an electric truck's efficiency and regeneration beat diesel on cost per move.

Operators scanning the Latin America electric truck market will recognize the pattern: the first electric wins in the region are fixed, heavy, short loops where fuel volatility hurts most, and Brazil's port corridors fit that template precisely.

Santos drayage economics

The case for the TE8P is cost per container move. Energy per move at a Brazilian depot rate is far below diesel, and the removed diesel engine service plus regenerative capture on the loaded return cut two persistent cost lines. For an operator running many short moves per day, those savings compound across the fleet faster than on a long single haul.

Terminal time also improves. A quiet, zero-local-emission tractor is welcome in inner-yard movement, and the predictable charging plan lets a dispatcher stack vessel cut-offs against depot windows without guessing fuel stops. The 8-year battery warranty turns the largest unknown, pack life, into a fixed planning input.

Charging and battery swap at the yard

For a heavy haul operator that cannot wait 150 minutes for a DC charge, the TE8P supports battery swap in 5-6 minutes at an equipped yard. A port operator can site a swap cabinet and keep tractors rotating through a double shift without a charge lull.

  • Overnight DC charging at the inland yard for the first shift.
  • Battery swap at the terminal side for rapid turnaround.
  • Regenerative capture on the loaded return to extend range.
  • Telematics that schedule charging on off-peak tariff windows.
  • Spare pack strategy so one swap unit serves several tractors.

Import duty outlook

Brazil's import framework for fully built electric trucks carries duty and tax considerations that differ from diesel, and these can move the landed cost materially. A fleet planner should model the FOB price against the current import regime and any state-level incentive for zero-emission commercial vehicles before committing a purchase order.

The hedge is TCO: even with a duty-loaded landed cost, the per-move energy and maintenance saving of a 600 kWh electric tractor can recover the gap over the contract, and a worsening diesel price environment only strengthens that case. Fleet procurement and deployment steps

A Santos operator should start with a measured pilot at 120 t GCW on the real drayage loop, because combination weight and gate queuing, not distance, set the energy number for the TE8P. Two weeks of telemetry will confirm whether the 600 kWh pack covers a full shift of heavy moves with margin and how much energy the loaded return recovers, which is the figure that separates a profitable electric yard from a marginal one once diesel and duty volatility are stripped out of the comparison.

Yard readiness is the second step. A port operator pairing a DC charger with a battery swap cabinet can keep tractors rotating through a double shift without a 150-minute charge gap. The right mix depends on move volume and terminal dwell, not on the headline pack size. A swap strategy also lets one spare pack serve several TE8P units, which lowers capital outlay compared with buying a charger for every tractor and keeps the per-move cost stable across the ownership cycle.

Operator training is the third step and is often overlooked. The LvKong motor delivers relentless low-speed torque, but crews used to diesel shunt need coaching on smooth pull-away and speed-set control so the regeneration benefit is captured rather than lost to hard braking. A short yard module improves gate safety, reduces tire scuff and protects the 120 t combination on the terminal approach, which matters under a tight vessel cut-off schedule.

Financing the switch should be modeled on cost per container move across the contract, including the import duty outlook. With the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty removing the largest unknown, the per-move case is stable enough for a terminal operator to defend to ownership, and the removed diesel engine service plus extended brake life are real recurring savings that compound across a high-cycle port where tractors run long hours.

Finally, phase the rollout. Introduce one or two TE8P units on the most regular drayage first, validate the charging and swap plan against telemetry, then scale.
