TZ3V Electric Dump Truck for Cerro de Pasco Mining, Peru

The TZ3V 600 kWh electric dump truck is engineered for the punishing haul roads of Cerro de Pasco, where mines sit above 4,300 m and a diesel engine quietly loses output with every meter of climb. Why Cerro de Pasco Mining Demands an Electric Dump Truck

Cerro de Pasco is one of the highest large mining districts on earth, and thin air is the enemy of diesel productivity. A naturally aspirated or even turbocharged diesel loses roughly 3.5% of its power for every 300 m of elevation gain, which means at 4,300 m a diesel dump truck can surrender close to half its rated output. The TZ3V sidesteps that physics entirely: its LvKong permanent magnet motor delivers rated torque regardless of altitude, so payloads and cycle times stay constant. For a mine where every haul counts, losing no power is a direct gain in tons moved per shift.

High Altitude Performance and Haul Cycle

On a 4,300 m haul road, the TZ3V maintains full motor output while a comparable diesel unit is derated by altitude. The regenerative braking system also recaptures energy on the descent back to the pit, which is where electric dump trucks earn back a large share of their energy cost. Operators report steadier climb speeds and fewer gear-hunting moments on steep grades, which improves both safety and round-trip time on a busy mine road.

Charging at the Mine Site

Mines can install DC fast charging at the maintenance bay and top the 600 kWh pack during shift changes and blasting stops. The 20 to 80% window fills in about 60 to 90 minutes, which aligns with natural downtime. For continuous two-shift operations, some sites add a second charger so one truck charges while its twin hauls. Because the thermal system is liquid cooled, the pack stays inside its comfort band even when ambient temperatures swing widely between day and night at altitude.

Operator Safety and Cabin

The TZ3V cab prioritizes visibility and restraint on rough haul roads, with reinforced structure and a low-noise interior that reduces fatigue across a 12-hour mining shift. Regenerative braking lowers wear on the friction brakes, which matters on long loaded descents where brake fade is a real risk for conventional trucks. Fewer hot exhaust components also reduce fire risk around combustible mine dust, an overlooked safety advantage of going electric underground and at surface.
