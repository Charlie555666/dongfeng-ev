Charging is the foundation of any electric fleet

The trucks are only half of an electric fleet; the charging infrastructure is the other half, and it is where most implementations succeed or fail. For a TZ3Z fleet in Ethiopia, the right charging design depends on the duty cycle, the site’s power supply and the tariff. Get it right and the fleet runs smoothly on cheap energy; get it wrong and the trucks sit waiting. We treat charging design as part of the vehicle specification, not an afterthought.

AC versus DC: choosing the right mix

Most charging should be AC, done overnight when the trucks are parked and power is cheap. For a TZ3Z with 350-422 kWh, an AC charge over several hours covers a normal landlocked import logistics and construction day. DC fast charging, which takes roughly 20-80% of the pack in the time stated in the spec, is for the occasions when a truck must return to service quickly. A typical Ethiopia depot runs a majority of AC points with a small number of DC chargers as a buffer.

Sizing the site power supply

The electrical supply is the constraint most often overlooked. Charging a fleet of TZ3Z trucks overnight draws a substantial, sustained load, and the site’s transformer may need upgrade. Smart charge management spreads the load across the night to stay within the existing supply, avoiding an expensive upgrade. For Ethiopian customers in Addis Ababa, Adama and Djibouti corridor, we assess the site supply early and design a charging plan that fits it.

Public and corridor charging

Not every charge happens at base. For longer routes, Ethiopia fleets rely on public or partner chargers along the corridor. The practical approach is to plan routes around known reliable chargers, keep a partner depot as a fallback, and never assume a charger is working without a phone call. On most TZ3Z duty cycles, public charging is the exception, which keeps both cost and range anxiety low.

Plug types, standards and compatibility

Charging standards vary by region, and the TZ3Z can be supplied with the plug and protocol your market uses — CCS2, GB/T or a market-specific combination. Getting the connector right at order stage avoids an adapter that can slow charging or, worse, fail to work. For Ethiopia buyers, we confirm the intended charging network before shipping so the truck and the infrastructure speak the same language from day one.

Operating and maintaining the charging installation

A charging installation is a piece of plant that needs care. Cables, connectors and the charger electronics require periodic inspection, and connectors should be kept clean and dry. In dusty or hot Addis Ababa, Adama and Djibouti corridor environments, this matters more. We include a simple maintenance schedule with every charger we supply and provide remote diagnostics so faults are spotted before they strand a truck.

Why Ethiopia is ready for electric trucks now

The conditions that make an electric truck viable are all present in Ethiopia. an outright ban on importing combustion vehicles and very cheap hydro power. Freight demand concentrates in and around Addis Ababa, Adama and Djibouti corridor, where routes are short and predictable and the depot is never far away. Fuel logistics add cost and delay that a fleet charging its own vehicles simply avoids. For a TZ3Z electric dump truck on a landlocked import logistics and construction duty cycle, this is not an experimental technology but a practical replacement for diesel, and the operators moving first are the ones who lock in the lowest cost per kilometre before their competitors do.

A step-by-step implementation plan

Electrifying a fleet in Ethiopia is a project, and running it in phases is what keeps it manageable. Phase one is a site and route audit: list every duty cycle, measure real daily distance and load, and map the depot power supply. Phase two is a pilot of two to five TZ3Z units on the most predictable routes, with chargers installed and drivers trained. Phase three is measurement — energy cost per kilometre, uptime, maintenance hours. Phase four is scaling what the data supports. Each phase de-risks the next and keeps the capital commitment matched to proven performance rather than optimism.

Choosing the right configuration

Within the TZ3Z range there are choices that matter more than cosmetics. The 6x4 layout suits the axle load and traction pattern of your duty cycle. Battery capacity of 350-422 kWh should be sized to the longest realistic daily route plus a safety margin, not to the biggest number on the brochure. Body and equipment specification — tipper, box, reefer, tank or compactor — should be matched precisely to the job in Ethiopia. Getting these four decisions right at order stage is far cheaper than modifying a truck after it lands.
