KT7A vs KT3F: Choosing Within the Water Range

Both are electric water trucks; they are not interchangeable:

  • KT3F — 166/200 kWh, 128/260 kW. Lower-pressure, higher-volume distribution: road watering, dust suppression, haul road and site work. Optimised for coverage.
  • KT7A — 232 kWh, 164/308 kW. Higher-pressure, heavier chassis: washing, degreasing, hard-surface cleaning, apron work. Optimised for cleaning power.

A simple test: if the job is putting water on a surface, it is KT3F. If the job is removing material from a surface, it is KT7A. Many municipal fleets run both, and the combined specification is covered in our water tank body engineering guide.

Decision 1: Pump Pressure and Flow

High-pressure washing is defined by two parameters, and specifiers routinely confuse them:

  1. Pressure (bar) determines what you can remove. Light street film needs 60–100 bar; baked-on deposits, oil and port contamination need 150–250 bar.
  2. Flow (litres per minute) determines how fast you cover area. High pressure with low flow cleans slowly.

The product of the two is power demand, and this is where the pack decision is made. A pump delivering 150 bar at 200 L/min requires roughly 55–70 kW of shaft power — continuous. Over a six-hour washing shift that is 330–420 kWh, which exceeds the 232 kWh pack. The operational answer, as with concrete mixers, is duty-cycled operation: washing is intermittent, with travel and repositioning between passes, and the real average duty lands at 25–45% of peak pump load.

Decision 2: Tank Capacity and Refill Logistics

Water weight governs the specification: 1,000 litres equals one tonne.

  • Confirm GVW and axle limits for the roads the vehicle will use, then subtract chassis and body tare.
  • Check both binding cases — rear axle when full, front axle when empty.
  • Plan refill points. At high-pressure flow rates a tank empties in 15–35 minutes. Without a hydrant or bowser plan, the vehicle spends more time fetching water than washing.
  • Consider water recycling where available: some municipal depots recover wash water, which changes the logistics entirely.

Decision 4: Chassis Package for Wet, Hot Duty

Washing trucks operate in the worst environment in a municipal fleet — continuous water, often salt or chemical contamination, and in Gulf markets extreme heat:

  • Corrosion protection on chassis, tank mounts and body — non-negotiable for coastal cities. See corrosion protection for tropical and coastal duty.
  • Sealed electrical connectors rated for continuous wet operation.
  • Thermal package for high ambient — washing duty is low-speed with little airflow through the cooling pack.
  • Night-operation lighting, since most municipal washing is scheduled outside business hours.
  • Cab filtration and demisting — a wet cab with poor demisting is a safety issue, not a comfort issue.

Energy Budget

Representative 9-hour municipal washing shift:

  • Traction: 70–110 km at 0.85–1.10 kWh/km = 60–120 kWh.
  • High-pressure pump, duty-cycled: 18–28 kW average = 160–250 kWh.
  • Cab HVAC and lighting: 2–4 kW = 18–36 kWh.
  • Total: 238–406 kWh.

The upper end exceeds the 232 kWh pack, which makes one operational point essential: for continuous high-pressure shifts, plan either a mid-shift opportunity charge of 30–40 minutes, or specify the washing pattern so pump duty is genuinely intermittent. We model this against the customer's actual route before quoting, because it is the specification decision most likely to cause disappointment if guessed.

Gulf Deployment

Gulf municipalities run heavy street washing programmes because sand accumulation on carriageways is continuous, and the working window is night or pre-dawn to avoid traffic and heat. The KT7A is well suited: quiet enough for residential-adjacent night work, zero emissions in street canyons, and a thermal package specified for 45 °C operation. Bottom Line

Specify the KT7A by cleaning task first — pressure and flow requirement — then size the tank to GVW and the refill plan, then confirm the energy model against a realistic pump duty cycle. Done in that order, the vehicle covers a full municipal shift on one overnight charge, removes a diesel engine and its entire service schedule from the fleet, and works the night windows that modern city cleaning schedules depend on.
