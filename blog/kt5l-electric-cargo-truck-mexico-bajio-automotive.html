The Milk-Run Profile

Automotive milk runs are the most disciplined freight in the world: fixed routes, fixed windows, fixed loads of racks and containers. A KT5L on a Bajio supplier loop runs 150-250 km per day across 3-6 plant deliveries, with dock waiting time at each gate. The 348 kWh pack covers the longest of these days on one overnight charge with 20% reserve; the dock waiting that punishes diesel - engines idling at plant gates - costs the electric truck almost nothing.

Box volume matters as much as weight in this duty: auto parts cube out before they weigh out. The KT5L's 65-70 m3 body capacity with body lengths from 7,700 to 9,800 mm matches the standard rack and pallet configurations of the sector, and the tail-lift and side-door options fit plant dock conventions.

Charging at the Logistics Base

The shuttle pattern makes charging simple: trucks return to the logistics centre or carrier depot every evening and leave before dawn - a 10-12 hour window that overnight AC or low-power DC fills easily. One 120 kW DC unit covers exceptions and the peak-season double runs. Mexican industrial tariffs keep the overnight energy cost low, and several Bajio industrial parks now offer embedded renewable supply contracts that sharpen both the cost and the carbon number that OEM customers request.

  • Number every bay and post; milk-run discipline collapses when a truck misses its charge.
  • Charge to 90% for standard loops; the weekly long loop gets the full charge.
  • Meter kWh per loop; a drifting number on a fixed route flags issues before they strand a delivery window.
  • Coordinate the charge window with the plant's own peak tariff hours - overnight avoids them entirely.

Altitude and Climate

The Bajio sits at 1,800-2,000 m - altitude that cuts diesel engine power by 15-20% and does nothing to an electric motor. The KT5L holds full performance on the region's grades where equivalent diesels labour. The temperate highland climate is gentle on the battery: 15-28 C for most of the year, minimal seasonal range variation, and none of the extreme-heat management that coastal Mexico demands. It is, in EV terms, some of the easiest operating territory in Latin America.

Economics and the OEM Carbon Question

The financial case: energy at Mexican industrial tariff runs $0.15-0.18 per km against $0.50-0.58 for a diesel rigid; on 70,000 km per year of milk-run duty, that is $24,000-30,000 saved per truck annually, with maintenance halved. The strategic case may matter more: automotive OEMs now cascade scope-3 decarbonization requirements to their logistics providers, and a verifiable electric fleet increasingly appears in contract award criteria.
