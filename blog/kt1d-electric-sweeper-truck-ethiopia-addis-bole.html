Why Bole district needs a quiet electric sweeper

Bole is Addis Ababa's commercial and diplomatic hub, with avenues that must be cleaned before the 07:00 rush but sit inside dense residential blocks where diesel sweepers draw noise complaints. A diesel sweeper burns 12 to 16 litres per hour of idling sweep duty, costing $13 to $18 per hour at Ethiopia's diesel price near $1.10 per litre, and runs at 80 to 85 dB. The KT1D sweeps at under 70 dB and draws grid electricity at $0.07 to $0.16 per kWh, so an 8-hour shift costs a fraction of the diesel equivalent while letting the district start at 23:00 instead of dawn.

The second benefit is air quality. Sweeping generates dust, and a diesel unit adds exhaust on top; the KT1D's electric brooms and vacuum keep the street clean without a local emissions plume that residents can see and smell.

Duty cycle: a full overnight shift

A Bole shift runs 23:00 to 07:00, covering 40 to 60 km of avenue at 6 to 10 km/h sweep speed. The KT1D draws about 18 to 26 kWh per hour of active sweeping, so an 8-hour shift with breaks uses 180 to 260 kWh, comfortably inside the pack. At $0.10 per kWh the energy cost is $18 to $26 per night, against $100 to $140 of diesel for a comparable unit. The quiet operation lets the district add the busy Bole Road and airport corridor to the night route without resident objections.

Regenerative braking on the gentle Addis grades and the constant stop-go of block cleaning recovers a small but useful 5 to 8 percent, and the flat torque makes the side brooms steadier than a diesel hydraulic drive.

Total cost of ownership for an Ethiopian municipality

Over a 5-year, 80,000-hour-equivalent life, the KT1D wins on three lines:

  • Energy: electricity at $0.05 to $0.18 per kWh versus diesel at $0.9 to $1.4 per litre — a 65 to 75 percent lower hourly cost.
  • Maintenance: no engine oil, no exhaust system, fewer brake jobs — about 35 percent lower service spend.
  • Compliance: the 70 dB rating meets Addis Ababa's night-noise rules that diesel sweepers breach.

A 10-truck district fleet avoids roughly $250,000 in fuel and $120,000 in maintenance over five years, against an FOB outlay of $550,000 to $700,000 at $55,000 to $70,000 each. The payback is 28 to 36 months, after which the silent night cleaning becomes a pure budget gain.
