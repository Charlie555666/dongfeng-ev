The Lusail Mixer Cycle

Ready-mix work around Lusail runs plant-to-pour loops of 8-25 km, with 30-60 minutes of discharge and pump waiting per load. A TZ8J completes 6-9 loads per shift; total shift energy - drum drive, hydraulics, traction and waiting - consumes 55-70% of the pack. The decisive difference from diesel appears at the pump: a diesel mixer idles at 8-12 litres per hour keeping the drum turning, while the electric truck's drum motor draws a modest constant load. Across a 45-minute pour wait, that saves 5-8 litres of diesel-equivalent energy per load - multiplied by 6-9 loads per truck per day.

Night pours near completed residential and hotel zones are the other structural advantage: the near-silent electric mixer meets noise restrictions that constrain diesel fleets, unlocking working windows that compress project schedules.

Plant-Based Charging

The batching plant anchors charging: it holds the site's largest power service, and mixers return every 60-120 minutes regardless of charging needs. One 240 kW DC unit per 4-5 mixers, positioned on the washout lane, turns existing cycle time into charging time - a 15-20 minute plug-in per loop restores 8-12% of the pack, and overnight slow charging sets the morning baseline. Qatari industrial tariffs keep the energy cost per cubic metre delivered near one-third of the diesel figure.

  • Site chargers on the washout lane; every truck passes it after every pour.
  • Charge in slices during loading; reserve full charges for the overnight window.
  • Meter kWh per m3 delivered; Lusail duty should hold 2.2-2.8 kWh/m3-km by loop length.
  • Keep one diesel mixer on standby during the first month of pour-critical schedules.

Gulf Summer Operation

Doha's summer - 42-48 C with coastal humidity - tests every system on a mixer: drum hydraulics, cab cooling, and now battery thermal management. The TZ8J's liquid-cooled pack and motor hold performance through afternoon pours, with an honest 8-10% summer range allowance built into shift planning. The scheduled-midday-charge pattern works with the climate: the hottest hours coincide with the plugged-in window at the plant, and night pours - a Gulf concrete convention - run cooler and longer per charge.

Economics and Tender Position
