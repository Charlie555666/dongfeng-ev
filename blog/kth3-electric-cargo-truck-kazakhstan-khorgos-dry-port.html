KTH3 Electric Cargo Truck at Khorgos Dry Port, Kazakhstan

At Khorgos dry port, where Chinese and Kazakh freight converges on the border and winter temperatures drop to minus 20 C, fleet operators are finding that a heavy electric cargo truck can out-earn a diesel unit on the cross-border corridor. The KTH3 heavy electric cargo truck mounts a 424 kWh CATL LFP battery with an LvKong motor up to 510 kW, delivering an EV truck that runs the Almaty-to-Khorgos shuttle on a single charge even in deep cold.

Why the Khorgos corridor suits electric cargo

The Khorgos gateway handles tens of thousands of containers a month, and most of that volume moves on a predictable 300-400 km loop between Almaty, the dry port and back. That fixed, repeatable route is exactly what a battery-electric truck needs: a depot at each end, a known distance, and a return load that lets regenerative braking recover energy on the descending leg. Diesel tractor units on this loop burn 0.48-0.52 L/km, and at Kazakh diesel prices near $0.95/L that is roughly $0.46/km in fuel before maintenance.

Electric operation flips that math. At a depot electricity rate of $0.09-0.14/kWh and an energy use of 1.4-1.6 kWh/km loaded, the KTH3 costs about $0.20/km in energy - less than half the diesel figure. On a 350 km round trip that saves roughly $90 per run, and a two-shift truck completing two loops a day banks close to $180 daily in fuel alone.

Cold-weather package for minus 20 C operation

  • Battery liquid heating keeps cell temperature above 10 C during minus 20 C nights so range loss stays under 15%.
  • Cabin heat is drawn from the high-voltage pack through a heat pump, not a diesel burner, saving 8-12 L/day.
  • Low-temperature tyres and reinforced suspension are specified for icy border-yard manoeuvring.
  • The BMS pre-conditions the pack while still on depot shore power, so the first cold-mile efficiency is protected.
  • Connectors and wiring use cold-rated insulation rated to minus 40 C for reliable winter starts.

Maintenance savings: what 35-40% really means

An electric cargo truck removes the diesel engine's oil, filters, injectors, turbo, EGR and exhaust after-treatment - the parts that drive most unscheduled border-truck downtime. Our fleet data shows EV trucks cut scheduled maintenance by 35-40% versus a diesel equivalent, which on a cargo unit running 250 days a year is roughly $4,000 in saved workshop bills. The LvKong motor's sealed drivetrain also shrugs off minus 20 C cold starts that crack diesel blocks and thicken fluids, so winter reliability is a bonus rather than a risk.

  • No engine oil changes: save about $1,300 per year in oil and filters.
  • No injector or turbo service: save about $1,100 per year.
  • Brake wear cut by regenerative braking on the descending leg: save about $900 per year.
  • Longer tyre life from smooth electric torque: save about $600 per year.
