Why Heat Reduces EV Truck Range

A lithium battery is most efficient near 25 C to 35 C. Above that, the management system limits charge and discharge power to protect the cells, which trims usable energy and slows acceleration. At 45 C ambient, an uncooled 424 kWh CATL LFP pack can shed 10 to 15 percent of its available range simply to stay safe. How Liquid Cooling Works on a Dongfeng EV Truck

The pack sits in a sealed enclosure with cooling plates between the modules. A low-conductivity coolant loop carries heat to a radiator with an electric fan, holding cell temperature in the 25 C to 35 C band even when the truck is parked in sun. The key parts are listed below.

  • Cooling plates bonded to each module for even heat pickup.
  • Low-conductivity coolant rated for the 25 C to 35 C loop.
  • Electric water pump running whenever the pack is above 30 C.
  • Rooftop or front radiator with a variable speed fan.
  • Battery management system that logs cell temperature every second.

The same loop also warms the pack in winter, so the KTH3 electric cargo truck performs in both 50 C summer and 0 C winter without range surprise. On a 45 C afternoon the pump may run continuously, drawing about 0.5 to 1.0 kW, a small price for keeping the pack in its happy band and protecting the 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty.

Coolant Service and Monitoring

The cooling loop needs care but little cost. Technicians check coolant concentration and conductivity at every service, replace it around 4 years or 200,000 km, and watch for pump faults through the dashboard. A failed pump shows as a rising pack temperature warning well before damage, giving the driver time to reach a charger or shade. Because the battery itself needs no fluid, this is the main climate-related service item on the entire electric truck. The gear oil at 40,000 to 80,000 km and the chassis grease at 20,000 km are unchanged by climate, so the cooling loop is the one desert-specific task on the plan.
