Callao Port Gate Schedules and Route Math

The Port of Callao operates gate appointments in windows that often force trucks to queue before dawn, and a diesel unit burns fuel idling in that line. The KT5M simply holds climate and lights on stored energy, then drives the 14 km to a Lima logistics park on a single charge. A typical distribution loop is 60 to 90 km round trip with 12 to 18 drops, which the 282 kWh pack covers with 40 percent reserve even in Lima's 1,500 m altitude, where diesel engines lose roughly 15 percent of their power.

Altitude is a quiet advantage for the KT5M: an electric motor loses no power at 1,500 m, so a loaded climb out of Callao to the eastern districts is identical in performance to sea level. Diesel rivals need turbo boost and lose torque, forcing downshifts that raise fuel burn and wear.
