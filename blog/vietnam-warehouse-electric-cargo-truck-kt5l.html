The Warehouse Shuttle Duty Cycle

Warehouse shuttle work - distribution center to satellite warehouse, DC to store, cross-dock transfers - is one of the most electric-friendly duty cycles in road transport. Distances are short and known, typically 30-150 km per round trip; vehicles return to a fixed base daily; schedules repeat week after week. This predictability removes the two classic objections to electric trucks: range uncertainty and charging access. The KT5L, with 260 km of certified range from a 140 kWh CATL LFP pack, covers multiple shuttle cycles per day with margin to spare, then charges overnight at the depot it already returns to. For Vietnamese logistics operators, shuttle routes are the obvious first electrification target.

Why Electric Trucks Fit DC-to-Store Work

Beyond range fit, shuttle duty exploits every electric strength. Stop-start urban running between warehouses is where diesel engines waste the most fuel and where regenerative braking recovers the most energy - the 160 kW motor on the KT5L electric cargo truck harvests braking energy at every traffic light. Noise matters too: early-morning and night deliveries to urban stores face tightening restrictions across Ho Chi Minh City, and a near-silent electric truck turns restricted hours into usable delivery windows. Payload is preserved because shuttle loads are typically volumetric rather than weight-limited. And the cost per kilometer drops by 60-75% on energy alone, which compounds across the dozens of weekly cycles a shuttle fleet accumulates.

Charging Strategy for Shuttle Fleets

Shuttle fleets rarely need public charging at all. The baseline strategy is overnight depot charging at the lowest tariff, sized so every truck starts the day at full charge. Where daily distance approaches the 260 km range limit, a midday opportunity charge of 30-60 minutes on a DC fast charger bridges the gap without changing the route plan. Charger count follows shift patterns, not truck count: a single-shift fleet of ten trucks typically needs two to three DC chargers plus overnight points, while a two-shift operation staggers charging across the changeover. In Vietnam, depot power upgrades should be confirmed with the utility early - transformer lead times, not charger prices, are the usual schedule risk.

Route and Shift Planning in Ho Chi Minh City

Shuttle electrification succeeds or fails in route planning, and the data needed already exists in any fleet's dispatch records. Map each shuttle lane by distance, payload, traffic window and return time; rank them by total daily kilometers; electrify the longest, most regular lanes first because fuel savings scale with utilization. In Ho Chi Minh City, congestion patterns matter: electric trucks lose almost nothing idling in traffic, which flips the usual diesel logic - the worst-congested routes often deliver the biggest savings. Driver shifts should align charging breaks with natural pauses - loading, paperwork, meal windows - so charging time never sits on the critical path. Telematics on the KT5L reports actual energy per lane, letting dispatchers refine assignments monthly.

Cost per Trip: Electric vs Diesel Shuttle

Shuttle economics are simple arithmetic. Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the KT5L earns acceptance quickly. The 160 kW motor delivers instant torque with no gear hunting, the cab is quieter than any diesel equivalent, and regenerative braking reduces fatigue in stop-start duty. Drivers typically adapt within a week, and most prefer the electric truck once they experience a full shift without engine noise and vibration. The habits that matter are small: smooth acceleration, anticipation to maximize regeneration, charging connection on return to depot, and honest reporting of dash warnings. Fleets in Vietnam report the fastest adoption when they involve senior drivers in the pilot phase and let peer experience do the convincing, rather than imposing the change top-down. Energy consumption per driver is visible in the telematics data, and a little friendly competition between drivers on efficiency measurably improves fleet-wide consumption in the first quarter.

Charging Planning for KT5L Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT5L supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 140 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Vietnam should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
