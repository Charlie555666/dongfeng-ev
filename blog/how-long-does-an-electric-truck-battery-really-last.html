How Long Does an Electric Truck Battery Really Last? An 8-Year Warranty Explained

Quick answer: The honest answer depends on duty cycle, but for a TE9L used on a typical giga-project construction and city logistics cycle in Saudi Arabia, the 420-466 kWh CATL LFP pack is warranted for 8 years or 4,500 cycles to 70% state of health, and real-world operators routinely exceed that. Most fleets replace the truck before they replace the battery.

What determines how long the battery lasts

Three factors decide pack life: chemistry, temperature and how it is charged. The TE9L uses LFP chemistry, the most thermally stable and cycle-tolerant mainstream option. Its liquid-cooled thermal management keeps cells in a comfortable band even in hot Saudi Arabia conditions. And keeping daily state of charge between 20% and 80%, rather than routinely hitting the extremes, is the single biggest lever an operator controls.

How the 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty works

The traction battery on a TE9L carries a separate warranty from the chassis: 8 years or 4,500 charge cycles, whichever comes first, to a floor of 70% state of health. In a giga-project construction and city logistics duty cycle in Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam, that is well beyond the first service life of the vehicle, which is why battery replacement is not a realistic cost for most fleet operators within their holding period.

What happens after 8 years

When a pack eventually falls below the traction threshold, it is not scrap. A retired 420-466 kWh pack still holds most of its capacity for gentler duty and is well suited to stationary storage or depot solar buffering. This second life, before recycling, preserves value for the operator and makes the lifecycle genuinely circular.

How to make your battery last longer

Shade the truck when parked in hot climates. Pre-condition the cab while still plugged in. Make depot AC charging the backbone and reserve DC fast charging for genuinely long days. Keep the cooling loop clean. Download the BMS health log monthly so degradation is visible early. Follow these and the pack will comfortably outlast the truck.

A step-by-step implementation plan

Electrifying a fleet in Saudi Arabia is a project, and running it in phases is what keeps it manageable. Phase one is a site and route audit: list every duty cycle, measure real daily distance and load, and map the depot power supply. Phase two is a pilot of two to five TE9L units on the most predictable routes, with chargers installed and drivers trained. Phase three is measurement — energy cost per kilometre, uptime, maintenance hours. Phase four is scaling what the data supports. Each phase de-risks the next and keeps the capital commitment matched to proven performance rather than optimism.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an electric truck battery really last?

A TE9L pack is warranted for 8 years or 4,500 cycles to 70% state of health. Real-world fleets usually see the truck retire before the pack reaches that threshold.

Should I charge to 100% every day?

No. For daily duty keep state of charge between 20% and 80%. Reserve 100% charging for occasional long runs, and do not leave the pack sitting full for days.

How much range does the TE9L lose over time?

With careful operation, capacity loss is gradual — often only a few percent per year. Fast charging in high heat accelerates it, so depot AC charging should be the default.

Is fast charging bad for the battery?

Occasional DC fast charging is fine and the TE9L supports it. Frequent fast charging at high temperature adds wear, so it is best used as an exception rather than the routine.

What is the battery warranty on a TE9L?

The traction battery is covered for 8 years or 4,500 cycles to 70% state of health, separate from the 3-year / 150,000 km chassis and drivetrain warranty.
