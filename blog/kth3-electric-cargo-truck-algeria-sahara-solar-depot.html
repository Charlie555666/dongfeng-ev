The Sahara Freight Profile

Interior Algeria freight runs long, flat and hot: Algiers to Ghardaia is about 600 km on the N1, with sustained highway speeds and summer temperatures past 45 C. The full corridor electrifies in segments - the immediate opportunity is the interior distribution work: Ghardaia-region distribution, oilfield supply shuttles between bases, and city logistics in the southern wilayas, all patterns of 150-300 km per day returning to a fixed depot. That is one-charge territory for the KTH3, and the depot sits under some of the world's best solar irradiation at 2,000-2,300 kWh/m2 per year.

Energy consumption on the flat interior highways runs 1.3-1.5 kWh/km loaded at 31-38 t gross, which means a 400 kWh pack covers 260-300 km of loaded summer duty with reserve - a full day's interior distribution on one overnight charge.

The Solar Depot Design

ComponentSizing for a 10-truck KTH3 fleetNotes
Solar canopy250-350 kWp over the truck parkingShade value alone justifies the structure
Buffer battery500-800 kWh stationary LFPShifts solar day into truck-charging night
Chargers2 x 120 kW DC + overnight AC postsStaggered overnight charging for 10 trucks
Grid connectionRetained as backupHybrid design, solar-first dispatch

The design logic: trucks work by day and charge by night, while solar peaks by day - so the buffer battery is the bridge, charging from the canopy through the afternoon and discharging into the fleet overnight. In Algerian interior irradiation, a 300 kWp canopy with 600 kWh of buffer covers 70-85% of a 10-truck fleet's annual energy, with the grid filling the rest.

  • Orient the canopy as truck shade first and generation second - both pay back in the Sahara.
  • Size the buffer for the overnight fleet charge, not the midday peak.
  • Keep the grid connection; solar-first does not mean solar-only.
  • Meter solar kWh versus grid kWh per truck - the split drives the expansion decision.

Heat Management at 45-48 C

Sahara summer is the extreme thermal case, and it is where liquid cooling proves itself. The KTH3's pack loop holds cells at 20-35 C while ambient air approaches 50 C; charging speed and motor output stay intact through afternoon heat that derates air-cooled equipment. The canopy's shade measurably helps the parked trucks - cabin heat soak drops, and pre-cooling energy falls. Plan summer range at 260-280 km loaded rather than 300; the honest number builds dispatch confidence.

Economics: Sunlight Versus Diesel

Algerian diesel is inexpensive by global standards but not free, and the solar comparison is striking: energy from the canopy costs the depreciation of the hardware - effectively $0.03-0.05 per kWh over 20 years - against diesel at $0.50-0.60 per km equivalent. On 80,000 km per year, a solar-fed KTH3 saves $35,000-45,000 per truck annually in energy alone. The solar infrastructure pays back in 4-6 years while the trucks pay back in under three.
