What "Entry Level" Means Here — and What It Doesn't

The KTH1 reaches its price point by fitting the smaller battery in the family: 264 kWh against the 352 kWh of the KTH2 and 400 kWh of the KTH3 flagship. Everything else stays full-grade:

  • The drivetrain is identical in class to its bigger siblings — LvKong 270/450 kW, the same 8x4 chassis architecture, the same 4AMT and the same dual-gun charging system. A KTH1 driver stepping into a KTH3 notices a smaller SOC gauge, nothing else.
  • The battery is the same CATL LFP chemistry with the 8-year/4,500-cycle warranty. Entry level means fewer kilowatt-hours, not lesser cells.
  • The chassis is the proven heavy platform — this is a 31 t truck built for steel, timber, brick, pallets and bagged cargo, not a light-duty chassis stretched to look heavy.

The Duty Cycles It Fits Best

The 264 kWh pack defines a discipline: daily distances under ~200 km. In practice that captures a large share of heavy rigid work:

  1. Industrial park shuttles. Plant-to-plant movement of components and packaging — fixed 20–60 km loops, return-to-base every night, single-shift charging. The single most common KTH1 application we ship.
  2. Intra-city building supply. Brick, cement, steel and timber from rail depots to construction districts under 100 km round trip.
  3. Port-side distribution. Container stripping to warehouse within the port zone — very high stop-start, very low kilometrage, where diesel idling waste is worst and electric advantage largest.
  4. FMCG trunking on short lanes. Distribution centre to city hub legs of 60–90 km with depot charging at both ends.

Real Economics at 31 t

Industrial shuttle profile: 150 km/day, 300 days, 45,000 km/year. Diesel 8x4 consumption at 33 L/100 km: 14,850 L — USD 13,000–16,500 at USD 0.90–1.10/L. The KTH1 at roughly 1.2 kWh/km: 54,000 kWh — USD 4,300–5,900 at USD 0.08–0.11/kWh. Maintenance difference adds another USD 3,500–5,000 per year in the electric column's favour. Annual operating advantage: USD 12,000–16,000. Against a purchase premium of roughly USD 40,000 over an equivalent diesel rigid, the KTH1 pays back in year 3 and compounds for the remaining life of the asset — with the CATL pack warranty running the full eight years.

Body Options and the 8x4 Advantage

The 8x4 layout carries more body and distributes weight better than a 6x2 at the same GVW, which matters for dense cargo like bagged cement and steel. We build to order: curtainsiders for pallets, steel-mesh cages for scrap and billets, flatbeds with headboards for timber and lengths, and dry boxes for bagged goods. The chassis PTO provisions support tail lifts and side-loaders. For operators wanting the same formula with more range, the KTH2 (352 kWh) and KTH3 (400 kWh, 9.8 m box capability) step up inside the identical family — a KTH1 fleet can grow without retraining drivers or re-specifying chargers.

Honest Limitations

  • Range discipline. Beyond 200 km/day, order the KTH2 or KTH3 instead. Stretching a 264 kWh pack to its limit daily accelerates cycle accumulation for no economic gain.
  • Fast-charge dependence for double shifts. A two-shift operation needs a mid-day 40-minute top-up; budget the DC charger accordingly.
  • Hilly terrain reduces range 10–15% on laden climbs; spec the battery for your worst route, not your average one.

Why the KTH1 Is Often the Right First Electric Truck

Fleets electrify successfully by starting on their most predictable route, not their hardest. The KTH1 puts a full-grade 31 t electric truck on that first route at the lowest entry cost in our heavy range, generates the kWh/km data and driver familiarity that de-risk every subsequent purchase, and remains a useful truck even after the fleet grows around it.
