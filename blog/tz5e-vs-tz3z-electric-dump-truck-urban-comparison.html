TZ5E vs TZ3Z Electric Dump Truck: Picking the Right EV Tip Truck for Urban Construction

On a specification sheet the Dongfeng TZ5E and TZ3Z look almost identical: both 6x4, both CATL 400 kWh LFP, both LvKong 315 kW rated / 510 kW peak, both with a 4-speed AMT. Buyers reasonably ask why two exist. The answer is in the axle package, the intended duty and the body options — and while the overlap is real, specifying the wrong one shows up as premature wear or a body that does not suit the material. This comparison explains the difference and gives a decision rule.

Where They Are Identical

  • Battery: CATL LFP 400 kWh, 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty to 80% state of health.
  • Motor: LvKong 315 kW rated / 510 kW peak.
  • Transmission: 4-speed AMT.
  • Charging: dual-gun GB/T DC; 20–80% in roughly 40 minutes on a suitably rated unit; swap-compatible configurations available.
  • Realistic range: 200–260 km on mixed construction duty, varying with load, gradient and road surface.
  • Typical consumables: same tyre sizes, same brake and coolant service regime.

In practice the price bands overlap heavily, so the decision should be made on duty and body rather than on cost.

The Decision Rule

Three questions, in order:

  1. Does the work involve significant public-road running? If yes, lean TZ5E — its package suits mixed site and road duty, and urban compliance matters. See TZ5E and urban emission rules.
  2. Is the material rock, spoil or demolition waste? If yes, lean TZ3Z with a wear-specified rock body. See TZ3Z in city redevelopment.
  3. Is the work night-time, urban and noise-constrained? Both benefit equally from electric operation, but the TZ5E's mixed-road package usually fits city programmes better.

Both sit comfortably inside the 400 kWh pack for single-shift work. Neither needs a larger pack for urban or site duty — if your calculation says otherwise, the constraint is probably road condition or gradient rather than vehicle choice.

Body Specification: The Real Difference

Because the platforms are close, the body is where the specification is won or lost:

  • Spoil and topsoil — standard tipper, steep tipping angle, tailgate sealing for public road. Either model.
  • Rock and demolition waste — wear-resistant plate, reinforced floor and sides, heavier tare. TZ3Z.
  • Asphalt — insulated body, release-agent discipline. TZ5E, per asphalt haulage practice.
  • Recycled aggregates — moderate wear, sealed tailgate. Either model; see aggregates recycling duty.

Full detail in our electric tipper body specification guide.

Charging: Identical Planning

Both models use the same energy model and the same depot approach:

  • Overnight depot charging — 44 kW AC per bay, or a shared 120 kW DC unit per 3–4 trucks.
  • Opportunity charge at shift change for two-shift operations.
  • Battery swap at 5–6 minutes for continuous earthmoving campaigns; see swap station deployment.
  • Smart load management above roughly five trucks on a site, because construction sites usually have constrained temporary supply. See load management.

Maintenance and Running Cost

Effectively identical, with two duty-driven differences:

  • TZ3Z on rock and demolition will consume tyres and body wear parts faster — budget accordingly.
  • TZ5E on public road accumulates more distance per tonne, so tyre and brake wear track mileage rather than cycles.
  • Both benefit from regenerative braking on loaded descents, which on site work meaningfully extends brake life.

Bottom Line

Choose the TZ5E for urban construction, mixed site-and-road work, and programmes with public-road compliance and noise conditions. Choose the TZ3Z for site-based earthworks, spoil and rock haulage, and redevelopment programmes with high cycle counts. Where a fleet does both, run both — the shared platform means one spares inventory, one training programme and one charging strategy across the two.
