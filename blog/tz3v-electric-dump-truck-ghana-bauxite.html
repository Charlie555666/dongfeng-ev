Regen Recovery on Haul Roads

Bauxite pits sit above the processing and rail infrastructure, so the loaded leg is predominantly downhill, the ideal electric profile. The TZ3Z's sibling regen strategy applies here in full: the TZ3V's LvKong motor retards the loaded descent and returns 12 to 18 percent of the cycle's gross energy to the pack, while the friction brakes barely engage. On Ghanaian haul roads operators report net consumption 20 to 30 percent below a flat-route equivalent, and brake lining life extended by half.

The empty return climb consumes the recovered energy, so the pack size of 350 kWh comfortably supports a full shift of 10 to 14 cycles on a 15-kilometer loop without mid-shift charging.

Charging at the Mine Yard

A 240 kW DC charger at the mine yard restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes during shift breaks and overnight. Mines running continuous operation can specify the battery-swap variant, exchanging the pack in 5 to 6 minutes so trucks never wait. Ghana's industrial tariffs make overnight charging the cheapest energy window, and a solar canopy over the yard further cuts daytime draw.

  • Site a 240 kW charger at the mine yard workshop.
  • Choose the swap variant for continuous multi-shift pits.
  • Route loaded legs downhill wherever pit geometry allows.
  • Seal drivetrain housings against bauxite dust ingress.
  • Log energy per tonne to support sustainability audits.
