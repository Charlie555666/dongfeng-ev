Electric Truck Drivetrains: Geared Motor vs Direct Drive Efficiency

Electric truck powertrains come in two flavours: a geared motor that multiplies torque through a reduction box, and a direct-drive motor bolted straight to the axle. For Vietnamese fleets climbing Hai Van Pass or running Hanoi ring-road freight, the choice changes gradeability, efficiency, and service interval. This guide helps a buyer match the right electric truck drivetrain to the lane and understand where each EV truck layout wins.

How the Two Layouts Work

A geared motor spins fast and uses a 2 or 3-speed reduction to deliver high wheel torque at low speed, ideal for steep grades and heavy start loads. A direct-drive motor is a large low-rpm unit turning the axle directly, with no gearbox, fewer parts, and a flat efficiency band at highway speed. The TE8M uses a 2-speed geared LvKong motor rated 420 kW peak, while a comparable direct-drive unit runs 380 kW continuous.

Efficiency Maps and Real Lanes

On the flat Hanoi to Hai Phong corridor at 80 km/h, direct drive holds a 3-point efficiency edge and saves about 4 percent energy, roughly 5 kWh per 100 km. On the 6 to 9 percent grades of Hai Van Pass, the geared motor sustains 85 km/h at full load where direct drive slows to 72 km/h, protecting schedule and reducing the risk of slow-vehicle bottlenecks that trigger escort costs.

Thermal and Maintenance Trade-off

Direct drive wins on simplicity: no gear oil, no reduction bearings, service every 200,000 km. The geared layout adds a sealed reduction box serviced every 120,000 km at about $600 per event. Over a 5-year, 600,000 km life the geared truck needs four services at $2,400 total, a small price for the gradeability it buys on mountain lanes.

Gradeability and Payload

Gradeability is the decisive metric for Vietnamese operators serving central highlands coffee and basalt routes. The geared TE8M climbs a 10 percent grade at 55 km/h with a 40-tonne combination, while direct drive manages 44 km/h. For fleets where the grade leg is under 15 percent of total distance, the highway efficiency of direct drive can still win overall; for grade-heavy routes, geared is clear.

  • Choose geared for mountain, mining, and heavy start-load lanes.
  • Choose direct drive for flat, high-mileage highway freight.
  • Match motor kW to combination mass, not to top speed.
  • Budget gear service every 120,000 km on geared layouts.

Five-Year Cost View

At $0.10 per kWh and 100,000 km per year, a geared tractor on mixed lanes uses about 142,000 kWh annually versus 138,000 for direct drive on flat lanes, a $400 per year gap, while the geared unit avoids one slow-grade escort event worth $300 per trip. Net, the geared layout shows marginally lower total cost on grade-heavy Vietnamese corridors.

Service and Parts Planning

The geared layout's sealed reduction box is serviced every 120,000 km at about $600, and we pre-position the gear set at our regional warehouse so the event is a morning job, not a two-week wait. Direct drive's 200,000 km interval is longer, but its larger motor is heavier to handle, so the workshop still needs the rated crane and the training we provide. Either way the elimination of oil changes, DPF, and exhaust after-treatment removes the bulk of diesel scheduled service, which is the durable saving regardless of layout.
