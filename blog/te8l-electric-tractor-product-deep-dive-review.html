Dongfeng TE8L Electric Tractor: Product Deep Dive for Long-Haul EV Truck Fleets

The Dongfeng TE8L is a 4x2 electric tractor built on Dongfeng's mature e-axis platform and powered by a CATL LFP battery pack of 350-420 kWh. This EV truck is engineered for regional line-haul, combining a LvKong traction motor rated at 410 kW with a real-world range of 280-350 km. Powertrain and CATL LFP battery

At the core of the Dongfeng TE8L sits a CATL lithium-iron-phosphate (LFP) pack. LFP chemistry is chosen deliberately for export markets: it is thermally stable in CIS cross-border freight and copper corridors, tolerant of partial-state-of-charge cycling, and free of cobalt and nickel, which lowers both cost and supply risk. The 350-420 kWh pack delivers the energy density needed for regional line-haul while keeping the centre of gravity low for a 4x2 layout, which improves stability when the body is loaded.

Dongfeng warrants the pack for 8 years or 4,500 full-equivalent cycles - roughly a decade of single-shift mining or construction duty. Even at end-of-warranty the LFP cells typically retain 70-80% capacity, protecting residual value for operators who finance the EV truck over a 5-year term. Because the pack is standardised across the Dongfeng range, second-life stationary storage and module swaps are straightforward, so the battery keeps working long after the truck is refinanced.

LvKong motor and drivetrain

The Dongfeng TE8L uses a LvKong permanent-magnet synchronous motor producing 410 kW of continuous power with strong low-end torque - the trait that makes an electric truck feel quicker than a diesel from a standstill. A 2- or 4-speed AMT (depending on variant) keeps the motor in its efficient band on grades, so the 280-350 km range holds up on regional line-haul rather than only on flat test tracks.

Regenerative braking recovers energy on every descent and every red light, and it extends foundation-brake life - a real maintenance saving on stop-start urban and port duty. The single-reduction final drive has roughly half the rotating parts of a diesel powertrain, which is why uptime improves the moment the EV truck enters service. Field data from similar Dongfeng EVs shows brake-pad life stretching 2-3x versus an equivalent diesel.

Cab, chassis and payload

The 4x2 chassis carries Dongfeng's reinforced EV frame with the battery packaged low between the rails. GCW reaches up to 49 t, and the flat frame leaves room for bodybuilders to mount tippers, mixers, boxes or tank bodies without re-engineering the HV system. Cab ergonomics mirror the diesel sibling, so drivers in Kazakhstan train in a day rather than a week, and familiar controls cut the learning curve on mixed fleets.

The dashboard clusters the traction battery gauge, ambient temperature and energy-recovery indicators in one view, while a secondary screen runs the telematics client. For operators running mixed diesel-and-EV yards, that consistency means one training programme covers both, and supervisors can compare drivers on a like-for-like scorecard regardless of fuel.

Charging and battery swap

Depot charging uses 120-360 kW DC with a CCS2 connector; a full 350-420 kWh charge lands in well under two hours on a 360 kW unit, and opportunity charging during breaks keeps the EV truck moving on multi-shift sites. Where uptime is absolute, the swap variant exchanges a depleted pack for a charged one in 5-6 minutes - faster than a diesel refill and ideal for regional line-haul.

Swap cabinets need only a buffered bay and a 400 V/800 V supply, so a site in {cn} can stand one up beside an existing workshop without a substation upgrade. For most fleets the lowest-risk path is depot charging overnight plus one swap cabinet as insurance against peak demand, which keeps the EV truck available even if grid power dips.

Planning depot charging for this EV truck

Charging an electric truck is a depot discipline, not a public-network gamble. A 350 kWh pack working a 280 km day typically returns with 25 to 35 percent in reserve, so overnight demand is about 210 kWh per truck. On a 120 kW DC charger the pack climbs from 20 to 80 percent in roughly 105 minutes; on 240 kW that falls to about 52 minutes, which lets two trucks share one charger on staggered shifts.

Size the site transformer for your busiest shift plus 30 percent headroom, keep cable runs under 30 metres to limit voltage drop, and meter each charger separately so energy cost per truck stays visible. In Kazakhstan, grid connection lead times of three to nine months are common, so apply for the connection the day you sign the truck order, not the day the ship arrives. Energy cost per kilometre: EV truck versus diesel

A comparable diesel 6x4 tractor burns about 0.38 litres per kilometre in mixed duty; at $0.95 per litre that is roughly $0.36 per km. The electric equivalent consumes about 1.15 kWh per km fully loaded; at an industrial tariff of $0.10 per kWh the cost is near $0.12 per km, and overnight or solar-coupled charging can push it below $0.08. Over 70,000 km a year the saving is about $17,000 per truck, before any carbon or low-emission-zone benefit.

The gap widens in stop-start duty, where regenerative braking returns 15 to 25 percent of traction energy instead of burning it as brake dust. In Kazakhstan, where diesel quality and price both fluctuate, locking in an electricity tariff also removes a major budgeting variable from the fleet P&L.
