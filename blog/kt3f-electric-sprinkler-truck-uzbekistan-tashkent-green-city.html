Why Tashkent's dust-control routes fit electric

Summer in Tashkent brings hot, dry winds that lift dust across the expanding districts, and the city runs sprinklers on 40-80 km loops through residential avenues, construction buffers and park perimeters. Those low-speed, stop-frequent routes are ideal for battery-electric trucks because the pump load is steady and regenerative braking recovers energy at every intersection. A diesel sprinkler on this duty burns 0.45-0.50 L/km, and at Uzbek diesel near $0.90/L that is about $0.43/km. The KT3F cuts that to roughly $0.16/km at municipal electricity of $0.09/kWh and 1.2-1.4 kWh/km.

Because the truck moves slowly and often idles the pump while parked, a diesel unit wastes fuel merely running the chassis engine to drive the water pump. The KT3F drives the pump from the high-voltage pack directly, so the chassis can stay silent at the kerb while watering proceeds.

Maintenance savings: what 35-40% really means

An electric sprinkler removes the diesel engine's oil, filters, injectors, turbo, EGR and exhaust after-treatment - the parts that drive most unscheduled breakdowns on low-speed municipal routes. Our fleet data shows EV trucks cut scheduled maintenance by 35-40% versus a diesel equivalent, which on a sprinkler running 200 days a year is roughly $2,500 in saved workshop bills. The LvKong motor's sealed drivetrain also avoids the pump-bearing wear that shortens diesel water-truck life in dusty summer heat.

  • Brake wear cut by regenerative braking at intersections: save about $600 per year.
