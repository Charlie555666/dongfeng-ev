The Road-Project Cycle

Road construction haulage runs borrow pit or crusher to the work front at 5-20 km, with the front advancing as the road builds. A TZ3Z on a 12 km loop completes 7-9 cycles per shift; measured consumption on comparable West African road projects runs 3.0-3.4 kWh/km loaded with the empty return lighter on Ghana's rolling terrain. The 400 kWh pack covers a full working day, and the dual-gun hardware turns a lunch break into 60% more range for double-shift pushes on schedule-critical sections.

The advancing work front shapes the infrastructure plan: chargers mount on skids at the crusher and camp, relocating with the project - a pattern road contractors already follow for their plants.

Site Power on Ghanaian Projects

Ghana's grid reaches most corridor projects, and crusher or batching-plant services support a 240 kW DC unit with a dedicated feed. The working layout is one fast charger per 5-6 trucks at the crusher - where trucks queue for loading anyway - with camp-based overnight charging as the base pattern. For feeder-road sections beyond the grid, containerized chargers with generator bridging cover the gap, phasing out as the project moves or power arrives. Ghanaian contractors already manage hybrid site power; the EV fleet slots into the same discipline.

  • Anchor charging at the crusher; loading queues are free charging windows.
  • Budget rain-season consumption 10-15% higher; soft tracks cost energy.
  • Track kWh per cubic metre hauled; laterite duty should hold 1.0-1.3 kWh/m3-km.
  • Weekly filter and cooling-pack service through the Harmattan dust season is non-negotiable.

Climate Across the Year

Ghana gives the electric truck a workable climate: 27-34 C with humidity on the coast, drier heat inland, and two rain seasons that soften tracks. The TZ3Z's liquid-cooled powertrain holds performance through all of it, with a modest 6-8% hot-season range allowance. The IP67-sealed high-voltage system handles rain-season site flooding within published wading limits - a genuine operational advantage over diesel when sections turn soft. Harmattan dust is the maintenance variable: filtration service intervals tighten, and the schedule should treat them as fixed appointments, not suggestions.

Fuel Savings Math

Ghanaian diesel pricing puts the operating gap among the widest in the region: a 6x4 diesel tipper on road-project duty burns $30,000-40,000 of fuel per year on a heavy schedule; the TZ3Z's electricity for the same cycles costs $8,000-12,000 on grid-fed power. Maintenance halves, and regen braking removes the brake-wear line that descending laterite tracks impose.
