Why Noise Curfews Block Diesel Trucks

Most city centres set a night noise limit of 60 to 75 dB at the property line between 22:00 and 06:00. A diesel truck at idle and reverse beep measures 80 to 90 dB, so it breaches the limit and attracts fines or a delivery ban. An electric truck has no engine clatter; the KT5L electric cargo truck with its 282 kWh pack runs at about 60 to 68 dB, below the threshold, which lets it legally deliver in the quiet window. For the South Africa electric truck market, where Johannesburg and Cape Town both restrict night freight, this access is a competitive edge. A diesel fleet locked out of the night must fight daytime congestion, while the EV fleet owns the empty roads.

Quiet-Scheme Permits and Paperwork

Many cities run a quiet delivery program that grants night access to certified low-noise vehicles. The steps below are typical for a retail fleet applying for the permit.

  • Measure the vehicle noise level with a calibrated 7 m test and keep the report.
  • Register the truck on the municipal clean-freight portal with the VIN.
  • Submit the route and time window, usually 23:00 to 05:00.
  • Fit the approved low-volume warning sound for reversing.
  • Renew annually and keep the on-board noise log for audits.
