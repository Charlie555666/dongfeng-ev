What a Depot Microgrid Is and Why Fleets Build Them

A depot microgrid is a self-contained energy system - solar generation, battery storage, grid connection and intelligent controls - that powers a truck depot as a managed unit rather than a passive consumer. Fleets build them for three reasons: the grid connection available is too small for fleet fast-charging and upgrading it costs more than the microgrid; grid tariffs or outages make energy expensive or unreliable; and locking in twenty years of solar electricity turns the fleet's largest operating cost into a fixed asset. For an electric truck fleet in Qatar, where grid constraints and tariff volatility are common, a microgrid is often the difference between a pilot that stalls and a fleet that scales.

Sizing Solar and Storage for a TE9L Fleet

Sizing starts from the trucks, not the roof. Each TE9L electric heavy-haul tractor running its daily duty consumes roughly 600 kWh at the charger per working day; a ten-truck fleet therefore needs on the order of ten times that figure daily. Under Qatari irradiance of 4-5.5 kWh per installed kWp per day, covering that load fully needs roughly 500-900 kWp of solar for ten trucks, phased as capital allows. Storage is sized for shifting, not survival: one to two hours of fleet charging demand buffers clouds and moves solar generation into evening charge windows. The TE9L's telematics energy data, captured during the first operating months, replaces spreadsheet assumptions with measured numbers before final storage sizing is committed.

Grid Independence and Outage Resilience in Qatar

The operational argument for microgrids in Qatar is resilience. Grid outages that idle diesel fleets at fuel queues also stop grid-dependent electric fleets - but not microgrid fleets. A solar-plus-storage depot keeps trucks charging through blackouts, and the trucks themselves, through their large batteries, become part of the resilience: the 600 kWh pack in each TE9L is substantial stored energy on wheels. For critical operations - cold chain, municipal services, port work - this energy independence has a balance-sheet value that simple payback models understate. Fleet managers who have run through one major outage on microgrid power describe it as the day the system paid for itself in reputation alone.

Levelized Cost of Microgrid Energy

The financial case rests on levelized cost: the lifetime cost per kWh delivered. Turnkey commercial solar in the region runs $600-$900 per kWp installed, with storage adding cost proportional to shifting depth. Against typical Qatari industrial tariffs, solar microgrid energy lands 30-60% below grid prices over a twenty-year life; against diesel generator power - the real alternative at many depots - the saving reaches 60-75%. Payback on the energy infrastructure alone runs three to six years, after which the fleet charges on nearly free energy for fifteen-plus more. Combined with the 60-75% per-kilometer energy saving already built into the electric truck case, a microgrid turns a good fleet investment into a structural cost advantage.

Controls, Chargers and Energy Management

The microgrid's intelligence sits in its energy management system, which decides every minute whether trucks charge from solar, storage or grid, at what power, and in what priority. Fleet charging software schedules the TE9L fleet around shift patterns: fast 60-120 kW DC sessions for turnaround needs, slow overnight charging for base load at the lowest marginal cost. Good systems also manage demand charges, capping simultaneous draw below the grid connection limit so the microgrid and grid supply complement rather than fight each other. CCS2 charger selection, grid-code certification for Qatar and integration between charger and solar inverters are specification details we support with every fleet order, because the control layer is where theoretical savings either materialize or leak away.

Project Steps: From Feasibility to Commissioning

A depot microgrid project runs six to twelve months in practice. Feasibility comes first: meter the site, model the fleet's charging load, survey roofs and yard for solar. Design follows: array sizing, storage depth, charger layout, protection and grid-code compliance for Qatari authorities. Permitting and utility agreements take the calendar time most projects underestimate. Construction and commissioning then run in weeks, ideally timed before truck delivery so the fleet lands on working infrastructure. Deployment Timeline from Order to Operation
