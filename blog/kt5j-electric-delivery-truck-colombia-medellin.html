KT5J Electric Delivery Truck for Medellin Urban Logistics

The KT5J electric truck is a light-medium delivery platform unusually well suited to Medellin, a city built across steep valley walls where every delivery round is a series of climbs and descents. That terrain punishes diesel vans, which burn fuel climbing and burn brakes descending, while the KT5J climbs with full electric torque and descends while recovering energy. This review covers the KT5J's hill performance, range, charging plan, and the Colombian incentives available to Medellin fleets.

Why Medellin's Terrain Rewards Electric

Medellin's comunas climb hundreds of vertical meters from the valley floor, so a delivery round is a repeated cycle of loaded climb and loaded or empty descent. The KT5J's 180 kW LvKong motor delivers full torque from a standstill on any gradient, and regenerative braking recovers 10 to 15 percent of the climb energy on the way back down, a free energy harvest a diesel simply cannot match. Hill Performance and Energy Recovery

Loaded at 5,000 kilograms the KT5J climbs a 25 percent grade confidently, covering Medellin's steepest working streets, and drivers report the single-pedal regen mode transforms descents: instead of riding friction brakes down the valley walls, the motor retards the van and returns charge to the pack. Brake wear falls by half or more on hilly rounds, and the energy recovered measurably extends the day's range.

On mixed hilly urban duty the KT5J returns 200 to 240 kilometers per charge, roughly double a typical Medellin round, so overnight depot charging alone covers daily operation.

Charging and Fleet Operations

A 120 kW charger at the depot restores 20 to 80 percent in about 70 minutes, and one charger serves four to five vans on staggered rounds. Colombia's hydro-heavy grid keeps overnight electricity cheap, and Medellin's moderate climate, neither desert-hot nor freezing, is kind to battery longevity, helping the pack hold capacity across years of service.

  • Charge overnight at the depot on hydro-powered off-peak tariffs.
  • Train drivers to use single-pedal regen on descents.
  • Sequence drops downhill where the schedule allows.
  • Track energy per drop by barrio to benchmark routes.
  • Spec the curtain body for mixed pallet and parcel work.

A diesel van on Medellin's hills burns 14 to 18 liters per 100 kilometers, while the KT5J uses about 0.85 kWh per kilometer including the regen harvest, an energy saving of 65 to 70 percent at Colombian tariffs. Warranty, Resale, and Lifecycle Confidence

The battery warranty is the foundation of the ownership case: 8 years or 4,500 cycles on the CATL LFP pack, transferable to a second owner, which means the truck can be sold or refinanced mid-life with a factory guarantee still running. No diesel powertrain offers anything comparable, and that difference shows up in residual value, where documented, warranted electric trucks are clearing stronger percentages of original price than worn diesel equivalents. Fleets that keep disciplined charging habits, staying between 20 and 90 percent state of charge in daily operation, routinely show 85 to 90 percent pack capacity after five years, and the telematics health report converts that directly into resale price.

Maintenance economics reinforce the point. The sealed LvKong motor has roughly one-third the moving parts of a diesel powertrain, with no oil, injectors, turbocharger, clutch, or exhaust aftertreatment to service, and regenerative braking cuts friction brake wear by an estimated 40 percent. The parts that remain are conventional wear items, tires, suspension bushings, filters, and body-specific consumables, all pre-stocked in the Fenghan regional parts buffer for the first year of operation.
