KTH3 EV Truck Preventive Maintenance Plan for Jordanian Operators

The KTH3 electric truck is a 6x4 battery-electric cargo vehicle that Jordanian distributors and manufacturers are adopting for the hardest-working medium-truck duty in the region, and Jordanian operators run some of the most demanding routes between Amman, Zarqa, Irbid, and the Aqaba gateway. For operators who have taken delivery of the KTH3 electric cargo truck, a disciplined preventive maintenance plan is what protects uptime on the long Amman to Aqaba corridor. This guide lays out the 10,000 km service checklist, brake, tire, and high-voltage intervals, the realities of corridor operation, maintenance cost per km versus diesel, and how to upskill local technicians.

The 10,000 km Service Checklist

The KTH3 is a 6x4 electric cargo truck with a 300 kWh CATL LFP pack, a 282 kW LvKong motor, GVW 25t, and a 280 km range. The recommended preventive plan centers on a 10,000 km inspection that catches wear before it becomes failure. The checklist is deliberately simple so that a competent workshop can complete it in under three hours.

  • Inspect high-voltage cabling and connectors for heat discoloration or moisture ingress.
  • Check thermal coolant level and test the chiller loop for correct pressure.
  • Measure brake pad thickness and disc condition, noting that regen reduces wear.
  • Rotate and balance tires, and record tread depth against the corridor duty profile.
  • Update motor and battery management firmware from the depot laptop.
  • Confirm charging-port latch and emergency disconnect function correctly.

Operators who complete this checklist on schedule protect the 8-year / 4,500-cycle battery warranty, because the warranty desk treats skipped thermal service as a coverage condition breach. The export team supplies the checklist in Arabic and English and trains the nominated workshop to log each item digitally, so the service history travels with the vehicle and supports a stronger resale value when the fleet refreshes.

Brake, Tire, and High-Voltage Intervals

Electric trucks invert the usual maintenance hierarchy. On a diesel truck, the engine and exhaust dominate service; on the KTH3, the high-voltage system is inspected often but rarely repaired, while brakes and tires still wear with load. Because regenerative braking carries most of the deceleration load on the corridor, many Jordanian KTH3 operators report brake pad life two to three times longer than on the diesel trucks they replaced. Tires remain the largest recurring wear cost, and we advise a corridor-specific pressure setting that balances Aqaba heat with Amman's climb loads. Under-inflation on the desert section is the most common avoidable tire failure, so the checklist records pressure hot and cold.

Operators also benefit from the telematics trend report, which the depot portal generates after each service and which flags a pack or motor parameter drifting outside the expected band before it becomes a roadside fault. This predictive signal is unique to the electric platform and is the reason the KTH3's unplanned downtime, measured across our Jordanian reference fleet, runs below that of the diesel trucks it replaces despite the newer technology. The report is shared with the upskilled workshop so the right part is ordered before the next 10,000 km visit, which keeps both the maintenance cost per km and the surprise failures low.

Amman to Aqaba Corridor Operation

The Amman to Aqaba run is about 330 km each way, beyond a single KTH3 charge, so operators plan a charge at one of the intermediate stops or split the duty into a relay. The descent from the highlands to the Rift Valley is a strong regen opportunity that tops the pack before the flat desert section, while the return climb draws it down. Summer temperatures near Aqaba exceed 45°C, so the thermal loop earns its keep, and the 280 km range is planned with a 20% heat buffer.

  • Depart Amman with a full charge and pre-cooled cab for driver comfort.
  • Use regen on the descent to recover energy for the valley crossing.
  • Charge at the midpoint depot during the mandatory rest, 20% to 80% in about 60 minutes.
  • Return climb draws the recovered and recharged energy without a second stop.

For pure in-kingdom distribution, the KTH3's 280 km range covers the daily loop with margin, and the Jordan EV truck market is increasingly specifying electric cargo units for exactly this urban and corridor pattern. Operators report that the quiet, fume-free cab also improves driver retention, a real factor in a market where qualified long-haul drivers are in short supply.

Maintenance Cost Per km Versus Diesel

The decisive number for any fleet manager is maintenance cost per kilometer. A comparable diesel 25t truck in Jordan typically costs 0.05 to 0.07 USD per km in routine maintenance, driven by oil changes, filters, clutch, and exhaust components. The KTH3 removes most of those items and adds only coolant, brake, tire, and the periodic high-voltage inspection. Measured across our operator reports, the electric truck runs about 0.02 to 0.03 USD per km, a 50% to 60% reduction.

Local Technician Upskilling

Seasonal Planning and Spare Parts Strategy

Jordan's climate swings from cold highland winters to hot Aqaba summers, and the maintenance plan should follow the season rather than a fixed calendar alone. In winter, the focus shifts to cab heating load and to the slightly slower charge acceptance of a cold pack, so the 10,000 km checklist adds a battery thermal pre-check before the first cold snap. In summer, the cooling loop is inspected more frequently and the tire pressure is set for sustained 45°C road heat. A plan that treats every month identically misses the failures that actually occur on the corridor, because heat and cold stress different subsystems and the logbook should show that distinction.

Spare parts strategy is the other pillar. Because the KTH3 is a durable electric platform, the parts that actually move are tires, brake pads, coolant, and a small set of HV service items. We recommend Jordanian operators stock one set of the fast-moving consumables per five trucks and rely on the regional hub for modules, which keeps working capital low while avoiding a roadside wait. The depot laptop flags a part before it fails by trending resistance and temperature, so the workshop can order during the weekly resupply rather than during a breakdown. Combined with the upskilled local technicians, this approach is what lets the KTH3 electric cargo truck match diesel uptime across a full year of Amman to Aqaba operation, and it is the discipline we audit at each scheduled service visit.

The final pillar of a working maintenance plan is people. The KTH3 does not need more labor than a diesel truck, but it needs different skills: high-voltage safety, battery management system diagnosis, and thermal loop service. We run a two-week upskilling course for the nominated Jordanian workshop, ending in a practical assessment. Covered topics include safe isolation, use of the diagnostic laptop, coolant handling, and interpretation of the state-of-health report. Certified technicians can perform the 10,000 km checklist independently, escalating only pack-level faults to the regional hub. This local capability is what turns the KTH3 electric cargo truck from a supported import into a dependable part of the daily fleet, and it is the reason our Jordanian operators report near-diesel uptime within the first quarter of service.
