Why Bogota Zero-Emission Zones Favor an Electric Cargo Truck

Bogota has expanded its low-emission and zero-emission zones to clean up the high-altitude basin air, and diesel delivery trucks are the first vehicles restricted during peak pollution days. An electric cargo truck enters those zones any day, any hour, with no surcharge and no risk of being turned back at a checkpoint. With electricity at $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh versus diesel at $0.90 to $1.20 per liter, the KT5M also lowers the cost of every urban kilometer, which is where distribution fleets spend most of their operating hours and where stop-start driving wastes the most fuel in a combustion engine.

KT5M Core Specifications

The KT5M is Dongfeng's mid-size electric cargo truck for city and regional distribution. It uses a CATL LFP battery, a LvKong permanent magnet motor and a liquid cooled thermal system rated for a 25 to 35 °C operating window. The battery carries an 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, which fits the daily charge rhythm of an urban box truck. The 282 kWh pack supports a typical range near 250 km on a mixed urban duty cycle, enough for a full day of deliveries without a mid-shift charge.

Charging the KT5M Fleet

Most Bogota operators install DC fast chargers at the depot and charge overnight or between shifts. The 20 to 80% window fills in about 60 to 90 minutes, which fits naturally into driver breaks and load changes. The liquid cooled thermal system keeps the pack inside its 25 to 35 °C window, so charging stays fast even when the Andean night turns cold. Fleets that run two shifts add a second charger to keep every truck rolling through the day.
