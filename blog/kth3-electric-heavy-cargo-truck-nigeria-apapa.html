Queue Economics: The Hidden Saving

A diesel heavy truck idling in an Apapa queue burns 3 to 5 liters per hour accomplishing nothing, and on a bad day that is a third of the trip's fuel. The KTH3 stationary draws only cabin cooling, and in the crawl its regenerative braking recovers energy at every surge and stop. Operators report effective energy consumption in congested port duty 20 to 30 percent below the same truck on open road, the reverse of the diesel experience.

On open stretches the KTH3's 260 to 310 kilometer range covers a full day of port evacuation loops with margin, so overnight depot charging covers daily operation.

Charging Plan for Port Fleets

A 240 kW charger at the mainland depot restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes during shift breaks and overnight. Fleets running double shifts add a second charger and stagger rotations. Because the duty is port-anchored, there is no need for public charging at all in the first deployment phase.

  • Charge overnight at the mainland depot on a 240 kW unit.
  • Stagger double-shift rotations so one charger serves three trucks.
  • Use regen-heavy mode in queue crawling to harvest energy.
  • Track queue hours to quantify the idle saving versus diesel.
  • Keep the cab cooling on a timer in long queues.

Warranty, Resale, and Lifecycle Confidence

The battery warranty is the foundation of the ownership case: 8 years or 4,500 cycles on the CATL LFP pack, transferable to a second owner, which means the truck can be sold or refinanced mid-life with a factory guarantee still running. No diesel powertrain offers anything comparable, and that difference shows up in residual value, where documented, warranted electric trucks are clearing stronger percentages of original price than worn diesel equivalents. The parts that remain are conventional wear items, tires, suspension bushings, filters, and body-specific consumables, all pre-stocked in the Fenghan regional parts buffer for the first year of operation.
