Scope 3 Emissions and Why Your Freight Matters

Corporate carbon accounting splits emissions into three scopes, and scope 3 - the value chain, including purchased transport - is typically the largest and hardest line. Companies reporting to CDP, following GHG Protocol, or answering retailer and investor questionnaires must account for the trucks moving their goods, which is why shippers worldwide now ask carriers for emissions data with the same persistence they ask for rates. For carriers and fleet operators in Saudi Arabia, this converts electric trucks from an operating decision into a commercial document: zero tailpipe emissions freight, evidenced by data, wins contracts that diesel cannot bid for. The TZ5E electric urban dump truck generates exactly the documented, auditable energy data these reports require.

The Emissions Math: Electric vs Diesel Freight

A diesel urban dump truck on regional duty emits roughly 0.08-0.12 kg of CO2 per tonne-kilometer at typical payloads, varying with load factor and terrain. An electric truck's operational emissions depend on grid carbon intensity - in Saudi Arabia, typically 30-70% below diesel per kilometer today, improving yearly as grids add renewables, and approaching zero where fleets charge on solar or contracted green power. Well-to-wheel honesty matters: credible reports count electricity generation emissions, and even on coal-heavy grids, heavy electric trucks generally beat diesel on efficiency grounds alone. The TZ5E's 350 kWh pack and documented consumption per kilometer produce the precise input data - kWh per km, per route, per period - that defensible scope 3 calculations need.

What Shippers and Tender Committees Now Ask

Freight tenders in Saudi Arabia and for Saudi exporters increasingly contain emissions questions with scoring weight: grams CO2 per tonne-kilometer by mode, percentage of zero-emission vehicles in the serving fleet, data verification methodology, and reduction commitments with dates. Retail and manufacturing shippers with public net-zero targets cannot meet them with diesel carriers - the math does not close - so carrier electrification is becoming a contract retention issue, not merely a win theme. Fleets running electric trucks report the practical effects: preferential status in tenders, longer contract terms from shippers locking in green capacity, and in some corridors, rate premiums for verified zero-emission freight. The commercial case for the TZ5E now includes revenue protection, not just cost reduction.

Reporting Frameworks: What to Align With

Fleet carbon reporting converges on a few frameworks, and aligning early saves rework. The GHG Protocol Corporate Value Chain Standard governs scope 3 categorization; ISO 14083, the newer transport-specific standard, standardizes per-shipment calculations and is becoming the reference for freight emissions; CDP questionnaires drive disclosure cycles for thousands of companies; and customer-specific scorecards - retail and automotive especially - layer their own formats on top. The practical advice for Saudi fleets: collect primary energy data from day one, keep it trip-level, and let formats be outputs rather than collection constraints. Electric truck telematics data drops cleanly into every framework, because measurement beats modeling in all of them.
