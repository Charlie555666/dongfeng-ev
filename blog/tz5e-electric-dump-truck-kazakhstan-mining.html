Cold-Weather Performance at Minus 30

Kazakh winters are the hardest test in trucking, and the TZ5E answers with a heated, insulated CATL LFP pack that holds cell temperature in the efficient band down to minus 30 °C. Expect cold-weather range at the lower end of the 170 to 210 kilometer band, still enough for a full shift of short mine cycles, and the pack pre-heats on grid power before the shift so no traction energy is wasted warming the battery. LFP chemistry also avoids the severe cold degradation that sidelines NMC packs in Central Asian winters.

Battery Swap for Continuous Operation

Kazakh mines running continuous multi-shift operations should specify the swap variant: a 5 to 6 minute pack exchange keeps trucks earning through every shift change, where a 75-minute charge would idle them. A single-stall swap station at the mine workshop serves 25 to 35 trucks, and the removed packs charge slowly indoors, which is itself a cold-weather advantage because the cells never fast-charge while cold-soaked.

  • Specify the heated pack option for winter operation.
  • Pre-heat packs on grid power before each shift.
  • Choose the swap variant for continuous multi-shift pits.
  • House the swap station or charger indoors against the cold.
  • Log energy per ton to benchmark winter versus summer duty.
