The Real Difference #1: Which Side of the Road You Drive On

The TZ5Y exists for one primary reason: right-hand-drive mining markets. Southern and East African operations — Zambia, Zimbabwe, South Africa, Tanzania, Kenya, plus Indonesia's mixed road conventions and Australia's mine sites — run RHD. Converting a heavy tipper to RHD is not a steering-wheel swap: pedal box, HVAC ducting, mirror mounts, wiper sweep, door structure and cab reinforcement all change. The TZ5Y is engineered from the factory as RHD, which preserves the ECE R29-03 cab strength certification and keeps the service parts catalogue coherent. The TZ3V serves LHD territories: West and North Africa, the Middle East, Latin America, Central Asia and China-standard markets.

The Real Difference #2: Haul-Road Character

Both trucks are mining-grade, but their tuning suits different duty:

  • TZ3V — open-pit specialist. The 8+8+4 multi-layer frame and 10/10/13 suspension are tuned for continuous work on unsealed pit ramps, bermed haul roads and stockpile reversals. It is the choice for the hardest haul roads: copper pits in the DRC, laterite nickel in Indonesia and the Philippines, hard-rock quarries worldwide.
  • TZ5Y — heavy RHD haul with a bit more road bias. 12.00R24 tyres and its suspension package favour heavy mine service that includes longer on-highway segments — concentrate haul from mine gate to rail siding or port in RHD jurisdictions. It is equally at home on heavy quarry work, but its fitment skews toward operations mixing pit and public-road kilometres.

What Is Identical (and Why That Is Good News)

Because both trucks share the CATL 600 kWh pack, the LvKong 400/550 kW drive, the 4AMT and the dual-gun 600 A charging system, a mining group running both models across regions enjoys real advantages:

  1. One charging standard. The same GB/T dual-gun chargers (or CCS2 via the TZ4Y family in RHD CCS markets) serve every 80 t unit in the group.
  2. One battery logistics chain. Pack diagnostics, high-voltage safety procedures and second-life battery programs are common across the fleet.
  3. One driver training programme. The driving position is mirrored, but every operational habit — regenerative braking, ramp technique, charging discipline — transfers directly.
  4. One spares pool for all common components: axles, suspension elements, cab structural parts (mirrored), driveline and electronics.

Duty Cycle Model: 80 t Electric vs 80 t Diesel

A hard-rock operation running 90 km loaded-out cycles, 300 days a year, illustrates the shared economics. A diesel 8x4 in this class burns 45–55 L/100 km in pit duty — call it 50 L average, or USD 34,000–45,000 of fuel per truck per year at typical African mine-gate diesel cost (delivered to site, diesel at mines is always the expensive kind). The electric equivalent consumes roughly 1.7–2.0 kWh/km in heavy pit duty: about 54,000 kWh/year, or USD 4,300–7,600 where the mine can supply power at USD 0.08–0.14/kWh — and many pits now pair haul fleets with dedicated solar. Annual advantage: USD 27,000–38,000 per truck before maintenance savings, which add another USD 5,000–8,000. A 20-truck fleet transition is a USD 640,000–920,000 per year operating swing.

Selection Checklist for the Mine Fleet Manager

  • Which side of the road does the host country drive? RHD → TZ5Y; LHD → TZ3V. This single question resolves 80% of decisions.
  • What share of daily kilometres is unsealed pit ramp versus public road? Heavy ramp share → TZ3V; mixed road share → TZ5Y fitment.
  • What charging standard does the site already use? GB/T and CCS2 both available in the family; do not create a mixed-charger mine.
  • Is a swap corridor planned? Both architectures support battery exchange for 24/7 haul with 5–6 minute swaps.
  • Altitude and heat: both ships standard with HD battery thermal management; specify tropical or alpine calibration at order.
