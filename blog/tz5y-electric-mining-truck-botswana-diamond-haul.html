TZ5Y Electric Mining Truck for Botswana Diamond Operations

The TZ5Y electric mining truck is an electric truck engineered for Botswana's diamond haul roads, built on the proven TZ5E heavy electric dump truck platform and fitted with a 400 kWh CATL LFP battery that shrugs off dusty, high-heat pit cycles. For a diamond mine where the haul is fixed, the grade is known and the shifts run around the clock, electrification is less a leap of faith than a straightforward fuel-substitution project with a clear payback.

Why Botswana Diamond Mines Are Going Electric

Botswana's diamond operations around Orapa, Letlhakane and Jwaneng move millions of tonnes of kimberlite and waste rock a year. Diesel haul trucks burn heavily on the climb out of the pit and add heat and fumes underground and at the crusher, degrading both air quality for operators and the efficiency of ventilation systems. An electric truck removes tailpipe emissions from the working face and cuts the fuel bill that dominates a mine's operating cost. In the Southern Africa electric truck market, mining is the fastest-returning EV segment because routes are closed, repeatable and metered, so every kilowatt-hour saved is directly measurable.

The economics are doubly favourable in Botswana because grid power is relatively clean and cheap compared with trucked diesel to a remote pit. When the alternative is a fuel convoy crossing long distances to keep a diesel fleet running, a charged battery and a swap pack start to look like the lower-risk supply chain.

Duty Cycles in Dusty Heat

Botswana's dry season pushes ambient temperatures past 38 °C and throws fine dust through every seal on a conventional truck. The TZ5Y's LFP chemistry tolerates the heat without the thermal anxiety of NMC packs, and the sealed motor unit needs no air intake in the dust cloud, which is a meaningful reliability win when a single ingested grain can stall a diesel. Crews report steadier torque on the pit ramp because the electric drivetrain delivers full motor output from standstill, which matters on a loaded climb where a diesel would be labouring in a low gear.

Regenerative braking on the loaded descent is another quiet benefit: energy that a diesel would waste as heat in the retarder is recovered into the pack, extending effective range on the return haul and lowering the total energy drawn from the pit charging point.

Charging and Battery Swap Plan

  • Opportunity charging at the shovel during loading windows keeps packs topped.
  • A 5-6 minute battery swap at a mid-pit station eliminates range anxiety on long hauls.
  • Overnight depot charging on off-peak power resets the fleet for the next shift.
  • Thermal monitoring holds cell temperature in the safe band through the hot season.
  • Shared swap packs let one truck cover a second shift on the same battery pool.

The swap option is what turns the TZ5Y from a good truck into a continuous-duty asset, because the limiting factor becomes pack availability rather than recharge time.

Why the Platform Choice Matters

Standardizing on the TZ5E platform lets a Botswana fleet carry one motor type, one controller family and one battery form factor across trucks. That cuts workshop complexity and ensures a swap pack from one truck fits another. In a remote pit, that commonality is worth more than a few percentage points of spec because it determines whether a breakdown becomes a two-day wait for a couriered part or a ten-minute swap from the pool.

Battery Swap Station Layout

A mid-pit swap station needs only a level pad, a crane or shuttle and a climate-managed pack rack; it draws far less than a fast-charge farm and never strands a truck on a low state of charge. Place the station at the load-and-return midpoint so both directions can swap, and size the pack pool to cover the longest single haul plus one contingency pack. With a 5-6 minute swap, station throughput easily matches a busy shovel's loading rate.

Operator Training and Safety

A battery-electric haul truck changes the pit's risk picture in ways supervisors welcome. With no diesel exhaust, enclosed cab filtration is simpler and the working face meets occupational exposure limits more easily. Training focuses on high-voltage isolation, swap-pack handling and regenerative-braking behaviour rather than engine faults, and most diesel-experienced operators are productive on the TZ5Y within a few shifts.

Comparing Electric to Diesel Haul Cost

On a representative Botswana pit, a diesel haul truck burns hundreds of litres of fuel per shift, whereas the TZ5Y draws that energy from the grid at a fraction of the per-kilometre cost. Add the eliminated engine service, the longer service interval and the 8-year / 4,500-cycle battery warranty, and the electric truck's operating cost per tonne-kilometre typically lands well below diesel once the fleet is past break-even. For remote pits where diesel is trucked hundreds of kilometres, the grid advantage widens further, and the swap pool removes the single biggest failure mode of a battery fleet.

Grid Power and Pit Electrification

Electrifying the haul fleet is the easy half; the mine should also plan pit charging power so the swap pool and opportunity chargers never compete with processing load. Botswana grids are stable enough for a staged connection, and most pits already run high-voltage substations that can feed a swap station with modest upgrade. We model the peak draw of the pack pool against your existing demand so the EV fleet slides in without a costly new feeder. Mines that treat electrification as a site-wide program, not a single truck purchase, see the strongest return on the TZ5Y investment.
