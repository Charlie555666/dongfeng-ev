Why Ekiti quarries are switching to electric tippers

Ekiti's granite quarries move stone in 2 to 6 km cycles from the face to the crusher or load-out, with a truck completing 30 to 50 trips a shift. A 25-tonne diesel tipper in this duty burns 35 to 45 litres per 100 km loaded, costing $38 to $63 per 100 km at Nigeria's diesel price near $1.20 per litre, and the constant load-unload idling pushes consumption higher. The TZ3Z replaces that with electricity at $0.09 to $0.18 per kWh, and the regenerative braking on the loaded descent recovers energy a diesel unit wastes. For a quarry running 15 trucks, the fuel saving alone can exceed $400,000 a year.

The second driver is uptime. Quarry diesel tippers fail on clutches and cooling in the dusty Ekiti heat; the TZ3Z's sealed electric drive shrugs off dust and needs no engine cooling overhaul.

Duty cycle: granite haul loops

A typical Ekiti shift runs 10 to 12 hours with the truck cycling every 12 to 20 minutes. The TZ3Z draws about 2.0 to 2.6 kWh per km loaded and 1.1 to 1.5 kWh per km empty, so a 5 km loaded plus 5 km empty loop uses roughly 18 to 33 kWh. Across 40 loops a shift that is 720 to 1,320 kWh; the 282 kWh pack is topped during the crusher queue wait using an on-site DC charger, keeping the truck turning without a dedicated charge shift.

Quarry planners should place a 180 kW DC charger at the load-out and a second at the crusher. A 45 to 90 minute 20-80 percent top-up during mandatory loader queues covers the cycle, and the descent regen stretches each charge by 10 to 15 percent.

Cost per tonne-kilometre versus diesel

The metric quarry owners watch is cost per tonne-km. For the TZ3Z at 25 t and 2.3 kWh per km loaded:

  • Energy per tonne-km: 2.3 kWh / 25 t = 0.092 kWh per tonne-km.
  • Electricity cost at $0.12 per kWh: $0.011 per tonne-km.
  • Diesel equivalent at 40 L per 100 km and $1.20 per litre: about $0.048 per tonne-km, four times higher.
  • Maintenance saving: no DPF, no engine oil, regen brakes — roughly 30 percent lower per km.

Over a 400,000-km site life a single TZ3Z avoids roughly $150,000 in fuel and $35,000 in maintenance against a diesel tipper, covering the FOB premium of $85,000 to $105,000 within 18 to 24 months of operation.

Many Ekiti quarries sit far from a reliable grid, so we design off-grid charging around a 200 to 400 kW solar array with a 500 kWh to 1 MWh battery bank. The system charges the TZ3Z packs during the day from solar and tops up from a diesel generator only when the bank is low, cutting generator fuel by 70 to 80 percent versus charging trucks directly.
