The Yucatan Construction Duty Cycle

Spoil and aggregate haulage on Yucatan projects follows short, intense loops: excavation face to spoil bank or crusher, typically 4-15 km on unpaved site roads and short public-road sections. Loads run 25-35 t of limestone spoil. Ambient heat sits at 33-38 C with extreme humidity from May to October, and dust is a constant on the limestone tracks. A TZ5E on a 10 km loop completes 7-9 loaded cycles per shift; measured consumption on comparable Latin American sites runs 3.0-3.5 kWh/km loaded, with the empty return partly offset by regeneration on the region's gentle grades.

One full shift of 8-9 hours consumes 55-65% of the pack, which leaves margin for the unexpected - a rerouted spoil bank, an extended public-road leg to a batching plant - without mid-shift charging. Contractors running double shifts add a 45-50 minute DC charge at shift change, which the dual-gun charging hardware handles as a scheduled break rather than lost production.

Why Emissions-Free Matters on These Sites

Tren Maya-area construction operates under intense environmental scrutiny. Sites border protected jungle, archaeological zones and the cenote-fed aquifer system that defines the peninsula. Diesel spill and exhaust management consumes real contractor attention - fuel deliveries, spill kits, idling rules near sensitive zones. An electric truck fleet deletes most of that exposure: no diesel deliveries to remote sites, no exhaust particulates around workers in cuttings, and noise levels that let sites run extended hours near hotel zones without complaints. Several Mexican federal procurement frameworks now score low-emission equipment directly in tender evaluation.

  • Position the site charger at the workshop or batching plant where grid power already exists.
  • Run the dustiest loops early morning when regen efficiency and cooling load are optimal.
  • Use the quiet-operation window for night haulage near populated areas - a genuine schedule accelerator.
  • Track kWh per tonne moved; limestone spoil should model at 0.9-1.2 kWh/t-km on 10 km loops.

Heat, Humidity and Hurricane Season

Yucatan's humidity is the real test, more than the heat itself. The TZ5E's high-voltage components carry IP67 sealing and conformal-coated control boards; condensation management is handled by pack breather design rather than maintenance. In 38 C peak heat with full cab cooling, expect 8-10% range reduction - plan shifts around 65% pack consumption, not 75%. Hurricane season adds a flood-wading question; the sealed drivetrain tolerates site flooding that stops diesel trucks, within the published wading limits, and recovery after storms requires no engine de-watering work.
