TE9L Electric Tractor on the Saudi Riyadh-Jeddah Corridor

The 950 km corridor between Riyadh and Jeddah is Saudi Arabia's busiest freight artery, linking the capital's consumption market with the Kingdom's main Red Sea container gateway. Electrifying that full distance is an infrastructure project - but the corridor's real freight pattern breaks into depot-to-depot segments that an electric truck can run profitably today. The Dongfeng TE9L, with CATL LFP pack options up to 600 kWh, a 520 kW LvKong motor and dual-gun 600 A charging, is the long-haul tool for this corridor. Here is the segment plan and the economics.

Segmenting the Corridor

Saudi freight between the two cities moves in relay patterns already: Jeddah port to the dry ports and distribution centres on its outskirts (30-80 km), Jeddah to Taif (170 km), Taif to the corridor midpoint staging areas, and the final leg into Riyadh's industrial cities. A TE9L with the 600 kWh pack covers 320-360 km at 49 t GCW on Saudi highways in summer conditions, which turns the corridor into two or three charge-stop segments rather than a range problem.

The densest first application is Jeddah Islamic Port to the Jeddah and Rabigh logistics zones - short shuttle work where one pack covers multiple rounds daily. The second is the Riyadh end: dry port to industrial city distribution, 40-120 km patterns that return to base nightly. Both segments earn immediate fuel savings while highway charging along the corridor matures.

The Charging Stop Plan

Corridor charging strategy in 2026 Saudi Arabia blends private and public infrastructure. Fleet-owned 240-360 kW chargers at the Jeddah and Riyadh depots anchor the operation; corridor charging plazas - several under construction under the national EV infrastructure program - provide the midpoint stops. The scheduling rule that works: charge during the mandatory driver rest windows, never as a separate stop. A 40-minute dual-gun session adds 55-60% of pack capacity, which aligns almost exactly with the legal break after 4.5 hours of driving.

  • Anchor both ends with fleet-owned chargers before running any through-corridor schedule.
  • Model segments at summer range (minus 10-12%), not catalogue range.
  • Pre-negotiate priority access or reservation at corridor charging plazas - queues destroy schedules.
  • Track kWh/km by segment; Saudi highway duty should sit at 1.35-1.55 loaded at cruise speed.

Heat, Distance and Discipline

The corridor crosses serious desert: summer road-surface temperatures exceed 60 C, and the sustained 90 km/h cruise is the hardest thermal case for any electric truck. The TE9L's liquid thermal management holds pack temperature through sustained fast driving and fast charging - the combination that overheats lesser systems. Tyre management deserves equal attention: sustained desert highway running demands pressure checks at every stop, because underinflation costs 5-8% of range and destroys casings. Speed discipline matters the same way it does for diesel - 85 km/h instead of 95 km/h returns 10-14% more range.

Line-Haul Economics

At Saudi industrial electricity tariffs, a TE9L's energy cost per km lands around $0.15-0.20 against $0.55-0.65 for a diesel prime mover at 36-40 L/100 km.
