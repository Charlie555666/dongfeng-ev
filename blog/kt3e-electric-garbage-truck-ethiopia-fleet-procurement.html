Why Ethiopian Fleets Are Moving to Electric Refuse Trucks

Waste collection is stop-start work: hundreds of stops per shift, constant acceleration from a standstill, and long idle periods at transfer stations. That duty cycle is exactly where an electric drivetrain wins, because regenerative braking recovers energy at every stop and there is no diesel engine ticking over in traffic. For Addis Ababa, where altitude and gradients add load, the sealed LvKong motor also avoids the power loss a turbo-diesel suffers at 2,300 meters. How the Compactor and Lift System Works

The KT3E pairs a 14 cubic meter rear compactor with a standard bin lift that services 120 L, 240 L, and 1,100 L containers. A compaction ratio of up to 4 to 1 means fewer trips to the transfer station, which directly cuts the kilometers and energy a round consumes. For a city like Addis Ababa, where transfer stations sit on the urban edge, that ratio is the difference between two tip runs a day and four.

The electric power take-off drives the compactor hydraulics without a separate engine, so the whole body operates silently and without exhaust while parked at a collection point. Crews report lower fatigue on night rounds, and residents get a quieter service that meets the noise limits many Ethiopian districts are now writing into collection contracts.

Range, Charging, and Round Planning

A loaded collection round returns 200 to 240 kilometers per charge, which covers a full district loop for most mid-size cities. The 350 kWh pack reaches 20 to 80 percent in about 90 minutes on a 240 kW DC charger, so a depot can rotate units through a single charger across the day and night shifts. Battery swap is not required for refuse duty because the trucks return to a central depot every shift.

  • Site a 240 kW charger at the central depot for overnight and midday top-ups.
  • Map rounds so each truck returns below 20 percent only at the depot.
  • Use the 4-to-1 compactor ratio to halve transfer-station trips.
  • Train drivers on regen to recover energy on the long graded returns.
  • Standardize container types to speed the bin-lift cycle at each stop.
