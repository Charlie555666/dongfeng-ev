The Cairo Delivery Cycle

A Cairo last-mile rigid leaves the distribution centre at dawn, runs 25-40 drops over 120-180 km, and returns by mid-afternoon - distances that sit comfortably inside one charge of the KT5J. The defining feature is stop density: every drop means dock or curbside waiting, and in Cairo's traffic, a diesel spends 30-40% of its day idling. The electric truck draws essentially nothing stationary, and regenerative braking recovers energy from the constant stop-start. Measured city consumption on the KT5J runs 1.1-1.3 kWh/km loaded, giving 240-270 km of real Cairo range with reserve.

Daytime access rules in central districts add another dimension: several zones restrict heavy diesel entry at peak hours, and the quiet, zero-emission truck increasingly receives exemptions in regulated districts - a pattern Cairo shares with every megacity moving toward low-emission logistics.

Depot Charging for a Cairo Fleet

The working layout for a 15-20 truck fleet is overnight AC or low-power DC charging - trucks return by 16:00-17:00 and need a full pack by 06:00, a window long enough to make fast charging unnecessary - plus one 120 kW DC unit for exceptions and double-shift days. Egyptian industrial electricity tariffs keep the overnight energy cost low; a dedicated transformer at the distribution centre is the one infrastructure investment that matters, and it typically pays for itself within the first year against diesel savings.

  • Assign every truck a numbered bay and charge post; shared posts create morning failures.
  • Charge to 90% for standard routes - pack longevity improves measurably.
  • Meter kWh per route; a 15% jump on a fixed route flags driving style or a dragging brake.
  • Keep one diesel spare for the first 90 days while dispatchers learn the range model.

Heat, Dust and the Cairo Summer

Cairo summers push 40-45 C with dust that tests every filter on a vehicle. The KT5J's liquid-cooled battery holds performance through the heat with an 8-10% summer range reduction under full air conditioning - plan routes at 220-240 km in August rather than 260. The sealed IP67 high-voltage system shrugs off the dust; the maintenance change that matters is more frequent cabin and cooling-pack filter service, a 20-minute task. Nile-side humidity is a non-issue for the sealed drivetrain.
