KTH1 Electric Cargo Truck on Malaysia's Peninsular Freight Corridor

Why the Peninsular Corridor Suits a Heavy EV

The KTH1 heavy cargo truck is the right electric truck for Malaysia's north-south spine, the KL to Johor Bahru freight corridor that carries the bulk of peninsular factory and port volume. With a 424 kWh CATL LFP pack and a 510 kW motor the KTH1 holds 60 t GCW and still returns 280 to 320 km of real range, enough to run the 360 km Kuala Lumpur to Johor pair as a same-day out-and-back with a midway charge.

Malaysian fleets face toll-heavy highways and dense industrial clusters, so predictable per-km cost matters more than headline range. The KTH1's flat torque and regen on the gentle grades of the ELITE highway recover energy that a diesel would waste on engine braking, improving real-world economy versus the brochure figure.

KL to Johor Range and Charging Plan

The 360 km KL to Johor run is just beyond a single-charge envelope at full 60 t load, so the practical plan is a 20-minute top-up at the Ayer Keroh or Pagoh rest area using a 360 kW DC charger. That restores enough range to reach Johor and return, turning a two-day diesel trip into a one-day electric round trip.

  • Depart KL at 06:00 with 100 percent charge, 60 t GCW.
  • Top-up at Pagoh, 360 kW, 20 minutes, adds roughly 90 km.
  • Reach Johor port by mid-afternoon, unload, return same day.
  • Charge overnight at depot on off-peak tariff before the next cycle.

Toll Savings and Highway Economics

Malaysian tolls scale with axle count and class, and the KTH1's electric driveline does not reduce tolls directly, but the total cost per km still beats diesel because energy is cheaper. At MYR 2.05 per liter diesel and MYR 0.44 per kWh depot power, the KTH1 runs at about MYR 0.62 per km versus MYR 1.10 per km for a comparable diesel at 35 L per 100 km.

Depot Charging and Grid Connection

Most Malaysian logistics hubs already have three-phase supply, so a 360 kW DC charger is a straightforward install. Fenghan can specify a dual-gun unit serving two KTH1 trucks in staggered shifts, or a 240 kW unit where the connection is limited. Battery swap is not offered, so the schedule is the control lever.

Driver and Cabin Considerations

The KTH1 cabin is quiet and vibration-free, which reduces long-haul fatigue on the four-hour KL to Johor drive. Preconditioning on grid power means the driver steps into a cooled cab at 06:00 without idling, a real benefit given Malaysian heat that regularly exceeds 33 °C.

Body Types and Payload Options

The KTH1 chassis accepts box, curtain-side, tanker, and flatbed bodies, so a single platform serves general cargo, FMCG distribution, and bulk liquid fleets across peninsular Malaysia. The 424 kWh pack holds 60 t GCW regardless of body type, and the 510 kW motor keeps gradeability on the Genting and Karak climbs that challenge lighter EVs. Operators usually spec a 9.5 m curtain body for the KL-Johor FMCG lane and a tanker for edible-oil and chemical runs out of Port Klang.

Malaysian EV Incentives and Tax

Malaysia's national EV roadmap extends import and excise relief to commercial electric vehicles, and several states offer road-tax rebates for zero-emission trucks. While the KTH1 is imported complete rather than assembled locally, the saving against a diesel plus the lower per-km cost typically shortens payback to under 30 months for a 250,000 km per year operator. Fleets should budget the incentive paperwork into the commissioning timeline so the rebate lands in the same quarter the trucks enter service.

  • Depot needs 400 to 600 A three-phase supply for fast top-ups.
  • Driver roster pairs a senior and a trainee for the highway leg.

Telematics and Driver Score

The KTH1 telematics portal scores each driver on regen use, speed steadiness, and idle time, feeding a monthly bonus plan. Fleets that coach the bottom quartile typically recover 6 to 9 percent more range, which on the KL-Johor lane is the difference between one charge and a midway top-up. The portal also flags harsh acceleration that wears the reduction gear, extending drivetrain life and protecting the 8-year warranty position.

Battery Thermal and Tropical Operation

Malaysia's year-round 30 to 34 °C ambient is easy for the liquid-cooled LFP pack, which targets a 25 to 35 °C cell window. The KTH1 pre-conditions the pack only when plugged in, so highway range is unaffected by the heat, and the 8-year warranty covers tropical duty explicitly. Cell service, if ever needed, is handled from the regional stock Fenghan keeps for the ASEAN market so downtime stays under a week.

Cross-Border Run to Singapore

For operators running the KL to Singapore lane, the KTH1's 320 km rated range covers the 330 km one-way leg only at a light load, so the practical plan is a Johor Bahru top-up before the return climb. Fenghan maps charger sites on both sides of the causeway so the truck never enters the checkpoint queue with less than 25 percent charge, avoiding a stranded trailer at the border.

Resale and Residual Value

The KTH1's LFP pack holds capacity well past the 8-year mark, so trucks rotated at 4 years typically retain 45 to 55 percent of FOB on the regional used market. That residual strength, plus the predictable energy cost, makes the total cost of ownership case to a finance committee straightforward, and Fenghan publishes the inspection record that supports a clean resale to the next operator.
