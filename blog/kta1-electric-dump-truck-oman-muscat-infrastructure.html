Why Muscat coastal works suit electric dumps

Muscat earthworks run on 8-20 km loops between quarries, port reclamation and road fills, with depots never far from the coast. Those short, predictable routes are ideal for battery-electric dump trucks, and the salt-laden air that corrodes diesel exhaust systems is irrelevant to a zero-tailpipe chassis. Diesel tippers here burn 0.50-0.55 L/km under 25-tonne load, and at Omani diesel near $0.95/L that is about $0.50/km. The KTA1 drops that to roughly $0.19/km at site electricity of $0.10/kWh and 1.5-1.7 kWh/km loaded.

Regenerative braking on the descent from inland quarries to the coast recovers 15-20% of climbing energy, and the sealed electric drivetrain avoids the salt-corrosion failures that shorten diesel tipper uptime near the sea.

Maintenance savings: what 35-40% really means

An electric dump truck removes the diesel engine's oil, filters, injectors, turbo, EGR and exhaust after-treatment - the parts that drive most unscheduled breakdowns on coastal sites and suffer fastest in salt air. Our fleet data shows EV trucks cut scheduled maintenance by 35-40% versus a diesel equivalent, which on a tipper running 250 shifts a year is roughly $4,500 in saved workshop bills. The LvKong motor's sealed drivetrain also avoids the salt-corrosion failures that shorten diesel tipper uptime near the sea.

  • No engine oil changes: save about $1,400 per year in oil and filters.
  • No injector or turbo service: save about $1,200 per year.
  • Brake wear cut by regenerative braking on inland descents: save about $1,000 per year.
  • Longer tyre life from smooth electric torque: save about $900 per year.
