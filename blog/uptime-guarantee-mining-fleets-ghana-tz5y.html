What a Good Service Agreement Actually Contains

The agreement worth signing specifies five things in numbers, not adjectives. Response time: how fast support answers - remote diagnostics response within hours, because most electric truck faults are diagnosed from data before any spanner turns. Parts availability: the committed stock position - which components are held in Ghana or regionally, which ship from the factory, and the guaranteed dispatch windows for each class. Scheduled maintenance: what is included, at what intervals, at what price - the short electric schedule documented line by line. Technical support: the remote diagnostics subscription, software updates, and the training commitments for fleet technicians. And the availability commitment itself: the percentage the supplier stands behind, with the remedies - service credits, substitute vehicle arrangements - that give it teeth. Vague agreements are marketing; the Ghanaian fleets that run tight contracts hold their suppliers to numbers.

Measuring Availability Honestly

Availability math is simple to state and easy to game, so define it before signing anything. The honest formula: days fit for dispatch divided by total days, measured per truck per month, with agreed exclusions - scheduled maintenance windows count only beyond agreed duration, accident damage sits outside, customer-caused delays are documented separately. The TZ5Y's telematics makes the measurement automatic: the truck reports its own status daily, so the availability report generates from data both parties trust, rather than logs either party disputes. Benchmarks from operating 80t mining truck fleets: well-supported electric fleets hold 95-98% availability, comparable to or better than diesel benchmarks, because the electric failure modes are fewer and increasingly diagnosed remotely before they ground the truck. Fleets in Ghana running documented availability tracking report the side benefit: the data that enforces the SLA also optimizes the maintenance schedule.

Charging Planning for TZ5Y Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TZ5Y supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 600 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Ghana should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
