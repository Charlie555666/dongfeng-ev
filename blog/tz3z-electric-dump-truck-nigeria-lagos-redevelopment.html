The Lagos Urban Haul Profile

Urban redevelopment haulage in Lagos means short loops - site to approved dump or crusher at 10-30 km - run through the city's infamous traffic. Average speeds on a Lagos haul run rarely exceed 25 km/h, and idle time in go-slows consumes 30-45% of the working day. That profile punishes diesel: a tipper crawling in traffic burns 20-30% more fuel than highway running, and the idle hours stack up unproductively. The electric truck inverts the equation - stop-start traffic is where regenerative braking and zero idle consumption deliver their biggest advantage, and measured urban consumption on the TZ3Z runs 2.6-3.1 kWh/km loaded, traffic included.

Daily distance stays modest: 150-220 km across 5-7 cycles. The 400 kWh pack covers a full day on one overnight charge, with the dual-gun 600 A DC hardware available for a midday top-up when a project pushes double shifts.

The Noise Advantage Is a Schedule Advantage

Lagos enforces noise restrictions around residential and mixed-use zones, and demolition or night haulage permits come with conditions. The TZ3Z's near-silent operation at crawl speed - no engine idle roar, no exhaust bark - regularly wins working windows that diesel fleets cannot use: night demolition haulage on the Island, early-morning concrete spoil removal in Ikoyi, weekend work near schools and hospitals. Contractors report that the extra permitted hours compress project schedules by 10-15% on constrained sites, a benefit worth more than the fuel saving on penalty-heavy contracts.

  • Base the fleet at a depot with overnight charging; site-by-site power is the fallback, not the plan.

  • Use the traffic idle time: the truck recovers range while stationary, so dispatch tight cycles confidently.
