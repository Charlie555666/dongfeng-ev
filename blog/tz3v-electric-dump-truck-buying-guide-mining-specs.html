Step 1: Establish the haul profile before you specify anything

Every subsequent decision depends on four numbers. Get them from your mine plan, not from a brochure:

  1. Haul distance one way and whether it grows as the pit deepens.
  2. Net gradient — loaded uphill or loaded downhill. This is the single largest energy variable. A loaded-uphill profile at 6% can consume 2.4–3.0 kWh/km; the same road loaded downhill with regen can net 0.9–1.4 kWh/km.
  3. Material density. A 30 m³ body holding 1.4 t/m³ material carries 42 t; the same body in 2.4 t/m³ ore would be grossly overloaded. Body volume must be matched to density, not chosen by habit.
  4. Shift pattern — hours per day, number of shifts, and whether trucks run continuously.

Step 2: Choose the body for the material

Body specification is where most first orders go wrong. Three decisions:

  • Rock body versus coal/soft body. Rock bodies use higher-strength wear plate, thicker floors and reinforced side walls; they weigh more, which costs payload. Soft-material bodies are lighter and higher volume. Specifying a rock body for coal wastes payload every single cycle.
  • Heated floor (optional). In wet, cold or high-clay operations, exhaust-heated or electrically heated floors prevent carry-back. On electric units this draws pack energy — budget 2–5 kW when active.
  • Tailgate and sealing. Fine material and concentrate require sealing to prevent spillage on public sections of haul road; spillage is a compliance and community-relations issue as much as a cost one.

Because the 600 kWh pack is sized for a full shift of heavy duty, adding payload almost never requires reducing range below the operational requirement. Where it does bind — very long hauls or 24-hour campaigns — the answer is swap, not a bigger pack.

Step 4: Options that are worth the money, and those that are not

Worth specifying:

  • Tropical/high-ambient thermal package for operations above 35 °C continuous.
  • Enhanced air filtration and sealed electrical connectors for dust-heavy sites.
  • Telematics with energy and payload logging — this is what lets you run a cost-per-tonne analysis and defend the business case internally.
  • Spare wheel carrier, tyre pressure monitoring, and a site spares kit for the first 12 months.
  • Fire suppression provisions where the site insurer requires them; see battery fire suppression systems.

Usually not worth it:

  • Maximum-range pack variants on short-shuttle duty — you will pay for kWh you never use.
  • Premium cab trim on site trucks that never leave the concession.
  • On-board chargers sized for public networks where the site has its own depot.

Step 5: Price bands and what moves them

Indicative FOB ranges as of 2026, before freight, duty and local costs:

  • Base TZ3V, standard body: USD 140,000–155,000 per unit.
  • With heavy rock body, thermal and dust packages: USD 155,000–170,000.
  • Fully specified with telematics, fire suppression and spares: USD 165,000–180,000.
  • Volume orders (10+ units): tiered pricing, typically 4–8% below unit pricing.

Remember that FOB is 60–75% of landed cost on most mining import routes. Freight, insurance, duty, VAT and port handling add the remainder; our import, payment and shipping guide sets out the mechanics.

Step 6: Procurement sequence

  1. Technical specification workshop — haul profile, body, thermal and dust packages.
  2. Energy simulation against your actual profile; confirm shifts per charge.
  3. Quotation with configuration sheet — FOB, options, lead time, warranty terms.
  4. Site visit or reference deployment where available.
  5. Contract and deposit — 30% T/T with the production order, 70% against bill of lading copy; L/C at sight accepted.
  6. Production 45–75 days, pre-shipment inspection, UN 38.3 battery documentation.
  7. Shipping and commissioning — charger energised before trucks land.

Operator Training on Large Electric Tippers

An 8x4 electric tipper at 60 tonnes is not a vehicle that operators adapt to by instinct. Four training elements consistently separate fleets that hit their productivity targets from those that do not:

  1. Regenerative braking on haul roads. Most of the energy recovery available on a mine haul road comes from anticipating the descent and letting regeneration hold speed. Taught properly, this is worth 8–15% of shift energy.
  2. Payload discipline. Overloading an electric tipper does not cause more fuel burn — it causes range shortfalls and tyre and suspension damage. Crews need to understand the new failure mode.
  3. Charging and swap procedure. Who connects, when, and what the minimum departure state of charge is. Written, enforced, and reported through telematics.
  4. Fault reporting. What to photograph, what data to capture, who to call. This is what converts a warranty claim from a dispute into a service ticket.
