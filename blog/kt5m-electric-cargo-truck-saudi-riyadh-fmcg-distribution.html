The Riyadh FMCG Route Profile

A typical FMCG rigid in Riyadh leaves the depot at 05:00, completes 18-25 drops over 140-190 km, and returns by mid-afternoon. Night replenishment routes for major supermarket chains run similar distances after dark. Neither pattern exceeds 220 km in a day, which means the KT5M's 310 kWh battery - good for 260-300 km of loaded city work - covers every route with a healthy reserve, even with the refrigeration-style load of dock-door idling and full air conditioning.

Drop density matters more than distance. Each stop brings dock waiting time where a diesel burns 1.5-2 litres per hour at idle; the electric truck runs the cab cooling from the pack at a trivial draw. Across 20 drops a day, that idle elimination alone saves 25-35 litres of diesel-equivalent energy per truck per week.

Depot Charging Layout That Works

The proven layout for a 20-truck Riyadh FMCG fleet is overnight AC or low-power DC charging for the base fleet - trucks return by 17:00 and need a full pack by 05:00, a 12-hour window that makes expensive fast charging unnecessary - plus one 120 kW DC unit for the 3-4 trucks running double shifts. Grid connection of 400-600 kVA covers it comfortably. Saudi industrial tariffs keep overnight energy cost low, and smart charging software that schedules sessions between 23:00 and 06:00 avoids peak tariffs entirely.

  • Assign each truck its own overnight bay with a 20-30 kW post; sharing causes morning exceptions.
  • Charge to 90% for daily routes; save 100% charges for the weekly long route.
  • Meter energy per truck per route - the data finds inefficient drivers and failing chargers within weeks.
  • Plan one DC fast charger per 6-8 trucks if you run Ramadan double-shift peaks.
