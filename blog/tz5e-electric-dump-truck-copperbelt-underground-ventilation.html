TZ5E Electric Dump Truck Underground: Copperbelt Ventilation Savings

Why the TZ5E Wins Underground Copperbelt Mines

The TZ5E heavy electric dump truck is an EV truck built for the confined, ventilation-limited tunnels of the Copperbelt. With a 424 kWh CATL LFP battery and a 424 kW LvKong motor, the TZ5E hauls 40 tonne payloads on 2-5 km underground loops while producing zero diesel exhaust at the face. The single biggest saving is ventilation: a diesel equivalent must push huge volumes of air to clear NOx and particulates, whereas the electric truck cuts the mine's ventilation load by up to 40 percent, directly lowering fan power and the diesel generator burn at the surface that feeds the underground air system.

The 40 Percent Ventilation Saving Explained

Underground mines size their primary fans for the worst-case diesel fleet. A diesel dump of this class emits roughly 8-12 kg of NOx and 0.3-0.5 kg of particulate per operating hour, forcing air changes of 0.05-0.08 m3/s per kW of installed diesel. Removing the diesel from the loop lets the Ventilation Officer cut primary fan speed by 30-45 percent, which matters because fans are often the single largest electrical load at the shaft. At a Copperbelt mine paying USD 0.10-0.20 per kWh, a 40 percent fan reduction on a 2 MW fan system saves USD 700,000-1.4 million per year, far more than the truck's own charging cost, and it also reduces the surface diesel generator set that backs up the air supply during grid dips.

Heat Load and Battery Swap Underground

Because the TZ5E adds almost no combustion heat at the face, the mine's cooling load drops alongside the ventilation load, a double win in deep hot workplaces. For continuous 20-hour production the truck supports a 5-6 minute battery swap at a mid-level swap bay, so a single chassis runs two shifts without a charger queue. Where swap is not installed, a 360 kW DC charger at the workshop fills the 424 kWh pack from 10 to 80 percent in about 60-75 minutes during the blast and re-entry window, which is idle time anyway.

Energy Cost Versus Diesel Mine Trucks

Mine-site electricity costs about USD 0.08-0.20 per kWh, while diesel at the remote site runs USD 0.90-1.30 per litre after delivery. The TZ5E uses roughly 1.6 kWh/km loaded, costing USD 0.13-0.32 per km. A diesel equivalent burns 0.45-0.55 L/km, costing USD 0.40-0.72 per km, and that diesel also carries the ventilation and cooling penalty above. Across a 15 truck underground fleet running 20 hours per day at 60 km of loop per day, the direct energy gap is worth USD 2.0-4.4 million annually, before the ventilation saving and the 35-40 percent maintenance reduction on the truck itself.

Productivity and Cycle Times

The 424 kW motor holds torque on the 1-in-8 and 1-in-10 ramps common in Copperbelt declines, so loaded climb speed stays 8-12 percent higher than a diesel that loses 3 percent of power per 300 m of depth. On a 3 km loop the TZ5E completes 16-22 cycles per shift at 40 tonnes, moving 640-880 tonnes per truck per day. With regen recovering 10-20 percent of the descent energy, the effective energy per cycle drops further on the downhill return, and there is no diesel warm-up or exhaust purge delay at the start of each blast cycle.

Drivers complete a two-day course covering one-pedal control, regen tuning for the ramp descent and safe high-voltage isolation during pack service.
