Why Airports Are Switching to Electric Ground Support Equipment

Airports face a unique combination of pressures: airside emissions rules, airline customers publishing scope-3 targets, and ground support equipment that idles for hours every day. An electric truck fits this environment perfectly because airport duty cycles are short-range, stop-start and return-to-base - exactly where electric drivetrains outperform diesel. The KT3F, with a 262 kWh battery and 250 km of range, covers a full day of apron work on a single charge. Replacing diesel units also removes exhaust particulates from the apron, a growing occupational health priority at major Jordanian airports, and eliminates fuel spill and refueling infrastructure from the airside environment.

Charging Infrastructure at Amman Airports

Airside charging needs planning but not heroics. A typical layout places DC fast chargers at the GSE depot or maintenance yard, with overnight AC or slower DC charging where vehicles park. The KT3F charges from 20% to 80% in roughly 60-90 minutes on a 120 kW DC charger, so even opportunity charging during shift breaks keeps availability high. Airports should involve their electrical engineering team early to confirm transformer capacity and cable routing under apron pavement. Landside power in Amman is generally adequate for a first fleet of five to fifteen electric trucks; larger programs justify a dedicated substation, which airport authorities can often fund through green infrastructure budgets.

Safety and Compliance Standards for Airside Operation

Airside vehicles operate under stricter rules than road trucks: IATA ground handling guidance, airport-specific vehicle permits, driver airside licenses and equipment marking standards all apply. Electric trucks must additionally document battery safety - the KT3F carries UN38.3-tested CATL LFP packs with multi-layer thermal protection, which simplifies airport safety case reviews. High-voltage isolation monitoring, orange cabling and lockout procedures align with standard airside safety management systems. We provide the full safety documentation pack with each truck, including emergency response guides that airport fire services can fold into their incident procedures, which measurably speeds up airside operating approvals.

Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the KT3F earns acceptance quickly. The 250 kW motor delivers instant torque with no gear hunting, the cab is quieter than any diesel equivalent, and regenerative braking reduces fatigue in stop-start duty. Drivers typically adapt within a week, and most prefer the electric truck once they experience a full shift without engine noise and vibration.
