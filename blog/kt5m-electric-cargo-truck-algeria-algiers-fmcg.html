KT5M Electric Cargo Truck for Algiers FMCG Distribution, Algeria

The KT5M electric cargo truck is an 18-tonne GVW EV truck built for the dense, time-windowed delivery runs that define fast-moving consumer goods distribution in Algiers, Algeria. Why Algiers FMCG fleets are electrifying

Algiers FMCG distributors run 150 to 250 km per vehicle per day across 25 to 40 drops, mostly between 06:00 and 18:00. Diesel box trucks in this duty burn 22 to 28 litres per 100 km in traffic, costing $24 to $31 per 100 km at Algeria's diesel price near $1.10 per litre. The KT5M replaces that spend with electricity at $0.07 to $0.16 per kWh for commercial users, and its silent operation lets distributors add early-morning drops in the Casbah and Hydra without breaching residential noise rules. With the city considering low-emission delivery zones for 2027, electric trucks also future-proof the licence to operate.

The second reason is reliability. Fewer moving parts mean fewer roadside failures during the tight delivery window, and the KT5M's flat torque curve suits the constant start-stop of urban distribution far better than a geared diesel.

Duty cycle: a full urban delivery day

On a 200 km Algiers route with 35 drops, the KT5M draws about 1.3 to 1.6 kWh per km including refrigeration and lift-gate use, so the day consumes 260 to 320 kWh. At $0.10 per kWh that is $26 to $32 of electricity, against $55 to $70 of diesel for a comparable truck. The 282 kWh pack finishes the day with reserve, and an overnight charge at the depot restores it fully within a 6-hour off-peak window at roughly $0.08 per kWh.

Regenerative braking on the hilly Bab El Oued and El Madina grades recovers 8 to 12 percent per trip, and the driver spends less time at the fuel station and more time on the route. Telematics show most FMCG fleets recover the range anxiety they expected within the first week of operation.

Total cost of ownership for an Algerian fleet

Over a 5-year, 300,000-km life, the KT5M beats diesel on three lines:

  • Energy: electricity at $0.05 to $0.18 per kWh versus diesel at $0.9 to $1.4 per litre — a 60 to 70 percent lower cost per km.
  • Maintenance: no engine oil, no clutch, regen brakes — about 35 percent lower service cost.
  • Residual: the CATL LFP pack holds capacity past 4,500 cycles, lifting trade-in value.

A 30-truck distributor switching to the KT5M avoids roughly $1.1 million in fuel and $0.5 million in maintenance over five years, against an FOB premium of about $0.9 million at $58,000 to $72,000 each. The payback lands inside 30 months, after which the margin flows straight to the bottom line on a contract that is otherwise fuel-exposed.

Charging and depot setup in Algiers

A depot serving 30 KT5M trucks needs six 120 kW DC chargers on a managed load, capping site demand near 700 kW. Algiers commercial tariffs fall to about $0.08 per kWh off-peak, so scheduling charging between 23:00 and 05:00 cuts the bill by a fifth. For sites without that capacity, a 60 kW overnight charger fully restores a pack in 5 to 6 hours, and a 100 kW solar canopy offsets daytime top-ups during summer peak.
