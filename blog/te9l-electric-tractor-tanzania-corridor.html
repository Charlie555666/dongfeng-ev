TE9L Electric Tractor for Tanzania's Dar es Salaam Corridor

The TE9L electric truck is a heavy 6x4 tractor aimed at Tanzania's central corridor, where the Dar es Salaam-to-Dodoma highway carries the bulk of the country's inland freight over roughly 450 kilometers. For fleet buyers, the question is whether an EV truck can make that leg economically, and the answer lies in disciplined charging staging. This review covers the TE9L's battery, corridor charging plan, and haulage economics for Tanzanian operators.

Why the Central Corridor Is Ready

Tanzania's central corridor is a fixed, high-volume route with depots at both ends and established truck stops mid-route, which is exactly the structure electric haulage needs. Diesel on the corridor is imported through Dar es Salaam and priced with port and currency premiums, while Tanzania's grid electricity is substantially hydro and gas, decoupling energy cost from the import chain. Structuring the 450 Kilometer Leg

The Dar-Dodoma leg exceeds a single charge, so the practical model is one mid-route top-up. A 240 kW charger at a Morogoro-area logistics stop adds 60 percent in about 75 minutes, which coincides with the driver's rest break. Fleets running tighter schedules can specify the battery-swap variant and cut the stop to 5 to 6 minutes. Regenerative braking on the escarpment descents recovers 8 to 12 percent of the climb energy, trimming the net charge needed mid-route.

Loaded range on Tanzanian highway duty runs 280 to 330 kilometers per charge in the warm season, so the leg splits cleanly into two segments of roughly 230 and 220 kilometers with comfortable margin on each.

  • Stage a 240 kW charger at the Dar and Dodoma depots.
  • Add one mid-route charger at a Morogoro logistics stop.
  • Time the charging stop to the driver's rest break.
  • Use regen mapping on escarpment descents to recover energy.
  • Choose the swap variant for daily round-trip schedules.

Corridor Economics Versus Diesel

A diesel tractor on the corridor burns roughly 38 liters per 100 kilometers loaded, and Tanzanian diesel carries port and currency premiums that inflate every liter. The TE9L uses about 1.3 kWh per kilometer at a fraction of the diesel cost per kilometer, and maintenance falls sharply with no oil, injectors, or aftertreatment to service. Over a 130,000 kilometer corridor year the combined saving is decisive for a fleet's margin.

The FOB price of $128,000 to $168,000 is recovered within three to four years at corridor utilization, and the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty extends well past the payback point.

Warranty, Resale, and Lifecycle Confidence

The battery warranty is the foundation of the ownership case: 8 years or 4,500 cycles on the CATL LFP pack, transferable to a second owner, which means the truck can be sold or refinanced mid-life with a factory guarantee still running. No diesel powertrain offers anything comparable, and that difference shows up in residual value, where documented, warranted electric trucks are clearing stronger percentages of original price than worn diesel equivalents. Fleets that keep disciplined charging habits, staying between 20 and 90 percent state of charge in daily operation, routinely show 85 to 90 percent pack capacity after five years, and the telematics health report converts that directly into resale price.
