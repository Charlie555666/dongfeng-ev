The Copper Pit Cycle

Kazakh copper haulage runs ore and waste from pit face to crusher or dump on roads of 4-12 km, with ramp grades of 8-10% and controlled speeds. A TZ5Y completes 6-9 cycles per shift; measured consumption on comparable Central Asian operations runs 3.8-4.4 kWh/km loaded, with the empty return partly recovered by regeneration on descending sections. Double-shift operation consumes roughly two packs per truck per day - the duty pattern battery swap exists to serve, with a 5-6 minute exchange keeping trucks on cycle around the clock.

The pits' own infrastructure helps: crushers and workshops are fixed nodes with serious power service, the natural homes for swap stations and charging banks.

The -30 C Answer

Cold affects batteries two ways: available capacity drops temporarily at low temperature, and charging below 0 C damages cells unless managed. The TZ5Y's cold package addresses both: the thermal system pre-heats packs before release from the charging bank - a pack leaving the swap station is already in its operating window - and cab pre-heating draws from the station, not the pack. The working winter numbers: expect 15-20% reduced range in sustained -25 to -30 C operation, planned into shift scheduling, with full performance restored as packs warm through the first working hour. Swap stations hold packs indoors at temperature, which is why swap beats plug charging decisively in the Kazakh winter.

  • House the swap station charging bank in an insulated building; warm packs are the whole game.
  • Pre-heat cabs and packs from station power before shift start.
  • Budget winter shifts at 75-80% of summer range; schedule cycles accordingly.
  • Keep the winter tyre and chain discipline unchanged - traction, not energy, sets the winter limits.

Fleet Economics on the Steppe

Kazakh industrial electricity is inexpensive and the pits' power infrastructure is substantial: the TZ5Y's energy cost runs 60-70% below diesel per hour worked, with the delivered-diesel premium at remote sites widening the gap further. Winter heating of diesel fleets - fuel that burns to keep engines alive overnight, not to haul - is a cost line the electric fleet simply deletes.
