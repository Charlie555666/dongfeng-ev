Do Electric Trucks Need Oil Changes?

Quick answer: No, an electric truck does not need engine oil, oil filters, a diesel particulate filter or AdBlue. A Dongfeng EV truck drivetrain still needs gear oil every 40,000 to 80,000 km, a coolant check and chassis grease, but there is no engine to lubricate and the 8-year CATL LFP battery needs no fluid.

Do Electric Trucks Need Oil Changes in the Engine Sense?

A diesel truck burns fuel in a piston engine and relies on engine oil to lubricate bearings, pistons and turbochargers. That engine does not exist on an EV truck. The electric truck is driven by a 282 kW to 510 kW motor that uses no combustion oil. There is no sump, no oil pump, no filter canister and no drain plug for engine oil. A Dongfeng EV truck therefore arrives at the workshop with one major service category removed entirely. For fleet operators who run 200,000 km per year, that removes four or five engine oil drains annually per vehicle, each costing 30 to 45 liters of oil plus labor.

Removing the engine also removes the failure modes tied to it. Blown head gaskets, turbo failures, EGR clogging and injection pump wear simply do not appear on an electric truck. The maintenance planner can delete an entire spreadsheet tab of diesel-specific tasks. This is the single biggest reason total cost of ownership falls on an EV truck, before a single kilowatt of electricity is cheaper than diesel.

What Fluids Does an EV Truck Still Need?

Even without engine oil, an EV truck still carries fluids that need attention. The key items are listed below, and each has a fixed interval tied to mileage or time rather than to fuel quality.

  • Gear oil in the reduction gearbox and drive axle, changed every 40,000 to 80,000 km.
  • Battery and power electronics coolant, checked at every service and replaced around 4 years or 200,000 km.
  • Chassis grease on kingpins, steering and fifth wheel, every 20,000 km.
  • Brake fluid, flushed on a 2-year interval regardless of mileage.
  • Windshield washer fluid and cabin coolant for the HVAC system.

The 8-year or 4,500-cycle CATL LFP battery itself is a sealed unit and needs no fluid top-up. Compared with a diesel truck that also needs fuel filters, air filters, DPF ash cleaning and AdBlue injection, the fluid list is short and predictable. A buyer in the Saudi Arabia electric truck market can plan the whole service year from a single table rather than reacting to fault lights.

Are Brake and Coolant Services Different from Diesel?

Regenerative braking on an EV truck does most of the slowing, so brake pads and discs wear 50 to 70 percent slower than on a diesel truck. A fleet that changed pads every 80,000 km on diesel often reaches 150,000 km on an electric truck. Coolant is the bigger change: the battery pack and motor run on a dedicated low-conductivity loop kept between 25 C and 35 C. Technicians check coolant concentration and conductivity at every service because contaminated coolant can trigger a thermal fault. There is no exhaust aftertreatment, so no DPF, no SCR catalyst and no AdBlue tank to maintain.

Can Battery Cooling Extend Service Life in Hot Climates?

Yes. The liquid cooling system holds the CATL LFP pack near 25 C to 35 C even when ambient air reaches 45 C to 50 C. In the Saudi Arabia electric truck market, packs that stay cool retain capacity better after 4,500 cycles, protecting the 8-year warranty. A TZ3V electric dump truck with active cooling showed less than 15 percent capacity loss after 4,000 cycles in desert trials, versus faster loss in passively cooled packs. Coolant inspection is therefore a core part of EV truck service, not an option, and a failed pump warns the driver early through the dashboard.
