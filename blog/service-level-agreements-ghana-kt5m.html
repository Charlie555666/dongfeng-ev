Uptime Is the Product: Why Availability Beats Price

A truck is a revenue instrument: every day it runs, it earns; every day it waits on parts or workshop, it costs twice - the repair bill plus the lost freight. This is why experienced fleet buyers evaluate the support agreement as hard as the truck, and why availability - the percentage of days the fleet is fit for dispatch - is the metric that actually prices the decision. What a Good Service Agreement Actually Contains

The agreement worth signing specifies five things in numbers, not adjectives. Response time: how fast support answers - remote diagnostics response within hours, because most electric truck faults are diagnosed from data before any spanner turns. Parts availability: the committed stock position - which components are held in Ghana or regionally, which ship from the factory, and the guaranteed dispatch windows for each class. Scheduled maintenance: what is included, at what intervals, at what price - the short electric schedule documented line by line. Technical support: the remote diagnostics subscription, software updates, and the training commitments for fleet technicians. And the availability commitment itself: the percentage the supplier stands behind, with the remedies - service credits, substitute vehicle arrangements - that give it teeth. Vague agreements are marketing; the Ghanaian fleets that run tight contracts hold their suppliers to numbers.

Measuring Availability Honestly

Availability math is simple to state and easy to game, so define it before signing anything. The honest formula: days fit for dispatch divided by total days, measured per truck per month, with agreed exclusions - scheduled maintenance windows count only beyond agreed duration, accident damage sits outside, customer-caused delays are documented separately. The KT5M's telematics makes the measurement automatic: the truck reports its own status daily, so the availability report generates from data both parties trust, rather than logs either party disputes. Benchmarks from operating box truck fleets: well-supported electric fleets hold 95-98% availability, comparable to or better than diesel benchmarks, because the electric failure modes are fewer and increasingly diagnosed remotely before they ground the truck. Fleets in Ghana running documented availability tracking report the side benefit: the data that enforces the SLA also optimizes the maintenance schedule.

Negotiating the Agreement: Where Buyers Have Leverage

Fleet buyers hold more leverage in the support negotiation than they typically use, because suppliers price agreements expecting them to be unread. The leverage points: fleet size - multi-truck orders justify committed parts stock and named support contacts; documentation - demand the response times, parts classes and remedies in the contract text, not the brochure; diagnostics access - the remote support channel should be specified with its own response commitment; and the exit terms - what happens to parts supply and software support at agreement end, because a truck outlives its first contract. The KT5M's 8-year battery warranty anchors the long view: the largest component risk is covered in writing for the fleet's entire first ownership cycle, and the negotiation concerns the remainder. Running the SLA: the Quarterly Rhythm

A service agreement earns its keep through operation, not signature - and the rhythm that works is quarterly. The review agenda: availability actuals against commitment, per truck, from the telematics data; response times against the contractual numbers, with the outliers named; parts performance - what shipped fast, what waited, and the stock adjustments agreed; and forward planning - the next quarter's scheduled maintenance mapped against the fleet's freight calendar so service windows cost minimum revenue. The KT5M's reporting generates the review pack from fleet data, so the meeting runs on shared facts. Fleets in Ghana that hold this rhythm report agreements that improve over time - each quarter's review tightens the next quarter's service - while fleets that file the contract discover its gaps during breakdowns. Charging Planning for KT5M Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT5M supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 140 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Ghana should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect. We support every fleet order with a charging layout review and charger specification guidance matched to the trucks delivered.
