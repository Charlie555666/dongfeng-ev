Why Quarries Suit Electric Dump Trucks

A quarry loop is a closed, predictable circuit. The truck loads at the face, climbs a grade to the crusher, dumps, and descends to load again, all within a few kilometers of a charging point. There is no public-road range risk and no charging anxiety, because the depot charger sits at the loop. The KTA1's sealed LvKong motor also shrugs off the silica dust that clogs a diesel's air intake, cutting filter costs dramatically. Payload, Gradeability, and Cycle Time

The KTA1 carries a 20,000 kilogram payload and climbs a 30 percent grade while loaded, which covers the steepest ramps found on Nigerian granite benches. Cycle time on a typical 3 kilometer loop runs 12 to 15 minutes including load, climb, dump, and descent, so a single truck can complete 40 to 50 cycles per productive shift. Because the descent regenerates energy, the net battery draw per cycle is far lower than the climb alone would suggest.

Operators report that regenerative braking on the loaded descent recovers 10 to 15 percent of the climb energy, extending real-world range to 160 to 200 kilometers per charge even on grade-heavy loops. That is enough for a full shift without mid-shift charging, and the 450 kWh pack still leaves margin for auxiliary loads like the hydraulic tailgate.

Charging and Quarry Depot Design

The KTA1 returns to the quarry workshop every shift, so a 240 kW DC charger at the depot restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes during the lunch break and overnight. Quarries running two shifts can add a second charger to keep six or eight dump trucks in rotation. Battery swap is not needed because the truck never leaves the quarry radius.

  • Site a 240 kW charger at the workshop for break and overnight top-ups.
  • Pair one charger with four to five trucks on a staggered shift pattern.
  • Train operators to feather the throttle on grade to maximize regen recovery.
  • Seal the motor bay housing to keep silica dust out of the drivetrain.
  • Track cycles per charge to schedule maintenance before pack stress.

Operating Cost Versus Diesel

A diesel dump of similar payload burns roughly 30 liters per hour of quarry duty, about $24 at Nigerian diesel prices, while the KTA1 draws about $5 of electricity per hour at an industrial rate of $0.12 per kWh. Over a 3,000-hour annual duty that is roughly $57,000 saved on energy alone. The sealed LvKong motor also removes air-filter changes, which on a dusty bench can be a weekly cost on a diesel unit, plus oil changes and injector service.

The FOB premium of $120,000 to $165,000 versus a diesel tipper is recovered through these savings inside roughly two to three years for a busy quarry, after which the KTA1 runs at a clear per-ton cost advantage for the rest of its life. The 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty covers essentially the whole payback window.
