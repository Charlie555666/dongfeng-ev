Riyadh-Jeddah Corridor Duty

The corridor links the capital with the Red Sea port through desert highway with long grades and high summer heat. A freight truck runs loaded one way and often partially loaded on return, so the energy plan must cover the harder direction with margin. The KTH1's 424 kWh pack delivers a real-world range of roughly 200 to 350 km depending on load, speed and cabin cooling, which sets the spacing of charging stops along the route.

CATL LFP chemistry underpins the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty, giving fleet managers a known pack life over the asset period. The LvKong motor's high torque holds highway speed on climbs without the fuel penalty a diesel shows at full load, and the smooth electric delivery protects fragile cargo on the long flat runs between cities.

Charging Stops and Route Planning

A 424 kWh truck cannot run Riyadh to Jeddah non-stop, so the plan uses one or two highway charging stops. At each stop a fast charge restores enough range to reach the next, and the driver rest break overlaps the charge. For fleets with swap capability, a 5 to 6 minute battery swap at a corridor station removes the wait entirely. The key is siting chargers where trucks already stop, so the pause is productive rather than lost time.

Load, Cooling and Summer Operation

Summer cabin and battery cooling draws energy, so the TCO model must include it. The KTH1 manages this with a cooled pack and efficient cabin HVAC so range stays inside the planned window even above 45 C. Pre-conditioning the cabin while plugged in at the depot saves pack energy for the road, and a conservative cruise speed is the single biggest lever on corridor range.

Fleet Sizing and Depot Charging

A corridor fleet needs both trucks and a depot charge plan at each end. Overnight charging at Riyadh and Jeddah depots resets packs, while en-route fast charging covers the middle. Fleet managers should model total site load with the local utility before scaling unit count, because a row of depot chargers can exceed a small substation's capacity. Downtime and Maintenance Savings

An electric cargo tractor removes most of the failure points that idle a diesel unit on a long corridor. There is no engine oil to change, no diesel aftertreatment to clog, and far fewer rotating parts to wear in heat. Regenerative braking on every descent recovers energy and cuts friction brake wear, which extends component life on a high-mileage duty. For an operator that measures availability by kilometers per day, fewer unscheduled repairs mean more consistent freight flow and a smaller parts bill. The LFP pack's long cycle life also means the main energy component rarely needs attention inside the warranty window.
