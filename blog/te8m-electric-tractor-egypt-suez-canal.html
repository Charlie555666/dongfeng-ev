Why the Suez Zone Suits Electric Tractors

The Suez Canal Economic Zone concentrates freight into repeatable loops: Port Said, East Port Said, and Sokhna each feed industrial zones 20 to 120 kilometers away, and the trucks return to controlled yards every shift. Egypt's government has designated the zone a green logistics showcase, which brings both incentives and future emissions rules that favor early electrification. Port-to-Zone Haulage Performance

On the zone's flat, paved corridors the TE8M delivers 280 to 330 kilometers of loaded range per charge, so a Sokhna-to-zone round trip runs comfortably on one charge with margin, and longer Port Said loops need only a short depot top-up between turns. The 510 kW LvKong motor gives confident overtaking with a 49-ton combination, and regenerative braking recovers energy on every approach to the congested port gates.

Drivers report the low-vibration cab and single-pedal driving reduce fatigue on the repetitive port loop, which matters for retention in a corridor where skilled drivers are scarce.

Charging Plan for Zone Fleets

The zone model is a 240 kW charger at each fleet yard, restoring 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes during loading windows and overnight. Operators running two shifts add a second charger and stagger departure times. Egypt's industrial electricity tariffs make overnight charging particularly cheap, and the zone's solar resources make a photovoltaic canopy over the yard charger a genuine cost saver.

  • Install a 240 kW charger at the zone fleet yard.
  • Charge during loading windows and overnight on off-peak tariffs.
  • Add a solar canopy over the yard charger to cut energy cost.
  • Stagger two-shift departures so one charger serves four tractors.
  • Track energy per round trip to benchmark each port loop.
