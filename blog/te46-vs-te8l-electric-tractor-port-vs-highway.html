TE46 vs TE8L Electric Tractor: Port Yard or Highway Duty?

Two Tractors, Two Jobs

An electric truck tractor is not one product, and the TE46 and TE8L sit at opposite ends of the duty spectrum. The TE46 electric terminal tractor carries a 262 kWh pack at $75,000-95,000 for GCW 45-65 t yard duty, while the TE8L runs 350-400 kWh at $95,000-120,000 for 49 t highway haul. Picking wrong means a yard truck that cannot leave the gate or a highway truck too costly for the terminal, and either mistake shows up as idle iron the day the contract starts drawing against the forecast. The two are siblings in the catalog but cousins in use, and the buyer who specs by price alone usually buys the wrong one for the loop and discovers it when the first penalty clock starts.

GCW, Battery and Duty Cycle

The TE46 is built for terminal shuffling: short, slow, constant moves where a 262 kWh pack covers a full shift and the 45-65 t GCW matches chassis and trailer in the yard. The TE8L targets regional highway at 49 t GCW with a 350-400 kWh pack for 250-300 km legs between cities. Both draw on LvKong motors in the 282-510 kW band and CATL LFP cells with the 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty, so the reliability story is shared even though the missions could not be more different in pace and distance. The TE46 trades top speed for low-speed torque and a tight turning circle, while the TE8L trades those for aerodynamics and a pack sized for the open road where the nearest plug is 200 km away and the clock is the law.

Swap vs Charge on Each Duty

The TE46 in a port often uses battery swap in 5-6 minutes because a terminal never stops, while the TE8L on highway relies on DC fast charge at 120-360 kW, reaching 10-80 percent in 45-90 minutes during a mandatory rest break. A yard tractor that must charge for an hour loses throughput; a highway tractor that charges during the legal break loses none. Match the energy method to the clock, not the catalog, because the duty cycle is what decides whether the pack is an asset or an anchor on the shift schedule, and a port that swaps the TE46 can run it 20 hours a day on two packs with zero plug waiting and zero driver idle.

Shared Parts Across the Pair

Although the two tractors serve different jobs, they share the LFP cell format, the LvKong motor family and the 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty, which keeps the parts cage small for a fleet that runs both. A yard-and-highway operator can stock one motor type and one pack service routine, train mechanics once, and swap knowledge between the two trucks instead of building two support teams. The sharing also helps resale, because the used market for a common pack is deeper than for a one-off, and insurers price the familiar hardware more cheaply than a rare import they have never priced before. That commonality is why specifying both from the same maker beats mixing brands just to shave a few thousand off the yard tractor's ticket and then paying for it in doubled training.

Matching the Truck to the Operation

For a container port moving 60 t stacks across 2 km, the TE46's lower ticket and swap model beat a heavy highway tractor on cost per move. For a 280 km intercity run at 49 t, only the TE8L's range works. When in doubt, score the duty on the points below so the purchase order follows the physics instead of the sales deck, and the finance team sees a payback it can actually bank, because a tractor speced to the wrong leg bleeds money on every kilometer it was never built to run and never will:

  • Average leg length and whether a rest break exists
  • GCW of your heaviest regular combination
  • Swap cabin availability versus DC charger access
  • Shifts per day and the cost of a charge downtime
  • FOB budget and the 8-year battery cover

Kenya and East Africa Ports

Growing port and corridor trade needs both profiles, and the Kenya electric truck market is a good example where Mombasa yard tractors and Nairobi highway hauls run as one logistics chain. A fleet can deploy TE46 units inside the port and TE8L units on the inland route, sharing the same LFP pack and service know-how. Specifying by duty, not by brand, keeps the whole chain electric without a mismatch that leaves one link burning diesel while the other sits on a charger, and the shared parts story means a single spare pack can rescue either truck if a unit goes down far from a service bay on the corridor.

Charging Infrastructure for the Pair

The infrastructure each tractor needs is different, and planning both together is where yards-and-highway fleets save the most. The TE46 in a port wants a swap cabin or a bank of depot AC and DC plugs sized for short top-ups between moves, because it never leaves the fence and the energy is small per cycle. The TE8L wants a 120-360 kW DC site on the corridor and a charging plan that lands in the legal rest break, so the truck arrives home with a low pack and leaves full on the off-peak rate. A fleet running both should put the swap cabin inside the gate and the highway DC charger at the depot exit, then let telematics decide which truck pulls which plug instead of a manual rota that wastes the window and leaves a unit parked on a charger it does not need while another waits.

The shared LFP pack means one spare and one training cover both, and the capital for the two charger types is modest against the fuel it replaces across a year of operation. Specifying the plugs with the trucks, not after, is what keeps the first week of operation on schedule rather than a scramble to find a 360 kW connection the utility never promised in writing. It also protects the business case, because a tractor that cannot be charged on time quietly destroys the per-km saving the whole electric program was built on, and the cost of a missed charge shows up first in the late delivery penalty, not in the electricity bill where the planner was watching the rate and feeling pleased with the saving.

Choose the TE46 for the yard and the TE8L for the road, and your electric truck fleet will earn its keep at both ends of the logistics chain instead of struggling at one, which is the only way a mixed port-to-hinterland corridor actually goes fully electric without a costly mismatch that the diesel dealer will happily point out.
