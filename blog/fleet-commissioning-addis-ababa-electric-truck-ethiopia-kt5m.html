Why Commissioning Decides the First Year

The difference between electric truck deployments that thrive and those that struggle is rarely the truck - it is the first thirty days. Commissioning is where specification meets reality: chargers energized and tested, drivers trained, telematics configured, maintenance systems updated, and the first routes run under supervision instead of assumption. Fleets that treat delivery as a transaction improvise these steps and learn expensively; fleets that treat it as a program capture the electric truck's economics from week one. The KT5M electric box truck delivery program is built around this principle: a box truck engineered for its ordered duty, handed over through a structured commissioning sequence that this guide walks through - because the KT5M's 140 kWh pack and 280 km of range only deliver their value when the operation around them is ready.

Pre-Delivery: The Sixty Days Before the Truck Lands

Commissioning begins two months before arrival, with three parallel tracks. Infrastructure: chargers installed, commissioned and load-tested - the utility connection is the long-lead item in Addis Ababa, and it should be energized weeks before the truck, not the day after. People: drivers nominated and scheduled for training, technicians booked for high-voltage familiarization, a fleet champion named who owns the program internally. Paperwork: import and certification documentation - ESA conformity assessment for Ethiopia - completed against the vessel schedule so clearance at the Djibouti rail corridor is a formality, and registration, insurance and telematics accounts set up in advance. The fleets that execute pre-delivery properly take the truck from port to revenue service in days; the ones that improvise measure the gap in weeks.

Delivery Week: Inspection, Energization and First Charges

Delivery week runs a defined sequence. Day one: delivery inspection against the checklist - exterior, interior, underbody, fluid and coolant levels, torque markers, documented damage if any - with the pre-delivery inspection report as the reference. Day two: first charge to full on your own charger, verifying charge rate, connector behavior and telematics reporting against specification. Days three to five: driver training - regenerative braking technique, eco driving, charging procedure, instrument familiarization - followed by supervised first routes on the actual duty cycle. The KT5M arrives with commissioning documentation covering each step, and our remote support team monitors the first charges and runs with your technicians. Nothing in the week is complicated; everything in the week matters.

Driver Training: The Highest-Return Day of the Program

A single structured training day returns value for the life of the truck. The curriculum that works: regenerative braking anticipation - the habit that converts traffic into recovered energy and stretches both range and brake life; smooth launch discipline, which protects tires and energy consumption together; charging discipline - when to charge, how far, and why 20-80% is the fast window; and instrument literacy, so the range display becomes a tool rather than an anxiety. Drivers finishing the day consistently deliver 10-15% better energy economy than untrained drivers on the same route - on the KT5M's duty cycle, that is free range the fleet keeps forever. Refresher sessions at ninety days, using the fleet's own telematics data, convert training into permanent habit.

The First Ninety Days: KPIs and Course Correction

The first quarter establishes the empirical baseline every subsequent decision uses. Track five KPIs weekly: energy consumption per kilometer by route; state-of-charge at day end, which reveals whether capacity matches duty with margin; charge session timing and duration, which validates the charger schedule; unplanned stops, which should trend to near zero; and driver feedback themes, which surface the small frictions worth fixing early. The KT5M's telematics delivers all five without additional hardware. Expect one course correction in the quarter - a route reassignment, a charging schedule adjustment, a payload tweak - and treat it as the system working, not failing. Fleets in Ethiopia that run the ninety-day discipline end the quarter with a proven operating model and the data to justify the next units.

Charging Planning for KT5M Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT5M supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 140 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Ethiopia should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect. We support every fleet order with a charging layout review and charger specification guidance matched to the trucks delivered.
