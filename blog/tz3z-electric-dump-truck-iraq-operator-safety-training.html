TZ3Z Electric Dump Truck in Iraq: Operator Training and Safety Program

Why Iraq Fleets Need a Structured EV Truck Training Program

An EV truck changes the daily routine on an Iraq construction or reconstruction site, and the safest way to adopt it is through a formal operator program rather than trial and error. The TZ3Z electric dump truck is a 6x4 rigid hauler with a 350 kWh CATL LFP pack and LvKong motors up to 360 kW, built for the mixed duty of earthmoving, aggregate and debris clearing. Because the power train carries up to 600 V DC, the operator program starts with high-voltage awareness before anyone touches the controls.

Iraq's reconstruction push has pulled thousands of dumps onto sites where crews are used to diesel. The TZ3Z keeps the same body, same tipping gear and same controls layout, so the learning curve is mostly about energy, not driving. The training plan below turns that small delta into a certified skill set that sites can audit.

High-Voltage Safety Fundamentals

The first module teaches what a 600 V DC system can do and how the TZ3Z keeps it safe. The pack sits in a sealed, vented enclosure with automatic contactors that open the moment the key is off or a fault is sensed. Operators learn to read the high-voltage ready light, to never break a colored orange conduit, and to call the maintenance permit before any underside work.

  • Identify orange high-voltage cabling and never modify or route loads on it.
  • Confirm the ready light is off and the key is out before any service.
  • Know the location of the manual service disconnect and how to pull it.
  • Report any pack warning, cabin smell or thermal alert before continuing work.
  • Keep extinguishers rated for lithium fires at the depot and on the site.

These five points are the backbone of the permit-to-work system, and they are tested in writing before a driver earns the TZ3Z badge. Sites that skip this step see avoidable downtime; sites that enforce it run the electric dump like any other asset.

Training Curriculum and Certificates

The full program runs two days for a new operator and one refresher day per year. Day one is classroom on LFP behavior, thermal events and emergency response. Day two is yard practice: hoist, load, ramp and the energy screen. Drivers who pass both get a certificate valid for twelve months, after which the refresher renews it.

The certificate matters for Iraq tenders, where principals increasingly ask for evidence that high-voltage plant is run by trained staff. A fleet with 100 percent certified TZ3Z operators can show that evidence on demand and avoid the stop-work orders that hit untrained sites.

Site Safety Program and Isolation

The depot runs a controlled charging zone with a permit system, and only the licensed maintenance tech opens the pack. The TZ3Z uses a manual service disconnect that physically breaks the HV loop, and the program teaches every operator to pull it before any underbody task. That single habit removes the only real shock risk on a normal working day.

Dust and heat are the Iraq site constants. The TZ3Z pack is IP67 sealed, but the cooling fins need a weekly blow-out where fine dust is heavy, and the cabin filter is changed on a shorter cycle than in temperate climates. The program bakes both into the monthly plan so they are never forgotten under deadline pressure.

Cold and Dust Season Plan

Iraq swings from 50°C summer to near-freezing winter nights, and fine dust is year-round. The TZ3Z plan adjusts by season: in summer the chiller runs higher duty and the pack is charged at night; in winter the pre-heat is brief and the regen is stronger on the empty return. The dust plan is constant: a weekly blow-out of the motor and pack fins, because a clogged fin cuts cooling by 20 to 30 percent and quietly shortens pack life.
