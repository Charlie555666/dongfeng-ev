Planning depot charging for this EV truck

Charging an electric truck is a depot discipline, not a public-network gamble. A 262 kWh pack working a 240 km day typically returns with 25 to 35 percent in reserve, so overnight demand is about 157 kWh per truck. On a 120 kW DC charger the pack climbs from 20 to 80 percent in roughly 79 minutes; on 240 kW that falls to about 39 minutes, which lets two trucks share one charger on staggered shifts.

Size the site transformer for your busiest shift plus 30 percent headroom, keep cable runs under 30 metres to limit voltage drop, and meter each charger separately so energy cost per truck stays visible. In Nigeria, grid connection lead times of three to nine months are common, so apply for the connection the day you sign the truck order, not the day the ship arrives. Energy cost per kilometre: EV truck versus diesel

A comparable diesel washing truck burns about 0.38 litres per kilometre in mixed duty; at $0.95 per litre that is roughly $0.36 per km. The electric equivalent consumes about 1.15 kWh per km fully loaded; at an industrial tariff of $0.10 per kWh the cost is near $0.12 per km, and overnight or solar-coupled charging can push it below $0.08. Over 70,000 km a year the saving is about $17,000 per truck, before any carbon or low-emission-zone benefit.

The gap widens in stop-start duty, where regenerative braking returns 15 to 25 percent of traction energy instead of burning it as brake dust. In Nigeria, where diesel quality and price both fluctuate, locking in an electricity tariff also removes a major budgeting variable from the fleet P&L.

Maintenance and uptime economics

The KT7A has roughly one moving drivetrain part where a diesel has hundreds: no engine oil, no DPF, no turbocharger, no clutch, no multi-gear transmission service. Scheduled maintenance lands near $0.035 per km against $0.11 per km for a diesel equivalent, a saving of more than $5,000 a year at typical utilisation. Brake pads last two to three times longer because the motor does most of the deceleration.

Uptime matters more than parts cost. Fleet data from Chinese operators running the same e-axle platform shows 96 to 98 percent availability once drivers are trained, versus 92 to 94 percent for diesel trucks of the same age. What to confirm before you order

  • Duty cycle: longest daily route, loaded and empty legs, gradient profile, and ambient temperature range.
  • Charging: available transformer capacity, overnight window, and whether opportunity charging or battery swap fits the shift plan.
  • Homologation: which national or regional standard applies in Nigeria, and who holds the type-approval paperwork.
  • Payload: body weight versus rated GVW, so the 240 km real range holds with your actual load.
  • Support: local partner for HV work, spare-parts stock level, and response time commitments in writing.
  • Finance: FOB or CFR terms, letter-of-credit conditions, and currency cover for the balance on arrival.
