Electric Maintenance Is Simpler - but It Is Not Nothing

The electric drivetrain deletes the diesel engine's entire service schedule - no oil, filters, injectors, turbo, clutch, gearbox oil or exhaust aftertreatment - and with it, most of the workshop hours a diesel fleet budgets. What remains is a shorter, cleaner schedule: brake fluid and pads (which last far longer under regenerative braking), coolant for the battery and power electronics thermal loops, gear oil in the reduction drive, suspension, steering, tires and the software checks that replace mechanical inspections. The TE8M electric 6x4 tractor on typical 6x4 tractor duty runs service intervals that a diesel equivalent cannot match, and fleet data across Uzbekistan shows maintenance cost per kilometer settling 40-60% below diesel. The mistake is swinging from over-maintenance to neglect: the remaining items are fewer, not optional, and the fleets with the best uptime are the ones that run the short schedule religiously.

The Electric Truck Service Schedule, Line by Line

The working schedule for the TE8M in Uzbek conditions breaks into four rhythms. Daily, driver-level: tire condition and pressure, lights, brake feel, coolant and washer levels, charge-port inspection - five minutes, no tools. Monthly, technician-level: brake pad measurement (regen carries most braking, so wear is slow but must be verified), suspension and steering inspection, thermal-system coolant check, software and telematics fault-code review. Quarterly: reduction-drive oil condition, high-voltage connector inspection, battery health report pull, alignment check on steer tires. Annually: full high-voltage system inspection by a certified technician, thermal-loop coolant replacement per schedule, comprehensive battery diagnostic with documented state of health. The entire annual program consumes roughly a third of the workshop hours of the diesel equivalent - which is precisely why skipping it is tempting and wrong.

Technician Skills and High-Voltage Safety

The skills question intimidates fleets unnecessarily. A competent diesel technician becomes a competent electric truck technician through structured training - the mechanical systems are familiar, and the new content is electrical: high-voltage safety isolation procedures, diagnostic software, battery and power electronics fundamentals. The safety discipline is real but learnable: orange-cabled systems carry up to 600-plus volts, isolation and verification procedures are mandatory before any high-voltage work, and insulated tooling is standard equipment. The TE8M is engineered for safe service - clearly marked isolation points, interlocks that depower the system when opened, and service documentation written for working technicians. Parts Planning: What to Stock and Why

The electric parts list is short, which changes inventory economics fundamentally. The stock that matters: wear items - brake pads and discs, tires, wiper blades, lighting; consumables - coolant for the thermal loops, reduction-drive oil, filters for cab and cooling systems; and a small strategic reserve of electric-specific components - charge-port assemblies, contactors, thermal-system pumps, selected sensors - chosen by failure-rate data rather than anxiety. The TE8M's commonality across the Dongfeng EV range keeps the list shorter still for mixed fleets. What disappears from the parts budget is the diesel's endless appetite: injectors, turbochargers, clutch kits, DPF units, AdBlue. Fleets in Uzbekistan report parts inventory value falling by half or more against their diesel operation, with the bonus that remote diagnostics mean parts are ordered against confirmed faults instead of stocked against guesses.
