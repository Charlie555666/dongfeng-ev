KT5L Electric Cargo Truck for Cote d'Ivoire Cocoa Export Routes

Why the KT5L Suits Cote d'Ivoire Cocoa Export Routes

The KT5L electric cargo truck is an EV truck built for the 350 km Abidjan to San Pedro corridor that carries most of Cote d'Ivoire's cocoa crop to port. With a 262 kWh CATL LFP battery and a LvKong motor rated 282 kW, the KT5L moves a 12 tonne payload of bagged cocoa at a real-world 1.2-1.5 kWh/km, giving a loaded range of 210-320 km that covers a single-leg port run on one charge. For cooperatives and logistics firms shipping under sustainability-linked buyer contracts, the truck also produces verifiable zero tailpipe emissions on the busiest export road in West Africa, which supports premium certification and lower port-side queuing fees.

Abidjan to San Pedro Charging Map

The corridor is well suited to depot charging because both ends already run 24-hour logistics yards. A 240 kW DC charger at the Abidjan consolidation warehouse returns the 262 kWh pack to 80 percent in about 60 minutes during load-out, and a second 180 kW unit at the San Pedro port gate tops the truck for the empty return leg. For operators running two round trips per day, a single overnight charge plus a midday top-up covers 700 km of total movement. Where grid power is unstable, a 200 kW battery buffer smooths the load and protects the pack from brownout events that are common in the rainy season.

Energy Cost Versus Diesel Box Trucks

Cote d'Ivoire industrial electricity costs about USD 0.10-0.20 per kWh, against diesel at USD 0.90-1.20 per litre. The KT5L uses roughly 1.3 kWh/km loaded, so energy runs USD 0.13-0.26 per km. A diesel box truck of the same class burns 0.45-0.55 L/km, costing USD 0.40-0.66 per km. Across a 40 truck fleet travelling 250 km per day for 300 days, the energy gap is worth USD 1.6-3.8 million annually before counting the 35-40 percent maintenance saving from eliminating the diesel engine, exhaust and manual gearbox. At current cocoa freight margins that energy saving is the difference between a 4 percent and a 9 percent operating margin on the transport leg.

Payload, Cycle Times and Cocoa Season Peaks

During the October to March main harvest, volumes spike and night running becomes normal. The KT5L's instant torque and smooth one-pedal control let drivers hold 70-85 km/h on the corridor with less fatigue over a 10-hour shift. A 12 tonne load moves 480-600 km per day across two legs, and the sealed electric drivetrain shrugs off the dust and humidity that shorten clutch life on diesel rivals. Quiet operation also allows earlier morning departures from cooperative yards without disturbing nearby housing, which matters where collection points sit inside villages rather than at industrial sidings.

Maintenance and Uptime in Tropical Conditions

The 262 kWh pack sits in an IP67 enclosure rated for tropical storms and road spray, and the motor has a single moving assembly versus hundreds of reciprocating diesel parts. Key service intervals to plan for:

  • Brake linings every 80,000-100,000 km thanks to regen braking doing most of the slowing.
  • Coolant and drive lubricant every 60,000 km, not every 15,000 km like a diesel.
  • No diesel particulate filter, turbo or EGR valve to fail in humid heat.
  • 12 V auxiliary battery and cabin filter are the only monthly consumables.
  • Over-the-air diagnostics flag cell imbalance before it costs range.
  • Tropical-rated tyres and wheel bearings remain the main wear items at 50,000 km.

Driver Comfort, Cabin and Load Security

Cocoa logistics runs on driver retention as much as fuel cost. The KT5L's silent electric drivetrain removes the vibration and heat of a diesel inline-six, so drivers finish a 10-hour Abidjan-San Pedro shift with less fatigue and fewer complaint calls to the depot. The sealed battery tunnel leaves more floor space for a flat, low-step cabin, and the instant torque means smoother pull-away under a 12 tonne load, which protects sacked cocoa from the jolts that break bags and invite moisture loss at the port weighbridge where a rejected lot can cost a cooperative thousands of dollars in a single morning.

Cabin and load features that matter on this route:

  • Low-noise cabin keeps in-cab sound under 70 dB versus 80 dB plus in a diesel box truck.
  • Flat cargo floor and rated lashing points secure 25 kg cocoa sacks against cornering.
  • Pre-conditioning cools the cabin on grid power before the driver boards, saving pack range.
  • One-pedal driving reduces left-foot workload on the congested Abidjan ring road.
  • Telematics log speed, payload and energy per trip for buyer sustainability reporting.
  • Regen on the gentle grades back to Abidjan recovers 10-20 percent of the climb energy.

For cooperatives exporting under traceability programs, the per-trip energy and emissions log is directly reusable as evidence in carbon-linked premium contracts, turning the EV truck from a cost item into a documentation asset that supports a higher farm-gate price. A 40 truck fleet logging 250 km per day generates roughly 3,000 auditable trip records per month, enough to satisfy the strictest European buyer and to defend the sustainability claim if it is ever challenged at the port or in the importing country's customs review.

The KT5L also simplifies the driver pool. Because there is no clutch and no gearshift, new operators reach productive speed in one day instead of two weeks, which matters in Cote d'Ivoire's tight haulage labour market during the October to March harvest peak when every extra truck on the road earns premium freight. Combined with the USD 0.13-0.26 per km energy cost against USD 0.40-0.66 per km for diesel, the cabin and documentation advantages compound into a freight margin that is both wider and more stable across the season.
