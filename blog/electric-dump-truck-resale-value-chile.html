How Electric Trucks Depreciate vs Diesel

Depreciation is the largest hidden cost in any truck fleet, and electric trucks follow a different curve than diesel. A diesel truck loses value fastest in years one to three; an electric truck's value tracks battery health more than odometer kilometers. Fleets in Chile are discovering that a well-documented EV truck with verified battery state of health holds value better than early skeptics predicted, precisely because the second buyer can quantify the most expensive component. The TZ3V, with an 8-year/4,500-cycle battery warranty that transfers to the second owner, enters the used market with a factory-backed floor under its value that no diesel truck can match.

Battery State of Health Drives Resale Price

In a used electric truck transaction, the battery report is the valuation. A 600 kWh CATL LFP pack showing 85-90% state of health after five years still delivers most of the original 220 km range, which keeps the truck fully usable for urban and regional duty. LFP chemistry degrades slowly and predictably, so buyers trust the data. Sellers should pull the BMS health report, keep full charging records, and note whether the truck fast-charged daily or mostly charged overnight - gentle charging history adds measurable value. Refurbishment Checklist Before Resale

Preparing a used electric truck for sale is cheaper than diesel refurbishment - no engine overhaul, no injectors, no exhaust system. The checklist focuses on cosmetics, brakes, tires, suspension bushings and a full high-voltage system inspection by a certified technician. Update the BMS software to the latest release, replace cabin filters and wiper consumables, and document every item in a sale pack. A professional detail and a fresh battery health report do more for the sale price than any mechanical spend. Budget $1,500-$3,500 for a thorough preparation on a truck that has been fleet-maintained, and recover it several times over in the sale price.

Maximizing Residual Value from Day One

Residual value is built on the day the truck enters service, not the day it is listed for sale. Specify a battery size matched to real duty cycles - an oversized pack degrades slower per kilometer but costs more to buy. Keep charging below 90% for daily use, avoid sustained operation at very high or very low state of charge, and keep every service record in one file. Fit telematics from day one so utilization data supports the resale story. Fleets that follow these practices on TZ3V units consistently achieve resale values 10-15 percentage points higher than fleets that treat the truck as a disposable asset.

Deployment Timeline from Order to Operation

Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the TZ3V earns acceptance quickly.
