TZ3V EV Truck Warranty Guide: What South African Mining Fleets Actually Get

The Dongfeng TZ3V electric truck has become a popular EV truck choice for open-pit and surface mining fleets operating around Johannesburg, Pretoria and the Bushveld. South African buyers are rightly focused on the warranty because a 31 tonne GVW electric dump truck is a high-utilisation asset where downtime is measured in lost tonnes rather than lost kilometres. This guide explains exactly what the standard warranty covers, what the claim process looks like on the ground, where the authorised service points sit, and how the cover compares with the typical diesel dump truck warranty you may be replacing.

What the standard TZ3V warranty covers

Every Dongfeng TZ3V electric dump truck ships from the Xi'an plant with a two-layer factory warranty. The first layer is the high-voltage battery and energy system, covered for 8 years or 4,500 charge cycles, whichever comes first, with a guaranteed 70 percent state of health at the end of that period. The second layer is the driveline and chassis, covered for 3 years or 150,000 km. These terms are written into the commercial invoice and the certificate of conformity that accompanies every unit shipped to South Africa, so the cover is enforceable through Shaanxi Fenghan Trading rather than through a local dealer you may never meet again. Fleet buyers should keep a single warranty file per VIN containing the invoice, conformity certificate and the battery health baseline report captured at commissioning.

Battery warranty in detail

The CATL LFP battery in the TZ3V is rated at 350 kWh and is the most expensive single component on the EV truck. The 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty is designed around a typical mining duty cycle of two to three charge events per 24-hour shift. At 4,500 cycles the pack will have delivered well over one million kilometres of equivalent service before the warranty ceiling is reached, which for most South African fleets means the battery is still covered for the entire financing term of the vehicle. The 70 percent state-of-health floor protects residual value and gives fleet managers a clear, contractually defined replacement trigger rather than a vague degraded battery dispute. State of health is measured with the factory diagnostic tool, and the reading is logged during every scheduled service so the trajectory is documented from day one.

Driveline and chassis coverage

The LvKong 360 kW traction motor, the reduction gearbox, the portal axles and the air braking system are covered for 3 years or 150,000 km. Because an electric truck has roughly one-tenth the moving parts of a diesel driveline, the failure modes are far fewer, but the cover still matters for the high-torque components that see the heaviest load on a mine haul road. The warranty expressly covers the motor windings, the resolver, the gear set and the HV contactors, while wear items such as brake pads, tyres and wiper blades are excluded as consumables. Fleet managers should photograph the chassis number at delivery and store the pre-delivery inspection sheet, because these two documents are the first things the claims desk asks for when a fault is logged.

How to make a warranty claim

The claim process is designed to keep a working mine moving rather than to create paperwork. Follow these steps when a covered fault appears:

  • Log the fault code with the onboard diagnostic screen and photograph it alongside the VIN plate.
  • Contact the Shaanxi Fenghan Trading warranty desk within 48 hours of discovery, quoting the VIN and the service file number.
  • Receive a remote diagnosis; roughly 60 percent of faults are resolved by a parameter reset or a module swap.
  • For hardware faults, book the nearest service point; Johannesburg carries LFP modules and motors in stock.
  • Fit the warranty part, capture the closed-work order with before and after photos, and submit for credit within 30 days.

Most battery-module swaps are completed in under four hours because the pack is built in serviceable modules rather than a sealed monolith. The claims desk works on South African business hours plus a 24-hour emergency line for round-the-clock mining operations, so a failed truck at 2 a.m. on a Saturday is still a same-day event. Keeping the digital service history synced to the cloud means the desk can see the truck's record before the technician arrives, which removes a common cause of claim delay.

What voids the warranty

Like any commercial vehicle warranty, the TZ3V cover has clear exclusions. The most common ways South African operators accidentally void cover are listed below, and all of them are avoidable with discipline.

  • Skipping the scheduled service interval, which is every 10,000 km or 6 months for the electric driveline.
  • Using a non-approved DC charger that exceeds the 750 V pack ceiling or delivers uncontrolled ripple current.
  • Submerging the HV connectors or pressure-washing the battery enclosure beyond the IP67 limit.
  • Re-flashing the motor controller with unauthorised software or tampering with the BMS seals.
  • Operating the truck beyond its 31 tonne GVW rating, which the BMS logs and can be read back during a claim.

None of these exclusions is a trap. They mirror the operating guidance in the owner manual, and the Fenghan Trading desk will happily walk a new fleet through the do and do-not list during operator onboarding so the first six months of service stay clean. A five-minute pre-shift walk-around that checks the connector faces and the tyre condition is enough to keep most voiding risks out of the picture entirely.

Comparison with a typical diesel dump truck warranty

A comparable 31 tonne diesel dump truck in South Africa typically carries a 2-year or 200,000 km powertrain warranty and offers an extended cover up to 5 years at extra cost. The diesel warranty excludes the clutch, turbo, injector set and exhaust after-treatment, which are exactly the components that fail most often under mine duty. The TZ3V electric truck flips that ratio: the expensive battery is covered for 8 years, and the simple driveline has fewer parts to fail in the first place.
