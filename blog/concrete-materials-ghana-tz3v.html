The Quarry-to-Plant Cycle: Short, Heavy, Continuous

The economics of aggregate and materials hauling live in cycle time, and the electric truck improves every element of it. A typical Accra quarry-to-plant cycle runs 15-50 km loaded, with loading and discharge queuing that idles a diesel for 30-60 minutes per cycle - fuel burned standing still, multiplied by every cycle of every day. The TZ3V idles at zero energy, and its instant torque pulls loaded off the quarry floor without the clutch and gear work that wears diesel drivetrains on this duty. Regenerative braking on the loaded descent from elevated quarries returns measurable energy to the pack - on suitable quarry geometries, fleets report 10-15% of cycle energy recovered. Daily distance rarely exceeds 200-250 km even on three-shift work, which the 220 km range covers with opportunity charging between shift changes.

Mixer and Pump Duty: Powering the Drum Electrically

For ready-mix operations, electrification reaches beyond the drivetrain. An electric mixer truck drives the drum from the traction battery through an electric power take-off, replacing the diesel engine's hydraulic drive - drum turning consumes 2-4 kWh per hour, a fraction of the energy an idling diesel burns for the same rotation. The practical gains compound: no engine hours accumulating at the pour, no exhaust around the pump crew and the placement crew working beside the truck, and noise low enough for urban pours under night-work permits that diesel mixers cannot obtain. The TZ3V's 600 kWh pack runs drum, drive and hydraulics through a full pouring shift with margin, and the electric PTO meters energy per cubic meter delivered precisely - a number ready-mix fleets have never had from their diesel trucks and now build into their pricing.

Charging Planning for TZ3V Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TZ3V supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 600 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Ghana should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
