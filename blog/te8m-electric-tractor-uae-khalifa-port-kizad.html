Why Khalifa Port and KIZAD Need an Electric Tractor

The Khalifa Port to KIZAD drayage run is short, repetitive and predictable, which is the ideal profile for battery electric trucks. A tractor spends its day shuttling loaded and empty containers between the quay and the free zone warehouses, rarely exceeding 50 to 80 km per cycle but doing many turns. Diesel tractors burn fuel mainly while idling at gates and creeping in terminal traffic, exactly where an electric drivetrain wins.

The UAE has set aggressive decarbonization targets for logistics and free zones, and KIZAD tenants are increasingly required to report scope 1 emissions. An electric tractor lets a 3PL show zero tailpipe emissions on the first and last mile of the import chain, a selling point when bidding for multinational tenants.

Solar Depot Charging Model

Abu Dhabi's high solar irradiance makes depot charging from rooftop or ground mount photovoltaic arrays economically attractive. A 200 kW to 360 kW DC charger at the KIZAD yard restores 20 percent to 80 percent in 45 to 90 minutes during driver breaks, and a solar canopy can offset most of the daily energy need. At electricity priced around USD 0.05 to 0.18 per kWh, a full charge costs roughly USD 20 to 72, against USD 80 to 140 of diesel for an equivalent tractor shift.

For fleets near the UAE electric truck market hubs in Abu Dhabi and Dubai, battery swap is an option where a swap station is available, replacing a charge stop with a 5 to 6 minute swap that keeps the tractor turning.
