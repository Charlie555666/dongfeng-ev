The Quarry-to-Plant Cycle: Short, Heavy, Continuous

The economics of aggregate and materials hauling live in cycle time, and the electric truck improves every element of it. A typical Nairobi quarry-to-plant cycle runs 15-50 km loaded, with loading and discharge queuing that idles a diesel for 30-60 minutes per cycle - fuel burned standing still, multiplied by every cycle of every day. The TZ3Z idles at zero energy, and its instant torque pulls loaded off the quarry floor without the clutch and gear work that wears diesel drivetrains on this duty. Regenerative braking on the loaded descent from elevated quarries returns measurable energy to the pack - on suitable quarry geometries, fleets report 10-15% of cycle energy recovered. Daily distance rarely exceeds 200-250 km even on three-shift work, which the 250 km range covers with opportunity charging between shift changes.

Mixer and Pump Duty: Powering the Drum Electrically

For ready-mix operations, electrification reaches beyond the drivetrain. An electric mixer truck drives the drum from the traction battery through an electric power take-off, replacing the diesel engine's hydraulic drive - drum turning consumes 2-4 kWh per hour, a fraction of the energy an idling diesel burns for the same rotation. The practical gains compound: no engine hours accumulating at the pour, no exhaust around the pump crew and the placement crew working beside the truck, and noise low enough for urban pours under night-work permits that diesel mixers cannot obtain. The TZ3Z's 350 kWh pack runs drum, drive and hydraulics through a full pouring shift with margin, and the electric PTO meters energy per cubic meter delivered precisely - a number ready-mix fleets have never had from their diesel trucks and now build into their pricing.
