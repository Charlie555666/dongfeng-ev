Coastal fleet buyers ask about corrosion early, and they are right to - salt air attacks chassis, bodywork, electrical connectors and brake components on any truck, and coastal fleets have retirement photos to prove it. The question for electric trucks is whether high-voltage systems add vulnerability. What Salt Actually Attacks on a Truck

Understanding the attack surfaces focuses the maintenance budget. Chassis and frame: surface oxidation at welds and coating damage, managed by coating quality and washdown. Body mountings and subframes: crevice corrosion where dirt holds salt moisture. Brake and air system components: external corrosion on valves, tanks and fittings. Electrical connectors: the classic coastal killer - green corrosion in unsealed connectors causing intermittent faults that consume diagnostic hours. And on diesel trucks, the exhaust aftertreatment system: salt and heat combining on expensive emissions hardware. The TZ3Z eliminates the last category entirely and ships with sealed, automotive-grade connectors across the high-voltage system, which removes the coastal fault mode that generates the most workshop detective work.

The Washdown Discipline: Cheapest Corrosion Protection Sold

Coastal fleets that control corrosion share one habit: scheduled washdown with fresh water, including underbody, at intervals matched to exposure. Weekly for daily beach-adjacent or port duty, monthly for inland-from-coast operations. The washdown is not cosmetic - it removes the salt film that keeps corrosion chemistry active between rains. Electric trucks tolerate washdown routines comfortably: the TZ3Z's high-voltage components are sealed against exactly the water exposure that washdown and monsoon deliver, so the cleaning discipline coastal fleets already own transfers directly. Two additions worth adopting: a corrosion-inhibiting underbody treatment at delivery, renewed annually in high-exposure service, and a connector inspection item in the quarterly service, which takes minutes and catches the rare unsealed aftermarket addition before it strands a truck.
