Why the Water Truck Is the First EV to Buy

Mines electrifying their fleets usually start with the biggest trucks. The smarter sequence starts with the water truck. Sprinkler trucks run the most hours of any support equipment - 12-16 hours daily in dry season, circulating continuously over a fixed 15-40 km road network. They never leave the site, so charging is trivial. Their diesel engines idle and crawl at 15-25 km/h all day, the least efficient operating point, burning 25-35 litres per hour to deliver water. And their exhaust rolls directly through the dust cloud workers breathe. An electric truck on this duty converts the worst diesel application on site into the best EV application.

The KT3F's pump and spray systems run off a power take-off driven by the traction system, so spray pressure stays constant regardless of road speed - an operational upgrade over diesel PTO behavior that operators notice on day one.

Water Economics in the Atacama

Atacama mines increasingly run on desalinated or long-distance piped water costing $2-4 per m3 delivered. That price changes dust-control math: spray pattern efficiency is money. The KT3F's electric pump drive meters flow precisely - 0.3-0.5 litres per m2 per pass - and holds it constant where diesel PTO systems wander with engine speed. Mines measuring both report 10-15% water savings from metering alone, worth $15,000-40,000 per truck per year at desalinated prices. Some sites pair the truck with dust-suppressant additives; the tank and pump package is compatible with standard chloride and polymer agents.

  • Map the road network into spray zones and program passes by zone, not by driver habit.
  • Fill at night from the water storage that fills by day - the truck's schedule fits naturally.
  • Use the cannon option for stockpile and blast-area wetting to stretch each fill further.
  • Log litres sprayed per km in telematics; drift above 0.5 L/m2 signals valve or calibration issues.

Altitude and Heat Performance

Chilean copper districts operate at 2,500-4,500 m elevation, where diesel engines lose 25-40% of power to thin air. The electric truck loses nothing - motor output is altitude-independent, which is why a 260 kW KT3F out-climbs a nominally stronger diesel water truck on the road up to the pit. Battery thermal management handles the 30 C swing between desert day and night; the liquid loop warms the pack on cold mornings and cools it through midday spraying. Range at altitude with a full 12 t water load: plan on 130-160 km of circulation per charge, comfortably covering a double-shift spray schedule with one midday top-up.

Compliance and Community Pressure

Chile's environmental enforcement on mine particulate has tightened steadily, and community monitoring near operations makes visible dust a reputational risk, not just a regulatory one. Electric water trucks strengthen both positions: zero exhaust particulate in the dust-control operation itself, and a documented emissions reduction for sustainability reporting that increasingly features in community agreements.
