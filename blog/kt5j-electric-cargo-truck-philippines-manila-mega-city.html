The KT5J electric cargo truck is sized for Mega Manila's stop-and-go delivery grid, where a 210 kWh EV truck threads EDSA congestion without the fuel burn and emissions of a diesel panel or box truck. Why Mega Manila Delivery Favors an Electric Cargo Truck

Metro Manila's traffic is among the most congested in Asia, and diesel trucks burn fuel while crawling through EDSA and the inner-city distribution belts. An electric cargo truck recaptures energy at every stop and pays nothing to idle in a jam. With electricity at $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh versus diesel at $0.90 to $1.20 per liter, the KT5J cuts the cost of every kilometer stuck in traffic, which is most of them. Quiet operation also helps fleets serve residential and commercial drops without adding to the city's noise load during curfew-sensitive hours.

KT5J Core Specifications

The KT5J is Dongfeng's compact electric cargo truck for dense city delivery. It uses a CATL LFP battery, a LvKong permanent magnet motor and a liquid cooled thermal system rated for a 25 to 35 °C operating window. The battery carries an 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, which suits the daily charge rhythm of a Manila fleet. The 210 kWh pack supports a typical range near 200 km on a mixed urban duty cycle, enough for a full day of short-hop deliveries across the metro.

Charging the KT5J Fleet

Manila operators typically install DC fast chargers at the depot and charge overnight or between driver shifts. The 20 to 80% window fills in about 60 to 90 minutes, which fits naturally into breaks and load changes. The liquid cooled thermal system keeps the pack inside its 25 to 35 °C window despite tropical heat, so charging stays reliable through the wet and dry seasons. Fleets adding a second shift simply add a charger to keep every truck powered.

Operator Comfort and Metro Safety

The KT5J cab is designed for city driving with good sightlines, a low step-in and a quiet interior that reduces fatigue in stop-heavy Manila traffic. Regenerative braking extends friction brake life on signal-dense routes, cutting maintenance and improving safety near jeepneys, cyclists and pedestrians. A low-noise drivetrain also lets fleets serve early-morning and late-night drops in dense neighborhoods without the complaints that follow diesel trucks.

Maintenance and Urban TCO

City delivery wears out brakes, clutches and cooling systems fast in diesel trucks, but the KT5J removes engine oil, fuel filters, diesel exhaust fluid and a transmission from the service plan. With energy at $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh against diesel at $0.90 to $1.20 per liter, the per-kilometer saving is largest exactly where Manila fleets drive most: in traffic. Over the 8-year battery warranty the KT5J typically reaches total cost parity with a diesel city truck while also cutting noise and emissions in the metro.
