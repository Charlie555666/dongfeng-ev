The Mixer Duty Cycle on Giga-Project Sites

Ready-mix work on Red Sea Project sites follows the classic mixer loop: batching plant to pour location at 5-25 km, drum turning throughout, with 30-60 minutes of discharge and waiting at the pump. A TZ8J on a 15 km average loop completes 6-9 loads per shift. The critical energy consumers are the drum drive and the hydraulic systems, both electrically driven from the traction battery - total shift consumption runs 55-70% of the 333 kWh pack, leaving margin for the long pours and delays that define concrete work.

Unlike a diesel mixer, the electric truck consumes almost nothing while waiting at the pump with the drum turning - the drum motor draws a modest, constant load instead of an engine burning 8-12 litres per hour at fast idle. On a 45-minute pour wait, that difference alone saves 5-8 litres of diesel-equivalent energy per load.

Charging at the Batching Plant

The batching plant is the natural charging home: it already has the site's largest power service, and mixers return to it every 60-120 minutes. The working layout is one 240 kW DC charger per 4-5 mixers, positioned so trucks plug in during washout and reloading - windows that exist in the cycle regardless of charging. A 15-20 minute plug-in per loop replaces 8-12% of pack capacity, which means the fleet effectively runs all day without a dedicated charging stop. Night-time slow charging tops the fleet to the morning setpoint.

  • Site chargers on the washout lane - every truck passes through it after every pour.
  • Charge in slices during loading; save full charges for the overnight window.
  • Meter kWh per m3 delivered; Red Sea site duty should hold 2.2-2.8 kWh/m3-km depending on loop length.
  • Hold one diesel mixer as standby during the first pour-critical month while schedules stabilize.

Why Emissions-Free Matters Here

Red Sea Project contracts carry explicit environmental performance requirements - dust, noise and emissions are monitored, and non-compliance carries real penalties. An electric mixer fleet removes diesel exhaust from pour crews' working environment, cuts noise enough to extend permitted working hours near finished zones, and produces zero local emissions that auditors can measure. Heat and Remote-Site Reality

Coastal Saudi sites combine 40-48 C heat with high humidity from the Red Sea - a punishing combination for air-cooled equipment. The TZ8J's liquid-cooled battery and motor hold performance through the summer; plan on 8-10% range reduction in peak months with full cab cooling and continuous drum operation. Salt air demands the marine coating option on chassis and connectors, and the washout water routine needs no change - the sealed IP67 high-voltage system tolerates the wet environment that mixer life involves.
