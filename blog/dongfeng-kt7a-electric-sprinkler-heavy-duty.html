Where the Heavy Sprinkler Actually Works

The KT7A's weight class earns its keep in four environments:

  • Mine haul roads. Dust on a 100 km/h haul road is a fatality risk and a production stopper. Watering trucks crawl these routes continuously, in heat, on grades, all shift — exactly the duty where an electric drivetrain with instant low-speed torque and zero idle cost beats diesel hardest. Paired with our electric tippers, the mine gets a fully electric haul ecosystem.
  • Port stockyards and terminals. Coal, clinker, grain and aggregate yards need continuous dust control near communities and berths. Electric sprinklers water yards at night without diesel fumes mixing with the cargo and without noise carrying to the neighbourhood.
  • Highway construction and median irrigation. Long corridor works — new alignments, road widenings — require dust control along the live carriageway; median and verge irrigation on completed highways adds slow-lane duty that destroys diesel after-treatment.
  • Airport and industrial perimeter roads. Air-quality-sensitive zones where diesel equipment near runways or clean-room industries is restricted.

The 6x4 Advantage in Watering Duty

  1. Bigger tank on the same road footprint — 25 t GVW across three axles carries a meaningfully larger water volume than the KT3F's 4x2 18 t format, cutting refill cycles per shift.
  2. Traction on unpaved roads — a second driven axle keeps the loaded sprinkler moving on wet laterite and mine ramp surfaces after the tank empties and weight distribution shifts.
  3. Stability at spray speed — a partially filled water tank sloshes; the longer wheelbase and extra axle tame the pendulum, letting drivers hold consistent spray speed on rough surfaces.

Daily Economics of a Mine Watering Truck

Consider a West African gold operation: two watering trucks, each covering 180 km/day of haul-road watering, 340 days a year. A diesel 6x4 sprinkler in this duty burns 28–32 L/100 km including idle at refill and pump operation: about 55 L/day, USD 58–66 at mine-gate diesel prices. The KT7A covering the same route and pumping the same water draws about 210–240 kWh/day including pump: USD 19–26 at mine industrial tariffs of USD 0.09–0.12/kWh — and several sites now pair watering fleets with solar, cutting that further. Daily saving: USD 35–45 per truck; annual: USD 12,000–15,000; plus the elimination of the turbo-EGR-DPF maintenance chain that slow-duty diesels punish. For a two-truck watering programme the KT7A pair pays back its premium in roughly four years while never once delaying a blast for a breakdown.

Refill Logistics — The Real Constraint to Plan

Every sprinkler fleet's efficiency is set at the water point. Our recommended setup for mining and port clients:

  • Refill hydrants positioned on the watering loop — every 8–12 km on long haul-road circuits — so trucks refill without dead-heading to a single dam or tank.
  • Depot charging overnight with a 180 kW DC charger per 3–4 trucks; opportunity charging at the main refill point during shift breaks.
  • Can pumping at night where permitted — the quiet electric PTO enables off-peak watering that diesel trucks cannot schedule near communities.
