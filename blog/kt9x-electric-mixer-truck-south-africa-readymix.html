Why Ready-Mix Is a Perfect EV Truck Use Case

Ready-mix delivery is a closed loop. The truck leaves the plant loaded, drives 10 to 40 kilometers to site, discharges, and returns. There is no long-haul uncertainty, the depot is the charger, and the drum load is known. That profile removes the two fears that delay electric truck adoption: range anxiety and charging access. Drum, Load, and Electric PTO

The KT9X carries an 8 cubic meter drum rated for about 19,200 kilograms of wet concrete, with an electric power take-off that turns the drum during transit and discharge. Removing the separate diesel drum engine is a big win on a construction site: no second exhaust source, no extra fuel, and far less noise during early-morning pours. The electric PTO also holds drum speed steady regardless of road speed, protecting mix quality on the long graded returns from hillside sites.

Because the drum draws from the same 420 kWh pack that drives the truck, operators must plan energy for both. In practice the drum load is modest next to traction, and a 30 kilometer round trip with discharge uses roughly 18 to 22 percent of the pack, leaving comfortable margin for 10 to 14 deliveries per shift before a depot recharge.

Charging Strategy and Depot Design

The KT9X returns to the batching plant every shift, so the simplest model is a 240 kW DC charger at the depot that restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes during the lunch break and overnight. Plants running two shifts can add a second charger to keep four or five mixers in rotation. Battery swap is unnecessary because the truck never leaves the plant's charging radius.

  • Install one 240 kW charger per four mixers at the batching plant.
  • Schedule charging during discharge and loading windows to avoid idle draw.
  • Pre-cool the cab on grid power before the first morning run.
  • Track per-truck energy per cubic meter to spot inefficient routes.
  • Keep a diesel mixer as backup only for out-of-radius remote pours.

Cost per Cubic Meter Versus Diesel

The headline comparison fleet managers want is delivered cost per cubic meter. A diesel mixer on a 30 kilometer round trip burns roughly 14 liters per load, about $11 at local diesel prices, plus higher service cost. The KT9X uses about $2.50 of electricity per load at an industrial rate of $0.12 per kWh. Across 12 loads a day that is roughly $100 saved per truck per day, or about $28,000 a year, before maintenance. The electric PTO also removes drum-engine servicing from the ledger.

Over a five-year lifecycle the KT9X typically beats a diesel mixer on total cost once the FOB premium is amortized, and the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty covers most of that period. For South African ready-mix operators competing on price, that margin per cubic meter is directly defendable in a tender.
