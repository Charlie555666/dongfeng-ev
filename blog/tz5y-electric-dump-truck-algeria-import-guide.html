TZ5Y Electric Dump Truck Algeria Import Guide: Customs to Registration

Importing an electric truck into Algeria means clearing customs, proving homologation and building a realistic landed cost before the first unit rolls off the ship. The TZ5Y 8x4 dump truck pairs a 400 kWh CATL LFP pack with a 420 kW motor for 240-300 km of site range, and its 6x4 sibling the TZ5E 6x4 electric dump truck shares the same 400 kWh battery at a lower $105,000-$135,000 FOB. This guide walks importers through each step.

Algeria Customs Duty and VAT on Electric Trucks

Algeria applies a customs duty and value-added tax on imported complete vehicles. For a heavy electric dump truck the combined import charges typically land between 30% and 45% of CIF value depending on the current tariff schedule and any temporary incentive for clean vehicles. On a TZ5Y with a $125,000 FOB price, a 35% combined charge adds roughly $43,750 before local fees, so the declared value and freight terms matter enormously to the final number. Importers should confirm the live rate with a licensed clearance agent before quoting a customer.

Homologation and Technical Inspection

Homologation confirms the vehicle meets Algerian technical standards. For an EV this covers the high-voltage system, braking, lighting and the battery safety certificate. Importers should budget 6 to 12 weeks for the process and supply the UN ECE test reports, the battery MSDS and the 8-year battery warranty document. The Algeria electric truck market guide notes that pre-cleared documentation shortens the wait because the inspection office can match papers to the arriving unit and avoid a second physical inspection.

Shipping Options From China to Algerian Ports

Most heavy trucks ship roll-on/roll-off from Shanghai or Tianjin to Algiers or Oran, with transit of 30 to 40 days. Containerised shipping is possible but awkward for an 8x4 dump, so ro-ro is standard. Buyers should specify moisture protection for the battery and request a pre-shipment charge to 60% state of charge, which keeps the pack healthy during the voyage without breaching carrier rules. A declared value that matches the invoice prevents customs disputes that can add weeks to clearance.

Total Landed Cost Build-Up

The realistic landed cost is the sum of FOB, freight, duties, port fees and homologation. For a mid-spec TZ5Y at $125,000 FOB with $5,000 freight and a 35% charge, the maths is:

  • FOB plus freight equals $130,000 CIF.
  • Customs and VAT at 35% equals $45,500.
  • Port and clearance fees equal about $2,500.
  • Homologation and first registration equal about $4,500.
  • Total landed cost is roughly $182,500 per unit.

Why the TZ5E Sibling Lowers Entry Cost

Buyers watching budget can specification the TZ5E 6x4 electric dump truck, which keeps the 400 kWh pack and 240-300 km range but drops to a 6x4 axle layout at $105,000-$135,000 FOB. The lower declared value trims both duty and VAT, often saving $10,000-$15,000 in landed cost versus the 8x4. Either way the CATL LFP pack carries the same 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, so the operating case is unchanged and the site productivity is nearly identical.

Insurance and Financing the Import

Importers should insure the unit from FOB to depot, not just at the port, because the battery pack is the most valuable single component in transit. Marine cargo cover for a $125,000 TZ5Y typically adds $300-$600 for the voyage, a small sum against the $28,000 pack exposure. Local banks in Algeria often require this cover before issuing the import letter of credit, so the policy should name both the buyer and the forwarding agent. The Algeria electric truck market guide recommends binding cover at the same time as the freight booking to avoid a gap on the high-voltage component during the 30 to 40 day sea leg.

Registration and Plates After Clearance

Once customs releases the unit, the importer registers it with the provincial transport office, fits local plates and completes the high-voltage operator declaration. The Algeria electric truck market guide recommends training the driver on regen braking and charging before the first site run, because a 420 kW motor delivers torque instantly and untrained operators waste battery in the first weeks.

Choosing Between TZ5Y 8x4 and TZ5E 6x4 for the Duty

The 8x4 TZ5Y suits heavy quarry and construction haul with its 420 kW motor and 240-300 km range, while the TZ5E 6x4 electric dump truck covers general site work at lower weight and lower landed cost. Both share the 400 kWh CATL LFP pack, so the battery logistics, charger spec and warranty process are identical across the two models. Importers should match axle layout to the load: if payload stays under 25 tonnes, the 6x4 saves roughly $10,000-$15,000 in duty and VAT per unit and still delivers the same daily energy profile. The Algeria electric truck market guide recommends a mixed fleet where the 8x4 takes the heaviest runs and the 6x4 handles lighter distribution, which smooths the battery and charger load across the depot.

Operating Cost After Registration

Once registered, the operating case is what justifies the import. Removing diesel and cutting maintenance by roughly 40% against a diesel dump means a TZ5Y recovering its $165,000-$215,000 landed cost faster than buyers expect. Electricity at an Algerian depot runs about $0.08-$0.15 per kWh, so a 400 kWh pack costs $32-$60 to fill for 240-300 km, against $0.30-$0.45 per km for diesel on the same duty. Over 40,000 km a year that is $12,000-$18,000 saved in fuel alone. The 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty removes the largest unknown, and disciplined charging inside the 20-80% band keeps the pack healthy for the full service life of the vehicle in Algerian heat.

Resale Value and Depot Charging

A registered TZ5Y holds value well because the 400 kWh CATL LFP pack with years of warranty left is attractive on the secondary market. Depot charging at 360 kW keeps turnaround fast, and a documented charge log supports the resale battery health claim when the operator upgrades. The Algeria electric truck market guide notes that fleets selling into the used market recover more when the pack state-of-health is provable, which telematics makes straightforward for the Algerian buyer and lender alike.

Spare Parts and Local Support in Algeria

A quarry operator cannot afford a truck parked for weeks waiting on a part, so local support matters as much as the import maths. The TZ5Y shares common Dongfeng drivetrain and high-voltage components with the wider range, which simplifies the spare inventory and shortens lead times for the 420 kW motor and the 400 kWh pack. Importers should agree a stock of wear items and a pack-handling procedure with the dealer before the first delivery, and confirm the 8-year battery warranty claim route runs through a local service point. The Algeria electric truck market guide notes that fleets with a named local workshop and a spelled-out warranty process report far higher uptime than those importing on price alone. Pairing the TZ5Y or the TZ5E 6x4 electric dump truck with committed local support turns the import from a one-off purchase into a dependable operating asset for Algerian sites.
