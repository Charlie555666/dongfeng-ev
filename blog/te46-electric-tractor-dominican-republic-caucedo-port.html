Charging Inside the Terminal

The layout that works: two 180-240 kW DC chargers at the tractor yard on the natural return path from the crane lines, canopied against the tropical rain. Tractors plug in during the 30-45 minute shift handover; that window restores a full shift of energy. Dominican industrial tariffs keep the energy cost per container move near one-third of diesel, and the terminal's crane-scale power infrastructure absorbs fleet charging without drama. Hurricane season adds a preparedness note: the chargers' elevation and the fleet's pre-storm full-charge protocol are standard tropical-terminal practice, unchanged from diesel-era fuel discipline.

  • Zone chargers by berth so tractors never deadhead across the yard to plug in.
  • Stagger shift changes by 15 minutes to avoid charger queues.
  • Set telematics SOC alarms at 25%; terminal tractors should never deep-discharge in queue.
  • Canopy the charging bays - tropical downpours and salt air punish exposed connectors.

Caribbean Durability: Salt, Rain, Heat

Caucedo's marine environment tests equipment three ways: salt air corrosion, intense rainy seasons, and 30-35 C heat with high humidity. The TE46's export specification answers with marine-grade coatings, sealed IP67 high-voltage enclosures and stainless exposed fasteners; the maintenance additions are monthly connector inspection and quarterly chassis wash. The liquid-cooled pack handles the heat with annual range variation inside 8% - the Caribbean climate is, in EV terms, gentle operating territory.
