Electric Truck Demo and Test Drive Evaluation Program

Before committing a multi-unit order, a serious fleet should run a structured electric truck demo, because a two-week trial on your own routes reveals the real energy number that a brochure cannot. This guide sets out how to run an EV truck evaluation and what data to capture.

Why a 2-Week Trial Beats a Showroom Visit

A showroom walkaround tells you nothing about range under your load, your gradients and your drivers. A Dongfeng TE8M electric tractor may show 350 km range in a light test but deliver 250 km under a full regional load in hot weather, and only a live trial exposes that gap. Two weeks lets you cover weekday peaks, a rest-day lull and at least one worst-case day, which is the data a finance team needs to approve a purchase.

The trial also de-risks the people side. Drivers who have only driven diesel will either love the instant torque or distrust the silence, and you want that reaction surfaced and trained away before you own twenty units. A two-week window is long enough for the novelty to wear off and for real shift behaviour, good and bad, to show up in the logs.

Structuring the Evaluation Window

Build the trial around your real operation, not a vendor route:

  • Week 1: day-duty loops with normal payloads and your regular drivers.
  • Week 2: night or peak-duty loops plus one overloaded stress day.
  • Mid-trial: a DC fast charge and one battery-swap session for comparison.
  • Final day: driver debrief and a cold-start range check.

Data to Collect Every Day

Log the following per vehicle so the report is defensible:

  • State of charge in and out, and kWh delivered at each charge.
  • Km driven and payload tonnage per trip.
  • Ambient temperature and the pack cooling window of 25 to 35 deg C.
  • Charge time from 20 to 80 percent on the 120 to 360 kW charger.
  • Driver notes on braking, regen and cab comfort.
  • Any thermal derating or warning events with time and location.

Acceptance Criteria Before You Order

Agree the pass thresholds with your exporter before the truck arrives, so the result is binary:

  • Delivered range within 10 percent of the promised figure under your load.
  • Charge time from 20 to 80 percent within the 60 to 90 minute window.
  • No thermal derating events outside the 25 to 35 deg C pack window.
  • Driver satisfaction score above 7 of 10 on the debrief.
  • Regen recovery meeting at least 15 percent of energy on hilly routes.

Who Should Be in the Trial

A demo is a team sport, not a procurement solo. Put the regular drivers behind the wheel, because their habits, not a tester's, are what the truck will see in service. Include the maintenance lead so they can read the telematics and judge service access. Bring the finance analyst who will model the total cost of ownership, because the per-km saving only lands if the energy and service assumptions are captured live. Finally, include one sceptic, the person most likely to flag a problem, because a trial that everyone agrees with too easily hides the issues you most need to see.

Set the acceptance criteria with this group before the truck arrives, not after. If drivers, maintenance and finance each write their own pass threshold, the final report is a clean yes or no rather than a debate. The TE8M or its 282 to 424 kWh sibling should be measured against the exact duty it will run, so the people who own that duty must own the criteria and sign off on the result.

Reporting the Result to Finance

The trial is worthless if the result stays in a driver's notebook. Capture everything in a single report that finance can read without translation: measured kWh per km, achieved range under load, charge time in the 20 to 80 percent window, any thermal events, and the driver score. Then convert those numbers into the two figures the board cares about, the cost per km versus diesel and the payback period on the higher EV price, and run the model at both your best and worst case electricity prices because a volatile tariff can swing the payback by a year.

  • State the measured energy use and your real electricity price.
  • State the diesel equivalent and the per-km saving at $0.13 to $0.32.
  • Add the annual service saving of $3k to $6k per truck.
  • Show the demand-response upside if your market allows grid service.
  • Attach the signed acceptance sheet as the commercial anchor.

With that report in hand, the decision to order becomes a spreadsheet, not a leap of faith.
