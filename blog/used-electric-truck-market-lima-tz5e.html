Battery Health: The Core of Used EV Valuation

In a used electric truck, the battery report is the valuation. A 350 kWh CATL LFP pack showing 85-90% state of health after four or five years still delivers most of its original 240 km range, and LFP chemistry degrades slowly and predictably enough that the remaining life is bankable. Buyers should demand the BMS health report, charging history and cycle count, and should understand what they read: daily fast-charging history is worth a discount versus gentle overnight charging, and any gaps in the record are a red flag. The 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty transfers with the truck, so a three-year-old unit still carries five years of factory coverage - a floor under value that no used diesel can offer.

Inspection Checklist Before You Buy

A used electric truck inspection covers familiar ground plus EV-specific items. The conventional checks - chassis, suspension, brakes, tires, accident repair evidence - follow standard practice. The electric layer adds: the battery health report and its consistency with the odometer; a full diagnostic scan for stored high-voltage fault codes; charging inlet and cable condition; a live fast-charge test confirming the pack accepts rated power; and verification that BMS software is current. Cosmetics matter less than records. A TZ5E with complete telematics and service history is worth a premium over a shinier truck with missing files. Import Process and Documents for Peru

Importing a used electric truck into Peru follows the same skeleton as any used commercial vehicle import, with battery-specific additions. The file includes the purchase invoice, deregistration or export documents from the origin country, bill of lading, and the conformity paperwork Peru requires - MTC homologation where applicable. The lithium battery adds shipping documentation: the UN38.3 test summary and a dangerous goods declaration, with state of charge limited to around 30% for ocean transit. Used vehicle age or inspection rules in Peru must be checked before purchase, not after - some markets restrict commercial vehicle imports by age. Clearance at the Port of Callao runs smoothly when the document pack is complete and pre-lodged with a licensed broker in Lima.

Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the TZ5E earns acceptance quickly. The 360 kW motor delivers instant torque with no gear hunting, the cab is quieter than any diesel equivalent, and regenerative braking reduces fatigue in stop-start duty. Drivers typically adapt within a week, and most prefer the electric truck once they experience a full shift without engine noise and vibration. The habits that matter are small: smooth acceleration, anticipation to maximize regeneration, charging connection on return to depot, and honest reporting of dash warnings. Charging Planning for TZ5E Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TZ5E supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 350 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Peru should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
