Do Electric Trucks Lose Range in Cold Weather?

Yes, but far less than many fleet managers fear when the pack is engineered for it. The Dongfeng EV range uses CATL LFP chemistry with liquid cooling and active heating, and in field and lab data those packs retain roughly 70-85% of their rated range at -20°C compared with mild-weather operation. The loss comes from two sources: the pack must warm itself to operate efficiently, and the cabin heater draws 3-6 kW in deep cold. With pre-heating on grid power before the shift, that warming energy does not come out of the traction budget, so the first cold miles leave with full available range.

For planning, a truck rated at 300 km in summer will realistically deliver 210-255 km at -20°C. That is still enough for a full urban or drayage shift, and operators in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan run exactly these duty cycles through winter without range failures. The key is to plan the route with the cold-range factor baked in rather than discovering it on the road.

How Do LFP Batteries Handle Sub-Zero Temperatures?

LFP is one of the most cold-tolerant mainstream chemistries, and the Dongfeng EV implementation adds a liquid loop that heats the pack to its efficient window before departure. Below freezing, DC charging slows by about 15-30% because the pack must be warmed before high current is accepted, but that is a charging-speed effect, not a permanent capacity loss. Once warm, the pack delivers its full power. The 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty remains valid because LFP does not suffer the cold-induced degradation that plagues some other cells.

In practice this means a cold-climate operator schedules a little extra time at the charger in winter and pre-conditions the pack on shore power. The battery itself is not damaged by the cold, which is why the same pack that runs a Kazakh copper mine at -30°C also runs a Gulf site at 45°C without a chemistry change.

What Is the Coldest Temperature an EV Truck Can Start In?

Dongfeng EV trucks with pack heating start and operate in ambient temperatures well below -20°C; the heating system brings cells to operating temperature on grid power, so cranking is never an issue as it can be with a cold diesel. Cabin and battery pre-conditioning draws from the main pack or shore power, and the liquid-cooled loop keeps the inverter and motor in their safe window. In Kazakhstan copper-belt operations, TZ3V dump trucks run through winters that reach -30°C with no cold-start failures.

The elimination of cold-start diesel vapor and glow-plug dependence is a real reliability gain in remote steppe sites where a failed start means a stranded load and a costly recovery. Electric pre-heating is deterministic: plug in, pre-condition, drive.

Does Cabin Heating Drain the Truck Battery Fast?

It draws a meaningful but manageable load. In deep cold the cabin heater pulls about 3-6 kW, which on a 500-600 kWh pack is a few percent of capacity per hour of heating. The key is to pre-heat the cab and pack on grid power while the truck is plugged in, then rely on efficient climate control during the shift. Operators report that planning the heater load into the route, rather than treating it as a surprise, keeps winter range predictable.

Some fleets add a small auxiliary heat pump or scheduled pre-heat so the main pack is never asked to both warm the cabin and propel a loaded truck at the same time. That discipline is what separates a winter range surprise from a winter range plan.

Are Electric Trucks Cheaper to Run Than Diesel in Winter?

In most fleet duty cycles, yes. Diesel engines are least efficient in cold start and idle, precisely when an EV truck is most efficient thanks to pre-heating and regeneration. Energy cost per km stays a fraction of diesel even after accounting for winter heater load, and maintenance is simpler with no cold-start wear on a combustion engine. For Central Asia winters, the TE9L electric tractor with its 600 kWh option gives fleet managers a buffer for the coldest routes.

The maintenance angle matters most in winter: no glow plugs, no frozen fuel filters, no cold-idle regen cycles. A Dongfeng EV truck's drivetrain is sealed and warm-conditioned, so the winter failure modes that plague diesel fleets largely disappear.
