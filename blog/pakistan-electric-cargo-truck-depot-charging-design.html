Charging is the foundation of any electric fleet

The trucks are only half of an electric fleet; the charging infrastructure is the other half, and it is where most implementations succeed or fail. For a KT5L fleet in Pakistan, the right charging design depends on the duty cycle, the site’s power supply and the tariff. Get it right and the fleet runs smoothly on cheap energy; get it wrong and the trucks sit waiting. We treat charging design as part of the vehicle specification, not an afterthought.

AC versus DC: choosing the right mix

Most charging should be AC, done overnight when the trucks are parked and power is cheap. For a KT5L with 282 kWh, an AC charge over several hours covers a normal urban distribution and mining support day. DC fast charging, which takes roughly 20-80% of the pack in the time stated in the spec, is for the occasions when a truck must return to service quickly. A typical Pakistan depot runs a majority of AC points with a small number of DC chargers as a buffer.

Sizing the site power supply

The electrical supply is the constraint most often overlooked. Charging a fleet of KT5L trucks overnight draws a substantial, sustained load, and the site’s transformer may need upgrade. Smart charge management spreads the load across the night to stay within the existing supply, avoiding an expensive upgrade. For Pakistani customers in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, we assess the site supply early and design a charging plan that fits it.

Public and corridor charging

Not every charge happens at base. For longer routes, Pakistan fleets rely on public or partner chargers along the corridor. The practical approach is to plan routes around known reliable chargers, keep a partner depot as a fallback, and never assume a charger is working without a phone call. On most KT5L duty cycles, public charging is the exception, which keeps both cost and range anxiety low.

Plug types, standards and compatibility

Charging standards vary by region, and the KT5L can be supplied with the plug and protocol your market uses — CCS2, GB/T or a market-specific combination. Getting the connector right at order stage avoids an adapter that can slow charging or, worse, fail to work. For Pakistan buyers, we confirm the intended charging network before shipping so the truck and the infrastructure speak the same language from day one.

Operating and maintaining the charging installation

A charging installation is a piece of plant that needs care. Cables, connectors and the charger electronics require periodic inspection, and connectors should be kept clean and dry. In dusty or hot Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad environments, this matters more. We include a simple maintenance schedule with every charger we supply and provide remote diagnostics so faults are spotted before they strand a truck.

Why Pakistan is ready for electric trucks now

The conditions that make an electric truck viable are all present in Pakistan. heavy fuel-import burden and a national EV policy framework. Freight demand concentrates in and around Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, where routes are short and predictable and the depot is never far away. Fuel logistics add cost and delay that a fleet charging its own vehicles simply avoids. For a KT5L electric cargo truck on a urban distribution and mining support duty cycle, this is not an experimental technology but a practical replacement for diesel, and the operators moving first are the ones who lock in the lowest cost per kilometre before their competitors do.

A step-by-step implementation plan

Electrifying a fleet in Pakistan is a project, and running it in phases is what keeps it manageable. Phase one is a site and route audit: list every duty cycle, measure real daily distance and load, and map the depot power supply. Phase two is a pilot of two to five KT5L units on the most predictable routes, with chargers installed and drivers trained. Phase three is measurement — energy cost per kilometre, uptime, maintenance hours. Phase four is scaling what the data supports. Each phase de-risks the next and keeps the capital commitment matched to proven performance rather than optimism.

Choosing the right configuration

Within the KT5L range there are choices that matter more than cosmetics. The 6x4 layout suits the axle load and traction pattern of your duty cycle. Battery capacity of 282 kWh should be sized to the longest realistic daily route plus a safety margin, not to the biggest number on the brochure. Body and equipment specification — tipper, box, reefer, tank or compactor — should be matched precisely to the job in Pakistan. Getting these four decisions right at order stage is far cheaper than modifying a truck after it lands.
