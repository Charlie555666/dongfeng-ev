How the Daily Cycle Works

The canopy generates through the day while trucks are out, charging the stationary battery. Trucks return in the evening and draw from storage overnight through the DC chargers, which can also top up directly from the grid if it is available and cheap. In markets with unreliable grids the storage carries the fleet through outages that would otherwise idle the operation, which is often the decisive commercial argument.

  • Generate by day, charge trucks by night through stationary storage.
  • Use grid connection as backup, not primary supply.
  • Size storage to cover one full fleet day for outage resilience.
  • Stagger charger rotation so two or three units serve the whole fleet.
  • Use the canopy as parking shade, protecting cabs and packs from heat.

The Energy Cost That Results

Once capital is recovered, the marginal cost of solar charging energy approaches zero, and even amortized over ten years the effective cost lands around $0.05 to $0.08 per kWh, roughly half a typical African industrial tariff and a small fraction of the diesel equivalent per kilometer. A fleet kilometer on canopy solar costs about $0.06 in energy, versus $0.35 or more on diesel at delivered prices, a gap that pays for the entire charging infrastructure inside the truck fleet's own payback period.

Practical Design Notes

Orient the canopy for the site's latitude, keep panels washable because dust is the enemy of yield, and place the chargers and storage in a ventilated, shaded equipment room. Specify the chargers to match the fleet: 120 kW units suit medium trucks, 240 kW for heavy tractors like the TE8P electric tractor.
