Chattogram's Port and Hills: TZ3Z Electric Dump Truck Strategy for Bangladesh's Second City

Chattogram is Bangladesh's trade engine — the port complex moves the garments-led export economy's containers and the country's imports — and it is simultaneously a construction economy: the port's own expansion (the Bay Terminal programme), the city's hill-side residential development, the Economic Zone build-outs along the Sitakunda corridor, and the road programmes that connect them. We covered Dhaka's construction fleets and the RMG corridor at national level; this piece is the port-city electric truck strategy — the TZ3Z 8x4 electric dump truck (CATL 400 kWh LFP, 510 kW, 55 t GCW) on the construction and materials flows, with the KT5M electric box truck on port-city distribution. Country import context is in the Bangladesh market guide.

The Chattogram Freight Map

Four flows define the city's truck work:

  • Port drayage and yard logistics: container movement between the port gates, private inland container depots and the city's packing yards — loops of 10–40 km, two shifts, intense queueing at gate and ICD.
  • Construction materials: sand from the dredgers and river points, aggregates and hill material from the Sitakunda quarries, fill for the Economic Zones — loops of 20–70 km at full loads.
  • Hill-cutting and earthworks logistics: the regulated hill development projects move cut material on short, steep, dusty loops — the hardest duty in the region.
  • City distribution: consumer goods from port-side warehouses to the metro and up the Dhaka highway — 80–150 km daily patterns.

The first two flows electrify immediately; the third is the showcase — the duty cycle where electric drivetrains humiliate diesel on wear costs.

Hill Duty: Where Electric Wins Hardest

The hill-cutting loops are the extreme end of stop-start: loaded descents, crawling climbs, dust, and constant directional changes. On this duty a diesel tipper's maintenance ledger runs riot — clutches, brakes, differentials, turbo ingestion — while the TZ3Z's drivetrain simply does not have the parts that fail:

The KT5M on Port Distribution

The port-city distribution fleet runs the same mission profile as our Dhaka analysis, in Chattogram's more compact geography: CATL 262/310 kWh on an 18 t GVW chassis, box and curtain bodies, and daily distances inside 150 km that a single overnight charge covers with margin. Charging rides on the same depot infrastructure as the construction fleet — the two fleets share yards, chargers and technicians, which is why we specify the mixed-fleet structure rather than single-mission deployments in port cities.

Monsoon Engineering as Standard

Chattogram is monsoon-hard: months of heavy rain, hill-side runoff and flood-prone yards. Our Bangladesh specification is the tropical package at full strength — IP68 HV systems, sealed electronics, e-coated chassis against the salt-humid port air — plus the depot flood-discipline items from our corrosion engineering piece: chargers on elevated plinths, drainage routed away from the electrical room, and a post-flood HV inspection routine after every major rain event. These are delivery-standard on Bangladesh-bound units, not options.

Charging Around the Port City

A mixed first-phase fleet — eight TZ3Z, eight KT5M — needs:

  1. Two 240 kW DC chargers at the quarry/materials yard, charging during loading queues.
  2. Three 120 kW points at the port-area depot for overnight distribution-fleet charging.
  3. Solar augmentation: 60–100 kWp on depot roofs — between monsoon bursts, Chattogram's solar yield is strong, and the array covers a third of daytime charging at near-zero marginal cost.

Budget near USD 220,000–280,000 all-in, repaid by the combined fleet's fuel savings inside two years at Bangladeshi fuel prices.
