Safety is a design feature, not an afterthought

An electric truck takes safety seriously by design. The KT5M carries its 180-220 kWh pack low in the chassis, which lowers the centre of gravity and reduces rollover risk, and it adds systems — battery management, thermal monitoring, and high-voltage isolation — whose whole purpose is to keep the energy stored safely. For fleets in Vietnam, the priority is to understand these systems so that everyday operations never compromise them.

High-voltage safety and the lockout principle

The single most important safety rule for any electric truck is lockout/tagout before working on the HV system. The KT5M isolates the pack automatically on impact, but anyone servicing it must follow the manual procedure: power down, verify de-energisation, and work only with rated tools. For Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Hai Phong workshops, the training required is modest but mandatory, and it is the difference between a safe EV bay and a dangerous one.

Driver safety and the low-noise hazard

Electric trucks are quiet, which is a benefit in residential Vietnam streets but also a hazard: pedestrians and cyclists do not hear them coming. The KT5M includes an acoustic vehicle alerting system at low speed, and drivers should treat pedestrians as if the truck were silent. Combined with the strong regenerative braking, drivers must also be trained not to rely on brake lights to signal, since a heavy regen can slow the truck without the pedal.

Battery thermal safety in hot climates

High ambient temperatures and heavy loads stress any battery, and safety depends on the thermal-management system doing its job. The KT5M monitors cell temperature continuously and derates if needed rather than overheating. Operators in Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Hai Phong should keep the cooling loop clean, act on any thermal warning rather than clearing it, and avoid charging a hot pack immediately after a hard climb. These habits protect both the crew and the pack.

Fire risk: the honest picture

Lithium-iron-phosphate (LFP) chemistry, which the KT5M uses, is among the most thermally stable battery chemistries available and is far less prone to the thermal runaway associated with some other lithium chemistries. That said, the correct response to any pack incident is a defined one: isolate, evacuate, alert the fire service and follow the emergency guide we supply. Building a safety culture around the fleet

Technology reduces risk but does not remove the need for discipline. A KT5M fleet in Vietnam should combine HV training for technicians, driver training on regen and low-noise awareness, a clear incident-reporting path and a periodic safety audit of the charging installation. Fleets that build this culture early report fewer incidents and lower insurance friction, because their records demonstrate control.

Why Vietnam is ready for electric trucks now

The conditions that make an electric truck viable are all present in Vietnam. Decision 876 EV targets and a low-emission-zone push in Hanoi. Freight demand concentrates in and around Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Hai Phong, where routes are short and predictable and the depot is never far away. Fuel logistics add cost and delay that a fleet charging its own vehicles simply avoids. For a KT5M electric cargo truck on a garment, seafood and electronics logistics duty cycle, this is not an experimental technology but a practical replacement for diesel, and the operators moving first are the ones who lock in the lowest cost per kilometre before their competitors do.

A step-by-step implementation plan

Electrifying a fleet in Vietnam is a project, and running it in phases is what keeps it manageable. Phase one is a site and route audit: list every duty cycle, measure real daily distance and load, and map the depot power supply. Phase two is a pilot of two to five KT5M units on the most predictable routes, with chargers installed and drivers trained. Phase three is measurement — energy cost per kilometre, uptime, maintenance hours. Phase four is scaling what the data supports. Each phase de-risks the next and keeps the capital commitment matched to proven performance rather than optimism.

Within the KT5M range there are choices that matter more than cosmetics. The 4x2 layout suits the axle load and traction pattern of your duty cycle.
