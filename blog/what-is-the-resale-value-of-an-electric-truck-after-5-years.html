What Is the Resale Value of an Electric Truck After 5 Years?

Quick answer: An electric truck retains about 35 to 45 percent of its original value after 5 years when it carries the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty and a battery health certificate, which equals or beats the 30 to 40 percent residual typical of a comparable diesel truck. The transferable warranty and documented battery state-of-health are the two factors that lift used EV truck prices in markets such as Saudi Arabia.

What is the resale value of an electric truck after 5 years?

Across the Dongfeng electric truck range, year-five residuals land in a 35 to 45 percent band of original FOB value. The TZ5E electric dump truck sits at the top of that band at 40 to 45 percent because its 424 kWh LFP battery and 450 kW motor stay in strong demand for construction and mining fleets, while city cargo models hold 35 to 40 percent.

This is a meaningful shift from early used-EV markets, where battery fear pushed residuals to 25 to 30 percent. The arrival of long, transferable warranties and cheap health-certificate testing has closed the gap with diesel, and in the Gulf the gap has reversed in favor of electric because diesel emission compliance costs rise steeply with age.

Does battery degradation kill electric truck resale?

No. CATL LFP packs retain roughly 80 percent capacity at 4,500 cycles, and a typical 5-year duty cycle uses about 1,800 to 2,500 cycles, leaving comfortable margin. A battery health certificate showing above 75 percent state-of-health reassures the buyer and supports a price at the upper end of the band. By contrast, a diesel driveline at 5 years often shows turbo, emissions, and clutch wear that an electric unit simply does not have.

How does the 8-year warranty transfer affect resale?

The 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty transfers to the second owner in most export markets, including Gulf states. At year five that leaves three years of battery risk protection for the buyer, which is a concrete asset the seller can price in. A transferable warranty converts the battery from a liability into a selling point, directly lifting resale above a warranty-expired diesel.

How does EV resale compare to diesel after 5 years?

Electric resale now matches or beats diesel. The electric drivetrain has fewer worn rotating parts, the battery is warranted and certifiable, and diesel emission compliance costs rise with age. A diesel dump truck at 5 years often needs a diesel particulate or turbo spend that the buyer prices out, while the electric unit needs mainly tires and brakes. Net, the TZ5E and its peers command 35 to 45 percent versus 30 to 40 percent for diesel.

The FOB band for the TZ5E is USD 85,000 to 108,000, so a 42 percent year-five residual implies a used value near USD 36,000 to 45,000, a strong recovery that improves the effective lease and TCO math for the first owner. That recovery also makes a finance lease on the TZ5E easier to underwrite, because the residual is no longer a guess.

How to protect your electric truck resale

Keep a battery health certificate updated every 12 months, retain charging and service logs, and avoid deep daily discharges that age the pack faster. Buy the transferable warranty option, and specify the LFP chemistry for cycle life. A documented, warranted TZ5E with a 78 percent state-of-health certificate will outsell an undocumented unit by a clear margin at auction or private sale, and the same discipline applies to every model in the Dongfeng electric range.

Where should you sell an electric truck after 5 years?

The used electric truck market in the Gulf and Africa is still thin but growing, so channel choice matters. A private sale to a second owner who values the transferable warranty captures the full residual, while a trade-in to a dealer is faster but typically 5 to 8 percent lower. Auction works for fleets offloading many units at once, provided the battery health certificate is published up front to avoid a weak bidding field.

In Saudi Arabia specifically, construction and mining buyers actively seek used TZ5E dump trucks because the 424 kWh pack and 450 kW motor remain in demand and the 40t GVW fits existing sites. Listing with the original warranty transfer letter attached turns a question mark into a closing document.

How do mileage and duty cycle affect resale?

Lower annual mileage within the 1,800 to 2,500 cycle band at year five supports a higher state-of-health, which lifts price. A TZ5E worked 20,000 km per year will show a healthier pack than one worked 40,000 km, even at the same age, because cycle count tracks distance more than clock time. Gentle charging, avoiding full 0 to 100 percent daily swings, and keeping the pack cool in summer all preserve the certificate.

Duty cycle also sets the buyer pool. A dump truck with clean construction history resells into mining or quarry work, while a cargo model with mixed urban use appeals to distributors. Match the sales story to the duty the battery actually saw, because the health certificate will confirm or contradict the claim and a mismatch kills the deal at inspection.

What mistakes lower electric truck resale?

The first mistake is skipping the annual battery health certificate. Without it, buyers discount the pack by assumption, and the gap between a certified 78 percent state-of-health TZ5E and an uncertified one can be 5 to 8 percent of value, or USD 4,000 to 8,000 on a USD 100,000 unit. The certificate costs little and pays for itself at sale.

The second mistake is deep daily discharge. Running the pack to near empty before charging ages the cells faster than the 4,500-cycle rating assumes, and the health certificate will show it. Keep daily cycling in the 20 to 80 percent band, charge on a managed schedule, and the year-five certificate stays high enough to defend the top of the 40 to 45 percent residual band for dump and mining models.
