Does the Electric Truck Business Case Work at Small Scale?

Yes - with one condition: utilization. The electric truck's economics - 60-75% lower energy cost per kilometer, 40-60% lower maintenance - scale with kilometers driven, not fleet size. A five-truck fleet running 200 km daily per truck captures the same per-kilometer savings as a five-hundred-truck fleet. What changes at small scale is the margin for error: a large fleet absorbs a poorly matched truck; a small fleet cannot. So the small-fleet rule is simple - electrify the highest-mileage, most regular route first, with the truck precisely matched to it. The KT5M electric box truck, with 280 km of certified range from a 140 kWh pack, covers the urban and regional duty cycles where small fleets earn their living.

What Is the Minimum Charging Setup for a Small Fleet?

Smaller than most buyers fear. A single depot charger - 60-120 kW DC - serves a fleet of three to eight trucks comfortably, because small fleets rarely need simultaneous fast charging: trucks charge overnight sequentially and top up during loading windows. The KT5M recovers 20-80% in about an hour on a 120 kW unit, so one charger supports a working day's staggered needs. The electrical service requirement - a three-phase connection of adequate capacity - exists at most commercial premises in Ethiopia, or can be upgraded for a fraction of the truck investment. What small fleets do not need: public charging dependence, battery-swap subscriptions, or million-dollar depot rebuilds. Charge where the trucks sleep, and the infrastructure question shrinks to one charger and a utility conversation.

What Financing and Support Options Exist for Small Buyers?

Small fleets actually have more structure available than ever. What Mistakes Should Small Fleets Avoid?

Five, in order of frequency. Buying for the exception rather than the rule - specify for the route you run daily, not the longest trip of the year; rent for exceptions. Undersizing charging - one reliable charger beats two cheap ones. Skipping driver training - a single day of energy-efficient driving instruction returns 10-15% range improvement, free capacity forever. Ignoring the data - the KT5M's telematics tells you within weeks which routes beat planning figures and which need adjustment; small fleets cannot afford to fly blind. And going unsupported - the Ethiopia electric truck market page outlines regional support structures for Ethiopia, and our team works with single-unit buyers as readily as hundred-unit ones. Small scale is a reason to be precise, not a reason to wait.

Charging Planning for KT5M Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT5M supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 140 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Ethiopia should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
