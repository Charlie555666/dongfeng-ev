When Does Generator Charging Actually Make Sense?

Four situations justify it confidently. Remote sites with no grid prospect - mines, quarries, construction camps where the generator already runs for other loads and truck charging adds to an existing fuel budget. Transitional operations - fleets electrifying now in locations where the grid connection arrives in twelve to eighteen months; the generator bridges the gap instead of postponing the savings. Backup resilience - storm-prone or unstable-grid regions where the generator guarantees the fleet charges regardless. And peak management - fleets using generators to avoid demand charges during grid-constrained hours. Where a reliable grid connection exists and is affordable, grid power wins on cost every time - generator charging is the answer to specific problems, not a general strategy.

How Do You Size and Specify the Setup Correctly?

Specification errors here are expensive and avoidable. Size the generator to the charger, not the truck: charger demand plus site loads plus 20-25% margin, with the generator running at 60-80% load where it is efficient - an oversized generator idling at low load burns fuel per kWh like a truck spinning its wheels. Confirm charger compatibility: quality DC chargers tolerate generator power happily, but specify the power source at purchase so settings match. Plan fuel logistics honestly: a fleet charging nightly adds a measurable diesel delivery schedule to the site. And sequence the transition: in Pakistan, we typically see fleets start generator-charged and migrate to grid or solar-plus-storage as connections arrive - the TZ5E's 240 km range and the site layout usually make the charging location obvious from day one.
