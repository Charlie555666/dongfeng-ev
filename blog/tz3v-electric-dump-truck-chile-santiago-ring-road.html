Why Santiago Ring Road Work Suits an EV Tipper

The Santiago ring road program runs heavily at night to avoid daytime congestion, and the Metropolitan Region enforces noise limits that penalize diesel dump trucks with exposed exhaust brakes. The TZ3V produces only tire and wind noise because there is no engine or compression brake, so a site can run closer to residential edges without breaching the 75 dB nighttime threshold. That quiet operation often lets a contractor add a night shift that a diesel fleet would be fined for running.

Short, looped haul routes also mean the TZ3V returns to a central charging hub every cycle, so one 360 kW charger serves four to six trucks across a 10-hour shift. Regenerative braking on the loaded descent from the cut to the tip recovers 10 to 15 percent of the energy spent climbing, which directly lowers the cost per cubic meter moved.
