Fuel Is a Risk Line; Electricity Is a Planning Line

Every diesel fleet carries an unhedged commodity position: diesel. The price moves with geopolitics, subsidy politics and currency, the fleet's largest operating cost swings beyond management's control, and budgets become forecasts of a market nobody can forecast. Fleets in Chile know the pattern - subsidy reform announcements, overnight pump price steps, quarterly budgets revised by events on another continent. The TE8M electric 6x4 tractor converts that exposure into a plannable input: electricity tariffs are regulated, published, contracted and stable by comparison, changing on announced schedules rather than overnight. The shift is strategic as much as economic - the CFO gains a five-year energy cost line that can actually be budgeted, and the fleet's competitiveness stops depending on fuel-market luck. That is the frame for the numbers that follow.

The Tariff Structure: Reading Your Electricity Bill Like a Pro

Electricity pricing rewards the fleet that reads the tariff before buying the truck. Industrial tariffs in Chilean markets typically combine an energy charge per kWh - often time-of-use, with off-peak overnight rates 30-50% below peak - and a demand charge on the site's peak draw. The electric truck fleet's duty pattern aligns with the structure's sweet spot: trucks work days and charge nights, landing squarely in the cheapest tariff window by nature. A TE8M charging its 400 kWh pack overnight at off-peak rates pays an energy cost per kilometer that peak-hour diesel cannot approach - typically $0.08-$0.15 per kilometer against $0.50-$0.90 for diesel on comparable duty. The demand charge is the variable to engineer: managed charging that staggers truck starts keeps the site peak low, and the software to do it is standard fleet equipment.

Locking the Price: Contracts, Solar and Storage

Where diesel offers no hedging instruments to a fleet, electricity offers three. Tariff contracts: industrial consumers in many Chilean markets can contract multi-year supply at agreed rates, fixing the energy line for the fleet's planning horizon. On-site generation: a solar canopy over the truck parking produces daytime energy at a levelized cost that undercuts most grid tariffs, and truck depots have exactly what solar wants - large shaded parking areas and predictable daytime load. Storage: battery systems shift solar or off-peak energy into the hours the fleet needs, flattening both tariff exposure and demand charges. Fleets combining the three report effective energy costs 40-60% below grid tariff - and every layer insulates the operation further from the fuel-price cycle the diesel competitors still ride. The Five-Year Budget: Diesel Volatility vs Electric Stability

Run the honest comparison on a five-year horizon, because that is where the structural difference shows. Diesel scenario: take today's pump price, then apply the historical volatility of Chilean fuel markets - annual swings of 20-40% are normal, subsidy reforms arrive unannounced, and the five-year average is a guess dressed as a number. Electric scenario: published tariff schedules, contracted rates where available, and a demand profile the fleet controls. The TE8M's consumption - 1.2-1.6 kWh per kilometer on typical 6x4 tractor duty - is a stable engineering figure, so the five-year energy budget becomes arithmetic rather than forecasting. Fleet CFOs running this comparison consistently report the same conclusion: the electric case wins on the average scenario, and wins by more in every scenario where diesel misbehaves - which is the risk the diesel fleet carries unpriced.

Carbon and Compliance: The Value Stack on Top

Energy procurement strategy now carries a compliance dimension that strengthens the electric case further. Carbon pricing, fuel-excise escalation and corporate Scope 1 reporting are advancing across Chile's trading partners, and diesel's price trend carries policy risk beyond commodity risk - governments taxing emissions are, in effect, promising that diesel gets structurally more expensive. The electric fleet hedges that promise by construction: zero tailpipe emissions, an electricity supply that greens as the grid greens, and documented energy data from the TE8M's telematics that drops straight into emissions reporting. Where carbon credits or green-fleet incentives apply, the electric fleet monetizes them; the diesel fleet pays the other side of the same ledger.
