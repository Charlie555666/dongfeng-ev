Sulawesi Nickel Haul Profile

Nickel laterite mines on Sulawesi run short, intense haul cycles of 5 to 15 km between pit and processing or port, repeated dozens of times per shift. A diesel dump in this class burns about 0.45 to 0.55 L/km under loaded climb, costing $0.40 to $0.72 per kilometer at Indonesian diesel prices of $0.90 to $1.30 per liter, delivered to a remote site at a premium. The TZ3Z draws about 1.1 to 1.6 kWh/km when loaded, and at mine-site solar or grid electricity of $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh that drops to $0.09 to $0.32 per kilometer.

The TZ3Z carries a 282 kWh CATL LFP pack with an 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, and FOB pricing runs from $75,000 to $95,000. On a 10 km round trip the truck completes many cycles per charge and tops up during shift change, so the battery is sized to the cycle rather than the range, which keeps the capital cost down.

Why Solar Plus Electric Beats Diesel

Diesel delivered to a remote Sulawesi site carries a logistics premium that the per-liter price understates, and supply interruptions can idle a pit. Solar plus storage removes both risks: the energy is generated on site, and the electric truck's fewer parts reduce the maintenance load that remote sites struggle to staff. The LvKong motor holds full power at the modest mine altitudes, and regen on the loaded descent back to the pit recovers 8 to 15 percent of trip energy.

Indonesia's nickel sector is also under pressure to cut emissions for export customers, and a zero-tailpipe haul truck directly supports that goal while lowering cost, a rare combination that aligns compliance with profit.
