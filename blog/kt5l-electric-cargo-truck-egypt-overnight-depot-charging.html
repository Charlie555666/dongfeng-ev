KT5L Electric Cargo Truck in Egypt: Overnight Depot Charging Playbook

An electric truck that charges while the city sleeps is the cheapest way to run a Cairo delivery fleet, and the KT5L electric cargo truck turns Egypt's off-peak night tariff into a per-km energy cost far below diesel. This playbook covers the KT5L's specs, how to build an overnight depot-charging setup, the off-peak tariff maths, and a Cairo delivery schedule that lands full each morning.

Why overnight depot charging works in Egypt

Egyptian distribution fleets return to a central depot each evening, which is exactly the pattern overnight charging rewards. The KT5L's 350 kWh CATL LFP pack is large enough for a 260-320 km delivery loop, and a night on a 240 kW or 360 kW depot post leaves it at 100% before the first Cairo run. Charging off-peak also evens grid load and qualifies some operators for industrial tariff bands lower than daytime rates.

Building the overnight depot setup

  • Sized supply: a 360 kW post needs roughly a 400-500 kVA incoming feeder; plan it before the truck lands.
  • Smart scheduling: stagger trucks so the depot peak stays under the supply cap through the night.
  • Load management: let the 350 kWh packs charge sequentially to avoid demand charges.
  • Cooling: liquid-cooled packs accept full rate even in 45 °C Cairo summer nights.

Cairo delivery schedule that lands full

A typical Cairo loop runs 200-280 km of stops between 6am and 6pm, returns to the depot by 8pm, and charges from 9pm to 4am at a 360 kW post that returns 20-80% in 45-90 minutes and tops the last 20% overnight. Because the KT5L's 260-320 km range covers the loop with margin, drivers start each day at 100% with no midday charging. The 300 kW motor handles airport-road grades and loaded returns without strain, and the 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty covers the daily deep charge for the full financing term.

Depot electrical and safety planning

Site the charger on a dedicated feeder with RCD protection, and keep the 350 kWh packs' charging bays clear of flammable storage. The KT5L's sealed CATL LFP pack and insulation monitoring make the overnight bay safe to staff, and the near-silent operation lets the depot run charging without disturbing neighbours. A daily log of kWh in versus km out proves the off-peak saving to finance and to any green-fleet auditor.

Total cost of ownership for Cairo delivery

Over a 60-month contract the KT5L's $1,450-$1,800 monthly lease is offset by energy and maintenance savings. At the $0.06/kWh off-peak rate the 350 kWh pack costs about $720 per 10,000 km against $1,620 of diesel, a $900 saving, and maintenance falls by $190 per 10,000 km, so a single truck saves roughly $6,500 a year. Used across a 20-vehicle Cairo fleet that is over $130,000 annually redirected from fuel to fixed leasing, a compelling case for depot-based operators.

Spare parts and local support

The 300 kW motor, the CATL LFP pack contactors, and the 360 kW charger modules are stocked through the Alexandria and Cairo service points, so charge-port, motor, and pack faults are resolved within days. Keep one spare charger module and one spare contactor on the depot shelf so a fault never idles more than one truck, and commonise spares with any other Dongfeng EV truck in the fleet.

Import and rollout steps

Buyers should size the national opportunity in the Egypt electric truck market before ordering, then align the body build with their cargo. The KT5L electric cargo truck arrives under FOB terms, clears at Alexandria or Suez, and is registered for commercial plates. Plan the 360 kW depot post and the incoming feeder in the same project so the first morning run leaves at 100%.

Body build options for Cairo cargo

Cairo operators choose between a box van for parcel and pharma, a flatbed for palletised goods, and a curtain-side for mixed loads, all carried on the same 350 kWh chassis. The 260-320 km range is body-neutral because the pack powers only the drivetrain, so a heavier box simply trims a few km off the loop. Specify the body to your dominant cargo so the KT5L runs a full shift without a midday charge, and keep the 300 kW motor's torque in mind for airport-road on-ramps with a loaded box.

Driver shift handover and charge logging

With multiple drivers per truck across the 6am-6pm window, a clean handover matters: the outgoing driver logs km, kWh in, and any fault, and the incoming driver confirms the 350 kWh pack state before leaving. The depot's overnight charge from 9pm to 4am should finish at 100%, so a quick morning check replaces a full inspection. A daily kWh-in versus km-out log is also the proof finance and green-fleet auditors want when the off-peak saving is questioned.

Scaling the Cairo depot

A single KT5L proves the model; a 20-truck fleet needs staged 360 kW posts and a sequenced charge plan so the depot peak stays under the incoming feeder cap. Because every unit shares the same CATL LFP pack and charger standard, spares and training scale linearly, and the $6,500 per-truck annual saving compounds across the fleet. Plan the feeder for the final fleet size from day one to avoid a costly upgrade after the first expansion.

Grid load and demand-charge control

Overnight charging avoids daytime demand charges, but a 20-truck Cairo fleet drawing at 360 kW each could spike the depot peak if all start together. A simple sequencer that offsets each truck's start by a few minutes keeps the aggregated draw under the feeder cap and away from demand penalties. Because the KT5L charges from 9pm to 4am, there is ample window to stagger without missing the 100% morning target, protecting the off-peak saving.
