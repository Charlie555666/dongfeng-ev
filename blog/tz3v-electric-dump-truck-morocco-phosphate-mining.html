The Phosphate Haul Duty Cycle

Phosphate mining haulage in Morocco is a study in electrification-friendly conditions: fixed routes of 3-12 km between pit face and crusher or railhead, grades of 6-10%, speeds capped at 30-40 km/h, and return legs that run empty downhill. That last point is where the electric truck changes the physics. A loaded TZ3V climbing out of the pit consumes peak energy, but the empty return on a descending grade feeds 25-35% of that energy back through regenerative braking. Diesel trucks recover nothing.

A measured duty cycle at a Moroccan quarry-style operation shows a TZ3V completing 8-10 loaded cycles per shift on a 9 km loop, consuming around 3.2-3.8 kWh per km loaded and recovering enough on the return to net roughly 2.6 kWh/km over the full loop. At that rate the 600 kWh pack supports a 9-10 hour shift with one 40-minute opportunity charge at midday.

Charging Infrastructure at the Mine

A phosphate site charging plan is simpler than buyers expect. The pattern that works: two 240 kW DC chargers at the crusher dump point or workshop, plus a 400 V service connection at the pit top for a mobile unit if needed. Trucks plug in during shift change and crusher queue time. Moroccan industrial power tariffs make the energy line compelling - and OCP's own renewable buildout means several sites can charge from solar-heavy grids, which strengthens the ESG reporting story that increasingly decides offtake contracts with European fertilizer customers.

  • Map every crusher queue and shift change as a charging window before sizing the pack.
  • Run loaded climbs in the morning on a full pack; schedule opportunity charging at midday.
  • Fit the high-capacity dust filtration option on cooling intakes - phosphate dust is fine and persistent.
  • Log per-cycle kWh through the telematics portal; a 10% drift signals road condition or driver issues early.

What the Heat and Altitude Do

Khouribga sits near 800 m elevation with summer peaks of 38-42 C - demanding, but well inside the TZ3V's liquid-cooled envelope. Expect 6-9% range reduction in peak summer with full cab cooling, and none of the power derating that afflicts air-cooled equipment. Dust sealing on battery contactors and the high-voltage junction boxes is IP67; the weak points on any mine truck are cooling pack fins and air filters, so the maintenance schedule moves those inspections to weekly in dusty months.

Economics Versus the Diesel Fleet

The diesel comparison in Moroccan mining runs through fuel logistics as much as price: diesel at remote sites carries delivery premiums and pilferage losses that EV energy simply does not have. On 60,000 km per year of haul-road duty, a TZ3V's electricity costs roughly 70% less than the diesel's fuel, maintenance spend falls by half, and the regenerative braking all but eliminates the brake relining that dominates diesel dump truck maintenance budgets on descending routes. Payback against a comparable diesel tipper lands around month 30-36, after which the truck banks its advantage for the remaining life of the pack.
