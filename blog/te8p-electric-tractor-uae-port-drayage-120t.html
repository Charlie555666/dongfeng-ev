Jebel Ali Shuttle Cycle

The classic drayage loop runs from the Jebel Ali container yards to the inland customer or the next port gate, about 30–60 km each way with a full box. At 1.75 kWh/km under 120 t load, the 422 kWh pack covers roughly 240 km of mixed duty before a top-up, enough for two to three round trips in a shift. The 510 kW motor holds the gate acceleration without bogging, and regenerative braking on the creep back into the terminal returns 4–7% to the pack, which is why the TE8P beats a smaller motor on this exact cycle. The recovered energy is the reason a heavy haul stays inside a single daytime charge for most shifts.

Heat Management in the Terminal

Terminal asphalt in July sits above 45 °C and the tractor queues with the pack under load, so the liquid-cooled loop is doing real work. The dedicated coolant circuit holds cell temperature in the 25–40 °C working band during a 360 kW charge so the 20–80% window stays near 60–85 minutes instead of stretching as an air-cooled pack would. Drivers pre-condition the cab on shore power before the shift, which protects the first 20 km of range that would otherwise be spent cooling the box in the heat. The thermal loop also protects the pack during the long idle at the gate, where an uncooled unit would slowly lose charge acceptance.

Charging at the Port Gate

A drayage fleet charges at the depot between dispatch windows rather than continuously.

  • Install 240–360 kW DC at the fleet yard inside the free zone.
  • Rotate trucks through the stall during the natural lull between morning and afternoon gates.
  • Use shore power to pre-condition the cab so range is not spent on cooling.
  • Keep one spare charger module on site; a fault must not stop the next dispatch.

Total Cost of Ownership

At a FOB of US$130k–160k, the TE8P lands at Jebel Ali with CIF adding roughly US$9k–13k. UAE industrial electricity at 0.30–0.45 AED/kWh makes the per-km energy bill about one-third of diesel for a 120 t load. Over a 90,000 km/year drayage duty cycle the energy and maintenance saving recovers the premium inside about 22–30 months. The 8-year battery warranty covers the full payback, and brake pads last about twice as long thanks to regen on the terminal creep. The lowest noise and zero tailpipe also help the fleet meet free-zone emissions targets.

Warranty and Service

The 8-year / 4,500-cycle battery warranty covers roughly 1.35 million full-equivalent cycles, far beyond drayage duty, so the pack is under contract for the whole payback. Scheduled service is light: reduction-gear oil check at 60,000 km, coolant swap at 200,000 km, and brake inspection at 50,000 km. Resale at 36 months runs about 45–52% of landed cost when the battery health report stays above 85%, which telematics proves to the next buyer. Because the TE8P shares charging hardware with the TE8M, fleets running both models standardize on one stall type.

Port Emissions Rules and Access Fees

Jebel Ali and the wider UAE port system are moving toward low-emission terminal access, and some gates already price diesel idling or restrict the oldest units at peak. The TE8P enters clean, with no tailpipe and no engine note in the queue, which protects the fleet's gate slot as rules tighten. A drayage contractor running electric also answers the shipping lines' own scope-3 targets, because the box moved by the TE8P carries a lower carbon tag than one moved by diesel, and that is starting to matter in the carrier's tender score. The 120 t capability means the same unit covers the heavy export boxes that a lighter EV tractor cannot, so the fleet does not need a diesel reserve for the big loads. Fleets that pilot the TE8P on the clean-access lane typically report the gate slot is easier to keep through the morning rush, because the terminal gives priority to zero-emission plant during the peak window.

How 120 t Payload Changes the Math

At 120 t GCW the energy per km is high, about 1.75 kWh/km, but the revenue per km is also high because the box is full and the lane is short. The TE8P recovers 4–7% of that on the terminal creep back, and the 510 kW motor means the acceleration out of the gate never costs time the way a small motor would. Over a 90,000 km/year drayage duty the per-km energy bill is roughly a third of diesel, and the heavy load means the absolute saving per truck is larger than on a light regional run. The 8-year battery warranty covers the full payback even at the higher duty, and the shared 360 kW stall with the TE8M keeps the depot build simple. For a port fleet the TE8P is the unit that makes the heavy boxes electric without a second fuel system, and the access-fee saving on every gate trip adds to the energy saving across the month.

The same payload also future-proofs the fleet. As export volumes grow, the TE8P already covers the heaviest boxes, so the contractor does not need to buy a second heavy chassis when the lane scales. The pack and motor have headroom for the next size of container without a new truck, which protects the capital already committed and keeps the depot layout unchanged as the business expands.

For fleets comparing the TE8P to a diesel drayage tractor, the honest gap is the higher FOB and the charger, but both are recovered inside roughly two to three years on energy and access fees alone. The 8-year battery warranty then runs the saving for years beyond payback, and the quiet, zero-emission operation keeps the fleet inside every tightening port rule without a retrofit that a diesel would need.
