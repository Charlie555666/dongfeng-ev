Malaysia Bauxite Haul Profile

Bauxite mines in peninsular Malaysia move ore 10 to 30 km from pit to port stockpile, repeating the cycle through heat, humidity, and monsoon. A diesel dump in this class burns about 0.45 to 0.55 L/km under loaded climb, costing $0.40 to $0.72 per kilometer at Malaysian diesel prices of $0.90 to $1.30 per liter. The TZ5Y draws about 1.1 to 1.6 kWh/km when loaded, and at Malaysian commercial electricity of $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh that drops to $0.09 to $0.32 per kilometer.

The TZ5Y carries a 400 kWh CATL LFP pack with an 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, and FOB pricing runs from $110,000 to $140,000. On a 20 km round trip the truck completes many cycles per charge and tops up at the pit workshop, so the 400 kWh pack covers heavy monsoon-load days with margin.

Pit-to-Port Cost Comparison

The bauxite business lives on per-tonne cost from pit to port, and the TZ5Y attacks it on three fronts: lower energy cost, lower maintenance, and fewer weather-driven stoppages. A 360 kW DC charger at the workshop refills the 400 kWh pack in 45 to 90 minutes during shift change, and the 5 to 6 minute swap supports 24-hour haul when the monsoon compresses the working window. Because the electric drivetrain has fewer parts, the wet-season maintenance burden drops exactly when access is hardest.

The 35 to 40 percent maintenance saving is especially valuable in Malaysia's corrosive coastal air, where exhaust and brake systems on diesel trucks corrode fastest. Removing those systems extends component life and cuts the skilled-mechanic load at remote pits.
