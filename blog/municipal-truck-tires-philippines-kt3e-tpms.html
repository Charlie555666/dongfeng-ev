Why Tires Deserve Their Own Budget Line on Electric Trucks

Tires are the second-largest consumable cost on any truck fleet after energy, and on electric trucks they deserve fresh attention rather than inherited diesel habits. Two electric-specific factors change the wear equation: instant torque, which stresses tread at every launch if drivers treat the throttle like a diesel, and battery mass, which raises axle loads on the steer and drive positions. Neither is a problem when managed - fleets running electric garbage truck duty report tire life comparable to diesel once drivers are trained and pressures are disciplined - but unmanaged, both quietly add 15-25% to tire cost per kilometer. The KT3E electric garbage truck pairs its 250 kW motor with a torque delivery that telematics can meter precisely, turning tire wear from a surprise into a measured variable.

What TPMS Actually Buys a Fleet

Tire pressure monitoring systems pay for themselves on any truck; on electric trucks they pay faster, because pressure discipline protects both tires and range. A tire running 15% under specification increases rolling resistance enough to cost measurable kilometers of range per charge - range the fleet paid for in battery capacity. TPMS on the KT3E reports per-position pressure and temperature continuously, alerting before slow leaks become roadside stops and before heat buildup signals overload or brake drag. For Philippine fleets, the practical gains are threefold: fewer roadside tire events, tread life stretched toward its engineered limit, and energy consumption held at the figure the range planning assumed. Specify TPMS at order time; retrofitting is possible but factory integration is cleaner and cheaper.

Electric Truck Tire Wear Patterns and What They Mean

Electric trucks wear tires differently, and reading the patterns tells you about driving and loading before it tells you about the tires. Shoulder wear on steer axles points to under-inflation or aggressive cornering; center wear points to over-inflation; rapid drive-axle tread loss with cupping points to harsh launch habits that a torque-limited eco mode cures in a day. The KT3E's telematics complements the visual inspection: launch current and acceleration data correlate directly with drive-tire wear rates, so fleet managers can coach the two drivers who burn tires instead of retraining all forty. On Philippine road surfaces - laterite, broken asphalt, concrete - alignment and rotation intervals matter more than on European highways; build local road reality into the maintenance calendar.

Rotation, Matching and Position Strategy

A disciplined rotation program is the cheapest tire life extension available. The standard pattern on a 6x4 or 8x4 garbage truck: rotate steer tires to trailer or trailing positions before shoulder wear passes the halfway mark, match duals by diameter within tolerance, and never mix tread depths across a drive axle, which forces the differential to work continuously. Electric trucks add one wrinkle: regenerative braking loads the drive axle differently under deceleration, which makes drive-position alignment checks worth shortening. Fleets in the Philippines running the KT3E on rotation schedules of 25,000-35,000 km, adjusted for route severity, report drive-tire life at or above diesel benchmarks - and casing quality good enough for retreading, which halves the per-kilometer cost again on positions where retreads are permitted.
