The Doha Site Cycle

Infrastructure spoil haulage around Doha follows short loops: site face to disposal or crusher areas at 8-25 km, mixed site track and public road, with heavy idle time at loading shovels. A KTA1 on a 15 km loop completes 6-8 cycles per shift. Measured consumption in comparable Gulf operation runs 3.2-3.6 kWh/km loaded and 1.8-2.0 empty, netting a full 8-hour shift on 60-70% of the pack. Double-shift programs add one 40-50 minute DC charge at the changeover, scheduled into the break drivers take anyway.

The idle elimination matters more than buyers expect. Qatar site trucks queue at excavators and crushers 25-35% of the working day; the diesel equivalent burns that time into the fuel bill, while the KTA1 draws almost nothing at standstill with the cab cool.

Site Charging in Practice

Most Doha-area projects already have the grid capacity a charger needs - batching plants, site offices and camps run 400 V three-phase services that support a 120-240 kW DC unit with a dedicated feed. The workable pattern: one fast charger per 5-6 trucks at the workshop or crusher, sited where trucks naturally queue. For remote sections without grid, a containerized charger with a diesel-buffered or battery-buffered supply bridges the gap, though grid connection is always cheaper where available.

  • Place chargers at the crusher or workshop, never at a standalone island - queuing time is charging time.
  • Charge to 90% for daily work; hold 100% for the longest haul loops only.
  • Meter per-truck kWh per cycle; a drifting number flags tire pressure, route or driver issues.
  • Plan cable runs and protection before mobilization - site power upgrades take weeks in Qatar's permitting environment.

Summer Operation at 45-50 C

The Gulf summer question is fair, and the KTA1's answer is the liquid-cooled pack and motor with Gulf-rated cab cooling. Cells hold 20-35 C internally while ambient hits 48 C; charging speed is protected by the same loop. Expect 8-12% range reduction in peak summer with full air conditioning, and plan shift energy budgets at 70% pack consumption rather than 80%. Dust sealing is IP67 on high-voltage enclosures, with high-capacity intake filtration for the Shamal dust season - the maintenance schedule moves filter checks to weekly during those months.

Contractor Economics in Qatar

Qatar's fuel pricing and industrial electricity tariff produce a wide energy gap: a 31 t diesel tipper on Doha infrastructure duty burns $30,000-40,000 of fuel per year on a two-shift pattern; the KTA1's electricity for the same work costs $8,000-11,000.
