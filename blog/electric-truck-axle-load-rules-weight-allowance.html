Electric Truck Axle Load Rules and Weight Allowances

Electric truck axle load rules are the hidden constraint on every EV truck purchase, because batteries are heavy and a heavier chassis can eat into legal payload. A Dongfeng TZ5E heavy electric dump truck carries a 424 kWh CATL LFP pack that adds substantial mass versus a diesel equivalent, so buyers must plan axle loads against local limits. This article explains the key weight allowances worldwide, the EU zero-emission exemption, bridge formulas, a permitting workflow, and how to spec payload for African and GCC corridors in 2026.

Why Batteries Change the Weight Math

A 424 kWh LFP pack weighs roughly 2.6 to 3.2 tonnes depending on cell density and enclosure, before the heavier electric motors and reinforced frames. Against a diesel dump truck of similar size, the EV version can be 1.5 to 3 tonnes heavier empty. On a fixed gross vehicle or gross combination weight limit, that heavier tare directly reduces the payload you can legally carry. The regulatory answer in several regions is a zero-emission weight allowance that lets EVs exceed the standard limit by a set margin to restore cargo capacity.

The TZ5E is built as a heavy-duty platform, so its frame, axles and suspension are rated for demanding duty. The question is not whether the truck is strong enough; it is whether the law lets you load it to its mechanical rating. That is a permitting and routing problem, not an engineering one, and it is best solved before the purchase order rather than at the weighbridge.

The EU 2-Tonne Zero-Emission Allowance

The European Union allows zero-emission heavy trucks to exceed the standard maximum by up to 2 tonnes, specifically to compensate for battery weight without cutting payload. Several member states and the wider reform direction extend this toward 3 to 4 tonnes for heavier configurations. The intent is clear: do not punish electrification with lost cargo capacity. For a fleet running EU-spec routes, this allowance restores most of the payload gap on a TZ5E-class vehicle and makes the EV competitive on a per-tonne basis.

Buyers should note the allowance is applied at the national level and varies, so the usable figure depends on where the truck is registered and operated. It also typically applies to the whole vehicle rather than per axle, so you still must respect individual axle limits on bridges and weighbridges, which is where the bridge formula comes in.

Bridge Formula Basics

Many markets use a bridge formula that limits axle spacing and loads so heavy trucks do not damage pavements and structures. In simple terms, the more distance between axles, the more weight an axle group may carry. An EV dump with a long wheelbase and a tag axle can spread its battery and payload weight across more axles, keeping each under the per-axle ceiling even though the total is high. Understanding this lets a fleet choose a configuration that is both heavy-duty and legal, rather than simply hitting the GVW cap on one overloaded axle.

GCC and African Limits

Gulf Cooperation Council states generally permit higher gross weights than Europe, often 40 to 50 tonnes for rigid configurations and up to 60 to 65 tonnes for combinations, with per-axle ceilings around 10 to 13 tonnes on driven axles. That headroom is friendly to heavy EV dumps because the battery weight sits comfortably under the limit and payload remains strong. African limits vary widely by country, from 18 to 28 tonne general limits to higher mining and corridor concessions, and enforcement quality differs, so local legal advice is essential before spec'ing the pack size.

Axle Distribution Planning

Electric dumps usually place the pack between the chassis rails and the motors on the rear axles, which can load the driven axles heavily. Good spec'ing spreads mass with battery position, tag axles and suspension tuning so no single axle breaks its limit. A weighbridge check at 80 to 100 percent load is the only way to confirm, and Dongfeng provides axle-load diagrams per configuration to support permitting and to show the authorities during inspection.

  • Confirm the national GVW and per-axle limits before choosing pack size
  • Request the axle-load diagram for your exact TZ5E configuration
  • Weigh a loaded unit on a certified bridge before fleet rollout
  • Use tag or lift axles to balance the driven axles if needed
  • Document the zero-emission allowance in your operating permit
  • Train drivers to distribute load evenly in the body

Permitting Workflow

A clean permitting path runs as follows. First, capture the route profile and the strictest limit on it. Second, send Dongfeng the target GVW and axle caps to get a configuration and load diagram. Third, run a loaded weighbridge test and keep the certificate on the truck. Fourth, file the zero-emission allowance where applicable and train drivers to carry the paperwork. Fifth, audit weights quarterly, because tire wear and body buildup change tare over time and can quietly push an axle over its line.

Ghana and West African Corridors

The Ghana electric truck market illustrates the planning challenge. National weight limits are tighter than the GCC, and road enforcement on corridors to the ports and mines is active. A fleet there should spec the TZ5E with axle limits in mind, possibly accepting a slightly smaller battery or using the 8-year warranty to justify a heavier configuration under a mining concession. The 15 to 25 percent regen recovery on graded haul roads also reduces total energy per tonne moved, which improves the economics even when payload is modest.

Practical Payload Conclusion

Do not assume an EV dump carries less. With the right allowance or a higher base limit, a TZ5E can match diesel payload while cutting fuel and maintenance. The work is in the permit: know your GVW, know your per-axle ceiling, get the axle diagram, and weigh the first unit.

Comparing TZ5E Tare to a Diesel Dump

Alike-for-like matters. A diesel 8x4 dump of similar capacity might weigh 12 to 14 tonnes empty, while the TZ5E with its 424 kWh pack sits around 15 to 17 tonnes depending on body spec, a gap of roughly 2 to 3 tonnes. On a 32-tonne GVW limit, the diesel carries about 18 to 20 tonnes of payload and the EV about 15 to 17 tonnes. Where the market allows a higher EV limit or a zero-emission allowance, that gap closes or disappears. Fleets should compute payload per trip, not just purchase price, because a slightly lighter EV that still meets the payload still wins on fuel and maintenance over its life, and the regen recovery on graded haul helps energy per tonne moved, narrowing the operating gap further.

Multi-Axle Configuration Options

Spreading weight is the lever that keeps an EV legal. Adding a tag or lift axle distributes the battery and body mass across more points, lowering the load on each driven axle and satisfying stricter bridge formulas. The TZ5E platform supports configuration choices that balance stability, maneuverability and axle caps, and Dongfeng can advise the right wheelbase for your typical routes. For a market like Ghana with tighter limits, the right multi-axle layout plus disciplined loading can be the difference between a compliant 17-tonne payload and an illegal overload that gets weighed and fined at the gate, so the configuration decision is a compliance decision first and a cost decision second.

Fleets that skip this step risk a truck that is mechanically capable but legally under-loaded, which destroys the business case on day one and exposes the operator to fines.
