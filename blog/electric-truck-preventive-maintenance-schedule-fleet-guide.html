Electric Truck Preventive Maintenance Schedule for Fleets

A preventive maintenance schedule is the backbone of a reliable electric truck fleet. Because an EV truck has roughly 70 percent fewer moving parts than a diesel equivalent, the service list is shorter, but the items you do have are critical. This guide sets out a 10,000 / 30,000 / 60,000 km cadence, contrasts it with diesel, and explains why a structured plan protects your 8-year battery warranty and keeps resale value high even after years of hard duty.

Why EV Truck Maintenance Is Different

A diesel truck lives or dies by its engine, turbo, DPF, and transmission. An EV truck replaces those with a LvKong motor, a single-speed reduction gear, and a CATL LFP battery. There is no oil to change every 15,000 km, no fuel filter, no EGR valve. The maintenance burden shifts to brakes, tires, coolant, and high-voltage inspections. Fleets that understand this reallocate technician time from engine bays to wheels, chassis, and electrical health, and they discover that workshop hours drop even as vehicle count rises, which frees capacity for body-specific work that actually earns revenue.

The 10,000 km Service Items

At every 10,000 km, focus on the items that keep a Dongfeng EV truck safe and efficient. These are quick checks that any workshop can perform without specialised high-voltage certification beyond basic isolation training, so they can be done at a regional satellite bay rather than only at the main depot.

  • Tire pressure and tread, including dual fitments on 6x4 and 8x4 axle sets.
  • Brake pad and disc wear; regen-heavy duty extends intervals well beyond diesel.
  • Battery pack visual inspection for casing, seals, and cooling lines.
  • High-voltage connector torque check and insulation resistance test.
  • Cabin and HVAC filter, plus wiper and light function.
  • Door, latch, and suspension inspection on rough-site bodies.

Protecting the 8-Year Battery Warranty

Dongfeng EV trucks carry an 8-year or 4500-cycle battery warranty. That warranty assumes reasonable care: avoiding sustained 100 percent state-of-charge storage in heat, using specified charging profiles, and logging cooling system service. The Dongfeng KT5M electric cargo truck logs cooling and cycle data through telematics, so a missed 60,000 km coolant service can be seen during a claim review. Keep records meticulously; a simple service logbook often makes the difference between an approved and a rejected claim, and between a strong and a weak resale story.

Charging Upkeep and Building Your Schedule

DC fast charging at 1.5 to 2 hours for a 20-80 percent window stresses connectors and cables. Inspect charging plugs for heat discoloration every 30,000 km and verify the depot's breaker and cable management. In the Tanzania electric truck market, dust is the enemy; sealed enclosures and monthly air-filter cleaning on charging cabinets prevent nuisance trips that would otherwise idle an otherwise healthy truck and push drivers back to diesel rental cover at premium cost.

Start your schedule from the odometer, not the calendar. Set telematics alerts at 10,000, 30,000, and 60,000 km and assign a responsible technician. Because the EV truck has fewer moving parts, you can run a leaner workshop while still hitting every critical checkpoint. The result is lower downtime, longer brake life, and an intact battery warranty at resale. Pair the schedule with driver training so that regen discipline and smooth handling do half the maintenance work before the wrench is ever picked up, and review the data quarterly to catch drift before it becomes failure.

To make the schedule stick, turn it into a cost story your finance team can see. Compare your EV truck's scheduled labour hours against a diesel equivalent over a 5-year horizon: the eliminated engine oil, fuel filters, DPF regenerations, and transmission services compound into hundreds of saved workshop hours per vehicle, which you can redeploy to body and uptime work that actually earns revenue. Build the parts inventory around the short EV list, concentrating spend on tires, brake components, coolant, and a small set of high-voltage service items, rather than the deep diesel parts bin that ties up capital on a shelf. Train at least two technicians per region on isolation and insulation testing so a single absence never blocks a service window. And keep the battery coolant and connector checks non-negotiable, because those two items are the only ones that can void the 8-year warranty if neglected. A preventive plan is only as good as its enforcement; the fleets that benefit most are the ones that audit compliance and celebrate the depot with the fewest missed intervals, turning maintenance from a cost centre into a quiet competitive advantage.

Do not overlook the human side of the schedule. Technicians trained only on diesel can be anxious around high-voltage systems, and that anxiety leads to over-cautious, slow, or avoided service that defeats the whole point. Invest in proper isolation and insulation training so your workshop treats the EV truck with confident routine rather than fear, and the time saved per vehicle quickly offsets the training cost. Document each service with dated photos of the connector torque check and the coolant conductivity reading, because that evidence is what protects your warranty claim if a pack ever fails. Finally, align the maintenance calendar with operations so trucks are serviced during natural lulls rather than pulled off a paying job; the flexibility of the short EV checklist makes this easy compared with a diesel overhaul that blocks a bay for a day. Fleets that integrate maintenance into the weekly rhythm, rather than treating it as an interruption, keep both uptime and warranty intact, and they end up with vehicles that look and perform like new at year five, which is exactly what the resale market rewards when the time comes to rotate the fleet.

A useful way to sell the schedule internally is to track the avoided-cost line explicitly. Every diesel service you no longer perform is labour and parts you did not spend, and every brake pad you did not replace is metal and friction material kept out of the waste stream; tally these monthly and the preventive plan pays for itself in the reporting alone. The same discipline protects the battery, which is the component whose health drives both warranty claims and resale, so the 60,000 km coolant and connector checks are never optional line items but the foundation of the asset's value. Where operations push back on downtime for service, show them the alternative: an unplanned failure on a remote route costs far more in recovery, lost payload, and angry customers than a planned half-day in the bay. Preventive maintenance is therefore not a cost centre to minimise but an insurance policy to fund deliberately, and the EV truck makes that policy cheaper and easier to keep than any diesel it replaces.
