Kumasi Construction Goes Electric: TZ5E and KTA1 Electric Truck Strategy for Ghana's Garden City

Kumasi is West Africa's fastest-moving construction market that most fleet planners have never modelled. Ghana's second city and the capital of the Ashanti Region is in the middle of a sustained build-out: road dualisation projects radiating toward Obuasi, Ejisu and Sunyani, the Kejetia market redevelopment, hospital and university expansions, and a housing programme that consumed record volumes of sand, chippings and cement in 2025. Every tonne of that material moves by truck from quarry to site — and most of those quarries sit within a 40 km ring of the city centre.

That geography is the reason an electric truck fleet works in Kumasi before it works almost anywhere else in the region. Aggregates haulage runs on fixed loops of 20 to 60 km, loaded one way and empty the other, returning to the same quarry yard every night. The TZ5E 6x4 electric dump truck with CATL 400 kWh LFP and the KTA1 8x4 electric dump truck with CATL 352 kWh were practically designed for this duty cycle. The Kumasi Aggregates Map

Kumasi's building-material supply comes from four main clusters. Understanding them determines your charging strategy:

  • The Barekese and Offin basin sand dredgers, 25–35 km northwest — sand loops running dawn to dusk, six days a week.
  • The granite quarries around Barekese, Abofour and Konongo, 30–70 km out — chippings and armor rock on 40–60 km single-leg hauls.
  • The Ofoase and Jacobu laterite pits feeding road-works on the southern corridors, 50–80 km from site.
  • In-city demolition and muck haulage — short, dense, stop-start loops of under 15 km around Kejetia, Ahodwo and Asokwa.

A diesel tipper burning through these loops consumes fuel at its worst efficiency point: constant loading queues, crawler-speed site tipping, and gradient on the Kumasi plateau. An electric tipper turns that same duty cycle into its best case. Regenerative braking recovers 15–25% of energy on the loaded-descending legs, and the electric PTO hoist raises the body without a drop of burned fuel while the truck is stationary.

Charging Design for a Quarry-Based Fleet

The elegant feature of quarry logistics is that the loading point is fixed — and a fixed loading point is a charging point. Kumasi-area quarries typically have grid supply for crushers and screens, with room on the loadout apron for chargers. A practical two-site design:

  • One 240 kW DC charger at the quarry, charging during the 20–40 minute loading queue — the truck would be waiting anyway. Ten minutes on a 240 kW charger returns roughly 40 kWh, about 30 km of loaded range.
  • Two 120 kW points at the city yard for overnight base charging of the full fleet.
  • Solar hybrid option: a 60–100 kWp array at the quarry cuts daytime charging cost by 40–60% and keeps haulage running through grid interruptions, which remain part of life in Ashanti Region.

This quarry-plus-yard architecture means you buy fewer chargers than trucks — typically one DC fast point per four to five tippers — because the loading queue does half the charging work.
