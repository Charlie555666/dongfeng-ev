What Vehicle-to-Grid Means for Truck Fleets

Vehicle-to-grid, or V2G, lets an electric truck push energy back into the grid through a bidirectional charger instead of only drawing power. A heavy EV truck is a mobile battery on wheels: the TE8P carries a 528 kWh CATL LFP pack, which is ten to twenty times the storage of a passenger EV. For a fleet that parks overnight or between shifts, that parked capacity has real monetary value in markets with peak-and-off-peak pricing. V2G does not replace the truck's transport job - it monetizes the hours when the truck would otherwise sit idle. Fleet operators across Saudi Arabia are starting to treat their charging depots as small power assets rather than pure cost centers.

Why Saudi Arabia Is a Candidate for V2G

The economics of V2G depend on the spread between cheap and expensive electricity, and on how reliably the grid buys flexibility. Saudi Arabia has sharp daytime demand peaks, growing solar penetration and, in many districts, fragile grid capacity that utilities would rather support than rebuild. A depot of electric trucks that charges at night and discharges for even two hours during the evening peak can earn meaningful tariff arbitrage. Where formal V2G markets are still developing, fleets can start with peak shaving behind the meter - using truck batteries to cut the depot's own demand charges - which needs no utility contract at all and works with standard energy management software.

Hardware Requirements: Bidirectional Charging

V2G needs a bidirectional DC charger and a vehicle that supports reverse power flow. The TE8P electric 6x4 heavy tractor uses a CCS2 charging interface, and bidirectional CCS2 chargers in the 60-120 kW class are now commercially available from several suppliers. A practical depot design for a ten-truck fleet pairs one or two bidirectional units with conventional fast chargers, so daily operations never depend on the V2G hardware. Specify chargers with grid-code certification for Saudi Arabia before ordering, and confirm with us the battery management software version on your TE8P units, because V2G operation is enabled and logged at the BMS level to protect warranty coverage.

Battery Warranty and V2G Cycling

The first question every fleet CFO asks about V2G is whether extra cycling eats the battery warranty. The CATL LFP pack in the TE8P is rated for 4,500 cycles under an 8-year warranty, and LFP chemistry tolerates shallow cycling far better than NMC. A daily V2G discharge of 50 kWh from a 528 kWh pack is a shallow 15-20% depth-of-discharge event, which contributes minimally to degradation compared with the truck's driving cycles. We document V2G operating windows in the warranty terms so fleets can participate in grid programs with written clarity on coverage, and the BMS keeps a full cycle log that protects both parties in any future claim.

Steps to Pilot V2G with Your Fleet

Start small and measure. A sensible Saudi Arabia pilot takes two to five trucks, one bidirectional charger, and three months of interval metering data from the depot. Step one: map your duty cycles and confirm overnight parked hours. Step two: get the utility tariff sheet and model peak windows. Step three: install the bidirectional charger with grid-code approval and run behind-the-meter peak shaving first. Step four: approach the utility or aggregator about formal grid services only after you have baseline data.
