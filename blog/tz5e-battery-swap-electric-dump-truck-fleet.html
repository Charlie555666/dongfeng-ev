Dump trucks on mining, quarry, and infrastructure loops run relentless shift patterns. When a project pays by the ton moved, a truck parked on a charger for over an hour each shift is a direct revenue loss. How the 5 to 6 Minute Swap Works

The TZ5E drives onto the station platform, where alignment rails position the chassis. A robotic hoist unlocks and lowers the depleted 350 kWh pack, lifts a charged pack into place, and re-locks it, with the whole exchange completed in 5 to 6 minutes. The driver never leaves the cab. The removed pack then charges at a controlled rate inside the station, which actually extends pack life compared with repeated fast charging because the cells charge cooler and slower.

Because pack and truck are separable assets, the station operator manages battery health and warranty, while the fleet pays an energy-plus-service fee per swap. That structure removes residual battery risk from the fleet's balance sheet.

Uptime Math: Swap Versus Charging

The comparison that matters is productive hours per day. A charging dump truck loses roughly 75 to 90 minutes per shift to its charging window; a swap truck loses about 6 minutes. Across a two-shift operation that is nearly three extra productive hours per truck per day, which at typical haulage rates repays the swap service fee many times over. For fleets where trucks would otherwise idle between shifts, swap is the difference between a marginal and a compelling EV business case.

  • Swap suits fleets running two or more shifts with no natural charging gap.
  • Single-shift depot fleets are usually cheaper on overnight charging alone.
  • Plan one swap stall per 25 to 35 trucks for smooth throughput.
  • Keep a 240 kW charger at the depot as backup and for weekend top-ups.
  • Size the pack pool at 1.2 times the truck count to buffer peak demand.

Station Planning and Site Requirements

A single-stall swap station needs a level concrete pad, a 400V three-phase supply sized for the pack pool charging load, and a canopy for weather protection. Throughput runs 120 to 200 swaps per day per stall. The station's packs charge across the day and night, which smooths grid draw and lets the operator buy energy at off-peak tariffs. For a remote mine, a solar-plus-storage canopy can supply a meaningful share of the charging energy and hedge against grid outages.
