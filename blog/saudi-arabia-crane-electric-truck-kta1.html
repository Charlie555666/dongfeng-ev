Why Crane Work Suits Electric Trucks

Truck-mounted crane duty is a natural fit for electrification, though few buyers realize it until they see the duty cycle on paper. Crane trucks drive to a site, then work stationary for hours - and on a diesel truck, those working hours mean an engine idling to power the hydraulic pump, burning fuel to stand still. On an electric truck, the crane draws from the battery through an electric power take-off at a fraction of the energy cost, in silence, with zero exhaust around the crew working beside the outriggers. The KTA1 electric dump truck supports crane body installations with a factory-engineered chassis and electric PTO interface, turning the working hours that cost diesel fleets dearly into the cheapest hours of the day.

Electric PTO: Powering the Crane from the Battery

The electric power take-off replaces the engine-driven pump with an electric motor driving the hydraulic system, drawing directly from the 262 kWh traction pack. Power delivery is instant and precisely metered - no revving to build pressure, no idle between lifts. Energy consumption for a typical crane working session runs at single-digit kWh per hour against duty, which means even a full day of lifting consumes a modest share of the pack: the KTA1's 220 km of certified range shrinks by an amount planners can model exactly rather than guess. Noise falls to conversation level, which matters on urban jobsites with early-morning or night restrictions - a crane truck that works silently wins permits a diesel cannot.

Chassis, Stability and Weight Planning

Crane installations live or die on weight and stability calculations, and electric trucks change the inputs in one important way: the battery pack sits low and between the axles, lowering the center of gravity that governs overturning moments. Bodybuilders must still do the math - crane capacity at reach, outrigger spread, axle load distribution with the crane mounted behind the cab or at the rear - but the starting point is favorable. The KTA1 chassis is rated with its 262 kWh pack integrated, so payload calculations begin from a known tare rather than an estimate. For Saudi bodybuilders, we supply chassis drawings, PTO interface specifications and weight data at quotation stage, so the crane supplier engineers against facts, not assumptions.

Duty Cycles: What a Working Day Looks Like

A representative crane-truck day in Riyadh: drive 40-80 km to the first site, work two to four hours of lifting, reposition, work again, return to base. Total drive distance rarely exceeds 150 km; total crane energy rarely exceeds 15-25 kWh. Both fit comfortably inside the KTA1's envelope with margin for weather and detours, and overnight depot charging restores everything by morning. The planning discipline is the same as any electric truck: match the pack to the worst day, not the average day, and let telematics confirm within weeks that the margin holds. Fleets in Saudi Arabia that have electrified crane duty report the surprise is not range - it is how much quieter and cheaper the working hours become.

Buying Checklist: Specifying a Crane Truck Correctly

Specify the crane first and the truck second - lift capacity at the reaches you actually work, outrigger geometry, and the body layout around them. Then match the chassis: the KTA1 configuration with the wheelbase, axle ratings and PTO interface the bodybuilder's drawings require. Confirm four items in writing before order: crane weight and center of gravity against the chassis payload calculation, hydraulic flow and pressure requirements against the electric PTO rating, stability calculations at maximum reach with maximum load, and certification requirements for the completed vehicle in Saudi Arabia - SABER/SASO certification paperwork we support with factory documentation. Crane trucks are engineered products; the fleets that treat procurement that way get years of silent, low-cost lifting from the day of delivery.

Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the KTA1 earns acceptance quickly. The 300 kW motor delivers instant torque with no gear hunting, the cab is quieter than any diesel equivalent, and regenerative braking reduces fatigue in stop-start duty. Drivers typically adapt within a week, and most prefer the electric truck once they experience a full shift without engine noise and vibration. The habits that matter are small: smooth acceleration, anticipation to maximize regeneration, charging connection on return to depot, and honest reporting of dash warnings. Fleets in Saudi Arabia report the fastest adoption when they involve senior drivers in the pilot phase and let peer experience do the convincing, rather than imposing the change top-down. Energy consumption per driver is visible in the telematics data, and a little friendly competition between drivers on efficiency measurably improves fleet-wide consumption in the first quarter.

Charging Planning for KTA1 Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KTA1 supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 262 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Saudi Arabia should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
