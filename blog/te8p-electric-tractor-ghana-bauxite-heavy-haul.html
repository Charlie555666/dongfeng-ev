The Bauxite Corridor Profile

Mine-to-port bauxite haulage in Ghana runs 80-240 km depending on the deposit, with loaded runs descending from the plateau mining districts toward the coast - a profile that suits an electric truck unusually well. The loaded leg loses elevation, letting regenerative braking recover 15-25% of the energy the climb segments consume, while the empty return climbs back at a fraction of loaded consumption. Measured corridor energy on comparable West African heavy-haul runs 2.2-2.8 kWh/km loaded and 1.2-1.4 empty at these weights.

The operating pattern that works: overnight full charge at the mine depot, loaded run to Takoradi with a 40-60 minute dual-gun DC top-up at the port during discharge, and return empty on the remaining capacity. The 600 kWh pack provides the headroom that heavy-haul margins demand - this is not a duty cycle to run at the pack's edge.

Regen on the Escarpment

The defining engineering feature of this corridor is the descent from the mining plateau. Diesel heavy haulers manage it with retarders and service brakes, converting the potential energy into heat and brake wear - and accepting fade risk on the longest sections. The TE8P converts the same descent into charge: high-power regenerative braking holds combination speed on 6-8% grades while returning meaningful energy to the pack. Brake lining life in this duty extends 4-6 times versus diesel, which alone removes a major maintenance line and a safety exposure that heavy-haul operators know intimately.

  • Map every descent on the route into the energy model; regen recovery changes pack sizing.
  • Set regen levels by route section - full strength on descents, moderated on flat public road.
  • Charge at the port during discharge; align charger booking with vessel schedules.
  • Audit per-trip kWh monthly; drift above the corridor baseline signals road, tyre or driver issues.

Heat, Rain Season and Road Reality

Ghana's corridor combines 30-35 C heat, heavy humidity and a rain season that degrades mine-road surfaces for months. The TE8P's liquid-cooled powertrain is indifferent to the heat; the real seasonal variable is road condition, which moves rolling resistance and consumption 10-15% between dry and wet season. The sealed IP67 high-voltage system tolerates standing water and mud that stop diesel air intakes. Driver technique on degraded surfaces matters more in heavy haul than anywhere else - momentum management is both a safety and a range discipline.
