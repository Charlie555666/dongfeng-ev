Which Electric Truck Types Work Best on the Apron?

Airport ground support splits into three electric truck roles. Terminal tractors like the TE46 electric terminal tractor handle baggage trains, cargo dollies and pushback-adjacent logistics - the highest-utilization role and the strongest electrification candidate. Electric cargo trucks in the 5-10 tonne class move freight between terminal, warehouse and aircraft. Specialist service trucks - sweepers, sprinklers and washing units - keep aprons, runways and stands clean and dust-controlled. All three share the duty profile electric drivetrains love: short distances, constant stop-start, long idle-free waits and nightly return to a fixed depot. Fleets should electrify in utilization order, starting where daily hours are highest.

How Much Range Does Airport Ground Support Need?

Less than buyers fear. Telematics studies of apron vehicles consistently show 60-150 km of daily movement at low average speeds - a fraction of what a road-going truck covers. The TE46 offers 200 km of certified range from its 262 kWh CATL LFP pack, which means a two-shift airport operation completes a full day on one charge with 30-40% reserve. Range anxiety belongs to highway trucking, not the apron. The real sizing question is not range but charging windows: map the schedule, find the natural parked hours, and the battery capacity almost always proves more than sufficient with opportunity charging as backup.

What Does Charging Look Like at an Airport?

Airport charging concentrates at the GSE depot or maintenance yard, keeping high-voltage infrastructure landside or in designated airside service areas. DC fast chargers of 60-120 kW restore the TE46 from 20% to 80% in about an hour, covering opportunity top-ups during shift changes, while overnight slower charging handles the base load at the lowest tariff. A first fleet of five to fifteen electric trucks typically runs on existing service-yard power with minor upgrades; larger programs justify a dedicated substation. Airport authorities should involve electrical engineers at specification stage and select CCS2 chargers to match the trucks' standard interface.

Charging Planning for TE46 Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TE46 supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 262 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. Frequently Asked Questions

How much range does an electric truck need for airport ground support?

Airport ground support trucks need far less range than highway trucks - typically 60-150 km of daily movement. A model like the Dongfeng TE46 with a 262 kWh battery and 200 km certified range covers a full two-shift day on one charge, leaving 30-40% reserve for schedule disruptions.

How long does it take to charge an electric ground support truck?

On a 120 kW DC fast charger, an electric truck such as the TE46 charges from 20% to 80% in about 60-90 minutes, which fits into shift-change windows. Overnight slower charging at the GSE depot covers the base load at the lowest electricity tariff.

Are electric airport trucks really cheaper than diesel GSE?

Yes. Energy cost per operating hour falls 60-75% versus diesel, and scheduled maintenance drops by over half because there is no engine oil, filters or exhaust aftertreatment. On two-shift airport duty, electric trucks typically reach total cost parity within three years.

What battery safety certification do airports require for electric trucks?

Airport safety departments generally require UN38.3 battery transport certification, an emergency response guide and documented high-voltage isolation design. Dongfeng EV trucks ship with CATL LFP packs carrying full UN38.3 test records plus first-responder documentation for airport fire services.

How many chargers does a small airport fleet need?

A starting fleet of five to fifteen electric trucks usually runs on two to four 60-120 kW DC chargers plus overnight charging points per parking bay. Exact charger count should follow a duty-cycle analysis of parked hours, which the truck supplier should support before ordering.
