Battery Warranty and Residual Risk in Rental

The battery is the residual value question in any electric truck rental. The TZ3Z carries an 8-year or 4,500-cycle CATL LFP warranty with guaranteed capacity retention, and - critically for rental operators - the coverage transfers to subsequent owners rather than dying at first sale. LFP chemistry degrades slowly and predictably, so a battery state-of-health report at contract end gives the next buyer a number to trust instead of a guess. Rental contracts should specify charging discipline - daily cycling within the 20-80% band where practical - because gentle usage measurably protects residual value. Operators in Chile who document charging and maintenance history from day one are achieving resale prices 10-15% above units with gaps in their records.

Contract Structures Available in Chile

Rental and lease structures for electric trucks in Chile mirror diesel practice, with EV-specific adjustments. Short-term rental suits project work and trial periods, priced daily or monthly with charging guidance included. Operating leases over three to five years shift residual risk to the lessor and keep trucks off the lessee's balance sheet - increasingly popular with Chilean fleets testing electrification. Full-service leases bundle maintenance, parts and even charging infrastructure into a fixed monthly cost per truck, which converts the entire EV transition into an operating expense. Finance leases suit buyers who want ownership at term end. The right structure depends on utilization certainty: the higher and steadier the daily kilometers, the stronger the case for ownership over rental.

What Renters Need: Charging and Support

A rental electric truck without charging is a stranded asset, so infrastructure questions come first. Renters need depot power sufficient for overnight charging of a 350 kWh pack, or access to a DC fast charger within the operating radius - the TZ3Z recovers 20-80% in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit. Rental operators increasingly bundle charging solutions: a portable charger with short-term hires, or a depot charger installation with longer contracts. Support matters as much as hardware - renters expect a helpline, quick parts access and clear procedures for faults. When Buying Beats Renting

Rental is the right answer for trials, seasonal peaks and uncertain utilization; ownership wins once utilization is proven and stable. The crossover typically sits around 18-30 months of continuous use: beyond that, rental payments exceed the ownership cost of the same truck even after financing. Fleets in Chile that started with one or two rental units are now buying outright, applying their rental-period operating data - real energy consumption, real maintenance events, real driver feedback - to specify exactly the right trucks. That is the healthy path: rent to learn, then buy with data. Deployment Timeline from Order to Operation

Realistic scheduling prevents the most common fleet electrification failures. A typical TZ3Z deployment runs five to seven months end to end: production takes 30-60 days depending on configuration and order size; ocean freight to the Port of San Antonio adds 25-45 days; customs clearance and homologation steps add two to six weeks depending on Chilean procedures. Running in parallel, the depot charging installation should complete before the trucks land, and driver and technician training should finish during commissioning week. Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the TZ3Z earns acceptance quickly.
