TE46 Electric Terminal Tractor for Egypt's Ain Sokhna Port

Why the TE46 Fits Ain Sokhna Port Drayage

The TE46 electric terminal tractor is an EV truck designed for the short, intense shuttle loops inside Egypt's Ain Sokhna port. With a 262 kWh CATL LFP battery and a 282 kW LvKong motor, the TE46 hauls 40-70 tonne container stacks between quay, yard and gate on 3-8 km cycles, completing 60-90 moves per shift. Because the duty is stop-start with constant braking, regen recovers 15-20 percent of energy, and a 5-6 minute battery swap option keeps the tractor moving during peak vessel calls instead of sitting on a charger while a ship waits.

Battery Swap in 5-6 Minutes Versus Charging

At a busy container terminal the cost of a tractor off-line is the cost of a idle quay crane, so swap beats charge for peak windows. A 5-6 minute battery swap returns a fully charged pack and keeps the chassis on the move across a 20-hour vessel call. Where swap infrastructure is not yet justified, a 240 kW DC charger fills the 262 kWh pack to 80 percent in about 60 minutes during the natural lull between vessel windows. Most Ain Sokhna operators run a hybrid: charge overnight for the base fleet and swap the peak-shift tractors that cannot afford the dwell. The 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty covers the pack across multiple swap partners in a shared pool.

Energy Cost Versus Diesel Terminal Tractors

Egyptian industrial electricity runs about USD 0.08-0.18 per kWh, while diesel at the port costs USD 0.90-1.20 per litre. The TE46 uses roughly 1.9 kWh/km loaded with a 60 tonne stack, costing USD 0.15-0.40 per km. A diesel terminal tractor of the same class burns 0.45-0.60 L/km, costing USD 0.40-0.72 per km. Across a 30 tractor fleet completing 70 moves per shift at 5 km per move for 330 days, the energy gap is worth USD 1.4-2.6 million annually, before the 35-40 percent maintenance saving from eliminating the diesel engine, exhaust and the clutch packs that fail fastest in stop-start yard duty. At a port where diesel fumes also trigger worker-safety audits, the zero-exhaust cabin and quay are an added compliance win.

Productivity, Cycle Times and Moves per Shift

On a 3-8 km quay-to-yard loop the TE46 completes 60-90 container moves per 8-hour shift at a 40-70 tonne gross stack, moving 2,400-6,300 tonnes of box per tractor per day depending on stack weight. Instant torque from standstill means fast acceleration out of the gate with a heavy 40-foot box, holding yard speeds of 20-30 km/h without the lag of a diesel pulling from low rpm. Regenerative braking on the constant deceleration into the stack returns 15-20 percent of the energy, which is why the TE46 still finishes a full shift on one charge despite the heaviest duty factor of any model in the Dongfeng EV range.

Operator Training, Safety and Shift Changeovers

Terminal tractors run the hardest duty cycle of any truck, and the TE46 earns its keep on reliability. The electric drivetrain needs no glow plug, no warm-up idle and no clutch grind, so a 45 C Ain Sokhna afternoon and a cool dawn both see full 282 kW on the first metre out of the gate with a 70 tonne stack. Drivers complete a one-day course covering one-pedal yard control, regen tuning for the constant deceleration and safe high-voltage isolation during pack swap. Dongfeng certifies each operator and issues a digital card the tractor reads before shifting, protecting the battery warranty and the terminal's safety audit that now limits diesel units near berthed vessels carrying sensitive cargo.

Safety and support points the terminal should plan for:

  • High-voltage isolation training is mandatory before any operator takes a shift.
  • The 5-6 minute battery swap keeps tractors moving through peak vessel calls.
  • Zero diesel exhaust at the quay improves air quality for crane and lasher crews.
  • Two-year recommended spares kit ships with every TE46 to protect terminal uptime.
  • Over-the-air diagnostics flag cell imbalance before it costs range or power.
  • Regen on constant deceleration returns 15-20 percent of the yard energy.

Because the TE46 adds no exhaust or noise at the berth, gangs report better communication and lower fatigue across 20-hour vessel calls, which directly lifts the moves per shift that the quay crane productivity bonus is built on.
