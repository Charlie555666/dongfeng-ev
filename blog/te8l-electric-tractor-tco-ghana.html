TE8L Electric Tractor TCO Analysis: 5-Year Numbers for Ghanaian Hauliers

The Dongfeng TE8L electric truck is a 49 tonne GCW 6x4 EV truck prime mover built for regional haulage, and for Ghanaian operators the question is never the sticker price but the total cost of ownership. This analysis builds a full 5-year TCO for a TE8L working the Tema-Accra corridor, comparing purchase, energy versus diesel at Ghana tariffs, maintenance and resale so a haulier can see the payback math before signing. The TE8L carries a 350 kWh CATL LFP pack, a 360 kW LvKong motor and a 250 km range that suits the short, dense Ghanaian logistics lanes.

Why TCO beats purchase price

A diesel tractor's fuel bill over five years typically exceeds its purchase price, so judging an electric tractor on FOB alone is a mistake. The TE8L FOB sits at $72,000 to $88,000, but the real story is the operating cost per kilometre. On the Tema-Accra corridor a diesel 49 tonne tractor burns roughly 33 litres per 100 km, while the TE8L consumes about 1.4 kWh per km from the grid. At Ghana's commercial electricity tariff the energy cost per km is a fraction of diesel, and that gap widens whenever fuel prices rise. The TCO table below makes the five-year picture explicit and shows why fleet finance teams should model the whole life rather than the headline number.

The TE8L lands at roughly half the five-year net cost of a comparable diesel, driven almost entirely by the energy and maintenance gap. Even with a higher upfront price and a conservative resale, the electric tractor saves about $154,000 per truck over the period, which on a ten-truck fleet is $1.54 million redirected from fuel and repairs to margin. The gap is large enough that even a pessimistic energy-price assumption still leaves the TE8L clearly ahead.

Tema-Accra corridor scenario

The Tema-Accra corridor is about 25 km of port-to-city haulage that a tractor can run multiple times per shift, so the 250 km range of the TE8L is never the constraint; the constraint is charger access at the depot. A Ghanaian haulier running this lane typically does four to six round trips per day, returning to Tema with margin to spare. The 350 kWh pack recharges from 20 to 80 percent in 45 to 75 minutes on a DC fast charger, which slots into the driver rest break, and an overnight charge covers the next day. Because the lane is short and predictable, the TE8L's strength, low per-km energy cost, is fully captured without needing a long-range battery. A second shift simply adds a fast top-up between drivers.

Energy cost at Ghana tariffs

At a commercial tariff around $0.16 per kWh, the TE8L's 1.4 kWh per km works out to about $0.22 per km in energy. A diesel at $1.10 per litre and 33 litres per 100 km costs about $0.36 per km in fuel, a 40 percent saving before you touch maintenance. The saving grows where hauliers secure off-peak or captive solar charging, and where Ghana fuel prices spike the TE8L becomes even more compelling. The catch is grid connection lead time, which in Ghana can run three to nine months, so the charger application should be filed the day the truck order is signed, not when the ship docks. We list the cost drivers that move the TCO most:

  • Energy tariff: off-peak or captive solar can cut the $0.22 per km figure by a third.
  • Diesel price: every $0.10 per litre move shifts the comparison by roughly $6,000 over five years.
  • Annual km: higher utilisation spreads the extra purchase cost faster and shortens payback.
  • Maintenance discipline: in-house electric tech work lowers the $9,000 five-year line further.
  • Resale: an in-warranty year-five sale protects the $34,000 residual assumption.

Maintenance and resale notes

The TE8L's electric driveline removes the diesel's most expensive failure points, cutting scheduled maintenance to about $1,800 per year versus $4,400 for diesel over the scenario. Resale at year five is modelled conservatively at $34,000, supported by the 8-year battery warranty that still has three years of cover left on the 4,500-cycle pack. A battery state-of-health certificate at sale lifts that number, and Ghanaian buyers are increasingly aware that an in-warranty electric tractor is a lower-risk asset than an out-of-warranty diesel. The near-silent operation is also a growing advantage in urban night freight where noise ordinances are tightening.

Payback period

The extra $13,000 of landed purchase cost on the TE8L is recovered in energy and maintenance savings in roughly the first eight months of operation on the Tema-Accra corridor. After that, every kilometre is pure saving against the diesel baseline. For a haulier financing the truck over five years, the monthly saving exceeds the extra finance cost from month one, which means the switch improves cash flow immediately rather than only at end of term.

Financing the switch

Most Ghanaian hauliers do not pay cash; they finance the tractor over the same five-year horizon the TCO models. The key point is that the TE8L's lower monthly operating cost more than covers the slightly higher finance payment on the higher purchase price, so the fleet's cash position improves from month one rather than worsening. Lenders are also warming to electric trucks because the predictable energy cost makes the repayment profile cleaner than a diesel exposed to fuel spikes, and the 8-year battery warranty gives the financier a tangible residual backstop. A haulier should therefore model the monthly number, not just the five-year total, because that is what the bank and the board actually feel each month.

Risk factors and sensitivity

No TCO is certain, so it is worth stressing the assumptions that move the result. If Ghana's commercial tariff rose 30 percent, the TE8L energy line would grow by about $15,000 over five years, still far below the diesel fuel line. If the diesel price fell 20 percent, the gap narrows but the TE8L retains a clear lead because maintenance and reliability savings remain intact. The real risk is not the energy math but the charging build-out: a depot without a live charger captures none of these savings. That is why the single most important action is to file the grid connection early and treat the charger as part of the vehicle purchase rather than a separate afterthought. With the depot ready, the TCO case for the TE8L on the Tema-Accra corridor is robust across a wide range of price moves, and the fleet owner sleeps without fuel-price anxiety.

The corridor itself is unusually forgiving for an electric debut. At roughly 25 km per leg the TE8L never approaches its 250 km ceiling, so range anxiety, the most common objection to electric trucks, simply does not apply on this lane. Operators can therefore prove the economics on familiar, low-risk local work before extending electric tractors to longer national routes, which de-risks the whole transition for a conservative Ghanaian haulier ready to modernise the fleet.
