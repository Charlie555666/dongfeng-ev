The Railway Construction Cycle

SGR earthworks and ballast haulage runs fixed loops between borrow pits, crushers and the rail formation - typically 8-20 km on site tracks and short public-road sections. A TZ3V on a 12 km loop completes 6-8 cycles per shift; measured consumption on comparable East African infrastructure runs 3.2-3.7 kWh/km loaded with the empty return partly recovered by regeneration on the corridor's rolling terrain. The 600 kWh pack covers a full 9-10 hour shift with one 40-minute midday top-up - and on many sections the shift completes without it.

Linear projects like railways have a unique infrastructure advantage: the work front moves, but the batching plants, crushers and camps - the natural charger sites - move on a planned schedule. Charging infrastructure rolls forward with the project rather than being stranded by it.

Site Charging on a Moving Front

The working model: containerized 240 kW DC chargers mounted on skids at the crusher and camp, relocatable by low-loader as the front advances. Grid connections at permanent installations - stations, major camps - anchor the pattern; mobile units bridge between them. Tanzanian grid extension along the SGR corridor is itself part of the national program, which means site power improves as the railway builds. The discipline that matters: charge booking at the crusher, so trucks top up during loading queues rather than stopping for energy alone.

  • Mount chargers on skids from day one - the project will move, and the infrastructure must move with it.
  • Charge during crusher and excavator queues; queuing time is free charging time.
  • Track kWh per cubic meter hauled; ballast duty should hold 1.1-1.4 kWh/m3-km on short loops.
  • Keep the weekly filter service sacred - ballast dust is fine, alkaline and relentless.

Climate and Altitude on the Corridor

The SGR route crosses coastal humidity, the central plateau at 1,000-1,400 m, and lake-zone heat - a full climatic range in one project. The TZ3V's liquid-cooled powertrain holds performance across all of it; the plateau sections actually improve range through cooler running and regen-friendly grades, while the coastal sections demand the 8-10% hot-and-humid range allowance. Rain season trafficability on unmade tracks is the seasonal variable that moves consumption most; plan energy budgets by season, not by annual average.

Contractor Economics

Tanzanian diesel pricing and site delivery costs put the energy saving at 60-70% per hour worked; maintenance on the electric drivetrain halves the diesel figure, and regen braking removes the brake-wear line that dominates descending-haul maintenance. On a multi-year SGR phase with 10-15 trucks, operating savings reach project-changing scale. Financing for Infrastructure Contractors

Infrastructure contractors acquire against the project cash flow, and EV fleet structures follow the same logic. Outright purchase suits contractors holding multi-year sections where the fuel saving compounds longest. Lease structures through East African finance partners convert the capital premium into a monthly line the fuel saving covers from month one - the pattern that suits contractors whose sections turn over faster.
