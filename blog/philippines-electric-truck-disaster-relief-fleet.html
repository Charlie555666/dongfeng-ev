Electric Trucks for Philippines Disaster Relief Fleets

Why LGUs Are Adding the Electric Truck to Relief Fleets

The Philippines faces an average of twenty tropical cyclones a year, and local government units (LGUs) are discovering that an electric truck is a powerful asset for disaster relief. Unlike a diesel vehicle that needs a fuel supply which itself may be cut off, an EV truck can be pre-charged and held in reserve, then deployed the moment a typhoon passes. The KT5J electric cargo truck is a flexible platform for relief work because it carries supplies, tows equipment and can export power to a blacked-out barangay.

For an LGU, the electric truck is not only cleaner but more resilient: it removes the relief fleet's dependence on diesel logistics that fail precisely when roads are blocked and depots are flooded.

Silent Power and Mobile Charging

A standout feature of an electric truck in relief work is vehicle-to-load (V2L) export. The KT5J can feed its battery into lights, radios, medical fridges and phone-charging points for a blacked-out community. This turns the truck into a mobile power station that needs no separate generator or fuel.

  • Pre-charge the truck to 100 percent before a storm makes landfall.
  • Deploy it to the worst-hit barangay as a 30 kW power source.
  • Use the flatbed for water, rice and medical kits.
  • Run a 240 kW DC charger from a restored grid to refill between runs.
  • Keep the sealed chassis clear of deep floodwater above the axle line.

Readiness Planning for LGUs

Relief readiness is mostly planning. An LGU should designate a charged truck, a trained driver-operator, and a staging point above the flood line. Charging should be on a protected circuit with a backup, because the whole point is to operate when the grid is down. Coordination With Barangay Councils

Relief works best when the truck arrives where it is needed, so the LGU should pre-map barangays by risk and assign the KT5J to the worst-hit after a storm. The council chair receives the vehicle's power and cargo, and a single operator runs both the supply drop and the mobile charging point. Because the electric truck is quiet, it can operate at night without adding stress to a community already coping with disaster, and its sealed powertrain is less likely to fail after wading through flooded access roads that would stall a diesel intake.

Flood and Weather Preparedness

The KT5J's battery sits low and sealed, giving it better water fording than a diesel with a high air intake, but operators still respect flood depth and keep the axle line as the limit. Before a named storm, the LGU charges the truck to full and parks it above the expected water line. After the storm, it leads the response with lights, power and supplies while roads are still clearing.
