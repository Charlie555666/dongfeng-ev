Weight Enforcement Is Tightening Everywhere

Axle-load enforcement is tightening across emerging markets, and the era of the routinely overloaded truck is closing. Weigh-in-motion sensors on toll roads, portable scales at checkpoints and fines scaled to the overload percentage have changed the economics: an overloaded truck is no longer profitable margin, it is a fine schedule, a delayed delivery and a black mark with the enforcement database. For electric truck buyers this shift is favorable news, because the TZ3V electric 8x4 dump truck was engineered to its rated loads from the start - the 600 kWh battery is integrated in the chassis weight calculation, axle ratings are documented, and what you specify is what the weighbridge sees. What the Battery Weighs - and What It Does Not

The first weight question every buyer asks: how much payload does the battery cost? The honest answer is specific rather than alarming. The TZ3V's 600 kWh pack adds roughly 2.5-3.5 tonnes against a diesel equivalent - but the diesel truck carries its own offsetting mass in engine, gearbox, exhaust aftertreatment and 300-400 liters of fuel, so the net tare difference typically lands at 1.5-2.5 tonnes depending on configuration. Against a legal GCW, that is payload capacity, and it must be planned honestly - but several jurisdictions now grant electric trucks an axle-load or gross-weight allowance specifically to offset battery mass, recognizing the policy value of the transition. In Tanzanian markets, our team confirms the current allowance position at quotation, because it changes the payload math in the buyer's favor where it applies.

Axle Loads: Where the Weight Actually Sits

Legal compliance is measured per axle, not just gross, and here the electric truck's architecture is an advantage. The battery pack mounts low and between the axles, distributing its mass across the chassis rather than concentrating it - the TZ3V's axle load distribution at rated payload sits comfortably within front and rear axle ratings, with the balance documented in the homologation papers. The practical discipline is load placement: heavy cargo positioned against the headboard loads the drive axles; positioned at the tail, it unloads the steer. Loading discipline is a warehouse training topic, not an engineering problem - and telematics with axle-load estimation removes the guesswork, flagging loads that would fail the bridge before the truck leaves the yard. Fleets that load by data rather than by eye stop seeing fines entirely.

Charging Planning for TZ3V Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TZ3V supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 600 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Tanzania should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
