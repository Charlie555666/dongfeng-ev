Peak Shaving and Demand Charges: Cutting Electric Truck Depot Power Bills

An TE9L electric tractor sitting in your depot is not just a vehicle, it is a large, flexible electrical load. Managed well, depot charging becomes a tool to cut power bills through peak shaving and demand charge control. For EV truck fleets, the biggest savings often come not from cheaper electrons but from avoiding the peak demand charge your utility levies per kilowatt of highest monthly draw. This guide shows how load curves, buffer batteries and smart charging software lower depot energy cost without touching the trucks themselves.

What Are Demand Charges and Why They Hurt Fleets

Most commercial tariffs bill two ways: energy in kilowatt-hours and demand in dollars per kilowatt of your single highest 15-minute draw each month. A fleet that plugs in twenty trucks at once can spike demand to 1 MW or more, and that peak is billed every month regardless of average use. Demand charges of $10 to $30 per kW per month mean a 1 MW spike adds $10,000 to $30,000 monthly before a single kWh is counted. Peak shaving keeps that spike low by spreading or buffering charging load across the day and night.

  • Demand charge: $/kW of monthly peak draw
  • Energy charge: $/kWh consumed
  • Time-of-use: higher rates in evening peak
  • Power factor and connection fees also apply

Reading Your Depot Load Curve

Start by logging your site load for a month: base load from offices and workshop plus the charging curve when trucks arrive. The EV truck charging hump usually lands in the evening when grid rates and demand windows are worst. Plotting the curve reveals the avoidable peak. A 50-truck depot charging at 120 kW each could draw 6 MW unmanaged; a managed plan flattens that into a steady overnight draw that never trips the demand threshold, protecting the fleet from five-figure monthly penalties.

The same load curve tells you when cheap off-peak energy is available, which is when you should fill both the trucks and the buffer battery. Many depots find their true peak is not the trucks at all but a coincidence of workshop machinery and charging starting at the same hour. Shifting charging by even two hours can halve the demand charge with no extra hardware beyond a scheduler.

Battery Buffers: 500 kWh to 1 MWh Stationary Storage

A stationary buffer battery of 500 kWh to 1 MWh charges from the grid during off-peak hours and discharges to trucks during the peak window. This lets you charge vehicles at high power without pulling that power from the utility at the wrong moment. The buffer also doubles as backup for essential loads during outages. Sized right, it pays back through avoided demand charges and arbitrage between off-peak and peak energy rates, especially where the spread between the two is wide and the demand tariff is harsh.

Smart Charging Software and Scheduling

Smart charging software reads the tariff, the buffer state and each truck's departure time, then allocates power so the fleet is ready without breaching the site demand cap. It can pause a charger when another truck plugs in, or slow charging during the utility peak and resume at midnight. For mixed fleets the software prioritizes vehicles with the earliest shifts. The result is the same charged fleet each morning at a materially lower monthly bill, with no driver behavior change required.

Finally, treat the data as a living asset: store a year of interval logs so you can prove the saving to management and to any green-finance lender who wants verified demand reduction. The same dataset helps you size the next expansion accurately instead of guessing. Fleets that measure consistently keep finding new peaks to shave, so the 20 to 40 percent saving tends to grow rather than erode as the operation matures and more trucks join the yard over time.

Real Savings: 20 to 40 Percent Off the Power Bill

Depots that combine a buffer battery, throttled chargers and tariff-aware software typically cut total electricity cost by 20 to 40 percent versus unmanaged charging. The saving scales with how harsh the demand charge and how concentrated the unmanaged peak was. A fleet paying $50,000 monthly can recover $10,000 to $20,000, often enough to amortize a 1 MWh buffer inside two to three years. Solar Pairing and Extra Upside

Adding 100 to 300 kWp of rooftop solar lets the buffer fill from free daytime energy instead of off-peak grid power, pushing savings toward the top of the 20 to 40 percent range. In sunny markets the solar-plus-buffer combination can cover a large share of daytime charging and still shave the evening peak. Even without solar, the buffer alone delivers most of the demand-charge saving, so it is the first upgrade any EV truck depot should consider after the chargers themselves.

Building Your Peak-Shaving Depot Plan

The single most common mistake is buying chargers first and metering never, which leaves the avoidable peak invisible and the demand charge unpaid. Invert the order: meter, model, then procure, and you will usually find the buffer and scheduler deliver most of the saving before any hardware expansion. Keep the design modular so each new truck simply adds a charger and a slice of buffer rather than a redesign of the whole yard. Document the tariff and re-run the model whenever the utility changes rates, because the optimal strategy shifts with the price of peak power. Operators who treat depot energy as a managed asset, not a fixed bill, consistently report the 20 to 40 percent saving landing and, just as importantly, staying, because the system keeps working unattended after the payback is banked. That durability of saving is what separates a one-time efficiency project from a permanent cost advantage on every electric truck you add.

Step-by-Step Depot Audit

Start the audit by exporting one month of interval meter data from your utility. Mark the highest 15-minute demand and the time of day it occurred, then overlay your truck arrival and charging schedule. The gap between the two is your avoidable peak. If charging lands on the same hour as workshop load, shifting it by two hours may be the cheapest win you will ever find, requiring only a scheduler and no new hardware at all to capture.

Next, model a 500 kWh to 1 MWh buffer against your demand charge rate. Multiply the avoided peak in kW by the $/kW monthly rate and by twelve to get annual saving, then compare with the buffer's installed cost. In most commercial tariffs the payback is two to three years, after which the buffer is pure margin. Add solar later if roof space allows, but the buffer alone delivers the bulk of the saving and de-risks every future truck you add to the site.

Begin with a month of metering, size the buffer to cover your avoidable peak, and choose chargers your site supply can actually support. Add energy management software before scaling truck count, and revisit the plan each time tariff rules change. Peak shaving turns an EV truck depot from a demand-charge liability into a controlled, predictable cost center that improves as your fleet grows, rather than a bill that grows faster than the trucks.
