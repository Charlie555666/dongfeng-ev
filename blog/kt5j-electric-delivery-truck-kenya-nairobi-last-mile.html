Rounds are 80 to 150 kilometers, vans return to the depot every night, and stop-start traffic lets regenerative braking recover energy continuously. Payload and Body for Parcel Work

The KT5J carries 5,000 kilograms in a 22 cubic meter box, sized for the mixed parcel and FMCG loads typical of Nairobi rounds. A roller shutter or tail lift handles 60 to 100 drops per day, and the low cargo floor made possible by the under-chassis battery speeds loading at the hub. The 180 kW motor gives brisk traffic-light acceleration even fully loaded, which drivers value on Nairobi's aggressive urban roads.

Body options on the same chassis include curtainside, refrigerated, and open flatbed variants, so a courier can standardize one platform across parcel, fresh-food, and general cargo contracts.

Range and Charging for Daily Rounds

On the urban cycle the KT5J returns 200 to 240 kilometers per charge, comfortably covering a full Nairobi round with margin for detours and idle time at delivery points. Overnight depot charging on Kenya Power's off-peak tariff is sufficient for most fleets, with a 120 kW DC charger restoring 20 to 80 percent in about 70 minutes for double-shift days.

  • Charge overnight at the depot on off-peak tariffs.
  • One 120 kW charger serves four to five trucks with staggered rounds.
  • Use regen-heavy mode in dense districts to stretch range.
  • Sequence drops with route software to cut empty kilometers.
  • Reserve fast charging for double-shift peak seasons.

Cost per Parcel Versus Diesel

A diesel light truck on Nairobi last-mile duty burns 12 to 16 liters per 100 kilometers in stop-start traffic, while the KT5J uses about 0.8 kWh per kilometer.
