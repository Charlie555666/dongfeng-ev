Electric Trucks for Malaysia Palm Oil Plantations: KT5J Field Review

Why Estates Are Testing the Electric Truck for Fruit Haulage

Malaysia's palm oil estates run dozens of trips a day moving fresh fruit bunches from the field to the mill, and the KT5J electric cargo truck is emerging as a credible electric truck for this repetitive estate duty. Because fruit must reach the mill within 24 hours of harvest, reliability and predictable range matter more than top speed. The KT5J electric cargo truck offers a closed, low-emission way to move tonnes of bunches without adding diesel smoke to the processing area.

Estate managers like the EV truck because the powertrain is simple and the torque suits the soft, rutted estate roads. With no engine heat soak and no exhaust near the mill intake, the KT5J also fits the sustainability reporting that buyers of certified palm oil now demand.

Range on Estate Roads

Estate roads are unpaved, undulating and often muddy, which raises energy use compared with sealed highways. Even so, the KT5J's 240 km real range covers the daily bunch-haul circuit for most mid-size estates. Regenerative braking on the return run, where the truck is lighter, recovers useful energy. For larger estates, a midday top-up on a 240 kW DC charger extends effective daily coverage.

  • Plan one charge per shift for loops under 200 km of mixed track.
  • Use a 240 kW DC charger at the mill for a 70-minute 80 percent top-up.
  • Rotate two battery packs via swap if the estate runs continuous harvesting.
  • Keep tire pressure high to reduce rolling resistance on soft laterite.
  • Train drivers to read state-of-charge against the loop distance.

Charging Setup at the Mill

The logical place to charge is the mill, which already has three-phase power for processing. A single 240 kW DC unit serves four to six KT5J trucks on a rotating overnight schedule, and solar or biogas generation at the mill can offset charging cost. Fruit Quality and Mill Throughput

Palm fruit must reach the mill within roughly 24 hours or free fatty acid rises and the crop is downgraded. The KT5J's reliability matters here: a diesel truck that breaks down on a remote estate track can spoil a full load, while the simpler electric drivetrain has fewer failure points. Operators report that predictable charging and the steady torque of the LvKong motor keep the daily bunch run on schedule, which protects both mill throughput and the premium paid for certified, high-quality crude palm oil.

Comparing the KT5J to Other Estate Options

Estates can choose a converted diesel retrofit, a new diesel truck or a purpose-built EV truck like the KT5J. Retrofits are cheap but inherit the old chassis' wear and emissions. New diesels are reliable but expose the group to fuel price swings. The KT5J sits in the middle on purchase price yet wins on operating cost and sustainability reporting, which is increasingly a condition of export sales to European buyers. For groups with mill power already on site, the electric option is the natural fit.

Buyers of RSPO and similar certified palm oil now ask for scope-three transport emissions, and an electric truck with mill-charged renewable power scores far better than a diesel haul. The KT5J lets a group report zero tailpipe emissions on the estate leg, which strengthens the sustainability dossier used in buyer audits.
