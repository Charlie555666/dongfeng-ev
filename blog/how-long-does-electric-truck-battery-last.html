How Long Does an Electric Truck Battery Really Last? EV Truck Pack Life, Warranty and Replacement Cost

Quick answer: A CATL LFP battery in a Dongfeng electric truck is warranted for 8 years or 4,500 full charge cycles to 80% state of health. In real fleet service that means 10–15 years and roughly 1.0–1.5 million km before range falls enough to matter. Most fleets replace the truck, not the pack.

Battery life is the first question every fleet buyer asks about an EV truck, and it is the right question: the pack is the single largest cost line in the vehicle. This article answers it with the numbers we actually contract on — CATL LFP chemistry, 4,500-cycle warranty terms, measured degradation rates across our export fleet, and what a replacement really costs in 2026. It also explains why the "battery dies in five years" myth persists, and how duty cycle, climate and charging behaviour shift the answer by years.

What Does the Warranty Actually Promise?

Every Dongfeng EV truck we export carries a CATL LFP pack with an 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty to 80% state of health (SOH). Two limits apply in parallel — you hit whichever comes first:

Warranty elementTermWhat it means in practice
Calendar term8 years from deliveryCovers low-utilisation fleets that cycle rarely
Cycle term4,500 full equivalent cyclesCovers high-utilisation fleets that cycle daily or twice daily
Capacity floor80% SOHBelow this the pack is remediated or replaced
ChemistryLithium iron phosphate (LFP)Thermally stable, cobalt-free, deep-cycle tolerant
Usable windowFull nominal kWh minus reserveBMS holds a buffer to protect cell life

A "full equivalent cycle" counts cumulative energy, not plug events: two half charges equal one cycle. A truck using 80% of a 400 kWh pack per day accumulates 0.8 cycles per day, or about 292 per year — meaning the cycle limit alone allows over 15 years of that duty.

How Many Kilometres Is That in Real Driving?

Translate cycles into distance using measured consumption. Our tractors run 1.25–1.45 kWh/km at 49–80 t GCW; 6x4 dump trucks run 1.5–1.9 kWh/km laden on haul roads; light box trucks run 0.55–0.75 kWh/km in city duty. Working the numbers:

  • TE8M electric tractor — 600 kWh pack, ~300 km usable range, 1.45 kWh/km on heavy bulk duty: 4,500 cycles ≈ 1,150,000 km.
  • KTH3 electric cargo truck — 400 kWh, ~280 km regional, 1.0 kWh/km: ≈ 1,260,000 km.
  • KT5M electric box truck — 262/310 kWh, ~250 km urban, 0.62 kWh/km: ≈ 1,050,000 km at 310 kWh.
  • TZ3V electric mining dump truck — 600 kWh, short high-load cycles, 2.4 kWh/km: ≈ 690,000 km, but mining trucks are often replaced on body and chassis life well before that.

A long-haul diesel tractor is typically retired at 1.0–1.6 million km. In other words, the battery is not the life-limiting component on most duty cycles — the chassis, cab and body are.

What Actually Degrades an EV Truck Battery?

Degradation is not uniform. Four variables dominate, and three of them are within the operator's control:

  1. Charge rate. Sustained high-C charging raises cell temperature and accelerates SEI growth. Dual-gun GB/T charging at 600 A (20–80% in roughly 40 minutes) is safe at design limits, but a fleet that DC-charges twice daily will sit at the lower end of the cycle-life band versus one that AC-charges overnight. The practical difference is roughly 10–15% of total life over a decade.
  2. Depth of discharge. LFP tolerates deep cycles far better than NMC, but routinely running 5%→100% is harder on cells than 20%→90%. Our BMS already reserves a buffer; fleet managers should still avoid parking trucks at 0% over weekends.
  3. Ambient temperature. Ch cells age faster above ~45 °C. Our packs use liquid thermal management with active cooling calibrated for 45–50 °C Gulf and West African ambient, which is why we specify tropical cooling packs for African and Middle East shipments. Cold is less damaging to LFP but temporarily reduces usable power — and pre-conditioning on grid power before departure recovers most of it.
  4. Calendar time. Even unused, cells age slowly, roughly 1–2% capacity per year. This is why the 8-year calendar term exists.

Why LFP and Not NMC for an Electric Truck?

We specify LFP across the Dongfeng commercial range for three reasons that map directly onto fleet economics:

  • Cycle count. LFP cells routinely deliver 4,000–6,000 deep cycles versus 2,000–3,000 for typical NMC truck cells.
  • Safety. LFP thermal runaway onset is far higher, which simplifies depot fire-safety engineering and insurance underwriting — a real consideration when you are permitting a charger bank.
  • Cost and supply. Iron and phosphate are cheap and geographically widespread; LFP pack pricing has fallen steadily, which is why replacement cost estimates should be treated as a declining curve, not a fixed number.

What Does Replacement Cost, and Will You Ever Pay It?

Budget USD 110–160 per kWh installed for a current-technology LFP pack in 2026. A 400 kWh pack replacement therefore lands at roughly USD 45,000–64,000 including labour, and a 600 kWh mining pack at USD 66,000–96,000. Three things reduce that exposure:

  • Warranty. If SOH falls below 80% within 8 years or 4,500 cycles, remediation is contractual, not a fleet cost.
  • Module-level repair. Packs are built from serviceable modules. A single weak module is replaced, not the whole pack — typically 5–12% of full pack cost.
  • Residual and second life. A pack retired from traction at 80% SOH still has 10+ years of stationary storage value. See our analysis of second-life battery value and EV truck residual values, and our guide to SOH diagnostics for resale certification.

How Do You Measure Battery Health Before It Becomes a Problem?

Fleet operators should pull pack data quarterly: module voltage spread, internal resistance trend, cumulative kWh throughput, and the BMS-reported SOH figure. A pack losing more than 3% SOH per year, or showing widening cell-voltage spread, warrants a diagnostic session. Our telematics export makes that data available remotely, and it is what turns a warranty claim from an argument into a data-backed service ticket. For buyers evaluating used units, the same data is the basis of a battery health certificate.

So: How Long Does an EV Truck Battery Last?

Long enough that the battery should not be the reason you retire the truck. On our export fleet, working duty cycles put LFP packs at 10–15 years and 1.0–1.5 million km, with a contractual floor of 80% SOH at 8 years or 4,500 cycles behind them. The cases where batteries fail early are almost always charging-strategy or thermal-management failures, not chemistry failures — which is why we spend as much time on depot charger design as on the truck specification itself. Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an electric truck battery last?

A CATL LFP pack in a Dongfeng EV truck is warranted for 8 years or 4,500 charge cycles to 80% state of health — in typical fleet duty that is 10–15 years of service, roughly 900,000–1,500,000 km, before capacity drops below the useful threshold.

How many kilometres can an EV truck battery cover before replacement?

Around 1.2–1.5 million km. A 400 kWh pack at 1.3 kWh/km delivers about 300 km per full cycle; 4,500 cycles therefore equates to roughly 1.35 million km before reaching 80% SOH.

What does an EV truck battery replacement cost?

Budget USD 110–160 per kWh for a current-technology LFP pack, so a 400 kWh replacement lands at USD 45,000–64,000 including labour — though cell prices have fallen every year, and most fleets never reach replacement inside a 10-year ownership window.

Does fast charging damage an electric truck battery?

Regular high-rate DC charging accelerates degradation modestly. Dual-gun GB/T charging at 600 A adds roughly 0.01–0.02% extra capacity loss per session versus overnight AC, so fleets that opportunity-charge daily should plan for the lower end of the cycle-life range.

What is battery state of health and why does 80% matter?

State of health is usable capacity as a percentage of new. 80% is the industry warranty floor because below that daily range falls short of most duty cycles — a 300 km truck becomes a 240 km truck, which breaks route planning.
