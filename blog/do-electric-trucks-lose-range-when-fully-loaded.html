Why Electric Trucks Handle Heavy Loads Better Than Expected

Three characteristics make heavy duty friendlier to electric than the range arithmetic suggests. Instant torque: the TZ3Z's 400 kW motor delivers maximum torque from zero rpm - a loaded launch that stresses a diesel drivetrain is the electric motor's comfortable home, with no clutch slip and no gear hunting on grades. Regenerative braking: heavy loads descending return energy in proportion to their mass - the heaviest duty cycles, quarry and mountain work, recover 10-20% of route energy on the descents, which partially refunds the climb. And driveline durability: the electric drivetrain's wear is largely indifferent to load - no clutch, no gear teeth under shock loading, no engine lugging - where heavy duty accelerates every diesel wear item. Fleets in Nigeria running loaded 6x4 dump truck duty report the combination plainly: the electric truck feels engineered for the heavy days, because electrically, it is.

What About Hills, Heat and Payload Combined?

The honest stacking: full load plus mountain grades plus Nigerian heat is the toughest case, and it plans conservatively but predictably. Consumption at full load on sustained grades can reach 2.0-2.5 kWh per kilometer on the climb legs - but the descents return a meaningful share through regeneration, so route-net consumption lands far below the climb-only figure. Heat costs the pack's thermal management plus cab air conditioning, typically 5-12% of range at 40 degrees and above. Stacked honestly, the planning figure for heavy mountain duty in hot conditions is 60-75% of certified range: for the TZ3Z, a working day of 130-165 km per charge at full load in the mountains - which matches the actual duty these routes run, because loaded mountain haulage is short-haul by nature. Frequently Asked Questions

How much range does an electric truck lose when fully loaded?

Plan on 70-85% of certified range at full rated load: consumption rises from 0.8-1.0 to 1.4-1.8 kWh per km. A 600 kWh truck rated at 350 km plans for 250-300 km fully loaded - diesel trucks lose a similar percentage of fuel economy under the same load.

Does payload reduce electric truck acceleration or hill climbing?

No - electric motors deliver maximum torque from zero rpm, so loaded launches and grades are their strongest suit. A 510 kW heavy truck climbs fully loaded without gear hunting, and regenerative braking recovers 10-20% of route energy on the loaded descent.

Can an electric dump truck work a full day at maximum payload?

Yes on short-cycle duty: mining and quarry loops of 100-180 km daily fit a single charge at full load, with opportunity charging during loading queues extending further. The daily figure is set by cycle distance, not payload - heavy duty is short-haul by nature.

Is an electric truck's payload lower than a diesel truck's?

Slightly: the battery adds roughly 1.5-2.5 tonnes net against the diesel drivetrain it replaces. Several jurisdictions grant electric trucks weight allowances offsetting battery mass, and lightweight body options recover much of the difference in legal payload terms.

Which electric truck carries the heaviest load?

The 8x4 heavy variants: 600 kWh packs behind chassis rated to 80 tonnes GCW, planning 150-180 km per charge at full load in quarry and mining duty. Tractor models with 400-600 kWh packs pair full trailer ratings with 250-350 km of loaded range.
