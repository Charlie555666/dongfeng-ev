Understanding Kenya Import Duty on Electric Trucks

Electric trucks enter Kenya under HS heading 8704, and most customs authorities now classify battery-electric goods vehicles in dedicated subheadings. Import duty on a fully built electric truck typically runs between 0% and 35% depending on Kenya trade policy, with VAT or GST added on top of the duty-paid value. Some governments waive or reduce duty on zero-emission vehicles to hit climate targets, so the first step in any import plan is confirming the current EV concession with a licensed broker. When we quote the TE8L electric 6x4 tractor at an FOB range of $120,000-$150,000, the duty base in most countries is the CIF value: truck price plus ocean freight and insurance. Getting the classification right at this stage avoids re-assessment delays that can leave a truck sitting at the Port of Mombasa for weeks while storage charges accumulate.

Documents You Need for Kenya Customs Clearance

A clean customs file for an electric truck shipment normally includes the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and the vehicle conformity certificate. For Kenya, importers should also arrange PVoC certification before the vessel sails, because retroactive certification is slow and expensive. Battery documentation matters as much as vehicle paperwork: customs and the shipping line will ask for the UN38.3 test summary for the 350 kWh CATL LFP pack, plus an MSDS and a dangerous goods declaration where required. We prepare the full document pack from our side in Xi'an, including English originals and notarized translations where Kenyan authorities request them, so the broker in Nairobi can pre-lodge the entry before the ship arrives.

Homologation and Certification Requirements

Beyond customs, an electric truck must be approved for road use in Kenya. The process usually combines PVoC certification with a national type approval or registration step. Expect inspectors to check lighting, braking performance, VIN plate language, and increasingly the charging inlet standard. Dongfeng EV trucks ship with CCS2 inlets as standard, which matches the infrastructure rolling out across most export markets. Budget four to eight weeks for the certification stage in a typical Kenyan approval cycle, and start it while the truck is still in production rather than after arrival. Buyers who skip this sequencing routinely pay for it in port storage and demurrage fees that erase the price advantage of buying direct from China.

Shipping and Port Handling at the Port of Mombasa

Most electric trucks we export move as RoRo cargo, driven on and off the vessel under their own power, which suits a self-propelled 6x4 tractor like the TE8L. Flat-rack containers are the fallback for destinations without RoRo service. Because the truck carries a large lithium battery, the shipping line will require the state of charge limited to around 30-50% for ocean transit, and the battery disconnect procedure documented. On arrival at the Port of Mombasa, the consignee needs a licensed handler for any dangerous goods paperwork and a truck-friendly customs terminal. We coordinate directly with forwarders who handle EV cargo every week, so the TE8L is discharged, inspected and released without the learning-curve delays that hit first-time EV importers.

Total Landed Cost Worked Example

Fleet buyers should model landed cost, not just FOB price. Take the TE8L at an FOB range of $120,000-$150,000: add roughly $4,000-$9,000 for ocean freight to the Port of Mombasa depending on vessel schedule and season, around 1% for marine insurance, then apply Kenya import duty and VAT on the CIF base. Incentives change the math quickly - several Kenyan programs rebate duty on zero-emission commercial vehicles, and some ports offer priority handling for EV cargo. Even without incentives, the landed cost of an electric truck must be compared against five to eight years of diesel fuel and maintenance savings, not against the sticker price of a diesel truck. On high-utilization routes the electric option typically reaches cost parity inside three years.
