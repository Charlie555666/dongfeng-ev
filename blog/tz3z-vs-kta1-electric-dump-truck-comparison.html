TZ3Z vs KTA1 Electric Dump Truck Comparison: Two 8x4 EV Trucks, Two Different Philosophies

Both of these EV trucks wear 8x4 badges, both tip aggregate for a living, and both run CATL LFP battery packs with the same 8-year, 4,500-cycle warranty. Yet a fleet manager who orders the wrong one will feel the mistake every single shift. The Dongfeng TZ3Z is the heavy construction 8x4 — 55 t GCW, CATL 400 kWh, 510 kW-class drive — built to move maximum sand, stone and demolition on demanding sites. The KTA1 is the compact 8x4 — 31 t GVW, CATL 352 kWh — built for space-constrained city work and tight quarries where agility, tare weight and battery-swap compatibility matter more than raw tonnage. This comparison lays out the trade and gives you the decision rules we use when specifying fleets for export customers.

The price row rewards attention: the KTA1 runs roughly USD 20,000–25,000 cheaper per unit. With payload differing by about 8–10 tonnes, the capital cost per tonne of payload is broadly similar — meaning the choice is genuinely about duty cycle, not value. What differs is what the truck does with its tonnage: the TZ3Z spends it on heavy site robustness, the KTA1 on compactness and swap-compatible energy logistics.

Philosophy 1: The TZ3Z — Maximum Material, Demanding Sites

The TZ3Z's working day is the big construction project: earthworks for highways, dam and rail construction, large demolition removal, sand and gravel supply to batching plants. Its heavy rigid suspension and 55 t-class ratings absorb the punishment of loaded hauls over unmade ground; its 400 kWh pack covers the longer site-to-tipper-to-market radius these projects run; and its high-output drive motors keep a full body moving on ramped site accesses. The body itself is the heavy plate specification contractors expect for rock and demolition, and the whole machine is tuned for the reality that construction hauls get rougher, not smoother, as projects progress.

Philosophy 2: The KTA1 — The City and Tight-Quarry Specialist

The KTA1's working day is space-constrained: new-capital-city construction (we have placed units on exactly this duty in Southeast Asia), urban redevelopment with lane-width restrictions, inner-city utility trenching spoil removal, and small-to-medium quarries where the loading face is cramped. Its compact wheelbase and tighter turning circle let it work where a full-size 8x4 spends its day three-point-turning; its lighter tare means more legal payload on weight-restricted municipal roads; and its CAS battery-swap compatibility means a mixed KTA1 fleet can run continuous shifts with a single swap station — 5–6 minute pack exchanges replacing 40-minute charges. For operators whose constraint is time and space rather than tonnage, the KTA1 is the correct geometry.

Three Decision Rules

  • Rule 1 — Follow the road law. If your routes enforce 31 t-class GVW limits (most urban Asia, many municipal roads globally), the TZ3Z's capability is unusable; order KTA1. If your sites are private or permit heavy rigid operation, TZ3Z payload wins.
  • Rule 2 — Follow the cycle geometry. Cycles under 15 km with tight turning = KTA1 territory. Cycles 15–60 km over rough site roads with full bodies = TZ3Z territory.
  • Rule 3 — Follow the energy plan. If you intend to run 24-hour shifts with battery swap as the backbone, the KTA1's swap compatibility is a structural advantage; if you charge overnight at a depot and run day shifts, both work and the choice reverts to Rules 1 and 2.

What They Share

Both trucks carry CATL LFP chemistry (thermal stability at 45 °C+ ambient, 4,500+ cycles), liquid-cooled pack thermal management, regenerative braking that triples brake life on tipper duty, insulated HV monitoring with emergency cut-off, and the same export package: UN 38.3 and IMDG documentation, pre-shipment inspection, commissioning with driver training on regen technique and high-voltage safety, and parts planning support. Both deliver the construction-site bonuses fleet owners report first: no diesel smell and noise at the loading face (69–72 dB(A) pass-by), no engine air filters choking on site dust, and no cold-start drama at dawn.

Final Word

Choose the TZ3Z when the job is maximum material over demanding construction ground at heavy ratings — USD 115,000–140,000 FOB, CATL 400 kWh, 55 t class. Choose the KTA1 when the job is compact, city-lawful, swap-friendly and throughput-driven — USD 95,000–115,000 FOB, CATL 352 kWh, 31 t GVW.
