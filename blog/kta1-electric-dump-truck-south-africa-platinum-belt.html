KTA1 Electric Dump Truck for South Africa's Platinum Belt: Mining Review

South Africa's platinum belt depends on heavy rigid trucks moving ore and rock between shafts and processing plants, and the KTA1 electric truck is stepping in as an 8x4 electric dump built for that duty. This electric tipper uses a 350 kWh liquid-cooled CATL LFP battery and a 360 kW LvKong motor to handle 8x4 payloads while returning 200-250 km of range on mine and plant roads. In this review we examine payload, gradient range, and the charging strategy South African fleets need in 2026.

Why the platinum belt is moving to electric dump trucks

Platinum operations run fixed, repetitive haul loops where an electric dump truck shines. The KTA1 recovers energy on every loaded descent and spends it climbing back, so the energy per cycle is far lower than a diesel tipper that wastes brake heat downhill. For South African mines managing diesel cost, engine downtime, and tightening emissions rules, the KTA1 EV truck converts a known loop into a predictable electric operation with lower lifetime cost.

KTA1 8x4 chassis, battery, and motor specification

The KTA1 is an 8x4 rigid built around a 350 kWh CATL LFP pack, liquid-cooled to manage heat on graded mine roads, paired with a 360 kW LvKong motor for strong takeoff under full tipper load. Dongfeng warrants the battery for 8 years or 4,500 cycles, which suits a high-utilisation mining truck. The 8x4 layout spreads axle load for rough access roads and gives the stability needed when tipping on uneven ground.

Payload and gradient range

The 360 kW LvKong motor delivers the low-speed torque an 8x4 tipper needs on mine grades, and the liquid-cooled pack sustains that output through heat. On working platinum-belt loops, operators report 200-250 km of range per charge at full tipper payload, enough for a full shift on most internal haul routes. Regenerative braking on the return descent is a major contributor, recovering energy that a diesel truck would lose as heat.

Charging strategy for mine fleets

The KTA1 supports DC fast charging at 120, 240, or 360 kW, reaching 20-80% in 45-90 minutes between shifts. Mine fleets typically install a 240 kW or 360 kW depot charger at the loadout or plant end and let trucks opportunity-charge during loading and breaks. Because the pack is LFP and liquid-cooled, the deep daily cycling the platinum belt demands is safe across the warranty window, and a single charger type can later service other Dongfeng models on site.

Operating cost versus diesel tippers

The FOB price of $90,000-$120,000 reflects the 8x4 chassis and 350 kWh pack, but the mine economics favour the EV truck. Diesel tippers burn the most fuel on the climbs they repeat all day, and engine maintenance on dusty sites is heavy. The KTA1 removes engine servicing, cuts brake wear through regen, and turns energy into a predictable grid cost. Over a multi-year haul contract those savings usually outweigh the higher sticker.

Financing and total cost of ownership modelling

South African mines should evaluate the KTA1 on a full haul-life basis rather than the $90,000-$120,000 FOB price by itself. The model needs to capture the avoided diesel and engine servicing on graded roads, the regen recovery on every descent, and the residual supported by the 350 kWh pack's 8-year LFP warranty. On a busy platinum loop the electric tipper often pays back within the contract window once the depot charging bank is amortised. Operators should also consider mining-finance products and any emissions-linked incentives that lower initial capital, and they should account for the battery as a managed, warrantied asset with a clear replace-or-repurposed path. That approach keeps the KTA1 financeable and the balance sheet transparent as the fleet converts from diesel. For finance teams the reassuring facts are the predictable energy line and the small number of wearing components, which together make the lifetime cost risk of an electric tipper lower than the sticker might suggest. Buyers should also factor import duty and any local-content rules into the comparison, since these can shift the relative position of electric and diesel options in either direction. Finally, the simplified drivetrain reduces the skill level needed for workshop staff and improves driver retention, two factors that protect the lifetime cost case once the trucks are in daily service.

Driver training and fleet onboarding

Introducing the KTA1 EV truck to a South African platinum operation begins with training tipper drivers on electric torque, regen braking on graded roads, and the charging window between shifts. The 8x4 layout and 360 kW LvKong motor feel familiar to operators used to heavy rigid trucks, so the learning curve is short, and the main operational change is planning the 240 kW or 360 kW depot charge around loading breaks. A phased rollout piloting units on the shortest internal loop lets the mine validate range at full tipper payload and prove the charging bank before the wider fleet converts, while also building maintenance crew familiarity with the electric drivetrain.

Residual value and warranty planning

The KTA1's 350 kWh LFP pack, warranted for 8 years or 4,500 cycles, anchors its residual value case. On a high-utilisation mine the pack will approach its cycle cap within the warranty, so operators should plan the replacement or second-life path in advance and account for the battery separately from the chassis. Securing service coverage for the LvKong motor and tipper hydraulics protects uptime, and the slow LFP degradation supports a structured trade-in. Managed this way, the electric tipper is a financeable mine asset rather than a speculative capital spend.

Spare parts and support readiness

Before the KTA1 enters mine service, operators should stock the consumables the LvKong drivetrain shares across the Dongfeng range and confirm a service partner for the motor, hydraulics, and charging bank. Because the electric tipper shares its pack and charger with sibling models, one spares kit covers much of the yard, which keeps workshop cost down and avoids downtime on a production-critical haul.

Conclusion

South Africa's platinum belt is well suited to electric haulage, and the KTA1 EV truck delivers with a 350 kWh pack, a 360 kW LvKong motor, and the 8x4 durability mining demands. With a fast-charge plan at the corridor ends and a service partner secured, it is a credible diesel replacement for fixed mine loops.
