Are Electric Trucks Good for Long-Distance Haulage?

Quick answer: Yes, on fixed corridors. A modern heavy electric truck with a 424 kWh CATL LFP battery covers 280 to 350 loaded kilometers per charge, so any route where trucks return to known depots, or where a charger can be staged mid-route, is electrifiable today. Open-ended long haul with unpredictable destinations is the segment that still favors diesel.

What Range Can a Heavy Electric Truck Actually Deliver

The honest number depends on load, terrain, and temperature. A 6x4 electric tractor such as the TE8P electric tractor at 49-ton GCW delivers 280 to 330 kilometers per charge on flat highway in warm conditions, rising toward 350 kilometers in mild weather and falling to roughly 240 to 260 kilometers in severe cold or on sustained mountain grades. These are production figures from CATL LFP packs of 424 kWh, not laboratory claims.

Regenerative braking changes the math on rolling terrain. On a corridor with long descents, a disciplined driver recovers 8 to 14 percent of outbound energy on the return leg, which effectively extends usable range without a larger battery.

How Do You Structure Charging on a Long Route

Long-distance electric haulage works on a hub-and-spoke charging model: a 240 kW DC fast charger at each depot restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes, and a mid-route opportunity charger handles legs beyond 300 kilometers. For fleets running continuous shifts with no 75-minute window, battery swap exchanges the pack in 5 to 6 minutes instead. The TE9L electric tractor is available in both fast-charge and swap specification for exactly this reason.

  • Legs under 300 km: charge at each end, no mid-route stop needed.
  • Legs of 300 to 500 km: one mid-route 75-minute top-up or a 6-minute swap.
  • Continuous two-shift operation: choose the swap variant.
  • Corridors with long descents: regen recovers 8 to 14 percent of energy.

Are Electric Trucks Cheaper Than Diesel on Long Routes

On corridors, yes, and the gap is large. A diesel tractor burns roughly 38 liters per 100 kilometers loaded; the electric equivalent uses about 1.3 kWh per kilometer. At typical industrial electricity tariffs the energy cost per kilometer falls by 60 to 75 percent, and maintenance adds further savings because the electric powertrain has roughly one-third the moving parts of a diesel. Over a 150,000 kilometer year, a corridor tractor commonly saves $25,000 to $35,000 in energy and service combined.

The FOB premium of $130,000 to $170,000 for a heavy electric tractor is recovered within three to four years at that utilization, and the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty extends well beyond the payback point. Which Routes Are Not Ready for Electric Yet

Be honest with the use case. Routes with unpredictable destinations, no depots, or legs beyond 500 kilometers through regions with no charging infrastructure are not electrifiable today without compromise. Cross-border routes through countries with unreliable grids also remain diesel territory for now. The practical strategy is to electrify the fixed corridors first, bank the savings, and extend as corridor chargers spread.
