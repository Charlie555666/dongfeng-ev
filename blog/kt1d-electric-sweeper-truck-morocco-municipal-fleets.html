Why Moroccan municipalities are electrifying street cleaning

Night and early-morning sweeping is a fact of life in dense Moroccan medinas and boulevards, but diesel sweepers wake residents and add exhaust to the very air cities are trying to clean. An electric sweeper removes both problems at once: it runs near-silently through residential streets and emits nothing at the curb. For city fleets facing tightening environmental targets, the KT1D EV truck is a visible, defensible investment that improves neighbourhood air quality while lowering the cost of each cleaning shift.

Shift range and route planning

On a typical city route with continuous sweeping, the KT1D delivers 200-260 km of travel per charge, which is more than enough for a full night of boulevard and medina cleaning. Because the brooms and fan draw from the same pack, route planning should balance road distance against sweep time: a slow, detailed pass consumes energy over time rather than distance. Operators report that a single overnight charge covers the busiest shift, and the liquid-cooled LFP pack holds capacity through repeated daily cycles.

Charging and depot infrastructure

The KT1D supports DC fast charging at 120, 240, or 360 kW, reaching 20-80% in 45-90 minutes. Most Moroccan depots can service it with an overnight 120 kW charger, while cities running multi-shift cleaning can add a 240 kW unit for a midday turnaround. The shared Dongfeng electric architecture means the same charger type can later service cargo and sprinkler variants, keeping depot build-out cost-efficient as the fleet expands.

Operating cost versus diesel sweepers

The FOB price of $68,000-$92,000 is competitive with diesel sweepers of similar class, but the running savings are the real story. There is no engine oil, no diesel exhaust aftertreatment, and far fewer moving parts in the LvKong drivetrain. Energy to run a full shift costs a fraction of the diesel a combustion sweeper burns while idling through a route, and brake wear drops thanks to regenerative braking in stop-start city driving.

Financing and total cost of ownership modelling

Moroccan cities evaluating the KT1D should model the purchase on a full lifecycle basis rather than the $68,000-$92,000 FOB price alone. The model needs to include the nightly energy draw, the eliminated diesel and exhaust-aftertreatment servicing, and the residual supported by the 8-year LFP warranty. For a municipality running daily cleaning, the avoided maintenance and quieter operation usually justify the switch within the first council term. Cities should also investigate national or EU-aligned clean-mobility grants and leasing structures that lower the initial outlay, and they should fold depot charging into the same budget so the operating line is stable. Treating the battery as a separately accounted, warrantied asset helps finance teams set a realistic trade-in or second-life value, which keeps the KT1D financeable across its whole service life. For procurement teams preparing a council business case, presenting the sweeper as a depreciable, low-running-cost asset rather than a novelty purchase is what unlocks approval and funds the next wave of electric cleaning vehicles. Buyers should also factor import duty and any local-content rules into the comparison, since these can shift the relative position of electric and diesel options in either direction. Finally, the simplified drivetrain reduces the skill level needed for workshop staff and improves driver retention, two factors that protect the lifetime cost case once the trucks are in daily service.

Driver training and fleet onboarding

Electrifying a Moroccan street-cleaning fleet with the KT1D EV truck is largely a scheduling change rather than a skills overhaul. Drivers adapt quickly to silent, single-pedal operation and to managing sweep width and fan speed from the cab, while supervisors shift their daily planning from fuel logs to charge and water checks. A sensible rollout pilots a few units on a defined boulevard and medina circuit, trains drivers on night-time regen braking and broom pressure, then scales across the city once the depot charging pattern is proven. Pilots also surface the best overnight charging windows that avoid peak grid tariffs.

Residual value and warranty planning

Municipal buyers planning a KT1D purchase benefit from the 262 kWh LFP pack's 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, which underpins predictable residual value. LFP degrades gently and the liquid cooling protects cells through hot summer nights, so the pack keeps working long after the warranty ends, with a credible second-life or trade-in path. Cities should budget the battery separately from the chassis, track cycles against the cap, and contract service for the LvKong driveline and suction system. With that accounting discipline, the electric sweeper becomes a standard depreciable fleet asset that councils can finance confidently over a full replacement cycle.
