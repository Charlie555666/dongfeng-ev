Constant-Pressure Spraying at Walking Speed

The KT3F's electric PTO decouples pump pressure from road speed, so the truck can creep along a haul road at 5 km/h while holding a full 60 cubic meters per hour across a 14-meter spray width. Diesel bowsers surge and fade with engine rpm, wasting water on some passes and under-wetting others; the KT3F's steady delivery cuts water consumption per hectare by roughly 15 percent while improving suppression consistency.

The 10,000 liter baffled stainless tank keeps the load stable on rough site roads, and a rear cannon handles stockpile and crusher feed-point suppression between road passes.

Range and Charging on Site

With the tank empty the chassis returns 220 to 260 kilometers of range; fully loaded on site duty a realistic figure is 150 to 180 kilometers, comfortably covering a full shift of dust runs. A 120 kW charger at the site office restores 20 to 80 percent in about 90 minutes during the midday break, and Morocco's cheap solar-heavy grid keeps the energy bill minimal.

  • Site a 120 kW charger at the project office for midday top-ups.
  • Pair two units so one sprays while the other charges or refills.
  • Use constant-pressure mode to cut water use per hectare.
  • Position refill points to minimize deadhead tank runs.
  • Run night suppression near residential zones without complaints.

Operating Cost Versus a Diesel Bowser

A diesel sprinkler burns 18 to 22 liters per hour of combined drive-and-pump duty, around $45 per shift at Moroccan diesel prices, while the KT3F draws about $11 of electricity for the same work. Over a 250-shift season that is roughly $8,500 saved on energy, before counting the elimination of oil, filter, and injector service. The sealed LvKong motor also escapes the dust ingestion that shortens diesel life on dry Moroccan sites.

The FOB price of $60,000 to $85,000 is recovered within about two seasons of continuous dust work, and the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty covers the full length of a typical infrastructure contract.
