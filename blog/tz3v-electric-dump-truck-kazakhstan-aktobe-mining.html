Why Aktobe Mining Needs a Heavy Electric Dump Truck

Aktobe oblast hosts iron ore and other mineral operations where haul trucks run constant shuttles between the working face and the processing plant, often inside a confined site with poor air exchange. Diesel dump trucks fill that pit with exhaust and noise, raising ventilation cost and operator health risk. The TZ3V's electric drivetrain removes in-pit emissions entirely and delivers full torque from standstill, ideal for the loaded climb out of a pit.

The 8x4 configuration and 31 tonne payload match the mid class mining tipper duty, and the 600 kWh pack is the largest in the Dongfeng electric dump range, giving the reserve needed for repeated climbs and for cabin and battery heating in deep winter. For Kazakh mine operators facing both fuel cost and ESG pressure from international offtakers, the TZ3V is a practical first step to electrified haulage.

Charging and Cold Weather Operation

Mine sites can host their own high power DC charging, and a 360 kW to 480 kW unit restores 20 percent to 80 percent in 45 to 90 minutes during the truck's staged break, or a battery swap station can turn that into a 5 to 6 minute pit stop. With Kazakhstan electricity priced around USD 0.05 to 0.18 per kWh, a full charge costs roughly USD 30 to 108, against USD 150 to 260 of diesel for a comparable tipper shift of repeated haul cycles.

For fleets near the Kazakhstan electric truck market hubs around Aktobe and Almaty, the cold weather package is essential. The TZ3V keeps the battery in a safe temperature band with pre heating before departure and recovers heat from the drivetrain to warm the cabin, preserving range when the thermometer falls to minus 30 deg C.
