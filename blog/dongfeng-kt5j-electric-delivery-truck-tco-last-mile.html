Last-Mile TCO: Dongfeng KT5J Electric Delivery Truck vs Diesel Over 8 Years

Last-mile distribution is a margin business measured in fractions of a dollar per drop. Every fleet director we meet asks the same question in different words: over the whole life of the vehicle, does the electric delivery truck cost less than the diesel one it replaces? This article answers it for the Dongfeng KT5J — the 5,000 mm-wheelbase, tight-street member of our electric cargo family — with a transparent eight-year model you can rebuild with your own prices. No hidden subsidies, no optimistic assumptions, all figures from our fleet deployments.

The Vehicle

The KT5J is a 4x2, 18 t GVW electric delivery truck with a 65 m³ body on a short 5,000 mm wheelbase, CATL 262 or 310 kWh LFP battery, and the LvKong 150/270 kW e-axle (342 peak horsepower). It exists for routes that punish long vehicles: old-town cores, narrow industrial lanes, residential delivery loops. FOB pricing typically runs USD 78,000–100,000 depending on battery option and body specification. The diesel comparator is a conventional 18 t city delivery truck at USD 42,000–52,000 FOB with equivalent body.

The KT5J finishes roughly USD 32,000 — 19% — cheaper to own over eight years, despite a purchase premium of USD 45,000. The crossover arrives in year 4 on this duty profile; every kilometre after that is margin. Where commercial electricity is cheaper than USD 0.11/kWh — much of Africa with solar-backed depots, the Middle East, and Asia — the crossover moves earlier.

Sensitivity: What Moves the Answer

  • Annual distance is the master variable. At 25,000 km/year the crossover stretches past year 6; at 50,000 km/year (double-shift last-mile, common in contract logistics) it arrives inside year 3. Electric trucks pay for utilisation — put them on your busiest routes.
  • Diesel price. At USD 0.70/L (subsidised markets) the electric advantage narrows to roughly break-even at 8 years; at USD 1.30/L (island and remote markets) the KT5J wins before year 3.
  • Battery health. The CATL LFP pack carries the 8-year/4,500-cycle warranty. On a single-shift 40,000 km/year profile the pack sees about 1,500 cycles in 8 years — well inside warranty, and at end-of-life retains 70–80% capacity with second-life stationary storage value that our recycling partners monetise.
  • Night-access revenue. Many cities permit quiet electric vehicles into zones and hours closed to diesel. Two extra operating hours per day is worth more than the entire fuel saving — but it only appears if you schedule for it.

Hidden Items Fleets Forget to Count

  1. Driver retention. Quiet, vibration-free, clutchless work measurably reduces driver fatigue; in high-turnover last-mile labour markets this has cash value.
  2. Fire and housekeeping risk. No diesel storage, no spills, no fuel theft — the last of which quietly costs some operations 3–5% of fuel volume.
  3. Brand value. FMCG and e-commerce shippers increasingly audit carrier emissions; an electric fleet wins RFP scoring that never appears in a fuel spreadsheet.
  4. Insurance and access levies in some low-emission zones differ by drivetrain — check locally; it occasionally adds USD 500–1,500/year in the diesel column.

Verdict

On dense multi-drop duty above 40,000 km per year, the KT5J electric delivery truck is not an environmental indulgence — it is the cheaper asset, full stop.
