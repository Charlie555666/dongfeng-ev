Payload and Load Planning for Beverage Work

Beverages are heavy for their volume - a full load of packaged product sits near legal payload limits - which makes payload planning the first engineering conversation. The KT5J is rated with its 100 kWh battery integrated into the chassis, so payload calculations start from documented tare weight, and several jurisdictions grant zero-emission trucks weight allowances up to two tonnes that restore full parity with diesel. Load planning details that matter in beverage work: weight distribution for mixed pallet and keg loads, securing systems for the stop-start urban cycle, and body specification - curtainside, box or insulated - matched to the product mix. Depot Charging for Distribution Fleets

Beverage depots are ideal charging sites: trucks park overnight in a yard the company controls, three-phase power is typically present for plant or warehouse loads, and the daily rhythm - load evening, deliver day, return afternoon - creates natural charging windows. A fleet of five to fifteen electric trucks charges overnight on sequential scheduling, with one or two 60-120 kW DC chargers covering turnaround needs; the KT5J recovers 20-80% in about an hour when a second daily wave demands it. In Bogota, the long-lead item is the utility connection, not the chargers - start that conversation at project conception. Telematics-managed charging staggers sessions to keep peak demand inside the connection limit, which is the difference between an affordable upgrade and an expensive one.

Charging Planning for KT5J Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT5J supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 100 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Colombia should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
