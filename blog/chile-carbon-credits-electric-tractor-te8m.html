How Carbon Credits Work for Truck Fleets

A carbon credit represents one tonne of CO2 avoided or removed, verified by an accredited registry. Electric trucks generate credits by replacing diesel fuel burn with grid or renewable electricity. The mechanism is straightforward: measure the baseline diesel emissions your fleet would have produced, measure actual electricity consumption, apply the grid emission factor, and the difference - after leakage and uncertainty deductions - becomes issuable credits. For fleet operators in Chile, this turns a procurement decision into a revenue line. Credits sell into voluntary markets, compliance schemes where they exist, or directly to corporates with net-zero commitments who pay premiums for verified transport decarbonization.

Chile Carbon Market Landscape

Carbon market maturity varies sharply across regions, and Chile sits in a landscape where voluntary standards - Verra, Gold Standard and Article 6-aligned bilateral deals - do most of the work for transport projects. Local governments increasingly publish decarbonization roadmaps that signal future compliance demand, which is quietly lifting forward prices for verified credits. Fleet operators should register projects early, because methodologies reward early movers and additionality is easiest to prove when electric trucks are still rare on Chilean roads. Even where no liquid market exists yet, corporates buying logistics services now score tenders on scope-3 emissions, which monetizes the same reductions through contract wins instead of credit sales.
