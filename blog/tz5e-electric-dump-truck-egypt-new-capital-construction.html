The Desert Construction Cycle

New capital earthworks follow the classic mass-excavation pattern: cut face to fill area or spoil bank over 5-15 km of site track, with crushers and batching plants as fixed nodes. A TZ5E on a 10 km loop completes 7-9 cycles per shift; measured consumption on comparable desert infrastructure runs 3.0-3.5 kWh/km loaded with partial regen recovery on the return. A full 9-hour shift consumes 60-70% of the pack, and double-shift programs add one 45-minute charge at the changeover - the dual-gun hardware restores 60% in that window.

The desert location sharpens the diesel problem: fuel arrives by tanker to sites with no infrastructure, adding delivery cost and spill management to every litre. Site-generated or grid-fed electricity - the new capital's grid buildout is part of the project itself - arrives without a supply chain.

Site Power and Charging Layout

The new capital's construction grid is unusually good for a greenfield project - the utilities go in early by design. The working charger layout: one 240 kW dual-gun unit at the crusher or workshop per 5-6 trucks, sited where trucks queue anyway. For sections ahead of the grid, containerized chargers with generator bridging cover the gap, phased out as permanent power arrives. The overnight window at camp handles the base charge; the site units handle the double-shift top-up.

  • Anchor chargers at the crusher; queuing time is charging time on earthworks.
  • Budget summer shifts at 70% pack consumption; the desert heat allowance is 8-10%.
  • Track kWh per cubic metre; mass earthworks should hold 1.0-1.3 kWh/m3-km on short loops.
  • Weekly cooling-pack and filter service is non-negotiable through the dust season.

Summer Heat Management

Egyptian desert summers run 40-45 C with surface temperatures far higher - the sustained thermal case that separates engineered EV cooling from marketing. The TZ5E's liquid-cooled battery and motor hold output through afternoon heat, with the cab HVAC keeping drivers functional; the honest planning numbers are 8-10% summer range reduction and a midday charging preference so the hottest hours coincide with the plugged-in window. Night earthworks - common on schedule-driven sections - run cooler and longer per charge, a scheduling lever diesel does not offer.

Contractor Economics in Egypt

Egyptian diesel pricing after successive reforms makes the operating gap wide: a 65 t class diesel tipper on desert earthworks burns $35,000-45,000 of fuel per year on a heavy schedule; the TZ5E's electricity for the same work costs $9,000-12,000 even on partially generated power. Maintenance halves. Egypt's state-led construction sector increasingly writes emissions and fuel-security considerations into procurement - context covered on our Egypt electric truck market page - and the electric fleet removes a fuel supply chain that remote desert sections make expensive and fragile.
