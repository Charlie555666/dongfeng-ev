What Determines the FOB Price of an Electric Dump Truck?

Four variables move the quotation more than anything else:

  • Battery capacity. CATL LFP packs run from 400 to 600 kWh across the dump-truck line. Battery cost is roughly $60-90 per kWh installed, so the jump from 400 to 600 kWh alone moves the price by $18,000-$25,000.
  • Configuration. 6x4 versus 8x4, steel thickness of the tipper body (Hardox 450 versus standard Q355), cabin spec (air-conditioning, heated seats for high-altitude or cold markets), and suspension type all shift the number by $5,000-$15,000.
  • Motor and driveline. LvKong permanent-magnet motors from 282 to 510 kW are priced in steps; the 410-510 kW units fitted to the TZ3V and TE8P-class tractors carry a premium over the 282 kW units.
  • Order volume and spares package. A 10-truck order with a container of critical spares prices 3-6% lower per unit than a single-truck purchase, purely on factory allocation and freight efficiency.

What Does the Total Landed Cost Look Like?

FOB is only the first line of your budget. A realistic landed-cost model for a TZ3V shipped to a West African port looks like this: $150,000-$180,000 FOB, plus $8,000-$12,000 RoRo or flat-rack ocean freight, plus 5-20% import duty depending on the country (Nigeria and Ghana run 10-20% on CKD-unfriendly complete units, while Kenya and Ethiopia offer EV-specific exemptions that can drop duty to zero), plus VAT and clearing charges of $3,000-$6,000, plus a first charging station at $25,000-$45,000 for a 240 kW depot unit with installation. Note the charging station is a one-time fleet asset, not a per-truck cost — truck three, four and five plug into the same infrastructure.

Savvy buyers also negotiate the battery separately. On the TZ3V-class mining trucks, a battery-swap arrangement can strip the pack out of the truck price entirely, cutting capex by 30-40% in exchange for a per-swap energy fee. For high-utilisation quarries swapping two to three times per day, that often beats owning the battery; for construction fleets running one shift, owning is usually cheaper. Our team models both scenarios against your actual route data before quoting.

How Fast Does the Premium Pay Back?

A diesel 8x4 dump truck doing 250 km a day in West Africa burns roughly 85-95 litres of diesel at $1.10-$1.30 per litre — call it $100-$115 per day, or $30,000-$34,000 a year. The same duty on electricity, even at a conservative $0.15/kWh blended tariff with 1.4 kWh/km consumption, costs about $53 a day, or $16,000 a year. Add maintenance: the electric drivetrain eliminates injectors, turbo, clutch and exhaust aftertreatment, saving another $4,000-$6,000 a year. That is $18,000-$24,000 of annual savings against a $75,000-$100,000 premium — a 3-4 year simple payback, and 2-3 years wherever diesel is taxed hard or electricity is cheap. In Chile, where solar-powered sites charge trucks for under $0.08/kWh, we have seen the gap close in under two years.

Which Hidden Costs Should Importers Watch?

Three line items catch first-time importers off guard. First, payment structure: China Export & Credit Insurance-covered deals and confirmed L/Cs add 1-3% in bank and insurance fees, but they protect both sides — expect exporters with our volume to insist on them. Second, pre-shipment inspection: many countries (Nigeria's SONCAP, East Africa's PVoC, ECCAS certificates) require inspection booked before sailing; budget $500-$1,500 per consignment. Third, first-year spares: even though the electric drivetrain needs little, a $5,000-$8,000 opening order of wear items — tyres, brake pads, cabin filters, HV coolant service kits — is prudent so your fleet never waits on an ocean freight cycle. None of these are large; all of them are predictable. The unpredictability in electric dump truck pricing comes almost entirely from specifying the wrong battery and configuration for the duty cycle — which is exactly what our engineering questionnaire exists to prevent.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an electric dump truck cost from China?

Between $95,000 and $180,000 FOB in 2026: the 6x4 TZ5E with a 400 kWh CATL pack runs $95,000-$120,000, the KTA1 quarry spec $120,000-$150,000, and the 8x4 TZ3V with the 600 kWh battery $150,000-$180,000, before project discounts on volume orders.

Why is an electric dump truck more expensive than a diesel one?

The battery: a 400-600 kWh pack adds $35,000-$60,000 to the build. The premium repays in 2-4 years through $18,000-$35,000 of annual fuel and maintenance savings on typical quarry and construction duty — after which the electric truck is cheaper every year it runs.

What extra costs come on top of the FOB price?

Ocean freight of $6,000-$12,000 (RoRo or flat-rack), import duty of 5-25% depending on destination — or zero in EV-exempt markets like Kenya and Ghana — VAT and clearing of $3,000-$6,000, and a first 240 kW depot charger with installation at $25,000-$45,000, shared across the fleet rather than per truck.

Is battery swap cheaper than buying the battery with the truck?

For high-utilisation duty, often yes: buying the truck without its pack cuts capex 30-40% against a per-swap fee of roughly $0.35-$0.50 per kWh. Quarries running 5-6 minute swaps two to three times daily usually win on swap finance; single-shift construction fleets almost always do better owning the battery.

How many years does an electric dump truck take to pay back?

Three to four years on the FOB premium at 250 km/day against a diesel 8x4 — $25,000-$30,000 of annual fuel and maintenance savings on a $75,000-$100,000 premium — compressing to 2-3 years where electricity is cheap relative to diesel, such as Ethiopia, Kenya, Chile's solar-fed sites and anywhere a solar PPA is available.
