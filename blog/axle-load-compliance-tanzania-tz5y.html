Weight Enforcement Is Tightening Everywhere

Axle-load enforcement is tightening across emerging markets, and the era of the routinely overloaded truck is closing. Weigh-in-motion sensors on toll roads, portable scales at checkpoints and fines scaled to the overload percentage have changed the economics: an overloaded truck is no longer profitable margin, it is a fine schedule, a delayed delivery and a black mark with the enforcement database. For electric truck buyers this shift is favorable news, because the TZ5Y electric 80t mining truck was engineered to its rated loads from the start - the 600 kWh battery is integrated in the chassis weight calculation, axle ratings are documented, and what you specify is what the weighbridge sees. What the Battery Weighs - and What It Does Not

The first weight question every buyer asks: how much payload does the battery cost? The honest answer is specific rather than alarming. The TZ5Y's 600 kWh pack adds roughly 2.5-3.5 tonnes against a diesel equivalent - but the diesel truck carries its own offsetting mass in engine, gearbox, exhaust aftertreatment and 300-400 liters of fuel, so the net tare difference typically lands at 1.5-2.5 tonnes depending on configuration. Against a legal GCW, that is payload capacity, and it must be planned honestly - but several jurisdictions now grant electric trucks an axle-load or gross-weight allowance specifically to offset battery mass, recognizing the policy value of the transition. In Tanzanian markets, our team confirms the current allowance position at quotation, because it changes the payload math in the buyer's favor where it applies.

Axle Loads: Where the Weight Actually Sits

Legal compliance is measured per axle, not just gross, and here the electric truck's architecture is an advantage. The battery pack mounts low and between the axles, distributing its mass across the chassis rather than concentrating it - the TZ5Y's axle load distribution at rated payload sits comfortably within front and rear axle ratings, with the balance documented in the homologation papers. The practical discipline is load placement: heavy cargo positioned against the headboard loads the drive axles; positioned at the tail, it unloads the steer. Loading discipline is a warehouse training topic, not an engineering problem - and telematics with axle-load estimation removes the guesswork, flagging loads that would fail the bridge before the truck leaves the yard. Fleets that load by data rather than by eye stop seeing fines entirely.

The Compliance Economics: Fines, Downtime and Reputation

Overload economics deserve an honest spreadsheet line. A single overload fine in most Tanzanian jurisdictions runs from hundreds to thousands of dollars, scaled to the excess; repeat offenses escalate to vehicle impoundment in the strictest regimes, and every weighbridge stop costs 30-90 minutes of schedule regardless of outcome. Across a year, a fleet running habitually at the margin pays a compliance tax in fines, delays and driver hours that never appears in the diesel-versus-electric comparison - because the electric truck, specified to legal loads and loaded by data, simply does not pay it. Add the commercial dimension: major shippers increasingly audit carrier compliance records, and a documented clean weighbridge history is becoming a tender qualification. Specifying Payload: The Configuration Conversation

Payload specification is a conversation worth having precisely at order time, because the variables interact. Body choice moves legal payload by tonnes: a lightweight steel or aluminum body returns capacity that a heavy standard body consumes. Chassis configuration - 6x4 versus 8x4 - sets the legal ceiling itself. And battery sizing trades range against tare: the TZ5Y configuration with 200 km of range covers most regional duty without the largest pack, returning payload to the revenue calculation. Our quotation process for Tanzania buyers models these trade-offs explicitly - body weight, battery capacity, legal axle limits under current Tanzanian rules including any EV allowance, and the resulting legal payload per configuration.
