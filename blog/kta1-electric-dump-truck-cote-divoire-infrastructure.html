Site Charging in Practice

Ivorian project power is better than its reputation: the national grid reaches most Abidjan-area and corridor sites, and batching plants already run three-phase services that support a 120-240 kW DC unit with a dedicated feed. The proven layout is one fast charger at the crusher or workshop per 5-6 trucks, sited where trucks queue for loading. For remote borrow pits, a containerized charger with generator bridging covers the gap - an arrangement Ivorian contractors already know from their camp power.

  • Anchor charging at the crusher; loading queues are free charging windows.
  • Charge to 90% daily; keep 100% for the longest public-road legs.
  • Track kWh per cycle per truck; laterite duty should hold 3.2-3.6 loaded.
  • Plan the dust-season filter service weekly - laterite fines are abrasive and persistent.

Climate: Humidity, Rain and Dust

Abidjan combines coastal humidity with 28-35 C heat and a serious rain season. The KTA1's IP67-sealed high-voltage system handles wading through flooded site sections that stop diesel intakes, within published limits, and the liquid-cooled pack is indifferent to the humidity that degrades air-cooled electronics. Rain-season trafficability on laterite tracks is the seasonal variable that moves consumption most - plan energy budgets 10-15% higher in the wet months, and let the motor's instant torque work for, not against, tyre life on soft ground.
