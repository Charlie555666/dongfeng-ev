TE8L Electric Tractor on Chilean Highways: Toll and Energy Savings Math

Why a Tractor Works on Chilean Highways

The TE8L electric tractor is a capable electric truck for Chile's long toll highways, where the Santiago to Valparaiso and Santiago to Rancagua corridors move most of the country's freight. With a 422 kWh CATL LFP pack and a 510 kW peak motor it covers 280 to 330 km per charge at full semi load, enough for a same-day round trip on the Valparaiso lane with a midway charge.

Chile's toll system is distance-based and scales with axle and emission class, so the zero-emission TE8L can qualify for reduced or waived tolls in some concessions, layering a saving on top of the already lower energy cost. The combined math is what makes the electric tractor competitive against a diesel prime mover.

Charging on the Central Corridor

Chile's highway concessions are adding high-power chargers at service areas, but depot charging remains the backbone. A 360 kW DC unit at the Santiago terminal adds 20 to 80 percent in about 55 minutes, so the TE8L can run a morning Valparaiso trip, charge while the trailer is unloaded, and return the same afternoon.

  • Depart Santiago 05:00 at 100 percent, 42 t GCW.
  • Charge at Casablanca, 360 kW, 20 minutes, adds 90 km.
  • Return by 16:00, overnight charge on off-peak tariff.
  • Reserve a second 360 kW gun for dual-tractor depots.

Energy Math and Regen

The grades toward the coast are gentle, but the TE8L still recovers 8 to 12 percent of energy through regen on the descent and around Santiago congestion. At 1.4 kWh per km and a depot rate of CLP 90 per kWh, energy runs about CLP 126 per km against CLP 330 per km for diesel at CLP 950 per liter and 35 L per 100 km.

Driver Comfort and Cabin

The TE8L cab is quiet on the long highway run, reducing fatigue on the four-hour round trip. Preconditioning on grid power means the driver steps into a warmed or cooled cab without idling, and the flat torque makes the climb out of Valparaiso effortless versus a diesel that downshifts on the grade.

Santiago Depot and Grid

Most Santiago freight yards already have the 400 A three-phase supply a 360 kW charger needs, so install is a matter of the charger cabinet and a cable run. Cab and Rest Rules

Chile's driving-hours rules mean the TE8L often sits while the driver takes the mandatory rest, and the electric truck uses that window to charge rather than idle a diesel. The sleeper cab stays cool on grid power with no exhaust in the cab, so the rest is genuine and the logbook clean for the inspector.

Carbon Credits and Access

Santiago's evolving low-emission zone already restricts older diesel tractors from central corridors at peak hours, and the TE8L's zero tailpipe keeps it inside the zone all day. Fleets can also monetize the avoided CO2 through voluntary carbon programs that Chilean shippers use to green their own reports and meet export-customer demands.

Fleet Sizing

Cross-Border Note

For fleets eyeing the Argentine or Bolivian crossover, the TE8L's left-hand-drive cab and 422 kWh pack suit the long empty roads south of Santiago, though charger coverage thins past Rancagua. Fenghan maps the viable corridor and advises a second on-board charge buffer for legs beyond 300 km from a known charger.

Resale and Residual Value

The TE8L's LFP pack holds capacity well past the 8-year mark, so a unit rotated at 4 years typically retains 45 to 55 percent of FOB on the South American used market. That residual strength supports the finance case for a fleet adding a second tractor, and Fenghan keeps the inspection record that proves battery health at trade-in.

Insurance and Risk

Chilean insurers increasingly discount electric tractors because the sealed battery removes the diesel fire and spill risk, and the telematics give a verified speed and rest record after any incident. That lower premium is a real line in the operating budget that favors the TE8L over an older diesel prime mover.

Driver Hours and Scheduling

Chile's split rest rules mean a Santiago-Valparaiso driver logs the mandatory break mid-route, and the TE8L turns that stop into a charge event rather than an idle. Dispatchers plan the Casablanca pause as both the legal rest and the 20-minute top-up, so no clock time is wasted and the truck rolls back to Santiago with margin.
