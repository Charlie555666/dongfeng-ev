Can a Diesel Generator Really Charge an Electric Truck?

Yes - technically and practically, with correct sizing. An electric truck charger is an electrical load like any other industrial load: size the generator to the charger's demand with margin, and the system works. A 60 kW DC charger requires a generator of roughly 75-100 kVA to cover charger efficiency, power factor and starting transients; a 120 kW charger calls for 150-200 kVA. What Does Generator Charging Cost per Kilometer?

The honest arithmetic, because it decides the case. A diesel generator producing one kWh of electricity consumes roughly 0.25-0.30 liters of fuel - call it $0.25-$0.40 per kWh at typical delivered diesel prices. An electric truck consuming 1.0-1.6 kWh per kilometer therefore costs $0.30-$0.60 per kilometer on generator power. Against a diesel truck burning 0.4-0.6 liters per kilometer on hard vocational duty - $0.50-$0.90 per kilometer at the same fuel price - the generator-charged electric truck still wins on energy, though the margin narrows from the 60-75% grid-charging delivers. When Does Generator Charging Actually Make Sense?

Four situations justify it confidently. Remote sites with no grid prospect - mines, quarries, construction camps where the generator already runs for other loads and truck charging adds to an existing fuel budget. Transitional operations - fleets electrifying now in locations where the grid connection arrives in twelve to eighteen months; the generator bridges the gap instead of postponing the savings. Backup resilience - storm-prone or unstable-grid regions where the generator guarantees the fleet charges regardless. And peak management - fleets using generators to avoid demand charges during grid-constrained hours. Where a reliable grid connection exists and is affordable, grid power wins on cost every time - generator charging is the answer to specific problems, not a general strategy.

How Do You Size and Specify the Setup Correctly?

Specification errors here are expensive and avoidable. Size the generator to the charger, not the truck: charger demand plus site loads plus 20-25% margin, with the generator running at 60-80% load where it is efficient - an oversized generator idling at low load burns fuel per kWh like a truck spinning its wheels. Confirm charger compatibility: quality DC chargers tolerate generator power happily, but specify the power source at purchase so settings match. Plan fuel logistics honestly: a fleet charging nightly adds a measurable diesel delivery schedule to the site. And sequence the transition: in Myanmar, we typically see fleets start generator-charged and migrate to grid or solar-plus-storage as connections arrive - the TZ5Y's 200 km range and the site layout usually make the charging location obvious from day one.
