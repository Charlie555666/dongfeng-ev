Why Altitude Favors Electric Haulage

Ethiopia's major dam sites operate between 1,800 and 2,500 meters, where air density cuts a diesel engine's output by 15 to 25 percent. An electric motor has no such penalty: the KTA1 climbs a 30 percent grade with a 20-ton payload at any altitude, and the loaded descent returns energy to the pack through regenerative braking. Cycle Performance on the Bench

On a typical dam-bench loop of 3 to 6 kilometers the KTA1 completes a cycle in 12 to 16 minutes, delivering 35 to 45 loads per shift. The loaded climb draws peak power, but the return descent regenerates 10 to 15 percent of that energy, so net consumption per cycle stays low and the 450 kWh pack supports a full shift without mid-shift charging. Instant electric torque also shortens the loaded pull-away at the face, a measurable gain across hundreds of cycles.

The sealed drivetrain matters here too: dam sites generate abrasive rock dust that destroys diesel air filters and accelerates engine wear, while the KTA1's sealed LvKong motor simply has no intake to clog.

Charging at a Remote Dam Site

Dam projects are often grid-connected precisely because they are hydroelectric projects, so site power is available and cheap. A 240 kW charger at the site workshop restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes during shift breaks and overnight. Continuous operations can specify the battery-swap variant for 5 to 6 minute pack exchanges.

  • Use site hydro power for the cheapest possible charging energy.
  • Site a 240 kW charger at the workshop for break-time top-ups.
  • Train operators to maximize regen on loaded descents.
  • Choose the swap variant for continuous multi-shift pours.
  • Log energy per cubic meter placed for project reporting.

Energy Savings Versus Diesel at Altitude

A diesel rigid dump at 2,200 meters burns more fuel per hour than at sea level because the engine works derated and inefficient, often 32 to 36 liters per hour on bench duty, at delivered remote-site prices. The KTA1 draws about $5 of site electricity per hour, an annual saving that can exceed $55,000 per truck at dam-project utilization. Maintenance falls in parallel: no derated engine strain, no filter punishment, no injector service.
