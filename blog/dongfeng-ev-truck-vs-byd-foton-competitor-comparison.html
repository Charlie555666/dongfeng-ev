Where Each Brand Genuinely Excels

  • BYD earns its reputation on vertical integration: making its own cells gives supply security and deep battery-data access, and its city-logistics and e-bus pedigree is real. The trade-offs for export fleet buyers: model range concentrates on tractors and distribution trucks, special bodies (mixers, sprinklers, tippers with mining suspensions) are thin, and the 80t-class mining tipper segment that dominates African and Indonesian demand is not its centre of gravity.
  • Foton is the volume play in light and mid electric distribution — competitive pricing, broad dealer networks in some regions, and a sensible choice for van-class and mid-range box duty. Heavy rigid and 49t+ tractor duty, mining-spec suspensions and special-body engineering are outside its main focus.
  • SANY and XCMG bring genuine mining-equipment DNA — heavy structures, off-road competence, and credible 80t+ electric dump offerings concentrated on pure mining duty. Their truck ranges are narrower (dump-centric), city and on-highway configurations are secondary, and mixed fleets (cargo + tractor + specials) need a second supplier.
  • Dongfeng competes as the full-matrix truck maker: a genuine conventional-truck heritage (frames, axles, cabs, suspensions — the unglamorous 70% of a truck that determines decade-long durability) electrified with CATL LFP energy and the LvKong drive family, spanning TE tractors (42–120 t GCW), TZ dump trucks (25–95 t), KTA swap-compatible units, KT/KTH cargo rigids, and KT-series specials including the WVTA-approved KT9X mixer and electric sprinklers.

The Three Arguments That Decide Real Tenders

1. Battery Strategy: CATL LFP Standardisation vs Vertical Integration

BYD's in-house cells are excellent — this is not a chemistry argument. The export-relevant difference is ecosystem: standardising on CATL LFP across the fleet aligns the truck with the largest independent battery service, diagnostics and second-life ecosystem in the world. Pack-level diagnostics, battery-passport documentation (increasingly relevant under the EU Battery Regulation's trajectory), swap-station compatibility (CATL's CAS standard), and residual-value channels all concentrate around the industry's dominant cell supplier. For a fleet operator, that alignment lowers long-run servicing and end-of-life risk in exactly the way horizontal supply chains are supposed to.

2. The Chassis Is the Moat

Battery chemistry gets the headlines, but fleets keep trucks 8–12 years, and what fails in years 4–10 is frames, suspensions, axles, cabs and body mounts. This is where Dongfeng's decades of heavy-truck manufacturing tell: the TZ3V's 8+8+4 multi-layer frame and 10/10/13 mining suspension, the ECE-grade cab structures, the axle and brake engineering — these are carryover disciplines from diesel platforms that electrification does not forgive a manufacturer for lacking. Newer entrants assembled around bought-in components face their steepest learning curves exactly in the years when fleet buyers cannot afford experiments. The KT/TE/TZ range's shared-parts philosophy also compounds: one common-parts pool across tractors, tippers and rigids cuts inventory cost for mixed fleets.

3. Export Reality: Documentation, Training and Support

A truck that cannot clear customs is not a truck. Our buyers' real comparison criterion is rarely the spec sheet — it is whether the supplier ships complete conformity files (UN 38.3, IMDG Class 9 handling, technical files for registration, certificates of origin for preferential duty), trains the maintenance crew in high-voltage safety, and stands behind a parts channel after the warranty champagne is finished. As an authorised channel we supply the full package as standard: pre-shipment inspection support, staged shipments for first-time fleet introductions, commissioning engineers, and remote telemetry monitoring from Xi'an. In frontier markets this support layer is frequently the difference between an electrification programme that scales and one that stalls after the pilot.

What We Tell Buyers Who Ask "Why Not Just Compare Price?"

  1. Compare landed cost, not FOB. Duty treatment, DG-handling brokers, and documentation quality swing landed cost more than the price gap between brands.
  2. Compare the duty cycle, not the brochure range. Send each supplier your actual routes and loads; the honest ones return a simulation, and the difference in honesty is itself information.
  3. Ask about the parts channel by name. Which parts, from where, in what lead time, for how many years. Vague answers here predict year-four regret better than any spec table.
  4. Check the special-body competence. If your fleet includes mixers, sprinklers or mining tippers, factory depth in those bodies matters more than any drivetrain difference.
  5. Price the ecosystem. Charging standards, swap compatibility, battery passport readiness, telemetry support — the features that determine decade-long cost of ownership.
