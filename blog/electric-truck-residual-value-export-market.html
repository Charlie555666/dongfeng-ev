Electric Truck Residual Value: What Export Fleets Get Back After 5 Years

Fleet buyers in export markets increasingly ask not just what an electric truck costs to run, but what it will be worth when they sell it. Residual value is where electric trucks were supposed to be weak, because of battery degradation fears, and where the real data now tells a different story. This analysis explains what drives EV truck residuals in African, Middle Eastern, and Latin American markets, and how to structure a purchase so the asset holds value.

What Actually Drives Electric Truck Residuals

Three factors dominate: remaining battery warranty, documented pack health, and powertrain simplicity. A truck sold with three years of its 8-year or 4,500-cycle CATL LFP warranty still running carries a transferable guarantee that a diesel simply cannot match, because no diesel engine comes with a transferable eight-year warranty. The TE8P electric tractor and its siblings carry exactly this warranty structure, which is why early resale transactions have cleared at stronger-than-expected percentages of original price.

The second driver is documented health. Fleets that keep telematics records of charge behavior and pack temperature can prove a battery has 85 to 90 percent of original capacity after five years of disciplined operation, and that documentation converts directly into resale price.

The LFP Advantage in the Used Market

Chemistry matters at resale. CATL LFP packs tolerate 3,000-plus full cycles with modest degradation, and their thermal stability means a used pack from a hot market like Saudi Arabia or Nigeria arrives at resale with less hidden damage than an equivalent NMC pack. Buyers of used electric trucks price chemistry risk, and LFP's reputation for durability supports the residual.

Five-Year Residual Benchmarks

Early export-market transactions and dealer guidance suggest a well-documented electric heavy truck retains roughly 35 to 45 percent of FOB value after five years of high-utilization corridor or site duty, versus 25 to 35 percent for an equivalent diesel with a worn engine and unknown internals. The gap widens in markets where diesel fuel is expensive, because the buyer of the used truck is also buying the energy saving.

  • Sell with remaining warranty: three-plus years left commands a premium.
  • Document pack health with telematics exports at sale time.
  • Operate between 20 and 90 percent state of charge to protect capacity.
  • Keep software and service records complete for the data room.
