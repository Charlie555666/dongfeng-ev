A mine-site haul loop is a closed circuit within a few kilometers of a fixed charging point, so there is no range risk at all. The dust load at laterite nickel operations destroys diesel air filters and shortens engine life, while the KTA1's sealed LvKong motor has no breathing intake to clog. Cycle Time and Regen on the Haul Loop

On a typical 3 to 5 kilometer pit-to-plant loop the KTA1 completes a cycle in 12 to 16 minutes, delivering 35 to 45 loads per productive shift. The loaded climb out of the pit is the energy peak, but the return descent regenerates 10 to 15 percent of that energy back into the pack, so net consumption per cycle is far lower than the climb alone would suggest. Real-world range holds at 160 to 200 kilometers per charge on grade-heavy loops, enough for a full shift without mid-shift charging.

The instant electric torque also shortens the loaded pull-away at the face, and with no clutch or gear changes the driveline wear that plagues diesel tippers on constant short cycles simply disappears.

Charging at the Mine Site

Because the KTA1 never leaves the mine radius, a 240 kW DC charger at the workshop restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes during shift breaks and overnight. Operations running continuous shifts can add a battery-swap variant that exchanges the pack in 5 to 6 minutes, keeping trucks earning through every shift change.

  • Site a 240 kW charger at the mine workshop for break-time charging.
  • Consider the swap variant for continuous multi-shift operations.
  • Seal the drivetrain housing against laterite dust ingress.
  • Train operators to maximize regen on loaded descents.
  • Track energy per ton of ore to benchmark each pit loop.
