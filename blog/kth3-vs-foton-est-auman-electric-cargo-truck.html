Dongfeng KTH3 vs Foton Auman EST Electric Cargo Truck: The 8x4 EV Truck Rigid Comparison

The 8x4 electric rigid is the workhorse class of regional cargo electrification: 31-tonne GVW trucks moving pallets, bagged commodities, construction materials and packaged goods on 150–300 km radials between ports, industrial zones and distribution centres. In the Chinese export market, the two names fleet buyers shortlist most often in this class are Dongfeng's KTH3 and Foton's Auman EST electric range. We published a KTH1-vs-Foton comparison two years into this programme; this article updates the comparison for the KTH3 — the 350 kWh battery of the KTH family against Foton's EST-A electric cargo platform — because the differences between these two EV truck philosophies matter more, not less, as fleets scale. We sell the Dongfeng side of this market and we are honest about it: the comparison below is written to be useful to a buyer who may well choose the Foton, and the decision framework at the end reflects where each truck genuinely wins.

The Two Philosophies

Dongfeng's new-energy commercial line is built around a standardised battery platform: CATL LFP packs (262–600 kWh across the range) with the 8-year/4,500-cycle warranty, LvKong permanent-magnet drive motors, CCS2 charging as the export default, and battery-swap compatibility on the models where duty demands it. The buyer knows exactly which cell maker and warranty stand behind every truck, and any CATL-capable service network can work on the pack.

Foton's Auman EST electric range takes a more variant-driven approach: multiple battery suppliers and capacities across the EST line (commonly CATL and other tier-one Chinese cell makers depending on variant and build slot), Daimler-influenced cab and chassis architecture from the Auman joint-venture lineage, and strong domestic-market volume economics. For an export buyer, this means the Foton quote you receive must be interrogated cell-by-cell: the EST nameplate covers a family, and the battery spec, warranty and charging interface can vary between offers from different channels.

Round One: Battery Certainty

This is the round that decides most procurement outcomes, and it is where the KTH3's platform discipline shows. The 350 kWh CATL pack is not merely a marketing line — it is the difference between a fleet whose second-hand value, warranty claims and service network are legible to a bank in Almaty or Lagos, and one where the answer depends on which battery variant happened to be in the build. Foton's flexibility is a genuine advantage for domestic Chinese buyers chasing price points; for an export fleet holding trucks for eight-plus years, variant-dependency is residual-value risk. Our rule for buyers comparing the two: demand the battery contract, not the brochure — cell manufacturer, capacity, cycle-life warranty, and the SoH curve, in writing, attached to the VIN.

Round Two: Duty Fit and Body Ecosystem

On the road, both are competent 8x4 electric rigids and the honest answer is that duty fit overlaps heavily. Differences emerge at the edges:

  • Payload-honest range: the KTH3's 350 kWh at 1.3–1.6 kWh/km sustains a full 220–280 km laden day; lighter EST variants save capital but surrender range exactly when a fully loaded 31 t truck faces a headwind market. Fleets running fixed radials under 200 km can take either; fleets at 250 km+ per day should compare the specific pack, not the nameplate.
  • Body building: both platforms carry the standard Chinese body-builder ecosystem (box, curtain-side, flatbed, stake, tipper deck). Dongfeng's export programme coordinates the body build into the factory inspection sequence — the truck ships with the body fitted and inspected, which matters when your warranty claim depends on the HV interface between chassis and body being certified.
  • Cold-weather file: for CIS and Central Asian buyers, the KTH3's liquid-cooled thermal management with −30 °C pre-heating documentation is proven on the sister TE8L line-haul platform in Kazakh winter duty. Ask the Foton channel for the same cold-climate validation file for the specific variant; where it exists, it is credible, but it must be variant-specific.

Round Three: Export Support and Parts Reality

An 8x4 electric cargo fleet is a ten-year relationship with a parts channel. The comparison here is between programmes, not products:

  • Dongfeng export programme: dedicated export documentation (UN 38.3, IMDG, PVoC/inspection coordination), first-line spares kits shipped with each order, regional parts hubs (Gulf and Southeast Asia) with air-freight backup, technician training at handover, and the telemetry platform that makes the fleet auditable for financiers. CATL packs are also serviceable through any regional CATL industrial-battery network — a genuinely independent channel.
  • Foton export channels: strong in markets where Foton's distribution network is established (Southeast Asia, parts of Latin America, Pakistan). Where the network exists, service is responsive; where it does not, buyers are effectively self-supporting or reliant on third-party integrators. The battery-supplier variance compounds this: parts for one variant's pack may not serve another's.

The buyer's question is geographic: where do your trucks sleep, and who is within 500 km of that depot with high-voltage certification? Whichever brand answers that question better for your map wins round three, and round three decides more fleet outcomes than spec sheets.

Round Four: The Money

Capital: the EST's lighter variants typically quote USD 8,000–15,000 below the KTH3 — real money on a 20-truck order. Operating: at parity of energy consumption the annual cost lines are within a few thousand dollars of each other, so the comparison is capital versus certainty. Lifetime: the KTH3's warranty and CATL pack residual (documented 85–92% SoH units commanding 55–70% of new price in the second-hand EV truck market) is the bankable line. A hybrid verdict most CFOs land on: if the trucks are financed with a residual-value covenant or the fleet resells at year 5–6, the KTH3's certainty is worth more than the EST's discount; if the fleet runs to destruction on owned cash, the cheaper unit can win on pure payback speed.

Decision Framework

  • Choose the KTH3 when: you need one battery spec across a 15+ truck fleet; you are financing against residuals; your market lacks a Foton service network but has CATL industrial-battery coverage; your duty runs 220–280 km days; or you are building a mixed Dongfeng fleet (tractors, tippers, rigids) on one charger-and-parts base.
  • Choose the Auman EST when: a Foton distributor with HV capability is already serving your region; your duty is under 200 km/day and the lighter variant's price wins the tender; or your financing is cash-based with no residual covenant.
  • Compare both and walk when: either vendor cannot produce a variant-specific battery contract, SoH curve and warranty assignment for the exact VINs being quoted. That failure is disqualifying in electric truck procurement regardless of brand.

The Tender Test: How to Run a Fair Head-to-Head

For fleets running a formal tender between the KTH3 and the Auman EST (or any pair of Chinese 8x4 electrics), the document that levels the field is a standardised technical annex. Require from each bidder: the battery contract per VIN (cell maker, capacity, cycle warranty, SoH curve); the charging interface and protocol certification; the cold-climate and hot-climate validation files for the specific variant; the regional service map with HV-certified points and response times; the spares price list for the 50 fastest-moving items; the software update framework and who controls rollout windows; and the residual-value policy — whether the vendor or dealer will underwrite a buyback or SoH floor. Score the answers, not the brochures. In the tenders we have participated in, the KTH3's standardised CATL specification wins the battery and residual lines; the Foton side is competitive wherever its local distributor is strong on service response and price. Both outcomes are legitimate — what the standardised annex prevents is the quiet loss on a line item nobody scored until year four of ownership, when it is too late to correct. Take the annex, make it binding to the contract, and either truck you buy arrives with the certainty the spec sheet implied.
