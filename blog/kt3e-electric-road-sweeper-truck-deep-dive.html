Dongfeng KT3E Electric Road Sweeper Truck Deep Dive: A Quiet EV Truck for Clean Streets

Street sweeping has a hidden diesel problem — and it is exactly the duty cycle where an electric truck wins biggest. A conventional sweeper truck sweeps at 5–10 km/h with its engine screaming at high idle to drive the suction fan and brush hydraulics; the drivetrain is barely working while the auxiliary systems drink fuel. The result is 30–45 L of diesel burned per 8-hour shift and a machine so loud it cannot legally operate near hospitals, schools or residential streets before dawn. The Dongfeng KT3E electric road sweeper truck eliminates that profile entirely: a CATL LFP pack powers the traction motor, the suction fan, the side brushes and the water pump as independent electric drives, each running at exactly the speed the job needs. This deep dive examines the KT3E's engineering, sweeping performance, energy economics and export configuration — and why, after garbage compaction, sweeping is the second-fastest payback in the municipal EV truck business.

Electric Auxiliary Drives: The Engineering That Changes Everything

The KT3E's decisive advantage is not the traction motor — it is what happens to the sweeping system when each function gets its own electric drive. On a diesel sweeper, the suction fan runs at a fixed ratio to engine speed. A fan big enough to lift gravel through a 250 mm duct is absurdly oversized for picking up leaf litter, so the operator either runs the engine hard all day or accepts poor pickup. The KT3E's fan is a variable-speed electric unit: 18 kW at light-litter duty, up to 45 kW for heavy construction debris, adjusted from the cab or automatically by the load sensor in the nozzle.

The same logic applies to the brushes. Independent electric brush motors let the driver drop the curb-side brush and raise the pavement-side brush independently, vary brush speed by surface, and reverse brush rotation to clear jams — all without touching hydraulic valves. Brush pressure is controlled electronically and held constant as bristles wear, which extends brush life from a typical 120–150 hours on mechanical systems to 250–300 hours. And because the brushes, fan and water pump are electric, the KT3E can sweep with the traction system in a low-power crawl — the whole machine draws 55–80 kW while sweeping, versus a diesel sweeper burning 38–45 L of fuel over the same shift.

Productivity: Square Kilometres per Shift

Sweeping productivity is a function of width, speed and downtime. The KT3E sweeps at up to 12 km/h with a 3.2 m working width in light mode — 3.8 hectares per hour of coverage — and at 6–8 km/h with the full-width nozzle engaged for heavy deposits. Over a 7-hour sweep day with hopper dumps and repositioning, a single KT3E routinely covers 18–22 km of kerbline or 60–75 hectares. Two features drive that number:

  • Zero-idle hopper operations: the high-dump ejects to 1.6 m directly into a transfer bin in 3 minutes, electrically. No engine warm-up, no PTO engagement sequence.
  • Quiet-shift capability: at 66–70 dB(A) at 10 m, the KT3E sweeps city centres at 4 a.m. in markets where diesel sweepers are restricted to post-7 a.m. — effectively adding 2–3 productive hours to every day without touching labour agreements.

For industrial sites — cement plants, port yards, mine haul-road edges — the 200 kWh pack plus the optional third brush and heavy-duty nozzle filter keep pickup above 95% on PM10-loaded surfaces, and the fully sealed hopper with electric dust-extraction keeps airborne discharge inside EU-style limits during dumping.

Energy and TCO: The Sweeper Business Case

Sweeper duty cycles are energy-dense for their speed. At full working configuration the KT3E draws 55–80 kW continuously, so a 7-hour shift consumes 400–520 kWh — which is why the pack is sized at 166–200 kWh and fleets opportunity-charge at midday. The standard operating pattern is 4.5 hours sweeping, a 60–70 minute DC charge during the crew's lunch and depot turnaround, then 3 more hours. Depots that cannot spare midday charging take the 200 kWh pack and split shifts.

Dust Control Without Water Waste

In water-scarce markets — the Gulf, the Sahel, Australia, northern Chile — a diesel sweeper's 3,000+ litre daily water consumption is a real cost and a sustainability problem. The KT3E's sealed suction path with electric dust extraction achieves compliant pickup with 40–60% less spray water, because the fan holds negative pressure in the nozzle chamber instead of relying on water to knock dust down. An optional pure-vacuum mode for airfield and port duty eliminates spray entirely. Fleets pairing the KT3E with the KT3F or KT7A electric sprinklers can now run a complete zero-emission, low-water street-cleaning operation from a single depot charger.
