What a Depot Microgrid Is and Why Fleets Build Them

A depot microgrid is a self-contained energy system - solar generation, battery storage, grid connection and intelligent controls - that powers a truck depot as a managed unit rather than a passive consumer. Fleets build them for three reasons: the grid connection available is too small for fleet fast-charging and upgrading it costs more than the microgrid; grid tariffs or outages make energy expensive or unreliable; and locking in twenty years of solar electricity turns the fleet's largest operating cost into a fixed asset. For an electric truck fleet in Iraq, where grid constraints and tariff volatility are common, a microgrid is often the difference between a pilot that stalls and a fleet that scales.

Sizing Solar and Storage for a TZ8J Fleet

Sizing starts from the trucks, not the roof. Each TZ8J electric concrete mixer running its daily duty consumes roughly 350 kWh at the charger per working day; a ten-truck fleet therefore needs on the order of ten times that figure daily. Under Iraqi irradiance of 4-5.5 kWh per installed kWp per day, covering that load fully needs roughly 500-900 kWp of solar for ten trucks, phased as capital allows. Storage is sized for shifting, not survival: one to two hours of fleet charging demand buffers clouds and moves solar generation into evening charge windows. The TZ8J's telematics energy data, captured during the first operating months, replaces spreadsheet assumptions with measured numbers before final storage sizing is committed.

Grid Independence and Outage Resilience in Iraq

The operational argument for microgrids in Iraq is resilience. Grid outages that idle diesel fleets at fuel queues also stop grid-dependent electric fleets - but not microgrid fleets. A solar-plus-storage depot keeps trucks charging through blackouts, and the trucks themselves, through their large batteries, become part of the resilience: the 350 kWh pack in each TZ8J is substantial stored energy on wheels. For critical operations - cold chain, municipal services, port work - this energy independence has a balance-sheet value that simple payback models understate. Fleet managers who have run through one major outage on microgrid power describe it as the day the system paid for itself in reputation alone.

Levelized Cost of Microgrid Energy

The financial case rests on levelized cost: the lifetime cost per kWh delivered. Turnkey commercial solar in the region runs $600-$900 per kWp installed, with storage adding cost proportional to shifting depth. Against typical Iraqi industrial tariffs, solar microgrid energy lands 30-60% below grid prices over a twenty-year life; against diesel generator power - the real alternative at many depots - the saving reaches 60-75%. Payback on the energy infrastructure alone runs three to six years, after which the fleet charges on nearly free energy for fifteen-plus more. Combined with the 60-75% per-kilometer energy saving already built into the electric truck case, a microgrid turns a good fleet investment into a structural cost advantage.

Controls, Chargers and Energy Management

The microgrid's intelligence sits in its energy management system, which decides every minute whether trucks charge from solar, storage or grid, at what power, and in what priority. Fleet charging software schedules the TZ8J fleet around shift patterns: fast 60-120 kW DC sessions for turnaround needs, slow overnight charging for base load at the lowest marginal cost. Good systems also manage demand charges, capping simultaneous draw below the grid connection limit so the microgrid and grid supply complement rather than fight each other. CCS2 charger selection, grid-code certification for Iraq and integration between charger and solar inverters are specification details we support with every fleet order, because the control layer is where theoretical savings either materialize or leak away.

Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the TZ8J earns acceptance quickly. The 400 kW motor delivers instant torque with no gear hunting, the cab is quieter than any diesel equivalent, and regenerative braking reduces fatigue in stop-start duty. Charging Planning for TZ8J Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TZ8J supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 350 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Iraq should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
