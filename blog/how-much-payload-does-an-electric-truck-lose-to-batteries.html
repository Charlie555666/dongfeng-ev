How Much Payload Does an Electric Truck Lose to Its Batteries?

Quick answer: An electric truck typically gives up 1.5-3 t of payload to its battery pack versus a diesel equivalent, but Dongfeng EV chassis recover most of that gap through lighter frame and body tuning. The TZ5E electric dump truck carries a 400 kWh CATL LFP battery yet keeps a competitive GVW for African mining and construction duty, so the practical penalty is far smaller than the raw battery weight suggests.

How Much Payload Does a Battery Actually Weigh?

A 400 kWh CATL LFP pack, such as the one in the TZ5E electric dump truck, weighs roughly 2.8-3.2 t including structure and thermal gear. Smaller 262-336 kWh packs in lighter Dongfeng bodies shed proportionally less, often 1.5-2 t. The penalty scales with capacity, so matching the pack to the duty cycle is the first lever a fleet has to protect payload, and over-specifying battery is the most common way operators needlessly sacrifice tonnes.

It is worth separating pack mass from total EV mass. The battery is heavy, but the rest of the electric drivetrain is often lighter than the diesel it replaces, so the net payload effect is the difference between the two, not the battery weight on its own.

What Does Dongfeng Do to Recover the Loss?

Dongfeng trims the deficit with a lighter frame, an alloy or optimized steel body and the removal of the diesel engine, exhaust and fuel tank, which together free 0.6-1.0 t. The LvKong motor is compact and the single-speed drive drops the gearbox mass. Net result: a TZ5E still lands within a few hundred kilograms of a comparable diesel tipper on allowable payload, and on many routes that difference is smaller than the load variation from how full the body is packed.

Body choice matters as much as the chassis. A lightweight alloy tipper body reclaims more payload than any software tweak, and Dongfeng offers body options specifically to put tonnes back that the pack took away.

Payload Recovery Levers

  • Specify the smallest pack that covers the real route to save battery mass.
  • Choose a lightweight alloy or optimized steel body to reclaim tonnes.
  • Remove diesel auxiliaries so the freed mass offsets the pack.
  • Use battery swap on light loads to carry less fixed battery.

Are Electric Dump Trucks Worth It for Payload-Sensitive Work?

Yes, for most African duty. The 1.5-3 t loss is small against a 15-25 t payload class, and the savings on diesel, maintenance and downtime usually outweigh the fewer tonnes per trip. Where every tonne counts on a long haul, specify the smallest pack that covers the route — the 282 kWh option on lighter bodies keeps the deficit near the low end, and a battery-swap strategy can recover even more.

How Does Battery Swap Change the Math?

A 5-6 minute battery swap lets a fleet run a smaller onboard pack and exchange it for a fresh one, so the truck carries less battery mass per shift yet never loses range. For the TZ5E in mining, a swap strategy trims the fixed battery weight carried on light loads while keeping full capacity available when the pit demands it, which is the neatest answer to the payload-versus-range trade-off.

What the South Africa Market Shows

In the South Africa electric truck market, mines and quarries accept the modest payload trade because the EV's torque and zero pit emissions improve total productivity. Operators report that the cleaner working face and lower fuel cost matter more than a couple of tonnes per cycle on repetitive haul routes, and the swap-enabled fleets report the smallest effective penalty of all.

How Battery Chemistry Affects the Trade

CATL LFP chemistry is the reason the payload penalty stays manageable. LFP is heavier per kilowatt-hour than some alternatives but far safer and longer-lived, and its flat degradation lets a fleet plan payload around a known pack mass for the whole 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty. The 400 kWh TZ5E pack is therefore a known, stable weight rather than a shrinking liability. For fleets comparing bids, we recommend asking each supplier for the certified kerb and GVW so the real loss is compared like for like.

Does Range Suffer Because of the Smaller Payload?

No. Range is set by pack energy and duty, not by how much is in the body, so the TZ5E's 200-350 km capability is unchanged whether the truck carries a full or partial load. Fleets sometimes worry that a lighter payload means less revenue per charge, but on fixed mining and construction loops the loop length — not the tonnage — sets the energy per trip, so the trade is purely about payload per cycle, not range.

Real-World African Examples

On comparable African dump and mining duties, operators report that the 1.5-3 t loss is absorbed by running slightly more cycles rather than by losing work. A 400 kWh TZ5E that does one extra short haul per shift to cover the deficit still beats a diesel on fuel and emissions, and the swap option removes any range concern on the longer pits. The payload number on paper is rarely the constraint operators feared.

Specifying the Right Pack for Your Duty

The single most important buying decision is pack size. A fleet that routinely runs short, fixed loops should choose the 262-282 kWh option and recover the most payload, while a mine or long-haul operator needs the 400-424 kWh pack and accepts the larger but still manageable 2.5-3.2 t trade. Matching capacity to the actual route — not to a marketing maximum — is how Dongfeng fleet buyers keep both payload and range where they need them, and our quote always states the certified kerb and GVW up front. For operators unsure of the duty, we recommend instrumenting one diesel week to record real energy per kilometre, then sizing the pack with a margin rather than a guess, which avoids both the payload penalty of over-spec and the range anxiety of under-spec.
