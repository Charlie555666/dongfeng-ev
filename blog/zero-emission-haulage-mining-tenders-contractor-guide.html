Zero-Emission Haulage Clauses in Mining Tenders: A Contractor's Guide

Mining majors are increasingly writing zero-emission haulage clauses into their contractor tenders, and a fleet that cannot demonstrate a credible electric truck plan is now losing points before the price is even read. This contractor's guide explains how zero-emission clauses appear in mining tenders, how bid scoring weights them, what compliance proof you must supply, and how to model the cost per tonne-kilometer so your EV truck bid actually wins.

Why Miners Are Adding Zero-Emission Clauses

Large mining operators have public decarbonization targets tied to finance and social license, and they push those targets down to haulage and site-service contractors through tender conditions. A zero-emission clause may require a percentage of the fleet to be battery-electric by a fixed date, or it may award scoring points for lower site emissions. Either way, an EV truck is no longer a nice-to-have in the bid; it is often a qualifying criterion that gates you from the shortlist entirely.

How Bid Scoring Weights Emissions

Tender scoring typically splits across price, technical capability, safety and sustainability. The emissions block can be 10-30 percent of the total, and within it the evaluator looks for concrete evidence rather than promises. A contractor who shows a deployed TZ5E electric dump truck with telemetry, charging infrastructure and a diesel-displacement figure will outscore one who merely states an intention to electrify. The scoring rewards proof of operation, not ambition.

Compliance Proof You Must Prepare

Before the bid closes, assemble the documents that prove your zero-emission capability:

  • Vehicle homologation and type approval for the EV model offered.
  • Battery safety certificates and the UN 3171 transport declaration.
  • Charging plan showing site power capacity and depot or pit-side chargers.
  • Telemetry or pilot data demonstrating achievable cycles per shift.
  • A diesel-displacement and CO2-avoidance calculation for the contract term.

The TZ5E electric dump truck carries a 500 kWh battery and a 510 kW motor, which suits high-payload pit haulage, and its spec sheet is the backbone of the compliance file because it documents the energy and power the miner cares about.

Pilot Fleet Strategy

If you have never run EVs in a pit, propose a pilot before committing the whole fleet. A pilot of two to five TZ5E electric dump truck units lets you gather real tonne-km data, prove charging reliability, and de-risk the full tender. Miners respect a staged plan because it shows operational discipline, and it gives you the telemetry to defend your cost model in the next bid round.

Common Bid Mistakes

  • Promising electrification without naming a specific certified model.
  • Missing battery safety certificates in the compliance annex.
  • Underestimating site charging power and causing downtime.
  • Quoting a diesel tonne-km without a like-for-like EV comparison.

Any one of these can drop your sustainability score enough to lose the contract, even if your price is the lowest. The fix is preparation: a certified model, a charging plan and real data.

Building a Winning Submission

Lead with the zero-emission clause head-on. Name the EV model, attach the homologation and battery certificates, show the tonne-km model with the diesel comparison, and propose a pilot if the fleet is large. The Chile electric truck market guide is a strong reference for the Andean mining context, and the TZ5E's 500 kWh pack gives you the payload headroom miners want. Done properly, the zero-emission clause becomes your strongest scoring block rather than a hurdle.

Site Charging and Grid Capacity

A zero-emission haulage bid is only credible if the mine site can power the trucks. Evaluate the grid connection, the available transformer capacity, and whether on-site solar or a buffer battery can shoulder peak charging. A pit with limited grid may need a staged rollout or a swap station instead of fast chargers. The TZ5E electric dump truck supports 120, 240 and 360 kW charging and five-to-six-minute swaps, so match the infrastructure to the shift pattern rather than over-building from day one and stranding capital on unused equipment.

Where the grid is weak, a containerized battery buffer charged off-peak can decouple charging from the site limit and still deliver the cycles the TZ5E needs, which is often cheaper than a transformer upgrade and faster to deploy. This flexibility is exactly what lets a contractor phase electrification without stranding capital, and it keeps the bid bankable even on remote sites where the miner expects rapid, low-risk deployment.

Telemetry and Reporting to the Miner

Miners want evidence, not assurances, so build a telemetry feed that reports tonnes moved, kWh consumed per tonne-km, charging uptime and avoided diesel. A monthly report framed against the tender's sustainability targets keeps your scoring strong at renewal and defends any price premium. The Chile electric truck market guide shows how copper operations there already expect this data discipline, and contractors who supply it win repeat work and preferential allocation on future zero-emission lots.

Risk Sharing in the Contract

Consider a performance-linked clause where part of the fee depends on achieved availability, which signals confidence and aligns you with the miner's output goals. Pair it with a clear maintenance plan for the 510 kW motor and 500 kWh pack so the miner sees longevity. The TZ5E's 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty underpins that plan and reduces the miner's perceived risk on a novel zero-emission fleet, making your bid easier to approve internally.

  • Assess grid and solar capacity before promising rollout.
  • Report tonnes, kWh per tonne-km and uptime monthly.
  • Link part of the fee to achieved availability.
