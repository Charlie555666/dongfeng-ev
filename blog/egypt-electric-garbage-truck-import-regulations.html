Understanding Egypt Import Duty on Electric Trucks

Electric trucks enter Egypt under HS heading 8704, and most customs authorities now classify battery-electric goods vehicles in dedicated subheadings. Import duty on a fully built electric truck typically runs between 0% and 35% depending on Egypt trade policy, with VAT or GST added on top of the duty-paid value. Some governments waive or reduce duty on zero-emission vehicles to hit climate targets, so the first step in any import plan is confirming the current EV concession with a licensed broker. When we quote the KT3E electric garbage truck at an FOB range of $90,000-$115,000, the duty base in most countries is the CIF value: truck price plus ocean freight and insurance. Getting the classification right at this stage avoids re-assessment delays that can leave a truck sitting at the Port of Alexandria for weeks while storage charges accumulate.

Documents You Need for Egypt Customs Clearance

A clean customs file for an electric truck shipment normally includes the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and the vehicle conformity certificate. For Egypt, importers should also arrange GOEIC registration before the vessel sails, because retroactive certification is slow and expensive. Battery documentation matters as much as vehicle paperwork: customs and the shipping line will ask for the UN38.3 test summary for the 262 kWh CATL LFP pack, plus an MSDS and a dangerous goods declaration where required. We prepare the full document pack from our side in Xi'an, including English originals and notarized translations where Egyptian authorities request them, so the broker in Cairo can pre-lodge the entry before the ship arrives.

Homologation and Certification Requirements

Beyond customs, an electric truck must be approved for road use in Egypt. The process usually combines GOEIC registration with a national type approval or registration step. Expect inspectors to check lighting, braking performance, VIN plate language, and increasingly the charging inlet standard. Dongfeng EV trucks ship with CCS2 inlets as standard, which matches the infrastructure rolling out across most export markets. Budget four to eight weeks for the certification stage in a typical Egyptian approval cycle, and start it while the truck is still in production rather than after arrival. Buyers who skip this sequencing routinely pay for it in port storage and demurrage fees that erase the price advantage of buying direct from China.

Shipping and Port Handling at the Port of Alexandria

Most electric trucks we export move as RoRo cargo, driven on and off the vessel under their own power, which suits a self-propelled garbage truck like the KT3E. Flat-rack containers are the fallback for destinations without RoRo service. Because the truck carries a large lithium battery, the shipping line will require the state of charge limited to around 30-50% for ocean transit, and the battery disconnect procedure documented. On arrival at the Port of Alexandria, the consignee needs a licensed handler for any dangerous goods paperwork and a truck-friendly customs terminal. We coordinate directly with forwarders who handle EV cargo every week, so the KT3E is discharged, inspected and released without the learning-curve delays that hit first-time EV importers.

Charging Planning for KT3E Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT3E supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 262 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Egypt should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
