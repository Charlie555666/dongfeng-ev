Tanzania's hilly haul routes

Many Tanzanian aggregate and mining loops climb from the Rift Valley floor to plateau quarries and back, with long graded descents that punish friction brakes. A 360 kW motor in the TZ3Z can also act as a generator, so instead of wasting descent energy as heat, the truck feeds it back into the 282 kWh pack. On a 200-250 km mixed route with repeated climbs, that recovery is the difference between one charge per shift and two.

How regenerative braking works on an EV truck

  • Off-throttle: lifting the pedal engages the motor as a generator, slowing the truck without the brake pads.
  • Downgrade: on a descent the motor holds a set speed and pushes energy back to the pack.
  • Blended braking: the last 20% of stopping still uses friction pads for a sure stop.
  • Thermal relief: less pad heat means longer brake life on hilly Tanzanian routes.

The 360 kW motor can absorb a large share of the descent energy, so the 282 kWh pack gains back charge that a diesel would shed as brake heat. Drivers set a descent speed and let regen hold it, which also keeps the truck stable on loose surfaces.

Range on hilly versus flat routes

On flat ground the 282 kWh pack yields the rated 200-250 km, but on hilly Tanzanian loops the up-to-20% regen recovery can add the equivalent of 40-50 km of extra reach per charge. Liquid cooling keeps both the pack and the motor in their comfort band through 40 °C afternoons, and the 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty covers the daily deep cycles that hilly hauling demands. Charging and duty-cycle planning

Because regen reduces net draw, many TZ3Z operators finish a hilly shift with more reserve than a flat-route model would predict. Operator training for regen driving

Hilly regen driving is a skill: teach crews to lift early and let the motor hold speed rather than riding the brakes, and to read the onboard recovery gauge so they trust the returned charge. The near-silent TZ3Z needs light-and-radio signalling around the pit, and a daily pack-health check feeds the 8-year warranty record. With trained drivers, the up-to-20% recovery becomes a measurable fuel-equivalent saving every shift.

Battery swap versus depot charging

Where a quarry fits a battery-swap bay, the TZ3Z's 282 kWh pack exchanges in 5-6 minutes, eliminating the 45-90 minute charge queue during peak aggregate demand and letting one truck run near-continuously. For most Tanzanian fleets a 360 kW depot post plus an overnight and midday charge is simpler and cheaper, covering the 200-250 km loop with margin. Choose swap only when daily km exceed two full loops and the site can justify the bay capital; otherwise depot charging already delivers the regen saving.

Gradeability and loaded start capability

The 360 kW motor delivers full torque from zero, so the TZ3Z starts a loaded climb on a 15-20% pit ramp without the clutch slip that wears a diesel. Gradeability this strong matters on Tanzanian plateau quarries where the loaded run is uphill both ways around the loop. Because regen recovers energy on the descent, the net climb cost is lower than the nameplate suggests, and the 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty covers the daily deep cycles that such grades impose on the pack.

Safety signage and pit communication

Because the TZ3Z is near-silent, quarry sites must add beacon lights and two-way radios so spotters track the truck without engine noise. A simple hi-vis cab flag and a reversing alarm cover the blind spots, and a daily walk-around catches tyre or hose issues before they become roadside faults. These low-cost habits protect the 8-year battery warranty record and keep a quiet EV truck as safe as the diesel it replaces on a busy pit floor.
