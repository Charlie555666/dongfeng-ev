TE8L Electric Tractor for Lagos Port Drayage: Nigeria Container Guide

Lagos port drayage is a stop-start world of container yards, traffic, and queueing, and the TE8L electric truck is built exactly for that duty cycle. This 6x4 electric tractor uses a 424 kWh liquid-cooled CATL LFP battery and a 360 kW LvKong motor to pull container trailers between Apapa port and inland yards while returning 250-300 km of range per charge. In this guide we examine yard range, the charging plan, and the savings versus diesel for Nigerian drayage operators in 2026.

Why Lagos port drayage suits an electric tractor

Drayage is a short, repeatable loop with heavy idle and creep time, which is the worst case for a diesel engine and the best case for an electric tractor. The TE8L draws energy only when it moves or powers ancillaries, and regenerative braking recovers energy in the constant stop-start of port traffic. For Nigerian operators fighting diesel cost, congestion, and emissions rules near residential port communities, the TE8L EV truck turns a fixed drayage lane into a clean, low-cost electric circuit.

TE8L tractor, battery, and motor specification

The TE8L is a 6x4 tractor built on a 424 kWh CATL LFP pack, liquid-cooled for stable performance in Lagos heat, paired with a 360 kW LvKong motor for strong container-pulling torque. Container yard range in practice

On a typical Lagos drayage loop with a loaded container, the TE8L returns 250-300 km of range per charge, comfortably covering multiple port-to-yard round trips before a top-up. The stop-start nature of drayage actually helps the electric case: regen on every braking queue recovers energy, and the motor draws nothing while the truck sits in traffic. Operators anchor charging at the inland yard and let the liquid-cooled LFP pack cycle daily within the warranty window.

Charging plan for drayage operators

The TE8L accepts DC fast charging at 120, 240, or 360 kW, reaching 20-80% in 45-90 minutes. For drayage, a 240 kW or 360 kW charger at the inland yard keeps tractors turning through crew changes, and the shared Dongfeng architecture means the same unit can service sibling models. Because the pack is LFP and liquid-cooled, repeated fast charging is safe across the full warranty period even with multiple daily cycles.

Savings versus diesel drayage

The FOB price of $95,000-$130,000 is in line with a diesel tractor of similar class, but the operating math favours the TE8L EV truck strongly. Diesel drayage burns fuel while idling in port queues, and engine maintenance in a dusty, humid environment is relentless. The electric tractor removes engine servicing, cuts brake wear through regen, and turns energy cost into a predictable grid line item that shields operators from diesel price swings near the port.

Financing and total cost of ownership modelling

Nigerian drayage operators should model the TE8L on lifetime cost rather than the $95,000-$130,000 FOB price in isolation. The model should weigh the eliminated diesel and engine maintenance against yard energy and charging cost, and should credit the regen recovered in every port-queue braking event as a genuine saving. On the Apapa loop the electric tractor typically reaches payback within a few years of steady drayage, after which it moves containers at a lower unit cost than diesel. Operators should also explore transport-equipment leasing and any port-modernisation facilities that reduce the upfront outlay, and they should build the inland-yard charger into the same budget so the energy line is stable. Treating the 424 kWh pack as a separate, warrantied asset lets finance set a realistic trade-in value and keeps the TE8L easy to refinance. For fleets scaling beyond Apapa, the predictable energy cost and minimal servicing are what make the electric tractor a confident expansion choice over diesel. Buyers should also factor import duty and any local-content rules into the comparison, since these can shift the relative position of electric and diesel options in either direction. Finally, the simplified drivetrain reduces the skill level needed for workshop staff and improves driver retention, two factors that protect the lifetime cost case once the trucks are in daily service.

Driver training and fleet onboarding

Deploying the TE8L EV truck for Lagos drayage is a quick win because the stop-start port loop is exactly where electric drivetrains excel. Drivers adapt to single-pedal operation and to planning yard charging around crew changes instead of diesel bays, and the 360 kW LvKong motor gives the low-speed grunt needed for loaded container trailers. A measured rollout pilots a couple of tractors on the busiest Apapa lane, trains drivers on regen in port queues and range with a full container, and proves the inland yard charger before scaling. Pilots also confirm the 250-300 km range against real drayage duty and congestion.

Residual value and warranty planning

The TE8L's 424 kWh LFP pack, covered for 8 years or 4,500 cycles, provides a solid residual foundation for Nigerian operators.
