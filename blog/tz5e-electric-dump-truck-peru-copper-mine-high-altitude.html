TZ5E Electric Dump Truck at 4,000 m: Peru Copper Mine Guide

At 4,000 m above sea level a diesel dump truck loses 30 to 35 percent of its rated power, and on a copper haul that penalty shows up as slower climbs, longer cycle times, and frustrated shovel operators. The electric truck removes that penalty entirely because an electric motor delivers full torque from zero rpm and loses no output as the air thins. For Peruvian mine operators running the EV truck on high-altitude pits, the TZ5E heavy electric dump truck is the unit that finally makes the math work above the snow line.

Mine Duty at 4,000 m and Above

Peru's copper belt sits on the western cordillera where many haul roads top 4,000 m. A loaded diesel unit rated at 350 kW at sea level effectively delivers only about 230 to 245 kW at the pit, which forces operators to de-rate payloads or accept slower round trips. The TZ5E's LvKong motor is rated 282 kW and holds that output unchanged at altitude, so the truck climbs the same grade at the same speed whether the pit is at 1,000 m or 4,200 m. That consistency is worth more than the headline number because it stabilizes the entire load-and-haul plan.

The TZ5E carries a 424 kWh CATL LFP pack with the standard 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, and FOB pricing runs from $95,000 to $125,000 depending on body, liner, and charging choice. On a typical mine cycle of load, haul, dump, and return across 8 to 15 km round trips, the truck easily completes multiple cycles per shift on a single charge and tops up during shift change.

Energy and Payload Economics

A diesel dump in this class burns roughly 0.45 to 0.55 L/km under loaded climb, which at Peruvian diesel prices of $0.90 to $1.30 per liter equals $0.40 to $0.72 per kilometer. The TZ5E draws about 1.1 to 1.6 kWh/km when loaded, and at mine-site electricity of $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh that falls to $0.09 to $0.32 per kilometer. The gap widens when the mine generates its own hydro or solar power, a common setup in the Peruvian highlands.

Charging Strategy for the Pit

Most high-altitude mines already run a workshop with three-phase power for drill and haulage maintenance, so adding a 360 kW DC charger is a modest step. The TZ5E charges from 10 to 80 percent in 45 to 90 minutes, which slots into the natural break between shifts. For operations that run three shifts, the 5 to 6 minute battery swap keeps a single truck hauling around the clock while a second pack charges in the battery barn.

Mine-site solar is especially attractive here. A modest 500 kW to 1 MW array paired with the existing 424 kWh packs can cover a large share of daily haul energy at $0.08 per kWh or less, turning the altitude disadvantage into a clean-energy advantage. The electric drivetrain has far fewer moving parts than a diesel powertrain, which cuts the 35 to 40 percent maintenance saving and reduces the skilled-mechanic load at remote sites.

Why Electric Wins on the Andean Cordillera

Diesel engines also need denser, colder air for combustion, so high-altitude pits see higher fuel consumption per tonne hauled exactly when the haul is hardest. The electric truck sidesteps this entirely. Combined with lower noise and no diesel exhaust in the pit, the TZ5E improves both the operating cost line and the working environment for equipment operators.
