KT7A Electric Washing Truck for the Philippines' Humid Tropical Cities

Why a Washing Truck Works in the Tropics

The KT7A electric washing truck is a practical electric truck for Philippine cities where daily street cleaning, dust suppression, and flood-channel washing are year-round jobs. With a 282 kWh CATL LFP pack and a 282 kW motor it covers 210 to 260 km per charge, enough for a full Manila shift of curb washing, median spraying, and market cleanup without returning to depot mid-day.

The Philippines climate is the real test: high humidity, salt air in coastal barangays, and monsoon soaking all attack a conventional truck's electrical and chassis systems. The KT7A is built with tropical corrosion protection from the factory, which is why it suits municipal fleets better than a converted imported chassis.

Tropical Corrosion Protection Details

Coastal Philippine cities like Manila, Cebu, and Davao expose trucks to salt spray that rusts a standard chassis in a few seasons. The KT7A uses a hot-dip galvanized frame, marine-grade wiring looms, and sealed connectors rated to IP67, plus a cathodic coating on the water tank and pump assemblies. The battery enclosure is a sealed aluminum case that shrugs off monsoon flooding up to 300 mm.

  • Frame: hot-dip galvanized with wax-oil internal cavity fill.
  • Wiring: tinned copper marine loom, IP67 sealed connectors.
  • Tank: stainless or epoxy-coated steel, cathodic protected.
  • Electronics: sealed to IP67, humidity-buffered control units.

Energy and Water Efficiency

The high-pressure pump is inverter-driven, so it spins only when spraying instead of running off a constant diesel PTO. That cuts energy per cubic meter of water moved by roughly 20 percent versus a mechanical drive. At 0.9 kWh per km plus pump load the KT7A costs about PHP 18 per km in electricity against PHP 42 per km for a diesel water truck at PHP 58 per liter.
