Step 1: Confirm Your Country's EV Duty Treatment Early

Duty is the single largest variable in the landed-cost stack, and electric trucks do not always follow diesel-truck tariff lines. A snapshot from our recent shipments:

Two practical rules: get the tariff classification (HS code) confirmed in writing with your customs broker before ordering, because misclassifying a battery-electric tipper as a diesel truck costs 5–15 points of duty in several jurisdictions; and ask whether your country applies duty on the battery separately (some do when packs ship uninstalled — the TZ3V ships installed, which normally keeps the whole vehicle on the better tariff line).

Step 2: Shipping — RORO or Flat-Rack?

An 8x4 dump truck is too tall and too heavy for standard containers, so the choice is RORO (roll-on/roll-off) vessel versus flat-rack containerised shipment:

  • RORO: the standard route for complete trucks — Mombasa, Dar es Salaam, Durban, Tema, Lomé, Casablanca all receive RORO services from Chinese ports. Transit runs 28–45 days depending on routing. Cost for a TZ3V-class unit: roughly USD 9,000–16,000 to East/West African majors.
  • Flat-rack/containerised: used when the destination port lacks RORO service or when buyers consolidate mixed fleets. The truck is lashed to platforms inside a frame; costs run 10–25% higher but schedule flexibility improves.

The EV-specific layer: a truck with a 600 kWh lithium battery ships as UN 3171 Class 9 dangerous goods. Our shipments include the IMDG-compliant DG declaration, UN 38.3 test summary for the pack, SOC (state-of-charge) documentation showing the pack at the transport-approved level (typically ~30%), and marking/labelling per the IMDG code. None of this is exotic for us — it is monthly routine — but the receiving broker must be told in advance. Trucks have sat in African ports for weeks because the broker had never handled a DG declaration for a vehicle. Brief them at contract stage, not at anchorage.

Step 3: Documentation Package

The complete TZ3V export file we prepare contains: commercial invoice; packing list; bill of lading; certificate of origin (Form E/A where preferential rates apply, e.g. China–ASEAN for transshipment routes, or non-preferential CO); UN 38.3 battery test summary; IMDG dangerous goods declaration; manufacturer's quality/inspection certificate; and the vehicle technical file ( VIN, weights, dimensions, motor/battery rating) needed for local registration. Buyers opting for third-party pre-shipment inspection (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Cotecna — required by some countries, wise everywhere) schedule it in the final week of production; we host inspectors at the factory as a standard service.

Step 4: Payment Structures That Work

  1. Letter of credit (first orders): irrevocable LC at sight, released against the document package above. Costs 1–3% in bank charges, protects both sides, and most African mining-sector buyers' banks handle them routinely.
  2. 30/70 T/T: 30% advance to open production, 70% against shipping documents before vessel departure — the most common structure for repeat private buyers.
  3. Staged multi-unit orders: for 10+ unit fleets, we ship in tranches: first 30–40% of the order, then the balance after the buyer confirms commissioning of the first units. This de-risks first-time EV programmes.
  4. Buyer's-credit / ECA routes: for large mining-fleet orders, insured buyer's credits can extend terms to 3–5 years; eligibility assessed per country and buyer.

Step 6: After Arrival — Commissioning and Support

Delivery is not the finish line. Our standard handover for African mining fleets includes: high-voltage safety induction for the maintenance crew (a half-day course with certification); driver familiarisation covering regenerative braking discipline on pit ramps and charging procedure; a commissioning checklist covering pack condition after transit (SOC state, isolation resistance, thermal system function); and an opening spares package covering wear items and critical electronics. Optional on-site engineer deployment for first 30 days is available and recommended for first-time EV mines. Telemetry: the TZ3V's fleet-management interface lets both the mine and our technical team monitor pack health, charging behaviour and fault codes remotely — which is how we support fleets across Africa without waiting for aircraft.

Common First-Timer Mistakes

  • Ordering trucks before the charging plan. The TZ3V needs a 600 A-class dual-gun charger at the mine; grid connection studies take months. Start both clocks together.
  • Assuming diesel-truck tariff codes apply. They sometimes do, often don't. Confirm in writing.
  • Skipping pre-shipment inspection to save USD 400. It has saved buyers from far more expensive surprises.
  • Not briefing the clearing agent on the DG declaration. The single most common cause of port delay on EV truck imports.
  • Under-ordering spares. Mines are remote; a 2–3%-of-fleet-value opening spares package is cheap insurance on uptime.
