The Fatigue Cost Nobody Puts on the Spreadsheet

Fleet spreadsheets capture fuel, tires, maintenance and depreciation with precision - and miss the cost that quietly decides recruitment and retention: driver fatigue. Noise and vibration are not comfort luxuries; they are fatigue accelerants with documented effects on reaction time, error rates and the length of shift a driver can work safely. A diesel heavy-haul tractor at cruise exposes the driver to 70-80 decibels of combined engine, driveline and road noise, plus continuous low-frequency vibration through seat and pedals. Over a nine-hour shift, that exposure accumulates into measurable performance decay. Electric trucks remove the dominant sources at the root - no combustion, no gearshifts, no idle shake - and the TE9L electric heavy-haul tractor cab at cruise is quiet enough for normal conversation, which is the practical definition of a different working environment.

What NVH Actually Measures

NVH - noise, vibration and harshness - is the engineering discipline quantifying what drivers feel. Interior noise at cruise, measured in decibels; seat and steering vibration, measured against ISO exposure limits; harshness, the subjective quality of how the vehicle transmits road inputs. Electric drivetrains transform all three: the TE9L's 510 kW motor produces a fraction of a diesel engine's acoustic output and none of its firing-order vibration, the single-speed drivetrain removes shift shock entirely, and regenerative braking removes the brake judder and engine-brake roar of diesel descents. What remains is wind and tire noise - which is why electric trucks at highway speed sound like the future and at urban speed sound like almost nothing at all.

Fatigue Science: What the Research Shows

Occupational research on professional drivers is consistent: whole-body vibration above exposure action values correlates with musculoskeletal disorders and accelerated fatigue; sustained cab noise above conversational levels elevates stress markers and degrades attention over long shifts. Fleet studies comparing electric and diesel trucks on identical routes show drivers reporting meaningfully lower end-of-shift fatigue on electric units, and the objective data backs the subjective reports - fewer micro-corrections late in shift, fewer minor incidents, lower absenteeism. For Chilean fleet managers, the translation is operational: a driver who finishes shift fresher starts tomorrow better, makes fewer expensive errors, and stays with the company longer in a labor market where good drivers choose their employers.

Urban Delivery: Where NVH Converts to Revenue

In dense urban work, NVH stops being a welfare topic and becomes market access. Noise-restricted delivery windows - early morning, late evening, residential zones, hospital and hotel districts - are expanding in cities worldwide, and they are enforced by complaint, not by legislation alone. An electric heavy-haul tractor working a 5 a.m. delivery generates no complaints; a diesel at idle outside a hotel generates enforcement attention. For Chilean distributors, quiet trucks open delivery windows competitors cannot use, which means more drops per truck per day from the same asset. The TE9L's near-silent low-speed operation, combined with zero exhaust at the dock, is a commercial capability - several fleets report winning contracts specifically on delivery-window flexibility the electric fleet enables.

Charging Planning for TE9L Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TE9L supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 600 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Chile should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
