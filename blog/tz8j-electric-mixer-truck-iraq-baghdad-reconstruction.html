Why Baghdad contractors are electrifying the drum drive

Diesel mixer trucks waste a large share of their fuel simply spinning the drum at a constant low speed while the chassis sits in traffic or waits at the batching plant. On Baghdad routes where average loaded speed sits near 25-35 km/h, that idling drum can consume 0.45-0.55 L/km before the truck even moves its payload efficiently. The TZ8J replaces that parasitic load with a direct electric drum motor drawing from the same 350 kWh pack that propels the chassis, and the driver recovers energy through regenerative braking on the city's frequent stop-and-go cycles.

For fleet owners bidding on government and donor-funded rebuild packages, the electric drum also removes a compliance headache: there is no exhaust stack near the chute, no diesel smell on the pour, and no generator required at the site. That matters when a project sits inside a hospital or school compound where engine noise and fumes are restricted.

Charging the drum without a diesel generator

  • Install a 240 kW DC fast charger at the batching plant yard; a full 10-80% top-up between morning and afternoon loads takes 60-75 minutes.
  • Use off-peak grid power priced at $0.08-0.12/kWh overnight to pre-charge the pack to 100% before the first dispatch.
  • Where the grid is unstable, pair the charger with a 200 kW solar-plus-storage buffer so the drum never stops for lack of electrons.
  • Share one mobile 360 kW charger between three to four mixers on rotating shifts to cut capital cost per truck.
  • Avoid running a diesel generator purely for the drum; the TZ8J's electric motor makes that redundant and saves 30-40 L of diesel per truck per day.

Battery, motor and cooling for 50 C summers

Baghdad routinely sees 45-50 C in July and August, and battery degradation is the first thing fleet managers ask about. The TZ8J uses liquid cooling on both the CATL LFP pack and the LvKong motor as standard, holding cell temperature in the 25-35 C band even when ambient hits 50 C. The drum motor is sealed to IP67 and the high-voltage connectors carry desert-grade gaskets, so dust storms around the southern suburbs do not shorten service intervals. We recommend a pre-cool cycle that runs the pack chiller for five minutes before the first morning load when ambient exceeds 45 C.

Maintenance savings: what 35-40% really means

An electric mixer removes the diesel engine's oil, filters, injectors, turbo, EGR and exhaust after-treatment - the components that drive most unscheduled mixer downtime on dusty Baghdad sites. Our fleet data shows EV trucks cut scheduled maintenance cost by 35-40% versus the diesel equivalent, which on a mixer running 250 days a year is roughly $3,400 in saved workshop bills. The LvKong motor has fewer than 20 moving parts in the drivetrain, so bearing and gearbox interventions that plague diesel drums almost disappear.

  • No engine oil changes: save about $1,200 per year in oil and filters per truck.
  • No diesel fuel-water separator or injector service: save about $900 per year.
  • Brake wear cut by regenerative braking: save about $800 per year in lining and disc costs.
  • Longer tyre life from smoother torque delivery: save about $500 per year.
