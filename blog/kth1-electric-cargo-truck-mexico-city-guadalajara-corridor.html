KTH1 Electric Cargo Truck on the Mexico City-Guadalajara Corridor

The Mexico City-Guadalajara corridor is one of the busiest freight lanes in Latin America, and its stop-and-go highway profile plus toll costs make it a strong case for the electric truck. The KTH heavy electric cargo truck platform, led by the KTH1 variant, turns a 350 kWh pack and steady highway speed into a per-kilometer cost that undercuts diesel even before toll savings are counted. Corridor Profile and Duty Cycle

The corridor runs roughly 550 km between Mexico City and Guadalajara on toll highways with moderate grades. A box truck in this class hauls 12 to 18 tonnes of general cargo and completes the lane in a single driver shift with a mid-route rest. Diesel units burn about 0.45 to 0.55 L/km at highway load, costing $0.40 to $0.72 per kilometer at Mexican diesel prices of $0.90 to $1.30 per liter. The KTH1 draws about 1.1 to 1.6 kWh/km when loaded, and at Mexican commercial electricity of $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh that falls to $0.09 to $0.32 per kilometer.

The KTH1 carries a 350 kWh CATL LFP pack with an 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty, and FOB pricing runs from $80,000 to $115,000 across the KTH platform depending on pack and body. The larger KTH3 variant adds a 424 kWh pack for longer lanes. For the 550 km corridor a single charge covers most of the trip, with a top-up at a highway depot or the Guadalajara yard.

Toll Savings and Highway Economics

Mexican toll roads price by axle and weight, and while an electric truck pays the same toll as diesel by class, the EV truck gains where operators can consolidate because the smoother torque curve and steady speed reduce wear-related delays. More importantly, the depot charging model lets a fleet avoid the time and cost of fuel stops, keeping the truck earning revenue. Where a state offers a low-emission vehicle toll discount, the KTH platform captures that saving directly on the corridor.

The 350 kWh pack gives the KTH1 a loaded range of 200 to 350 km, so the full 550 km lane uses a mid-route top-up. A 360 kW DC charger at the halfway depot moves the pack from 10 to 80 percent in 45 to 90 minutes, which fits a mandatory rest break. Operators running the longer KTH3 with 424 kWh can often complete the lane on a single charge in favorable conditions.
