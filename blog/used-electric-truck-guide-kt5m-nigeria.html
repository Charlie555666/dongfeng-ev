Battery Health: The Core of Used EV Valuation

In a used electric truck, the battery report is the valuation. A 140 kWh CATL LFP pack showing 85-90% state of health after four or five years still delivers most of its original 280 km range, and LFP chemistry degrades slowly and predictably enough that the remaining life is bankable. Buyers should demand the BMS health report, charging history and cycle count, and should understand what they read: daily fast-charging history is worth a discount versus gentle overnight charging, and any gaps in the record are a red flag. The 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty transfers with the truck, so a three-year-old unit still carries five years of factory coverage - a floor under value that no used diesel can offer.

Inspection Checklist Before You Buy

A used electric truck inspection covers familiar ground plus EV-specific items. The conventional checks - chassis, suspension, brakes, tires, accident repair evidence - follow standard practice. The electric layer adds: the battery health report and its consistency with the odometer; a full diagnostic scan for stored high-voltage fault codes; charging inlet and cable condition; a live fast-charge test confirming the pack accepts rated power; and verification that BMS software is current. Cosmetics matter less than records. A KT5M with complete telematics and service history is worth a premium over a shinier truck with missing files. Import Process and Documents for Nigeria

Importing a used electric truck into Nigeria follows the same skeleton as any used commercial vehicle import, with battery-specific additions. The file includes the purchase invoice, deregistration or export documents from the origin country, bill of lading, and the conformity paperwork Nigeria requires - SONCAP certification where applicable. The lithium battery adds shipping documentation: the UN38.3 test summary and a dangerous goods declaration, with state of charge limited to around 30% for ocean transit. Used vehicle age or inspection rules in Nigeria must be checked before purchase, not after - some markets restrict commercial vehicle imports by age. Clearance at Apapa Port in Lagos runs smoothly when the document pack is complete and pre-lodged with a licensed broker in Lagos.

Refurbishment Budget and Parts

Used electric truck refurbishment is cheaper than diesel refurbishment because there is no engine to overhaul. A realistic preparation budget of $1,500-$3,500 covers cosmetics, brake service, tires where needed, cabin consumables, a full high-voltage inspection and BMS software update. Battery module replacement, rarely needed on LFP packs with good health reports, is the one large-ticket possibility - which is why the health report matters so much at purchase. Deployment Timeline from Order to Operation

Realistic scheduling prevents the most common fleet electrification failures. A typical KT5M deployment runs five to seven months end to end: production takes 30-60 days depending on configuration and order size; ocean freight to Apapa Port in Lagos adds 25-45 days; customs clearance and homologation steps add two to six weeks depending on Nigerian procedures. Driver Acceptance and Daily Operation

Driver acceptance decides whether an electric fleet reaches its modeled savings, and the KT5M earns acceptance quickly. The 160 kW motor delivers instant torque with no gear hunting, the cab is quieter than any diesel equivalent, and regenerative braking reduces fatigue in stop-start duty. Drivers typically adapt within a week, and most prefer the electric truck once they experience a full shift without engine noise and vibration. The habits that matter are small: smooth acceleration, anticipation to maximize regeneration, charging connection on return to depot, and honest reporting of dash warnings. Fleets in Nigeria report the fastest adoption when they involve senior drivers in the pilot phase and let peer experience do the convincing, rather than imposing the change top-down. Energy consumption per driver is visible in the telematics data, and a little friendly competition between drivers on efficiency measurably improves fleet-wide consumption in the first quarter.

Charging Planning for KT5M Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT5M supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 140 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Nigeria should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
