The Model Mine

Assume a hard-rock operation with a 12 km loaded-out cycle: 12 km loaded to the crusher, 12 km return. Three cycles plus lunch break per 10-hour shift, 1.5 shifts/day, 330 operating days — roughly 120 km/day, 39,600 km/year per truck, all of it pit ramp, unsealed. Power at USD 0.10/kWh from a mine-connected solar-plus-grid hybrid; diesel delivered to site at USD 1.10/L (mine-gate reality — the expensive kind of diesel).

Reduced to Tonne-Kilometres

Payload in this class runs roughly 45–50 t per loaded trip. At 19,800 loaded km/year (half of total distance) and 47 t average payload, each truck moves ~930,000 tonne-km annually. Dividing the operating totals:

  • TZ3V electric: ~USD 0.020 per tonne-km
  • Diesel: ~USD 0.053 per tonne-km

The electric truck moves material at roughly 38% of the diesel's operating cost per tonne-km. Haul cost is typically 40–50% of a surface mine's operating cost — a mine electrifying its tipper fleet is attacking the largest controllable line on the P&L.

Where the Diesel Still Fights Back

  1. Weak-grid mines without power budget. No charger, no case — although hybrid solar-plus-storage installtions now flip even remote sites.
  2. Irregular long moves. Relocation runs between sites over 250 km need charging planning the diesel ignores.
  3. Liquid resale markets. Diesel tippers still resell faster in some jurisdictions; electric residuals are maturing but thinner outside mining-savvy markets.
  4. Energy price risk in reverse. A mine on expensive contracted power (USD 0.18+/kWh) sees its advantage compress — this is why we model each site's actual tariff before quoting.

The Availability Question Answered

Mining sceptics raise availability: doesn't charging put a truck out of service? The arithmetic says no, if the operation is structured:

  • Opportunity charging during shift breaks — a 40-minute lunch at a 600 A dual-gun charger returns 50–60% SOC; the truck never leaves the rotation.
  • Battery swap architecture — the TZ3V family supports exchange; a 5–6 minute swap makes the electric tipper more available than diesel, whose fuel stops and maintenance downtime are longer.
  • Regenerative braking on the return leg — downhill, empty returns recover 10–15% of the cycle's energy, shrinking the charging requirement in the first place.

Decision Framework for the Mine

  1. Compute your current haul cost per tonne-km from fuel and maintenance actuals — not fleet averages, per-route numbers.
  2. Price your true delivered power (grid tariff, solar LCOE, or hybrid blend) in USD/kWh.
  3. If electricity lands under ~USD 0.15/kWh and daily cycles stay under 250 km, the electric case is overwhelmingly positive — typically 50–60% operating cost reduction on haul.
  4. Pilot on the highest-tonnage route with two trucks and a single 350 kW+ charger; scale by route.
