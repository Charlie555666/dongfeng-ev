Electric Cargo Trucks for Bangladesh's Garment Corridor: Dhaka to Chattogram

Why the Garment Corridor Fits an Electric Truck

The Dhaka to Chattogram corridor is the spine of Bangladesh's garment export economy, carrying finished apparel from factories to the port around 250 km away. The KT5M electric cargo truck is a practical electric truck for this lane because the distance sits inside a single-charge range and the predictable highway duty is ideal for battery electric haulage. With fuel import costs a constant pressure on margins, an EV truck converts a volatile diesel expense into a stable grid-charging cost.

The KT5M electric cargo truck gives garment suppliers a clean, quiet way to move containers and cartons while meeting the sustainability audits that international buyers increasingly require.

Range Math and Charging Stops

The corridor is about 250 km, which fits the KT5M's 260 km range with a small buffer. Smart operators charge overnight at the Dhaka depot, drive to Chattogram, and use a 240 kW DC charger at the port logistics zone for the return leg. This removes range anxiety without building new infrastructure along the highway.

  • Depart Dhaka at 80 percent state of charge after an overnight depot charge.
  • Drive the 250 km corridor with payload; arrive with reserve margin.
  • Top up at a Chattogram 240 kW DC hub during unloading.
  • Return to Dhaka on the same charge plus the top-up.
  • Keep one spare tractor on battery swap for continuous daily lanes.

Factory Gate to Port Handoff

The Dhaka to Chattogram corridor is really a chain of handoffs: factory loading, highway transit, port gate queue and vessel loading. Each step risks delay, and an electric truck helps at the queue stage because it can hold the box at temperature-neutral state while idling with near-zero energy draw, unlike a diesel that keeps burning fuel in line. Suppliers who time departures to the port window and top up at Chattogram return with margin to spare, improving the on-time shipment rate that buyers score in their supplier audits.

Driver Training and Shift Planning

Bangladeshi drivers moving from diesel to the KT5M adapt quickly because the controls are simpler, but shift planning matters more. A corridor run is best done as a single charge outbound plus a port top-up, so drivers must learn to read state of charge against the 250 km distance and resist the habit of topping up unnecessarily. A short training covering safe charging, telematics and the regen brake feel is enough. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd. runs this session at handover so the first week runs without surprises.

Customs and Import Logistics at Chattogram

Getting the KT5M into Bangladesh means clearing Chattogram port, and commercial EV imports follow the same customs path as any truck, with the battery treated as part of the vehicle rather than a separate hazardous consignment when shipped built-up. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd. prepares the commercial invoice, packing list and certificate of origin so the file moves quickly, and recommends a local clearing agent familiar with electric commercial vehicles. Once cleared, the truck plates in Dhaka and enters the corridor directly. Because the KT5M has no engine emissions hardware to certify, the inspection step is shorter than for a diesel, which shaves days off the time from arrival to first revenue run and lets the garment exporter start recovering the purchase sooner through the per-trip savings that make the electric truck compelling on this specific lane.
