Why Urban Construction Favors an Electric Truck

Construction haul cycles in Lagos are short, typically 5 to 25 km between aggregate source, batching plant and building site, with heavy stop-start and low average speed. That profile is ideal for an electric dump truck because regen captures energy on every deceleration and the electric motor delivers the low-speed torque needed to pull a loaded tipper off a muddy site. Sites inside the metropolis also face tightening diesel-engine restrictions during peak pollution days, which an electric truck sidesteps entirely.

Construction Duty Cycle in Lagos

A typical TZ5E shift starts at a sand depot in Badagry or a gravel source on the mainland, makes repeated runs to a Lekki or Ikoyi building site, and returns empty. Each loaded run draws about 18 to 24 kWh, and regen on the return recovers 25 to 35 percent. Over a 14-cycle shift total draw is near 230 kWh, leaving comfortable reserve in the 400 kWh pack.

  • Load at depot: 6 minutes, 30 t aggregate
  • Haul to site: 6 to 12 km, mostly below 40 km/h
  • Tip and return: regen recovers climb energy

Charging at the Plant and Site

A batching plant or major site can host a 180 kW DC charger for about USD 45,000 and charge tippers during the operator break, or run two 90 kW units for a small fleet. Because the charger sits at the plant, trucks charge while loaded for the next run. Operators running the Dongfeng TZ5E electric dump truck pair charging with the natural lull between concrete pours, so charging adds no downtime.

Site Air Quality and Community Relations

Building sites near residential Lagos districts draw complaints about diesel dust and noise. An electric truck removes tailpipe emissions at the loading face and reduces noise during early-morning runs, improving relations with nearby communities and helping contractors meet corporate environmental targets for the electric truck market in Nigeria. Cleaner sites also reduce respiratory risk for the workforce.

Planning depot charging for this EV truck

Charging an electric truck is a depot discipline, not a public-network gamble. A 350 kWh pack working a 240 km day typically returns with 25 to 35 percent in reserve, so overnight demand is about 210 kWh per truck. On a 120 kW DC charger the pack climbs from 20 to 80 percent in roughly 105 minutes; on 240 kW that falls to about 52 minutes, which lets two trucks share one charger on staggered shifts.

Size the site transformer for your busiest shift plus 30 percent headroom, keep cable runs under 30 metres to limit voltage drop, and meter each charger separately so energy cost per truck stays visible. In Nigeria, grid connection lead times of three to nine months are common, so apply for the connection the day you sign the truck order, not the day the ship arrives. Energy cost per kilometre: EV truck versus diesel

A comparable diesel urban dump truck burns about 0.38 litres per kilometre in mixed duty; at $0.95 per litre that is roughly $0.36 per km. The electric equivalent consumes about 1.15 kWh per km fully loaded; at an industrial tariff of $0.10 per kWh the cost is near $0.12 per km, and overnight or solar-coupled charging can push it below $0.08. Over 70,000 km a year the saving is about $17,000 per truck, before any carbon or low-emission-zone benefit.

The gap widens in stop-start duty, where regenerative braking returns 15 to 25 percent of traction energy instead of burning it as brake dust. In Nigeria, where diesel quality and price both fluctuate, locking in an electricity tariff also removes a major budgeting variable from the fleet P&L.

Maintenance and uptime economics

The TZ5E has roughly one moving drivetrain part where a diesel has hundreds: no engine oil, no DPF, no turbocharger, no clutch, no multi-gear transmission service. Scheduled maintenance lands near $0.035 per km against $0.11 per km for a diesel equivalent, a saving of more than $5,000 a year at typical utilisation. Brake pads last two to three times longer because the motor does most of the deceleration.

Uptime matters more than parts cost. Fleet data from Chinese operators running the same e-axle platform shows 96 to 98 percent availability once drivers are trained, versus 92 to 94 percent for diesel trucks of the same age. What to confirm before you order

  • Duty cycle: longest daily route, loaded and empty legs, gradient profile, and ambient temperature range.
  • Charging: available transformer capacity, overnight window, and whether opportunity charging or battery swap fits the shift plan.
  • Homologation: which national or regional standard applies in Nigeria, and who holds the type-approval paperwork.
  • Payload: body weight versus rated GVW, so the 240 km real range holds with your actual load.
  • Support: local partner for HV work, spare-parts stock level, and response time commitments in writing.
  • Finance: FOB or CFR terms, letter-of-credit conditions, and currency cover for the balance on arrival.
