Why phosphate haulage suits an electric truck

Mine haul cycles repeat the same loop hundreds of times per shift: load, climb the ramp, dump, return empty. That predictable profile lets the battery be sized tightly and lets regeneration recover energy on every descent. A diesel tipper spends much of its life at low load factor while still idling at the face, wasting fuel and producing heat in a dusty environment. The KTA1 removes the combustion engine entirely, so there is no diesel exhaust in the cab and no coolant system to fail in abrasive mine air. For sites with grid or solar power at the workshop, the operating cost advantage is immediate and large.

Haul cycle and energy use

On a 6-10 km mine loop with a loaded climb, the KTA1 consumes about 1.4-1.6 kWh per km when fully loaded and far less on the empty return thanks to regeneration. A 350 kWh pack therefore covers 220-280 km of mixed duty before the mid-shift charge, which is enough for a full production window on most Moroccan phosphate benches. The liquid-cooled battery stays inside its 25-35 C operating window even in summer, and the 8-year or 4,500-cycle warranty matches the expected working life of the truck body.

Charging at the mine workshop

  • Place a 240 kW DC charger at the workshop and opportunity-charge during loader queues.
  • Schedule the 20-80 percent fill in 60-90 minutes between production shifts.
  • Add a solar canopy so daytime charging draws from site generation.
  • Keep a second pack on a swap cart only if the site runs continuous 24-hour haulage.
  • Monitor state of charge remotely from the dispatch office through the fleet portal.

Durability in dust and heat

Phosphate dust is fine and abrasive, so the KTA1 seals the battery enclosure to IP67 and routes intake air through dual filters. The liquid cooling loop keeps cells in the 25-35 C band even when ambient temperatures exceed 40 C at the bench, which protects cycle life. The CATL LFP chemistry resists thermal runaway better than nickel chemistries, an important point on a site where vehicles work close together. The 8-year warranty gives the mine a fixed battery cost for the full depreciation period of the asset.

Battery recycling and end-of-life

After 4,500 cycles or eight years the LFP pack still holds most of its capacity and is valuable as stationary storage at the mine, where it can buffer the solar canopy or smooth the workshop load. CATL supports a take-back path that recovers the lithium, iron and phosphate for reuse, so the pack never becomes hazardous waste. This second-life value lowers the true cost of ownership and helps Moroccan sites meet tightening environmental rules on battery disposal. We document the end-of-life plan in the original supply contract so the mine has a clear, funded route when the warranty period ends.

Safety and ventilation in the workshop

Although the pack is sealed and the high-voltage system isolates on fault, the workshop still needs basic electric-vehicle precautions: a marked charge bay, a fire blanket rated for lithium cells and a trained isolation officer. The KTA1 training covers these, and the dispatched telematics flag any pack anomaly before the truck returns. Moroccan sites already running electric shovels or drills will find the same rules apply, so adding the KTA1 needs no new safety regime, only a short refresher for the haulage crew and the night-shift supervisor.

Operator comfort and cabin ergonomics

The KTA1 cab is quieter and cooler than a diesel tipper because there is no combustion engine bay and no exhaust heat, which matters on a Moroccan bench where cabin temperature can climb through a long shift. Smooth electric torque removes the gearshift shock that wears out diesel drivers, and the low centre of gravity from the floor-mounted pack improves stability on ramps. Operators report less fatigue and fewer vibration-related aches, which lowers absence and helps the mine keep the haul fleet fully staffed across the production week.
