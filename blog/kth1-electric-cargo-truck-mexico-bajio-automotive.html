Why Bajio JIT Routes Favor an EV

The Bajio is a closed loop of OEM and Tier-1 plants 40 to 120 km apart, so trucks return to a central cross-dock every shift and a single charging hub serves the whole shuttle fleet. A diesel truck on these loops idles in plant queues and burns fuel in the stop-go crawl of industrial park access roads; the KTH recovers that energy through regeneration and never idles a starter or turbo. For a fleet moving 200 inbound containers of parts per week, the consistency of electric range removes the fuel-price volatility that has swung diesel 25 percent year to year.

Nearshoring has also raised the bar on ESG reporting. Global OEMs in the Bajio now score suppliers on Scope 3 transport emissions, and an electric shuttle with verified charging data gives your logistics contract a measurable decarbonization number that a diesel fleet cannot match without buying offsets.

Energy and Fuel Cost Comparison

Mexican industrial electricity runs about $0.09/kWh on negotiated CFE tariffs and up to $0.16/kWh at peak, while diesel sits near $1.05/L. The KTH1 consumes roughly 1.1 to 1.4 kWh per km under a 16 t load, so a 90 km loop costs about $9.00 to $20.00 in electricity versus $35.00 to $50.00 in diesel. Across 70,000 km per year the saving reaches $26,000 to $34,000 per truck before maintenance, which is the number your controller will use to approve the conversion.
