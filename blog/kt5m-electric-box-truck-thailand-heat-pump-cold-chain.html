KT5M Electric Box Truck with Heat Pump: Thailand Cold Chain Guide

Why a Heat Pump Changes the Cold Chain Math

The KT5M electric box truck is built for Thailand's fast-growing frozen-food and pharmaceutical distribution network, and the single biggest efficiency lever on a refrigerated electric truck is the cabin and cargo HVAC strategy. A conventional diesel reefer runs an independent combustion unit, but an EV must pull every watt from the same battery that drives the wheels. Dongfeng fits the KT5M with a heat-pump HVAC system that moves heat instead of generating it, cutting the energy the reefer box consumes by 8 to 12 percent per 100 km compared with a resistive heater. On a Bangkok to Ayutthaya loop that margin is the difference between completing four delivery runs per charge and returning to depot early with payload still on board.

Thai operators often underestimate how much of a battery budget the box itself eats. A poorly managed reefer can consume 18 to 22 kWh per 100 km of conditioned cargo space, which on a 336 kWh pack is the equivalent of 50 to 65 km of driving range. The heat pump recovers waste heat from the motor and inverter coolant loops, so the same thermal energy that would be dumped through the radiator is redirected to keep the box at minus 18 °C. That closed-loop thinking is why the KT5M is positioned as a true cold-chain tool rather than a converted dry box.

Range Impact: Resistive Heater vs Heat Pump Reefer

The reefer compressor itself is non-negotiable, but the way you condition the box matters. A resistive heater draws a flat electrical load regardless of outside temperature, while a heat pump inverts its duty cycle and recovers waste heat from the motor and inverter coolant loops. Field data from similar 336 kWh platforms shows the following split on a 280 km mixed urban run with a minus 18 °C set point:

Bangkok Delivery Cycle Planning

A typical Bangkok frozen-goods route starts at a Lat Krabang cold hub before 06:00, runs 9 to 12 drops through the city, and returns by mid-afternoon. With 260 to 310 km of certified range and aggressive regen on the Sukhumvit gridlock, the KT5M completes the loop with 25 to 35 percent state of charge remaining. Drivers charge during the 14:00 to 17:00 off-peak window at the depot using a 240 kW DC unit, reaching 80 percent before the evening shift.

  • Morning trunk run: Lat Krabang to Bang Sue, 38 km, 11 drops, pre-cooled box at minus 18 °C.
  • Midday replenish: Charoen Krung wholesale market loop, 27 km, mixed frozen and chilled sets.
  • Afternoon return: Sukhumvit residential delivery, 19 km, then depot for charge.
  • Charge window: 240 kW DC, 20 to 80 percent in roughly 55 minutes on off-peak tariff.
  • Driver habit: one-pedal regen recovers 10 to 14 percent of consumed energy in city traffic.

Operators running two shifts can add a second 240 kW charger and run the KT5M up to 320 km per day across both drivers without mid-day public charging.

Charging and Depot Setup

Most Thai 3PL operators already have three-phase power at their hub. A single 240 kW DC charger serves three to four KT5M trucks per shift with staggered plug-in. Where land and grid allow, a 360 kW unit brings 20 to 80 percent charge down to 45 minutes. Battery swap is not offered on the KT5M, so depot scheduling rather than public infrastructure is the planning priority.

For sites without high-power connection, Fenghan can specify a 120 kW unit paired with a 200 kWh buffer battery so the truck still charges overnight from a limited supply. This keeps the capital cost of the energy side below THB 2.5M for a four-truck depot.

Cold Chain Compliance and Telematics

Thai food exporters must log box temperature for EU and Japanese buyers. The KT5M supports a CAN-bus feed from the reefer controller to the fleet telematics portal, recording set point, actual temperature, and door-open events every five minutes. That data exports as a PDF audit trail, removing the paper logbooks that Thai customs and buyers increasingly reject.

  • Set-point range: minus 25 °C to plus 12 °C, switchable per compartment.
  • Alarm: door-open and temperature excursion push to dispatcher phone.
  • Report: automated daily CSV for cold-chain certification.

Driver Comfort and Night Curfew

Bangkok and Pattaya enforce tightening noise and emissions curfews near residential zones. The KT5M's near-silent operation lets it serve hotel and hospital deliveries before 07:00 without complaint, a window diesel box trucks lose to idling restrictions. The heat-pump cabin keeps the driver comfortable with the engine off, so the truck can precondition the box and cab on grid power before the driver even arrives.
