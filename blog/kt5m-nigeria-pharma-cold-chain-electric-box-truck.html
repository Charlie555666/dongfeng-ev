Nigeria's pharmaceutical supply chain loses an estimated 20 to 30 percent of temperature-sensitive medicines to cold-chain gaps between Lagos ports and up-country pharmacies. A purpose-built EV truck with an integrated refrigeration unit solves two problems at once: it removes diesel emissions from the medicine stream and it cuts the running cost of the chiller. The Dongfeng KT5M electric box truck carries a 140 kWh CATL LFP pack, a 4x2 insulated box body and a powered refrigeration unit holding 2 to 8 degrees Celsius, priced at USD 60,000 to 75,000 FOB China.

The Cold-Chain Gap in Lagos Pharma Distribution

Most Nigerian vaccine and insulin shipments arrive through Apapa and Tin Can ports, then move by road to distributors in Ikeja, Agege and the mainland. Traditional diesel box trucks run a belt-driven chiller that consumes 2 to 3 liters of fuel per hour even when the truck is parked at a pharmacy. In traffic-clogged Lagos that can mean 6 to 9 hours of continuous chilling per delivery day, burning 15 to 25 liters of diesel just to keep the box cold. An electric truck powers the chiller from the main traction pack, so the cooling cost tracks the far lower price of electricity.

How the Electric Chiller Changes the Math

A diesel chiller runs whether the truck moves or not. The KT5M electric chiller draws from the same 140 kWh pack that drives the wheels, managed by a single thermal controller thatprioritizes the cabin and cargo setpoints. In steady 2 to 8 degree Celsius operation the chiller consumes about 4.5 kW, which over a 7-hour delivery day equals 31.5 kWh, just 22 percent of the pack. The remaining energy moves the truck.

  • Ikeja cluster: 9 drops, 38 km, chiller steady at 4 deg C
  • Agege and Abule-Egba: 7 drops, 29 km, door openings averaged 4 minutes each
  • Return to depot at 16:30 with 30 percent charge, top-up to 100 percent in 75 minutes

Planning depot charging for this EV truck

Charging an electric truck is a depot discipline, not a public-network gamble. A 140 kWh pack working a 280 km day typically returns with 25 to 35 percent in reserve, so overnight demand is about 84 kWh per truck. On a 120 kW DC charger the pack climbs from 20 to 80 percent in roughly 42 minutes; on 240 kW that falls to about 21 minutes, which lets two trucks share one charger on staggered shifts.

Size the site transformer for your busiest shift plus 30 percent headroom, keep cable runs under 30 metres to limit voltage drop, and meter each charger separately so energy cost per truck stays visible. In Nigeria, grid connection lead times of three to nine months are common, so apply for the connection the day you sign the truck order, not the day the ship arrives. Energy cost per kilometre: EV truck versus diesel

A comparable diesel box truck burns about 0.38 litres per kilometre in mixed duty; at $0.95 per litre that is roughly $0.36 per km. The electric equivalent consumes about 1.15 kWh per km fully loaded; at an industrial tariff of $0.10 per kWh the cost is near $0.12 per km, and overnight or solar-coupled charging can push it below $0.08. Over 70,000 km a year the saving is about $17,000 per truck, before any carbon or low-emission-zone benefit.

The gap widens in stop-start duty, where regenerative braking returns 15 to 25 percent of traction energy instead of burning it as brake dust. In Nigeria, where diesel quality and price both fluctuate, locking in an electricity tariff also removes a major budgeting variable from the fleet P&L.

Maintenance and uptime economics

The KT5M has roughly one moving drivetrain part where a diesel has hundreds: no engine oil, no DPF, no turbocharger, no clutch, no multi-gear transmission service. Scheduled maintenance lands near $0.035 per km against $0.11 per km for a diesel equivalent, a saving of more than $5,000 a year at typical utilisation. Brake pads last two to three times longer because the motor does most of the deceleration.

Uptime matters more than parts cost. Fleet data from Chinese operators running the same e-axle platform shows 96 to 98 percent availability once drivers are trained, versus 92 to 94 percent for diesel trucks of the same age.
