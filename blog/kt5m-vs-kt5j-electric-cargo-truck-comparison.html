KT5M vs KT5J: Which Dongfeng Electric Cargo Truck Fits Your Fleet?

Choosing between two EV truck platforms in the Dongfeng cargo range comes down to one question: how much payload and range does your Vietnam operation actually need? The KT5M and KT5J are both battery-electric cargo trucks built by Dongfeng, but they sit in different weight classes and serve different duty cycles. The KT5M is a 12–18 tonne box platform with a larger pack, while the KT5J is a lighter 7.5–9 tonne city and regional delivery truck. This comparison breaks down battery, payload, range, charging and total cost so a fleet buyer can match the right Dongfeng electric truck to the right lane, and avoid paying for capacity that sits idle. It also addresses the practical rollout questions — chargers, driver training and scaling — that decide whether the saving lands on the P&L.

KT5M vs KT5J: Head-to-Head Specifications

The clearest way to compare the two is a side-by-side table. Both use CATL LFP chemistry and LvKong permanent-magnet motors, so the real difference is scale, not technology. The KT5M electric cargo truck carries more and goes longer between charges; the KT5J electric cargo truck is the lower-entry, higher-turn fleet tool. Neither is a compromise on the core electric architecture — they are simply sized for different freight.

Payload, Body and Duty-Cycle Fit

The KT5M’s 12–18 t envelope suits furniture, beverage, parcel and supermarket replenishment where a full 18 t load fills the box before the range runs out. The KT5J’s 7.5–9 t envelope is the right size for dense city distribution, food delivery and light industrial runs where weight is rarely the limit. Most Vietnam fleets run both: the KT5J for the stop-start urban last mile, the KT5M for the heavier inter-provincial trunk. Specifying the wrong size hurts twice — an oversized truck wastes battery on empty mass, an undersized truck leaves freight on the dock.

  • Urban last mile. The KT5J’s lighter footprint and tighter turning circle suit Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City lanes.
  • Trunk distribution. The KT5M covers HCMC – Can Tho or Hanoi – Hai Phong on a single charge.
  • Same charger. Both accept the same DC posts, so a depot needs one charger type for the whole cargo fleet.
  • Shared spares. LvKong motor modules and LFP packs are common across both, simplifying the parts pool.

Battery, Motor and Charging Reality

Both platforms use CATL LFP cells, which tolerate the partial-state-of-charge depot charging and high ambient heat of Vietnam better than NMC chemistry. The LvKong permanent-magnet motor gives strong low-speed torque for loading docks and ramps. DC fast charging restores 20–80% in 35–70 minutes depending on pack size; overnight AC at the depot covers the daily cycle for most operators. Battery swap is not offered on the cargo range, so charging infrastructure planning is the first procurement step and should be scoped before the trucks arrive.

Total Cost of Ownership in Vietnam Service

Vietnam industrial electricity runs roughly US$0.08–0.12/kWh, and diesel sits near US$1.00–1.10/l. A KT5M on 30,000 km/year at 1.1 kWh/km draws about 33,000 kWh; at US$0.10/kWh that is US$3,300. A comparable diesel at 24 l/100 km burns 7,200 l, about US$7,560. The energy gap alone is ~US$4,260/year, plus ~US$1,500 maintenance, a ~US$5,760 annual advantage before the FOB step. The KT5J shows the same ratio at lower absolute numbers, which is why the smaller truck often pays back first for an urban operator.

Charging Infrastructure and Depot Planning

The lowest-risk rollout is a 120 kW DC post at the main depot plus a 60 kW AC overnight post for topping up. The DC post restores 20–80% during driver breaks; the AC post handles the 18:00–05:00 idle window at the lowest tariff. Because both KT5M and KT5J share the connector standard, one DC post serves the mixed fleet on a staggered roster, so no unit waits for the charger during the day. Plan the power connection before the trucks land, because the utility HV application is usually the long pole in the tent.

Driver Training and Maintenance Discipline

Electric trucks reward smooth driving: hard acceleration burns pack faster and regen rewards anticipation. Train drivers on one-pedal driving and on keeping the pack in the 20–80% band to protect the 4,500-cycle warranty. Maintenance is lighter than diesel — no oil, no DPF, fewer brake events — but the high-voltage items (pack, motor, inverter) are field-swapped, not field-repaired, so hold one spare module centrally. A two-day technician course covers isolation and basic fault codes and keeps availability above 95%.

Vietnam Market and Deployment Notes

Vietnam’s electrification incentives and growing expressway network make the cargo EV truck case stronger every year. The Vietnam electric truck market guide covers homologation, left-hand-drive build, and HCMC/Hanoi depot charging layout. For most operators the right answer is a mixed fleet: KT5J units for the city and the KT5M for the trunk, charged from one shared DC post. Start with one trial unit on the easiest lane for 90 days, log kWh/km, then size the rest from real data.

Scaling From Trial to Fleet

Fleets that scale in waves beat those that buy everything at once. After the 90-day trial confirms kWh/km, the second wave should target the highest-volume lane first, because that lane’s daily return already fits a single charge. Cooperatives and 3PLs can pool capital to share one DC post and one solar array, which halves the per-truck infrastructure cost and makes the smaller operator’s payback competitive with the large fleet’s. Keep a shared telemetry dashboard so every operator sees the real energy cost per tonne-km, and the business case stays visible to finance.

Which Dongfeng Electric Truck Should You Buy

If your daily run is under 180 km and your loads stay under 9 t, the KT5J is the lower-risk first EV truck: cheaper FOB, faster charge, easier city fit. If you run heavier freight or longer provincial lanes, the KT5M pays back through higher utilisation and a single-charge trunk range. Neither is wrong; they are tools for different lanes. Spec the KT5J for the last mile and the KT5M for the trunk, and your Vietnam fleet gets the lowest total cost per tonne-km with one shared charger and one shared parts pool.
