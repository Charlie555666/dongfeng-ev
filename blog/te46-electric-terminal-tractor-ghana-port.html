Why Terminal Shunting Is the Ideal EV Duty

Terminal tractors live inside a fenced port. They never face public-road range risk, they return past the same charging point dozens of times per shift, and their duty cycle of crawl, stop, lift, and crawl again lets regenerative braking recover energy all day. Shunting Performance in the Yard

The TE46's 282 kW motor delivers instant torque for pulling laden containers off the quay, and its hydraulic fifth wheel lifts and couples trailers without the driver leaving the cab. In yard duty the limiting factor is never range but shift length, and the TE46 comfortably covers a 10 to 14 hour shift on a single charge because shunting speeds are low and regen recovers energy at every stop. Operators report 60 to 90 container moves per shift in typical Tema yard layouts.

The quiet electric drivetrain also matters in a terminal where tractors work meters from stevedores. Removing diesel exhaust and cutting noise improves working conditions and helps the terminal meet the occupational-health clauses increasingly written into port concession agreements.

Charging Plan for a Terminal Fleet

The practical model is one or two 120 kW DC chargers at the yard workshop. Because tractors rotate through breaks and shift changes, a single charger can keep three or four units in service across a 24-hour operation. Charging during meal breaks and shift handovers costs the operation nothing in lost moves.

  • Install a 120 kW charger at the yard workshop for break-time top-ups.
  • Rotate tractors through charging during shift handovers.
  • Use opportunity charging during meal breaks to extend shift coverage.
  • Track moves per charge to benchmark each yard loop.
  • Keep one spare tractor on charge during peak vessel windows.
