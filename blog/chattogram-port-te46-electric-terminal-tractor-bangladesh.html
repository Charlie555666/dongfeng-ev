Chattogram Port Drayage: TE46 Electric Terminal Tractor for Bangladesh's Gateway

Chattogram moves roughly 3.2 million TEU a year — over 90% of Bangladesh's containerized trade — through a terminal that still runs on diesel yard tractors averaging more than a decade old. Every one of those machines works the perfect EV duty cycle: short runs, low speeds, endless idling at the quay crane, and a home depot within 5 km at all times. For terminal operators and the off-dock ICDs ringing the city, the Dongfeng TE46 electric terminal tractor is the single fastest electrification win available in Bangladesh today.

Why Chattogram's duty cycle is tailor-made for electric trucks

Port drayage is the cheapest segment of trucking to electrify, and Chattogram is an extreme case. A yard tractor or short-haul drayage unit at the port typically covers 60-120 km per shift, rarely exceeds 40 km/h, and spends 30-40% of engine hours idling in container queues. Diesel engines hate this cycle — it is where fuel consumption per kilometer peaks and where DPFs and injectors die early. An electric drivetrain does the opposite: it uses almost no energy while stationary, recovers energy under the constant braking of stop-start yard work, and delivers full torque from zero rpm for moving 40-ton container loads off the line.

The economics: what does a TE46 save per year in Chattogram?

Run the numbers on a single-shift drayage operation, 300 working days a year: the diesel unit burns roughly USD 12,000 in fuel; the TE46 consumes USD 4,000-4,800 in grid electricity. Add maintenance — port operators we work with budget 40-50% lower workshop spend on the EV — and annual operating savings land between USD 9,000 and 11,000 per truck. Against the purchase premium of an electric tractor, that is a payback of roughly 2.5-3.5 years on a machine with an 8-10 year service life, and the CATL LFP battery carries an 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty that outlasts the payback period by a wide margin. Bangladesh's grid is gas-heavy but reliable in the port zone, and several ICD operators are already pairing depot chargers with rooftop solar to cut energy cost further.

Which operations around the port should electrify first?

  • Terminal yard tractors — quay-to-stack moves, the classic TE46 role with its fifth-wheel terminal configuration.
  • Off-dock ICD shuttles — the 5-15 km runs between the port and inland container depots at Patenga, Halishahar and the Bay Terminal area.
  • Bonded warehouse distribution — export-import cargo moving to CEPZ and the Karnaphuli EPZ, where a KT5M box truck covers urban delivery legs; see our Bangladesh EV truck market page for the full model lineup and import notes.
  • Future Mongla and Payra terminals — new-build terminals can spec charging infrastructure from day one instead of retrofitting.

What about charging infrastructure at a congested port?

Space is Chattogram's scarcest resource, so the infrastructure answer matters. A fleet of 10 TE46 tractors needs only two 240-360 kW DC fast chargers shared on rotation — trucks charge during shift changes and meal breaks, adding 60-70% charge in under an hour, with overnight AC charging as the backbone. Total charger footprint is smaller than a single container slot. Battery swapping, standard on several Dongfeng heavy EV configurations, is the alternative for three-shift operations: a swap takes 5-6 minutes, comparable to a diesel fill.
