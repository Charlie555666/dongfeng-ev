Spare Parts Planning for Electric Truck Export Fleets

The good news about electric truck spare parts is how short the list is. The bad news is that an export fleet that plans it like a diesel fleet will stock the wrong things and miss the right ones. This guide explains what actually wears on an electric truck, what never needs replacing, and how to size a first-year parts kit for a fleet operating far from the factory.

What Never Wears: The Diesel Parts You Stop Buying

The electric powertrain deletes entire maintenance categories: no engine oil, filters, injectors, turbocharger, exhaust aftertreatment, clutch, or multi-gear transmission. The LvKong motor is sealed and effectively service-free, and regenerative braking cuts friction brake wear by an estimated 40 percent. Fleets switching from diesel typically cut their parts spend by more than half before buying anything new. The KTH3 electric cargo truck and its siblings share this simplified architecture.

What Does Wear: The Real Replacement List

The parts an export fleet actually consumes are tires, brake pads and discs at longer intervals, suspension bushings, cabin filters, coolant and brake fluid, wiper blades, and body-specific wear items such as tipper seals, compactor pins, or sweeper brushes. High-voltage components are sealed and warrantied rather than serviced, so the kit focuses on conventional wear items plus a small set of electrical consumables like connector seals and coolant hose.

Sizing the Kit: Distance Decides Depth

The rule is lead time times failure rate. A fleet two weeks from the regional buffer stocks consumables and fast-moving wear items locally and relies on the buffer for the rest; a fleet six weeks away doubles the depth of everything and adds one contingency electrical set. Our Nigeria electric truck market guide shows how West African fleets use the regional hub to keep local stock lean without risking downtime.

  • Stock all fast-moving wear items locally from day one.
  • Size depth by resupply lead time, not by diesel-era habit.
  • Keep one contingency electrical set regardless of distance.
  • Pre-order body wear items matched to each truck's duty.
  • Review consumption quarterly and tune the kit.
