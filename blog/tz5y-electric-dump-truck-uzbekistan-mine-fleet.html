For mine fleet managers, a battery-electric dump truck delivers the two things surface mining needs most: low cost per tonne moved and clean operation in confined, dust-heavy pits. Why Uzbekistan Mines Choose an Electric Truck

Uzbekistan's principal mining regions sit at meaningful altitude and carry fine, abrasive dust that penetrates every sealset on a conventional truck. The TZ5Y is sealed to mine standards: inlets are high-mounted and filtration is upgraded for ore dust, while the electric drivetrain has fewer ingress points than a diesel engine. At Almalyk's Altymalik and Kalmakyr pits, where grades are steady and cycles are short, regenerative braking recovers energy on every descent - the single biggest reason electric haulage beats diesel on total cost. A loaded truck climbing out of the pit spends energy; the same truck coasting down recovers a meaningful share of it, and over a shift those recoveries add up to tonnes of avoided diesel.

Altitude is not a problem for an EV truck. Unlike a diesel engine that loses power as air thins, the LvKong motor delivers full rated torque from sea level to the high plateaus of Navoi region. That consistency protects cycle times when a diesel fleet would slow down, and it keeps the haul road flowing during the cold, thin-air mornings when productivity matters most. For a mine paid by tonne moved, a truck that never derates is a truck that never loses the shift, and the absence of diesel exhaust also cuts local particulate at the source for ESG reporting.

Dust, Altitude and Hot-Climate Protection

Mine air is the enemy of every component. The TZ5Y addresses it directly, and the savings show up as uptime rather than brochure claims:

  • High-mounted, pre-filtered cab and electronics inlets keep ore dust out of sensitive bays.
  • Sealed axle and motor housings rated for abrasive Almalyk copper dust.
  • Full motor torque at altitude, no power loss on Navoi plateaus.
  • Regenerative descent recovers 15-30% of haul energy on graded pit roads.
  • Liquid battery cooling maintains cell temperature under continuous load.
  • Fewer service points than a diesel powertrain mean fewer dust-driven failures.

Uzbekistan summers reach the mid-40s Celsius in open pits, and winters bring hard frost on the plateaus. The TZ5Y thermal system cools aggressively in summer and pre-conditions the pack in winter so range and charge acceptance stay stable year round. The 8-year / 4500-cycle warranty is valid across this cyclic duty because LFP tolerates the temperature swing better than alternative lithium chemistries, and because the battery management system logs every cycle so warranty claims are simple to evidence.

Charging, Swap and Fleet Sizing at the Pit

Uzbek mine fleets run two charging models. Depot DC fast charging at 360 kW takes 1.5-2.0 hours for a 20-80% window during shift change. Where a mine builds a swap station, the TZ5Y completes a full battery swap in 5-6 minutes, eliminating charging downtime entirely and letting a single truck run multiple shifts on rotated packs. Swap also extends pack life because charging is controlled at the station rather than abused by fast roadside charging, and it lets the mine balance energy cost by charging packs slowly overnight from cheap off-peak power. The combined model is common: fast chargers for the daily top-up and a swap cabinet for peak production weeks.

Fleets rarely convert all at once. A practical start is a pilot batch of TZ5Y units on the shortest, busiest pit loop at Almalyk, proving the energy recovery and uptime, then extending to Navoi with TZ3V units for the longest hauls. Because both share the CATL LFP pack and LvKong motor, the second batch adds no new spares or training. We size the pilot against your real haul distance, grade and load factor, then scale the pit charger and any swap cabinet in step with production so you do not over-build infrastructure ahead of demand. Driver onboarding covers high-voltage awareness and regen behaviour on graded roads; most crews cross-qualify in a single shift.
