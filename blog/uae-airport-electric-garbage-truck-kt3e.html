Why Airports Are Switching to Electric Ground Support Equipment

Airports face a unique combination of pressures: airside emissions rules, airline customers publishing scope-3 targets, and ground support equipment that idles for hours every day. An electric truck fits this environment perfectly because airport duty cycles are short-range, stop-start and return-to-base - exactly where electric drivetrains outperform diesel. The KT3E, with a 262 kWh battery and 240 km of range, covers a full day of apron work on a single charge. Replacing diesel units also removes exhaust particulates from the apron, a growing occupational health priority at major Emirati airports, and eliminates fuel spill and refueling infrastructure from the airside environment.

KT3E Duties on the Apron

The quiet drivetrain is a genuine operational benefit on a night-shift apron, cutting noise around crews working under aircraft. For airport authorities writing GSE electrification roadmaps, the KT3E class of truck is the proven, low-risk first fleet.

Charging Infrastructure at Dubai Airports

Airside charging needs planning but not heroics. A typical layout places DC fast chargers at the GSE depot or maintenance yard, with overnight AC or slower DC charging where vehicles park. The KT3E charges from 20% to 80% in roughly 60-90 minutes on a 120 kW DC charger, so even opportunity charging during shift breaks keeps availability high. Airports should involve their electrical engineering team early to confirm transformer capacity and cable routing under apron pavement. Landside power in Dubai is generally adequate for a first fleet of five to fifteen electric trucks; larger programs justify a dedicated substation, which airport authorities can often fund through green infrastructure budgets.

Safety and Compliance Standards for Airside Operation

Airside vehicles operate under stricter rules than road trucks: IATA ground handling guidance, airport-specific vehicle permits, driver airside licenses and equipment marking standards all apply. Electric trucks must additionally document battery safety - the KT3E carries UN38.3-tested CATL LFP packs with multi-layer thermal protection, which simplifies airport safety case reviews. High-voltage isolation monitoring, orange cabling and lockout procedures align with standard airside safety management systems. We provide the full safety documentation pack with each truck, including emergency response guides that airport fire services can fold into their incident procedures, which measurably speeds up airside operating approvals.
