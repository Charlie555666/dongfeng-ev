Terminal work at Lach Huyen runs 350-500 moves per tractor per day across two or three shifts, with trip lengths of 400-1,000 m and sustained speeds under 30 km/h. Diesel yard tractors idle 30-45% of engine hours in crane queues - pure fuel waste that the TE46 eliminates, drawing only cabin and systems power at standstill while regenerative braking recovers every deceleration. Measured terminal energy consumption runs 1.8-2.4 kWh per km; a full 20-hour double shift consumes 60-75% of the pack.

The port's expansion phases keep adding yard area, which stretches trip lengths - a trend that favours the 400 kWh pack's reserve over smaller terminal EVs, and keeps the tractor productive as the terminal grows into its deep-sea volumes.

Charging Inside the Terminal

The layout that works: two 180-240 kW DC chargers at the tractor yard on the natural return path from the crane lines, plus one unit serving any remote ro-ro or breakbulk apron. Tractors plug in during the 30-45 minute shift handover; that window restores a full shift of energy. Vietnamese industrial tariffs keep the energy cost per move near one-third of diesel, and the terminal's own grid capacity - sized for quay cranes - absorbs fleet charging without transformer drama.

  • Zone chargers by berth so tractors never deadhead across the yard to plug in.
  • Stagger team shift changes by 15 minutes to avoid charger queues.
  • Set telematics SOC alarms at 25%; terminal tractors should never deep-discharge in queue.
  • Canopy the charging bays - Haiphong's monsoon rain and salt air punish exposed connectors.

Monsoon and Salt Air Durability

Northern Vietnam adds two durability tests: a monsoon season with horizontal rain and occasional typhoon exposure, and salt-laden coastal air year-round. The TE46's IP67 high-voltage sealing handles the water; the marine coating package handles the corrosion. The maintenance additions versus inland duty are modest - monthly connector inspection, quarterly chassis wash, and cooling-pack cleaning after dust-heavy dry-season weeks. Heat at 33-37 C with high humidity sits comfortably inside the liquid-cooled envelope; annual range variation stays within 8%.

Economics for the Terminal Operator
