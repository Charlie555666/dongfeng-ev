Importing Electric Trucks into the Philippines: BOC Customs, EO 12 Zero-Tariff, LTO Registration and Homologation Guide

The Philippines is one of the most import-friendly electric vehicle markets in Southeast Asia, and the reason is policy. Executive Order 12 slashed tariffs on EVs to zero, the Board of Investments promotes EV assembly and fleet adoption, and major ports handle ro-ro and containerised vehicle imports routinely. For a fleet buyer, that means a Chinese-built electric truck can land in Manila, Batangas or Subic with a materially lower duty bill than a comparable diesel. This guide walks the full import path for an electric cargo truck like the Dongfeng KT5J — 106–130 kWh, FOB US$45,000–56,000.

What EO 12 Actually Covers

Executive Order 12 (2023) modified the tariff schedule to set most-favoured-nation duty on fully built-up electric vehicles, their parts and components, and certain charging equipment to zero for a defined period. The coverage is broad: passenger EVs, commercial EVs including trucks and buses, and a list of CKD/SKD parts. The practical point for fleet buyers is that a fully built electric cargo truck benefits from the zero line, removing the standard 5–15% or higher automobile duty that a diesel truck would carry. Always confirm the current HS code and the executive order’s validity window with a licensed broker, because tariff schedules change and the zero rate is time-bound. The zero rate is also a powerful budgeting tool: it lets a fleet commit capex to battery size and range rather than to duty, which is where the real operational value sits.

Note the trap: EO 12 zeroes the duty, not the 12% VAT. Imported vehicles still attract VAT on the customs value plus duty, so budget for it. A licensed customs broker is effectively mandatory — the HS-code and valuation details decide whether you pay zero or the fallback rate. Brokers also manage the bonding and the timeline so the truck is not sitting in a yard accruing demurrage while paperwork clears, which is where naive importers lose the savings the zero tariff created.

LTO Registration and Homologation

After release, the truck must be registered with the Land Transportation Office. For a fully built imported EV this means presenting proof of conformity, the imported motor vehicle form, and roadworthiness evidence. Where the model lacks a Philippine type-approval certificate, the buyer may need a single-unit or batch conformation pathway through LTO, supported by the manufacturer’s technical file — UN ECE braking, lighting and electrical safety where applicable, and a battery safety dossier. The KT5J ships with a CATL LFP pack and LvKong permanent-magnet motor 120–180 kW, and we supply the full conformity and test documentation pack to support LTO processing. Plan 4–10 weeks for registration depending on the conformity pathway. Starting the LTO file at the same time as the BOC declaration, rather than after release, is the single biggest time-saver most importers miss.

Ports: Subic, Batangas, Manila

  • Manila — highest volume, most broker competition, but congestion and demurrage risk; best for metro-delivery fleets.
  • Batangas — strong for south-Luzon distribution; often faster truck release than the capital.
  • Subic — free-port advantages and lower congestion; attractive for fleet consolidation and pre-delivery inspection before inland haul.

Choose the port by where the trucks enter revenue service. A Cebu or Davao deployment may route via Manila transhipment or a Visayas port — confirm ro-ro or feeder options before booking. The pre-delivery inspection at Subic or Batangas is also where you catch transit damage before the truck leaves the port, which protects both the warranty claim and the commissioning schedule.

ASEAN-China FTA and Payment Considerations

The ASEAN-China Free Trade Area gives Chinese-origin goods preferential treatment, but passenger and commercial vehicle lines sit outside the simplest FTA tariff elimination and the EO 12 zero line is the more relevant relief for EVs today. For payment and shipping: most imports are FOB or CIF with a 30% deposit and balance against bill of lading; arrange marine insurance covering the battery as a high-value item; and confirm the letter of credit terms with your bank before the vessel sails. The KT5J at FOB US$45,000–56,000 lands at a delivered Manila cost that, after zero EV duty and VAT, remains well below a comparable new diesel plus its first three years of fuel inflation. Keep a foreign-exchange buffer in the payment plan, because the peso cost of the balance is exposed to movement between deposit and arrival.

Common Importer Mistakes to Avoid

Three mistakes dominate failed Philippines EV imports. First, treating EO 12 as permanent — it is time-bound, so confirm validity before you commit, and do not let a lapsed window turn a zero-duty plan into a full-duty surprise. Second, under-scoping the LTO conformity path; a batch of ten needs a different filing from a single demonstrator, and the wrong pathway adds weeks. Third, forgetting the 12% VAT, which lands whether or not duty is zero and which decides the true delivered cost. The KT5J’s documentation pack — UN ECE test reports, battery safety dossier, spec sheets — exists precisely to keep the LTO step on the fast path. Importers who hand the broker this pack at deposit time, not at release, are the ones who take delivery on schedule and on budget, and who can quote a customer a firm landed cost instead of a range.

Build Your Philippines Import Checklist

Before you commit, lock five things: a broker experienced in EV HS codes; the current EO 12 validity and HS mapping; the LTO conformity pathway for your batch size; the destination port matched to your deployment; and the delivered-cost model including VAT, freight, insurance and pre-delivery inspection. Fleets in the Philippines that clear these early take delivery in one smooth window; those that discover an HS-code or conformity gap after the vessel sails lose weeks and cash. Delivery, Commissioning and the First 90 Days

Landing the truck is half the project; commissioning is the other half. On arrival, the KT5J should get a pre-delivery inspection, a charging assessment at the depot or a partner site, and a driver familiarisation session covering the single-speed drive, the regenerative braking feel and the energy gauge. The first 90 days are where the route-energy assumption meets reality, so log kWh per km against the import plan and reconcile. Philippine distribution duty is stop-heavy, which favours the KT5J’s regen recovery and its 106–130 kWh pack sized for 180–260 km daily loops. Fleets that treat the first quarter as a validated pilot, rather than business as usual, catch any charging or routing gap early and enter full operation with a defensible cost model for the next batch and a driver team that already trusts the truck.
