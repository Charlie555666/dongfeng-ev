The Addis Ababa-Djibouti corridor is the economic artery of landlocked Ethiopia, carrying containers from the Port of Djibouti up the 750-kilometer railway-adjacent road network to the capital. For freight that must run by road, a battery-electric EV truck on the corridor is becoming viable as charging stations multiply along the route. The Dongfeng TE8L electric tractor carries a 350 kWh CATL LFP pack on a 6x4 line-haul chassis, delivers about 280 km of real range, and is priced at USD 105,000 to 130,000 FOB China.

Why the Corridor Suits a Line-Haul EV Truck

The Addis-Djibouti road is graded, paved and relatively flat after the Awash escarpment, with predictable relay points at Modjo, Adama, Awash and Dire Dawa. A 6x4 electric tractor with 280 km range can run a single leg, charge during the mandated rest, and continue, turning the corridor into a chain of manageable electric stages. Right-hand-drive Ethiopia also means the TE8L is supplied in RHD configuration to match local traffic law, removing a common import blocker for East African buyers.

Charging Along the Corridor

Ethiopia's corridor already hosts 120 to 180 kW DC chargers at Modjo, Adama and Dire Dawa logistics parks, and the TE8L accepts CCS2 up to 150 kW plus AC up to 22 kW. A driver leaving Djibouti reaches Dire Dawa, charges to 80 percent in 75 minutes during the meal break, and completes the run to Addis. Operators running the Dongfeng TE8L electric tractor plan the route around three charge windows that align with legal rest times, so charging adds no calendar delay.

  • Leg 1: Port Djibouti to Dire Dawa, 350 km planned with one charge
  • Leg 2: Dire Dawa to Awash, charge during 45-minute rest
  • Leg 3: Awash to Modjo to Addis, arrive with 15 to 20 percent reserve
  • Depot: overnight AC charge to 100 percent for the return trip

Range in Ethiopian Conditions

Highland temperatures and the Adama grade reduce range versus flat-sea-level testing, but the TE8L still holds 260 to 290 km at 49 tonnes GCW. The LvKong 420 kW motor provides strong grade recovery, and regen on the descent back toward Djibouti recovers 12 to 18 percent of the outbound energy. For the electric truck market in Ethiopia this reliability is the difference between a demonstration unit and a fleet standard.

Planning depot charging for this EV truck

Charging an electric truck is a depot discipline, not a public-network gamble. A 350 kWh pack working a 280 km day typically returns with 25 to 35 percent in reserve, so overnight demand is about 210 kWh per truck. On a 120 kW DC charger the pack climbs from 20 to 80 percent in roughly 105 minutes; on 240 kW that falls to about 52 minutes, which lets two trucks share one charger on staggered shifts.

Size the site transformer for your busiest shift plus 30 percent headroom, keep cable runs under 30 metres to limit voltage drop, and meter each charger separately so energy cost per truck stays visible. In Ethiopia, grid connection lead times of three to nine months are common, so apply for the connection the day you sign the truck order, not the day the ship arrives. Energy cost per kilometre: EV truck versus diesel

A comparable diesel 6x4 tractor burns about 0.38 litres per kilometre in mixed duty; at $0.95 per litre that is roughly $0.36 per km. The electric equivalent consumes about 1.15 kWh per km fully loaded; at an industrial tariff of $0.10 per kWh the cost is near $0.12 per km, and overnight or solar-coupled charging can push it below $0.08. Over 70,000 km a year the saving is about $17,000 per truck, before any carbon or low-emission-zone benefit.

The gap widens in stop-start duty, where regenerative braking returns 15 to 25 percent of traction energy instead of burning it as brake dust. In Ethiopia, where diesel quality and price both fluctuate, locking in an electricity tariff also removes a major budgeting variable from the fleet P&L.

Maintenance and uptime economics

The TE8L has roughly one moving drivetrain part where a diesel has hundreds: no engine oil, no DPF, no turbocharger, no clutch, no multi-gear transmission service. Scheduled maintenance lands near $0.035 per km against $0.11 per km for a diesel equivalent, a saving of more than $5,000 a year at typical utilisation. Brake pads last two to three times longer because the motor does most of the deceleration.

Uptime matters more than parts cost. Fleet data from Chinese operators running the same e-axle platform shows 96 to 98 percent availability once drivers are trained, versus 92 to 94 percent for diesel trucks of the same age. What to confirm before you order

  • Duty cycle: longest daily route, loaded and empty legs, gradient profile, and ambient temperature range.
  • Charging: available transformer capacity, overnight window, and whether opportunity charging or battery swap fits the shift plan.
  • Homologation: which national or regional standard applies in Ethiopia, and who holds the type-approval paperwork.
  • Payload: body weight versus rated GVW, so the 280 km real range holds with your actual load.
  • Support: local partner for HV work, spare-parts stock level, and response time commitments in writing.
  • Finance: FOB or CFR terms, letter-of-credit conditions, and currency cover for the balance on arrival.
