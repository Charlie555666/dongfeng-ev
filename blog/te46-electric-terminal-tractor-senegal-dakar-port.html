Dakar Port Drayage in Numbers

Dakar is Senegal's gateway, and its terminals run containers from quay to yard to gate around the clock. Diesel tractors queue, idle and emit right where stevedores work, and the cumulative NOx and noise at the apron is a recognised health and productivity drain. An electric truck removes the fumes and the noise from the apron and, with a swap pack, never leaves the line for charging. Seen from the West Africa electric truck market via Morocco hub, port equipment is the cleanest EV business case in the region because duty is intense and contained, so the energy and emission savings are both large and easy to meter.

Ports also think in throughput per hour, and a tractor that never dwells at a charger is a tractor that keeps the quay moving. That operational argument often closes the business case faster than the fuel saving alone.

Senegal Drayage Fleet Math

A Dakar terminal tractor rarely leaves the port, so range is not the constraint — uptime is. The TE46's 262 kWh pack covers a full shift of short shuttles, and the 5-6 minute swap keeps a second pack rotating so the truck never stops. On a two-pack rotation a single tractor can cover three shifts with one charging window overnight, lifting asset utilization well above a diesel unit that loses time to refueling and servicing. The arithmetic is simple: if one swap replaces thirty minutes of refuel and queue per shift, a small fleet gains the equivalent of a free truck across the week.

Why Battery Swap Wins at the Port

  • Swap in 5-6 minutes beats any fast-charge dwell for line-haul continuity.
  • One shared swap pack pool serves a fleet of tractors.
  • No diesel exhaust in the terminal improves stevedore health.
  • Off-peak overnight charging cuts the port's energy cost.
  • Spare packs cost less than spare trucks for the same uptime gain.

Why the TE46

The TE46 electric terminal tractor is purpose-shaped for low-speed, high-torque shuttle work with a chassis built for king-pin loads and tight terminal turns. Its sealed drivetrain shrugs off salt-air corrosion on the Dakar waterfront better than an exposed diesel, and the low centre of gravity from a floor-mounted pack improves stability when pulling a loaded chassis.

Swap Station Siting at Dakar

At Dakar the swap station belongs near the quay-to-yard midpoint so tractors can exchange packs without leaving the container flow. A single pad with a shuttle and a small climate-controlled rack serves a dozen tractors, and because the TE46 never needs a long charge dwell the station footprint stays tiny relative to a diesel refuel island. Siting it inside the fence also keeps high-voltage work within the secure terminal.

Stevedore Health and Terminal Air Quality

Removing diesel exhaust from the apron is the clearest human benefit of the TE46. Stevedores working the same quay all shift breathe no NOx plume from the tractor, and the lower noise lets crew communicate without shouting over an engine. Ports that track occupational exposure report measurable improvement once the diesel tractors are retired, which supports both compliance and retention of skilled labour.

Comparing Terminal Tractor TCO

Against a diesel tractor the TE46 saves fuel, AdBlue and engine service, and the 8-year / 4,500-cycle warranty spans a terminal's equipment life. Because uptime is the metric that matters at a port, the swap model's elimination of charge downtime often outperforms a cheaper sticker price on a fast-charge-only rival. Most Dakar operators recover the premium inside two to three years on fuel and service alone, before counting the productivity gain.

Scaling the Dakar Fleet

Start with a pilot pair of TE46 tractors on the busiest quay and a one-pack swap pool, then scale as throughput grows. The shared pack pool means each added tractor needs only its own chassis, not its own charger farm, so marginal cost per tractor stays low and the terminal can react to volume spikes without a grid upgrade.

Grid and Energy Management

Charge the swap pool overnight on off-peak Senegal power and the port's energy cost per container move drops sharply versus diesel. Because the packs are centralized in the rack, the terminal can stagger their charging to avoid demand peaks and even participate in load-shifting if the utility allows, turning the EV fleet from a cost centre into a flexible energy asset.

Safety and Visibility

An electric terminal tractor is quiet, which is why the TE46 adds low-speed warning and 360-degree visibility aids suited to a busy apron. The flat pack lowers the centre of gravity for stable trailer coupling, and the instant torque response helps the driver hold precise control when nudging a loaded chassis into the stack.

Environmental and ESG Reporting

Ports under international shipping and ESG scrutiny increasingly report Scope 1 emissions at the terminal, and retiring diesel tractors is one of the fastest ways to cut that number. The TE46's zero tailpipe operation at the apron turns a Dakar terminal's equipment fleet from a liability into a reported win, and the centralized swap pool makes the energy draw easy to meter and disclose. Because the swap packs are charged off-peak, the emissions attributed to the terminal are both lower and easier to verify than a diesel fleet burning on-site.
