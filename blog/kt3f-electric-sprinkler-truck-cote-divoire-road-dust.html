How the Water and Pump System Works

The KT3F carries a 10,000 liter baffled tank that keeps the load stable on rough laterite roads, with a 60 cubic meter per hour pump feeding front and side spray bars plus a rear cannon for spot suppression. At a spray width of 14 meters, a single pass covers a wide haul road, and operators typically refill at a site water point every 4 to 6 hours of continuous work. Because the chassis is electric, there is no engine heat soak under the tank, which keeps water temperature down and reduces algae growth during multi-day storage.

The electric drivetrain also lets the pump run independently of road speed. A diesel bowser ties spray pressure to engine rpm, but the KT3F can hold a steady 60 m3 per hour flow while creeping along a dust zone at 5 km per hour, then climb a grade to the next sector without losing pressure. That control cuts water waste and extends the effective coverage of every fill.

Range, Charging, and Shift Planning

With the tank empty the chassis returns 220 to 260 kilometers of range, more than enough for a site shuttle. When fully loaded with 10,000 liters the range drops to roughly 150 to 180 kilometers, which still covers a full shift of local dust runs. The 120 kW DC fast charge restores 20 to 80 percent in about 90 minutes, so an operator can top up during the midday break and return to service for the afternoon.
