Why Port Shuttle Suits an Electric Tractor

Port drayage and shuttle work share three traits that favour electrification: fixed routes under 120 km per day, long idle periods at gates and cranes where a diesel burns fuel for nothing, and depot-based parking that makes overnight charging trivial. A typical Hamad Port to Doha logistics park round trip is 55-70 km. Even running three rounds per shift, a TE8L stays under 210 km daily, comfortably inside one charge of its 466 kWh pack with a 20% reserve.

Idle waste is the silent killer in diesel drayage economics. Port tractors idle 25-40% of engine hours waiting at quay cranes and gate queues. The TE8L draws essentially nothing at standstill - cabin air conditioning runs off the high-voltage pack at a fraction of diesel idle burn. Fleets that measured both technologies on the same Hamad Port shuttle report energy cost per container move dropping 55-65%.

Charging Layout for Hamad Port Fleets

The workable layout for a 10-truck shuttle fleet is simple: one 240 kW DC fast charger at the logistics park depot plus one 120 kW unit near the port gate as an opportunity point. Trucks charge 20-80% during the 45-minute shift change and top to 90% overnight on cheaper off-peak power. Qatar's grid is reliable and industrial tariffs run well below diesel-equivalent cost per kWh - the energy line of the budget practically writes itself.

  • Place the fast charger on the return path so trucks plug in without detouring.
  • Size the grid connection for simultaneous charging of 30% of the fleet, not 100% - staggered breaks do the rest.
  • Use the truck telematics API to trigger charge sessions by SOC, not by habit; shallow cycling extends pack life.
  • Keep one diesel or a spare EV as a reserve unit for the first 90 days while drivers learn range discipline.

Heat, Dust and the 45 C Question

Every Gulf buyer asks whether an EV truck survives August. The TE8L answer is engineering, not promises: the battery coolant loop holds cells at 20-35 C while ambient air sits at 45-50 C, motor and inverter share a second liquid circuit, and the cab HVAC is rated for Gulf specification with a high-capacity compressor. Air filtration on the cooling intakes handles the dust that comes with Shamal winds. What changes in summer is range - expect 8-12% less in peak heat with full air conditioning - and charging speed, which the thermal system protects by design.
