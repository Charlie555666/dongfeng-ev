TZ3Z Electric Dump Truck: An electric truck for Iraq Infrastructure Projects

The TZ3Z electric dump truck, an electric truck, is built for Iraq's road and housing infrastructure programmes, where sites often lack fixed grid power and operators need flexible charging. For Iraqi contractors, an EV truck that can be charged from a generator hybrid setup removes the diesel-fuel-supply risk while cutting cost per tonne moved. This article details the TZ3Z specification, generator-charging hybrid operation and how it supports national infrastructure builds, with reference to the TZ3Z electric dump truck detail page.

Why Iraq Infrastructure Needs an EV Truck

Iraq's reconstruction includes major road resurfacing, bridge approaches and housing compounds. These are short, repeated haul cycles from aggregate yard to site - the profile where battery-electric dump trucks win on energy and maintenance. The TZ3Z carries full payload with the LvKong motor's low-end torque, and regenerative braking recovers energy on every return run, lowering cost per cubic metre of moved material. On a road project the loaded truck climbs the embankment and the empty truck coasts back down; the descent recovery is the quiet saving that compounds across a season of earthworks.

Because the TZ3Z shares the Dongfeng electric dump platform with the TZ5Y and TZ3V, contractors running mixed sites can standardize spares and training, and a single swap cabinet can serve every truck on the programme regardless of size. For contractors reporting to donors on emissions, the TZ3Z also removes diesel exhaust from the site, cutting local particulate and NOx at the source, which is a direct, auditable win for infrastructure programmes with environmental conditions attached.

Generator-Charging Hybrid and Hot-Climate Duty

Many Iraqi infrastructure sites have no reliable grid. The TZ3Z supports generator-charging hybrid ops: a site generator or mobile solar-buffer feeds a 360 kW DC charger, topping the pack during mandated breaks. This removes dependence on fuel convoys while keeping trucks working. Where a swap station is built, the TZ3Z swaps a pack in 5-6 minutes, letting a single truck run multiple shifts on rotated packs. The generator can be a standard site unit already on the project for other plant, so no dedicated fuel line is needed for the trucks - the same diesel that runs the excavator can charge the dump truck, cutting a whole logistics stream.

  • Generator or solar-fed DC charging works off-grid at remote sites.
  • Swap-ready builds remove charging downtime on large projects.
  • No diesel fuel convoy means lower security and procurement overhead.
  • Regenerative descent recovers haul energy on graded road works.
  • One pack format across the fleet simplifies spares and training.

Iraqi summer ambient exceeds 45C and infrastructure sites are dust-heavy. The TZ3Z liquid battery cooling holds cells in the safe LFP window during charging and load, and high-mounted, filtered inlets keep abrasive dust out of sensitive bays. Overnight or generator-off-peak charging avoids the hottest hours, protecting the 8-year / 4500-cycle warranty. LFP chemistry avoids the thermal runaway risk that matters on remote, unattended sites, and the closed cooling loop survives dust ingress better than forced-air designs that would clog within a season of road works.

The defining decision for a TZ3Z fleet is the charge source. The truck supports generator-charging hybrid ops: a site generator or mobile solar-buffer feeds a 360 kW DC charger, topping the pack during mandated breaks, so the truck never waits on a fuel convoy. Station-controlled charging also protects the 8-year / 4500-cycle warranty by avoiding rushed roadside fast charging.

Iraqi contractors usually pilot with a few TZ3Z units on the shortest aggregate-to-site loop, prove the generator-charge saving and the fuel-security edge, then extend to longer road and housing works as the programme scales. Because the TZ3Z shares the Dongfeng electric dump platform with the TZ5Y and TZ3V, a contractor can standardize spares and training across sites. Driver onboarding covers high-voltage awareness and regen behaviour on graded road works; most crews cross-qualify in a single shift, and the absence of diesel exhaust improves air quality at confined sites compared with a diesel dump truck.
