Battery capacity, range and charging

The TZ3V’s 600kWh pack is the larger of the two and supports both DC fast charging (20–80% in roughly 75 minutes) and a 5–6 minute battery swap at equipped mine stations. The BYD model leans on its blade-cell LFP pack with strong thermal tolerance but is typically charged rather than swapped, which suits single-shift operations better than continuous 24-hour haul cycles. On a 3 km return haul road at 80% payload, the TZ3V delivers around 8–10 round trips per charge, while the BYD manages a comparable 7–9 trips before needing a top-up.

Price and total acquisition cost

Headline price is only part of the story. Buyers should budget the full landed and commissioned cost:

  • FOB truck price: TZ3V $160k–180k vs BYD $175k–205k.
  • Sea freight and insurance to Durban or Mombasa: $8k–14k per unit.
  • Local homologation, lighting and mine-spec roll cage: $6k–12k.
  • Charging or swap cabinet infrastructure: $40k–120k for a site.
  • Spare wheels, tyres and a starter parts kit: $10k–18k.

On a like-for-like 10-truck order, the TZ3V fleet typically lands $150k–250k below the BYD equivalent once freight and swap infrastructure are included, because the swap option defers the need for a large fast-charge yard.

Battery warranty and cycle life

Both brands warranty the pack for eight years, but the cycle counts differ. The TZ3V is warranted to 4,500 cycles at 70% state of health, which on a two-shift mine equals roughly 6–7 years of full daily use. BYD’s mining warranty is commonly quoted at 4,000 cycles. With LFP chemistry both packs retain usable capacity far beyond warranty end, so the real differentiator is how many swap-capable packs you cycle versus charge in place.

Parts support and after-sales

Parts availability decides uptime. For a fleet of 5–20 trucks, the TZ3V’s broader distributor footprint is a practical advantage. Which one fits your mine

Choose the TZ3V if you run multi-shift haul cycles, want battery-swap flexibility, and value a wider African parts network at a lower landed price. Choose the BYD if you already run BYD energy products, charge rather than swap, and operate a single large site with direct OEM backing. Both are credible electric truck platforms; the TZ3V wins on total cost and swap uptime for most mid-size African mines.

Planning depot charging for this EV truck

Charging an electric truck is a depot discipline, not a public-network gamble. A 600 kWh pack working a 220 km day typically returns with 25 to 35 percent in reserve, so overnight demand is about 360 kWh per truck. On a 120 kW DC charger the pack climbs from 20 to 80 percent in roughly 180 minutes; on 240 kW that falls to about 90 minutes, which lets two trucks share one charger on staggered shifts.

Size the site transformer for your busiest shift plus 30 percent headroom, keep cable runs under 30 metres to limit voltage drop, and meter each charger separately so energy cost per truck stays visible. In South Africa, grid connection lead times of three to nine months are common, so apply for the connection the day you sign the truck order, not the day the ship arrives. Energy cost per kilometre: EV truck versus diesel

A comparable diesel 8x4 dump truck burns about 0.38 litres per kilometre in mixed duty; at $0.95 per litre that is roughly $0.36 per km. The electric equivalent consumes about 1.15 kWh per km fully loaded; at an industrial tariff of $0.10 per kWh the cost is near $0.12 per km, and overnight or solar-coupled charging can push it below $0.08. Over 70,000 km a year the saving is about $17,000 per truck, before any carbon or low-emission-zone benefit.

The gap widens in stop-start duty, where regenerative braking returns 15 to 25 percent of traction energy instead of burning it as brake dust. In South Africa, where diesel quality and price both fluctuate, locking in an electricity tariff also removes a major budgeting variable from the fleet P&L.

Maintenance and uptime economics

The TZ3V has roughly one moving drivetrain part where a diesel has hundreds: no engine oil, no DPF, no turbocharger, no clutch, no multi-gear transmission service. Scheduled maintenance lands near $0.035 per km against $0.11 per km for a diesel equivalent, a saving of more than $5,000 a year at typical utilisation. Brake pads last two to three times longer because the motor does most of the deceleration.

Uptime matters more than parts cost. Fleet data from Chinese operators running the same e-axle platform shows 96 to 98 percent availability once drivers are trained, versus 92 to 94 percent for diesel trucks of the same age.
