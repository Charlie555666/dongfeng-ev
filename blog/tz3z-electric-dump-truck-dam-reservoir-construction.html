Why Earthworks Suits an EV Truck

Four characteristics make dam and reservoir sites unusually favourable:

  • Short, repetitive cycles. Typical haul distance is 1.5–6 km each way, meaning a truck completes 25–45 cycles per shift well inside a 200–250 km daily envelope.
  • Loaded uphill, empty downhill. The classic earthworks profile puts the energy-heavy leg in one direction and gives back a substantial share through regenerative braking on the return — net consumption typically 10–18% lower than a flat-road equivalent.
  • Fixed site with a depot. Charging infrastructure is installed once and used for the project's whole life, which spreads capex over a known volume of work.
  • Community and environmental licence. Large water projects are usually subject to environmental and social commitments. Zero tailpipe emissions and materially lower noise strengthen the case to regulators and local communities, and remove diesel particulate exposure for operators working in cuttings and enclosed spillway areas.

Haul Road Design Is an Energy Decision

On an electric fleet, haul road quality converts directly into kWh. Rolling resistance dominates at earthworks speeds, and a poorly maintained road can raise consumption by 30% or more:

Road conditionRolling resistance coefficientEffect on consumption vs maintained road
Compacted, well-graded, watered0.015–0.020Baseline
Moderate rutting and loose material0.030–0.040+15–25%
Deep ruts, soft subgrade, ungraded0.060–0.090+45–80%
Sustained gradient (per 1% average)—+3–5% uphill, partially recovered by regen downhill

The operational implication is concrete: the cost of a grader and a water bowser on the haul road is an energy line item on an electric fleet, not just a comfort item. Cycle-Time Modelling

Fleet sizing comes from the cycle, and the cycle has six elements. For a typical 3 km one-way haul:

  1. Load: excavator or loader fill, 3–5 minutes depending on material and bucket passes.
  2. Haul loaded: 3 km at 20–28 km/h on ramped road, 7–9 minutes.
  3. Spot and tip: positioning, body raise, discharge, body lower — 2.5–4 minutes.
  4. Return empty: 5–7 minutes, faster on the downhill return.
  5. Queue and manoeuvre: 3–6 minutes at busy periods.
  6. Total cycle: 21–31 minutes; roughly 17–22 productive cycles in a 10-hour shift.

At a nominal 25 tonnes per load, that is 425–550 tonnes per truck per shift. Against a 6,000 tonnes/day earthmoving target, the fleet requirement is roughly 11–14 trucks plus spares — and the energy per truck per shift lands at 160–230 kWh, comfortably inside one overnight charge on the 400 kWh pack with reserve for a second shift.

Energy Budget by Project Phase

Earthworks projects change character as they progress, and the energy plan should change with them:

  • River diversion and foundation excavation: short hauls, steep ramps out of the foundation box, high energy per tonne. Budget 1.9–2.4 kWh/km loaded.
  • Embankment fill placement: longer hauls from borrow area, better roads, moderate gradients. 1.5–1.9 kWh/km.
  • Spillway and channel works: confined areas, frequent spotting, more idling and manoeuvring — lower km but higher hours, so HVAC and hydraulic draw become a larger share.
  • Finishing and landscaping: light duty; consider rotating part of the fleet to other sites.

Charging Strategy on a Remote Site

Most dam sites are remote, which is often cited as a barrier. In practice it is a design problem with known answers:

  • Depot charging at the site camp is standard: 8–10 hour overnight window, distributed AC sockets or 120–180 kW DC units with staggered starts.
  • Opportunity charging at the shift change keeps two-shift operations running with a smaller fleet.
  • Battery swap at 5–6 minutes suits continuous 24-hour placement campaigns where charger waiting would otherwise cost tonnes — see swap station deployment.
  • Solar-hybrid or generator-backed charging covers sites ahead of grid connection; our solar-hybrid charging guide covers sizing.

Start electrical design early. On remote projects the grid connection lead time, not the truck lead time, is what sets the commissioning date.

Dust, Water and Body Specification

Earthworks dust affects health, visibility, equipment life and community relations. Two measures matter:

  • Water the haul road on a schedule, not reactively. The KT3F and KT7A electric washing truck cover road watering and hard-surface washing respectively; body and tank specification is covered in our water tank body engineering guide.
  • Specify the tipper body for the material. Rock from spillway excavation needs a wear-resistant, floor-heated body; cohesive fill needs a steep tipping angle and tailgate design that prevents carry-back. Our tipper body specification guide sets out the options.

Specification Checklist for an Earthworks Order

  1. Haul distance one way, and the loaded-versus-empty gradient split.
  2. Material type and density, and nominal payload per load.
  3. Road surface and rolling-resistance estimate.
  4. Shift pattern: hours per day, days per week, number of shifts.
  5. Site power availability and grid connection lead time.
  6. Ambient temperature range and altitude (both affect thermal and derating behaviour).
