Farm Logistics Duty Cycles and Seasonality

Agricultural transport is defined by peaks: harvest weeks when everything must move at once, and quieter months when vehicles support general farm duty. This seasonality has historically argued against specialized trucks, but it actually suits electric trucks well. An electric cargo truck like the KTH3 works the harvest peak on daily cycles within its 300 km range, then earns its keep off-season on feed, input and supply runs. Unlike a diesel, an electric truck sitting between peaks incurs no fuel-system degradation, no oil aging and minimal maintenance - and each harvest, energy cost per tonne runs 60-75% below diesel. For Kenyan agricultural businesses, the economics reward exactly the high-intensity seasonal use that defines the sector.

Charging Where the Grid Is Weak

Rural charging is the first objection raised, and the most solvable. Most farm operations electrify around a home base: the packhouse, depot or collection center where trucks return nightly. A three-phase connection at that base, plus one DC fast charger, supports a working fleet - the KTH3 charges 20-80% in about an hour on a 120 kW unit. Where grid capacity is genuinely constrained, solar canopies over the yard with battery storage carry the daily load; Kenya has the irradiance to make this arithmetic work well. Diesel generator backup, already standard on many Kenyan farms, covers exceptional peaks. The planning rule is simple: charge where the trucks sleep, and range stops being a rural problem.

Seasonal Utilization and Year-Round Value

Smart agricultural buyers plan the truck's year, not just its harvest. During harvest, the KTH3 runs maximum daily cycles on the crop evacuation it was bought for. Off-season, the same truck hauls feed, fertilizer, equipment and general supplies - work that would otherwise go to an aging diesel pickup or a contractor's invoice. Some Kenyan operators add revenue by contract hauling for neighboring farms in their own peak windows. The battery warranty structure supports this flexibility: 8 years or 4,500 cycles covers intensive seasonal use with room to spare, because LFP chemistry treats the off-season light duty as recovery time. Annual utilization planning is what turns an electric truck from a harvest expense into a year-round asset.

Cost per Tonne vs Diesel in Kenya

Agricultural margins are thin, so cost per tonne-kilometer decides. On harvest duty - short runs, heavy loads, constant stop-start - diesel trucks operate at their least efficient, while the electric KTH3 converts that same duty into its most efficient operating band, harvesting regenerative energy at every stop. Energy cost per tonne typically falls by half or more; maintenance adds further savings because harvest intensity on diesel engines drives exactly the wear items - clutches, injectors, cooling systems - that electric drivetrains do not have. At an FOB range of $75,000-$95,000, payback depends on annual utilization, which is why year-round duty planning matters so much: the farms that keep the truck working twelve months a year see the fastest returns.

Procurement Tips for Agricultural Buyers in Kenya

Agricultural procurement has its own rhythm, and a few practices prevent the common failures. Order early enough that production, shipping to the Port of Mombasa and PVoC certification complete before the harvest window, not during it - five to seven months of lead time is realistic. Size the battery to the longest harvest-day cycle with a 20% reserve, not to the average day. Confirm charging power at the actual operating base before signing. And negotiate the support package - parts stock, training, harvest-season response commitment - as part of the purchase, because mid-harvest downtime costs more per day than the truck did per month. Deployment Timeline from Order to Operation

Realistic scheduling prevents the most common fleet electrification failures. A typical KTH3 deployment runs five to seven months end to end: production takes 30-60 days depending on configuration and order size; ocean freight to the Port of Mombasa adds 25-45 days; customs clearance and homologation steps add two to six weeks depending on Kenyan procedures. Running in parallel, the depot charging installation should complete before the trucks land, and driver and technician training should finish during commissioning week. The sequence that works: confirm specifications and sign the contract, start certification paperwork immediately, order chargers at the same time as the trucks, and book training against the vessel arrival date. Fleets that run these tracks in parallel put trucks into revenue service within days of clearance; fleets that run them sequentially lose months.
