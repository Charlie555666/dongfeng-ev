Why the Abidjan metro project needs electric tippers

The Abidjan metro line runs through dense neighbourhoods where diesel tipper fleets create noise, smoke and a constant fuel bill. The project moves an estimated 4 to 6 million tonnes of tunnel spoil and fill over its construction phase, much of it in short 3 to 8 km shuttle cycles between the excavation face and the spoil yard. Those short cycles are exactly where an electric truck wins, because regenerative braking on the loaded return and the absence of idling recovery the energy that a diesel unit wastes. At Cote d'Ivoire's diesel price near $1.05 per litre, a 30-tonne diesel tipper burns 38 to 45 litres per 100 km loaded, costing $40 to $47 per short haul round trip.

Beyond cost, the project's environmental and social impact conditions increasingly favour low-emission plant. Electric tippers help the concessionaire hit its carbon targets and reduce on-site respiratory risk for the 2,000-plus workers near the tunnels.

Duty cycle: short shuttle hauls on site

A typical metro-site shift runs 10 to 12 hours with the truck cycling every 25 to 40 minutes. The KTA1 draws about 1.8 to 2.4 kWh per km when loaded and 1.0 to 1.4 kWh per km empty, so a 6 km loaded plus 6 km empty loop uses roughly 22 to 34 kWh. Across 25 loops a shift that is 550 to 850 kWh, well inside the 350 kWh pack's range because the truck charges during the mandatory loader waiting time at the face.

Site planners should schedule a 120 kW DC charger at the spoil yard and a second at the aggregate intake. A 45 to 90 minute 20-80 percent top-up during the lunch and break windows keeps the fleet turning without a dedicated charging shift.

Cost per tonne-kilometre versus diesel

The metric that wins infrastructure tenders is cost per tonne-kilometre. For the KTA1 at 25 t payload and 2.0 kWh per km loaded:

  • Energy per tonne-km: 2.0 kWh / 25 t = 0.08 kWh per tonne-km.
  • Electricity cost at $0.12 per kWh: $0.0096 per tonne-km.
  • Diesel equivalent at 40 L per 100 km and $1.05 per litre: about $0.042 per tonne-km, or four times higher.
  • Maintenance saving: no DPF, no engine oil, regen brakes — roughly 30 percent lower per km.

Over a 500,000-km project life a single KTA1 avoids roughly $160,000 in fuel and $40,000 in maintenance against a diesel tipper, a saving that easily covers the FOB premium of $95,000 to $120,000 within the first 18 to 24 months of the build.

Sizing a charging hub for the fleet

For a 12-truck KTA1 fleet, two 180 kW DC chargers and one 120 kW unit provide enough throughput if paired with a load balancer that caps site demand at 500 kW. The Abidjan grid supplies the site through a dedicated 630 kVA transformer, which the utility typically installs as part of the construction power package. A 100 kW solar canopy over the parking area offsets about 15 percent of daytime top-up and signals the project's green credentials to financiers and the public.

Safety and operator training on a live site

Construction sites are unforgiving, so the KTA1's low noise is itself a safety feature: pedestrians and plant operators can hear an approaching tipper instead of being surprised by a silent diesel. We run a half-day high-voltage awareness course for the yard team and a one-day operator clinic covering the electronic parking brake, the regen descent setting and the load-sensitive traction control that prevents wheelspin on wet spoil. The local kit includes the contactors and the tipper hydraulic valve most likely to need service in the first year.

Site managers see adoption improve once crews trust the range. Telematics show the KTA1 holds 15 to 20 percent reserve even after a 12-hour shift, and the quiet operation lets the contractor add night shifts on the tunnel approach without breaching Abidjan noise limits that diesel plant would exceed.

At project end the KTA1 has options a diesel tipper lacks. The battery itself can be repurposed as stationary storage for the contractor's next site office, deferring disposal costs. Comparing the KTA1 to a diesel tipper fleet

For procurement teams still weighing the switch, the cleanest comparison is total cost per loaded km over the build. A diesel 8x4 tipper at 40 L per 100 km and $1.05 per litre costs $0.42 per km in fuel alone, before the $0.05 to $0.08 per km maintenance of a construction diesel. The KTA1 at 2.0 kWh per km and $0.12 per kWh costs $0.24 per km in energy, plus roughly $0.05 per km maintenance, a $0.21 per km saving. At 100,000 km a year that is $21,000 per truck, or $252,000 across a 12-truck fleet, enough to fund the charging hub and still show a net gain before the duty incentives are counted.

Climate and community benefits for the concession

The Abidjan metro is partly financed against environmental and social criteria, and electric plant helps the concessionaire report lower scope-one emissions to its lenders. A 12-truck KTA1 fleet displaces roughly 600 tonnes of CO2 a year versus diesel, a figure the project can cite in its sustainability reporting and use to negotiate green financing terms. Neighbouring communities along the route also report less dust and noise during night works, which reduces the complaint volume that previously delayed permits and kept the build on schedule.
