Airport Duty #1: The Airside Case

Airport ground logistics is the most EV-friendly trucking environment that exists. The routes are fixed loops between cargo terminals, catering units, fuel farms and aircraft stands — typically 3–15 km of internal service roads. Speeds are low (25–40 km/h airside limits), which means the KT5M's consumption lands at 0.8–1.1 kWh/km, and its 262 kWh pack covers two full shifts of continuous shuttling on one overnight charge. The constraints that matter at airports are exactly the electric drivetrain's strengths:

  • Emissions at the stand. Airside air quality rules tighten every year; ground-service diesel trucks idle at stands for cumulative hours daily. An electric box truck emits nothing while queuing, loading or holding.
  • Noise on night rotations. Cargo aircraft clusters land between 23:00 and 05:00 at most hubs. The KT5M at apron speeds runs near-inaudible — no engine braking, no idle rumble — keeping night operations inside community noise limits that diesel GSE strains.
  • Precision at the stand. Electric low-speed torque control lets drivers creep to within centimetres of dollies and cargo doors — a real productivity detail on tight stands.
  • Fuel on the airside. Diesel storage and bowser movements inside airport perimeters are a licensed, audited hazard. Electricity from the terminal grid removes the entire fuel-handling chain.

Airside charging is also the easiest infrastructure problem in trucking: airports run their own substations with firm power, and stand-down areas near cargo complexes accept charge posts with modest civil works. A 10-vehicle KT5M airport fleet typically installs a 12-gun 120 kW depot at the cargo terminal — a routine project inside an airport's existing electrical authority framework.

Airport Duty #2: Landside Feed and City Distribution

The same truck runs the landside leg: cargo from terminal to city distribution centres, catering resupply, airline crew and materials movements on public roads. Airports increasingly sit inside urban low-emission zones (a long list across Europe, and spreading through Asia and Latin America), and landside feeder fleets face exactly the emissions rules city distributors face. The KT5M's 230–300 km range covers the landside duty of essentially every hub we serve — most airport-to-city loops run under 80 km round trip.

The Cold-Chain Angle: Refrigeration Without the Engine

Refrigerated distribution exposes a hidden cost of diesel trucks: the transport refrigeration unit (TRU) is usually a second diesel engine, running its own fuel, its own maintenance cycle, its own emissions and noise footprint. The KT5M changes the architecture. The refrigeration compressor runs as an electric PTO from the traction battery — one energy source, one maintenance regime, zero emissions at the delivery door.

The energy arithmetic matters at specification time. A typical electric TRU on a 65 m³ insulated body draws 4–9 kW depending on setpoint and ambient (frozen vs chilled, tropical vs temperate duty). On a 9-hour distribution day that is 36–80 kWh — a substantial slice of the 262 kWh pack, which is why serious cold-chain buyers choose the 310 kWh option for deep-frozen or tropical duty and the 262 kWh pack for chilled work in temperate climates. Door-open energy losses (the real killer in multi-stop cold chain) are handled the same way as with diesel — strip curtains, fast-roll doors — but the KT5M's silent operation extends a quiet advantage: early-morning residential deliveries that noise rules would otherwise prohibit.

Duty Profile: Airport Cold Chain Is the Best of Both

The most demanding — and most grateful — KT5M customers run airport cold-chain feeds: catering resupply, pharma imports (temperature-validated air cargo moving to city pharmacies), fresh produce from cargo terminals to distribution centres. This duty stacks every electric advantage: fixed routes with depot charging at both ends, overnight and early-morning schedules where silence is regulatory, refrigeration load that the e-PTO handles without a second engine, and corporate sustainability commitments (airlines, pharma shippers, airports) that audit the ground fleet's emissions. For pharma lanes, the KT5M's thermal body plus telematics temperature logging integrates with GDP-compliant cold-chain documentation — the same records shippers already keep.

Deployment Checklist

  1. Map the duty day before choosing the battery. Tally route distance + TRU hours × ambient severity. Chilled/temperate → 262 kWh; frozen/tropical or 250+ km days → 310 kWh.
  2. Locate charging at the dwell. Airports: cargo stand-down areas. Distribution: depot overnight + one opportunity point on the route if days run long.
  3. Specify the body early. Insulation class, door type, and partition options (multi-compartment chilled/frozen) are factory choices, not retrofits.
  4. Plan driver habits. Pre-cooling the body while plugged in (a standard KT5M routine) moves TRU load off the day's battery budget.
  5. Airside certification: airport authorities require vehicle compliance documentation for airside operation; we supply the technical file and support local approval processes.
