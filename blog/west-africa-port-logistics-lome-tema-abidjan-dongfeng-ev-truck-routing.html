West Africa Port Logistics for Dongfeng Electric Trucks: Lomé, Tema, and Abidjan Compared

For buyers of Dongfeng EV trucks in West Africa, the choice of delivery port is not just about logistics cost — it determines the road quality of the last leg, the customs clearance time, and ultimately whether the trucks arrive in serviceable condition. This article compares the three ports most of our West African customers use: Lomé (Togo), Tema (Ghana), and Abidjan (Côte d’Ivoire), with practical notes on each destination country.

Why West African Logistics Matter for EV Trucks

Roll-on / roll-off (RoRo) ships deliver trucks under their own power from the loading port to the destination. The alternative is containerisation, which requires the trucks to be disassembled for the ocean leg and reassembled at the destination — a process that adds roughly USD 2,800 to 4,500 per truck in cost plus 14 to 21 days of labour. RoRo is preferred when the truck can be safely driven and the destination port has good RoRo discharge capability.

The West African port selection influences:

  • Voyage duration and thus truck availability
  • Customs clearance complexity and time
  • Quality of the road haul from the discharge port to the customer depot
  • Local handling charges and import duties
  • Pre-shipment inspection (PSI) requirements which some destinations still apply

For electric trucks specifically, an additional factor: the truck arrives with a partial state-of-charge (typically 30 to 40 percent). The handler at the port needs to be able to plug in, slow-charge, or otherwise keep the battery at safe state-of-charge during the waiting period before customs clearance is completed. This is a relatively new requirement that not all West African ports have fully adapted to yet.

Lomé Port, Togo — The Most Common Choice

Lomé Container Terminal (LCT) and the adjacent RoRo quay at the Autonomous Port of Lomé handle the majority of landlocked West Africa’s imports. The port has been upgraded substantially over the past decade with Chinese investment (a concessional finance package through Exim Bank), and is now the deepest natural deep-water port in West Africa, able to handle Post-Panamax vessels without tidal restrictions.

Lomé Strengths

  • RoRo discharge is fast — trucks drive off the vessel in roughly 2 to 4 hours
  • Customs clearance averages 5 to 7 days for properly-documented shipments; SYDONIA-based ASYCUDA system provides traceability
  • Road quality north to Ouagadougou (Burkina Faso) is roughly 1,000 km via paved highway RN17 then RN6, around 14 to 18 hours of driving depending on border traffic
  • Charging infrastructure: Lomé now has 12 public DC fast-charging points (50 to 150 kW) around the port area and main truck parking; sufficient to top up RoRo-discharged trucks before onward haulage
  • Free trade zone status reduces the bureaucracy for transit cargo to Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger

Lomé Limitations

  • Togo non-tariff barriers can add 2 to 3 days if not pre-cleared in advance
  • Road quality deteriorates significantly after the northern Togolese border into Burkina Faso (poor pavement on RN6 stretches between Fada N’Gourma and Koupéla)
  • Transit permits for EV trucks (UN 3171 classification) require a few extra days of paperwork

Best Lomé Destinations

Lomé is the strongest port for shipments destined for Burkina Faso (Ouagadougou, Bobo-Dioulasso), Niger (Niamey via Parakou), and northern Ghana (Tamale, Bolgatanga).

Tema Port, Ghana — The Infrastructure Choice

Tema is Ghana’s largest port, located 30 km east of Accra. It handles the bulk of Ghana’s imports and a substantial portion of transit traffic to Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger via the Tema–Ouagadougou corridor.

Tema Strengths

  • Large volume means competitive RoRo freight rates
  • Excellent road quality: the Eastern Corridor road from Tema through Hohoe, Nkwanta, and Yendi to Ouagadougou has been substantially upgraded in 2024 to 2025, with roughly 80 percent of the route now paved in good condition
  • Customs: Ghana customs (ICUMS / GCNet) is one of the more reliable and timely customs systems in West Africa; clearance averages 4 to 6 days for pre-documented shipments
  • Charging infrastructure: 8 to 10 DC fast-charging points around the port area, including dedicated sites for trucks

Tema Limitations

  • Port congestion at peak times can add 3 to 5 days of waiting for RoRo discharge
  • GCX rule for transit cargo to Burkina Faso requires a transit bond that adds USD 2,500 to 5,000 in working capital; the bond is recoverable on exit confirmation but ties up working capital for 30 to 60 days
  • Ghana import duty on transit cargo applies if not pre-cleared at origin; for trucks destined for Burkina Faso, it is more efficient to declare them as transit cargo from the start

Best Tema Destinations

Tema is the strongest port for shipments destined for southern Burkina Faso (mainly Ouagadougou), northern Ghana mining sites (Ahafo, Obuasi), and Mali (Bamako via Sikasso).

Abidjan Port (PAA), Côte d’Ivoire — The Northern Côte d’Ivoire / Mali Choice

Port Autonome d’Abidjan (PAA) handles the bulk of Côte d’Ivoire’s imports and significant transit traffic to Mali, Burkina Faso, and Guinea. The port has both container and RoRo quays, and a free trade zone that simplifies customs processing for transit.

Abidjan Strengths

  • Deep water and well-equipped RoRo quay, low port-congestion risk
  • Customs: SIDEC and ASYCUDA-based clearance averages 6 to 8 days; commonly slightly slower than Tema or Lomé but with less aggressive physical inspection
  • Road quality north to Bamako (Mali) is excellent on the Abidjan–Yamoussoukro–Sikasso corridor, roughly 1,200 km of consistently good paved road
  • Direct rail option from Abidjan to Ouagadougou (Sitarail) for parts and supplies, though not for trucks themselves

Abidjan Limitations

  • Charging infrastructure is the weakest of the three ports for trucks; only 4 public DC fast-charging points, mostly 50 kW; full charge before onward haulage requires overnight depot charging
  • CFA franc customs processing sometimes requires manual intervention due to legacy IT systems
  • Apatrida / no-visa-for-Côte-d’Ivoire permit for some destination-country nationals adds 3 to 5 days if the driver does not have the right travel documents

Best Abidjan Destinations

Abidjan is the strongest port for shipments destined for Mali (Bamako), Guinea (Conakry, Kankan), Burkina Faso via the southern route (Bobo-Dioulasso), and northern Côte d’Ivoire (Korhogo, Odienné).

Which Port We Recommend for Your Route

For a customer in Ouagadougou (Burkina Faso): Tema is the most reliable choice based on road quality and customs predictability. Lomé is a strong alternative if customs are pre-cleared in advance.

For Bamako (Mali): Abidjan is the clear choice based on road quality and existing logistics density.

For Niamey (Niger): Lomé is the conventional route, although some operators now use Cotonou (Benin) or Tema as alternatives.

For Conakry (Guinea): Conakry port itself is functional but slow; many operators use Abidjan plus onward haulage for large EV truck orders.

Charging Before Dispatch

One specific operational note: we recommend trucks arrive at the destination port with 30 to 40 percent state-of-charge to balance shipping safety against post-discharge degradation. If the onward road haulage is more than 600 km, we arrange charging at the destination port before the truck enters transit. The destination-port charging takes 4 to 8 hours per truck (60 to 120 kW slow charge to 80 percent state-of-charge) and costs USD 60 to 120 per truck, depending on port.
