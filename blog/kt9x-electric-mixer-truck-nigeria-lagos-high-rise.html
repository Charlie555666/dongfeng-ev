KT9X Electric Mixer Truck for Lagos High-Rise Construction

The KT9X electric mixer truck is an electric truck designed for Lagos high-rise sites, mounting an 8 m3 drum on the TZ8J electric mixer truck platform and carrying a 336 kWh CATL LFP battery for plant-to-site cycles. Concrete is unforgiving: once mixed it has a clock on it, so the truck that arrives with the mix at the right temperature and slump wins the contract, and the EV version does that while burning no diesel in the jam.

Lagos High-Rise Boom Needs Quiet Concrete

Lagos is stacking towers faster than its roads can cope, and concrete delivery is the bottleneck. Diesel mixers idle through traffic jams while the drum keeps turning, burning fuel and pumping fumes into Ikoyi and Victoria Island. An electric truck can hold the drum speed on battery and arrive with the mix at temperature, then recharge at the batch plant instead of idling at the site gate. In the Nigeria electric truck market, mixers are a smart first EV because they return to a known plant, which solves the charging question before it is asked.

The quiet factor is real money in Lagos, where night pours and early permits are won or lost on noise. A mixer that can approach a residential tower at 05:00 without a clattering diesel is a mixer a developer will book again.

Plant-to-Site Range Planning

Most Lagos pours run within 40 km of the batch plant, well inside the KT9X's 200-350 km range even with the drum load. The key is charging discipline: plug in at the plant between loads so the truck never leaves below 80%. Because the plant already has three-phase power for the batch system, adding mixer charging needs no generator and no new grid connection, which is the single biggest cost saving over a diesel mixer backed by a site genset.

Generator-Free Charging Advantage

  • Charge from the plant's existing supply during loading and wash-down.
  • No diesel generator noise at the site gate during early pours.
  • Lower whole-life cost than a genset-backed diesel mixer.
  • Quieter night pours keep residential permits easier to win.
  • Plant power is cheaper per kilowatt-hour than trucked diesel by a wide margin.

Building on the TZ8J electric mixer truck platform gives Nigerian fleets a proven chassis, shared LvKong motor parts and a common 336 kWh pack with other body variants. Driver Training and Drum Management

An electric mixer changes almost nothing about concrete quality but changes a lot about how the cab feels. Drivers learn that the drum motor is independent of traction, so the mix keeps turning even if the truck is stopped in traffic, and that the 336 kWh pack prefers a gentle recharge between loads. Drum speed, slump retention and wash-down routines are identical to a diesel unit, so a Lagos operator is productive on the KT9X within a single shift.

Weather and Wet-Season Planning

Lagos wet season changes the job, not the truck. When rain interrupts pours, the KT9X simply sits on charge at the plant drawing almost nothing, whereas a diesel idles and still burns. Plan the battery buffer for the longer dry-season days when traffic is worst, and the same 336 kWh pack comfortably covers the shorter, rain-broken wet-season runs with energy to spare.
