Baghdad Municipal Routes

A Baghdad collection route runs 80–140 km across a shift of short hops: stop, lift, compact, move 300–600 m, repeat. At 1.2 kWh/km for the chassis and roughly 0.3 kWh per compaction cycle, a full shift of 120 stops costs about 200–230 kWh, which the 262 kWh pack covers with margin for a second pass. The stop-start profile is ideal for regen, and the 282 kW motor handles the grade to the higher districts without strain. Because the truck returns to the depot each morning, charging is a simple overnight job rather than a mid-route scramble.

Quiet Night Shifts

Baghdad's narrow residential streets and early prayer calls make noise a real complaint driver, and a diesel compactor is loud at the lift. The KT3E runs the lift and compact on battery power with no engine note, so the only sound is the hydraulic pump, roughly 10–15 dB quieter than diesel at the curb. Municipalities can shift more routes into the pre-dawn window without resident pushback, which spreads the fleet across cooler hours and protects summer range. The quiet operation also lets crews start earlier, finishing before the worst of the 45 °C afternoon heat.

Compactor Power and Throughput

The compactor is driven from the traction battery through a dedicated inverter, so compaction force is constant regardless of chassis speed. That holds throughput on the long climbs to the eastern districts where a diesel unit would moderate the pump with engine rpm. The liquid-cooled pack keeps the working band through 45 °C summer days, and because the drum and chassis share one pack, there is no second fuel system to maintain. Crews report the same pack-out per shift as the diesel units they replace, with none of the fumes in the cab.

Depot Charging

A collection fleet charges at the base between the morning return and the evening dispatch.

  • Install 120–240 kW DC at the depot; overnight is enough for a full shift.
  • Schedule the bulk charge after 23:00 to use the lower night tariff.
  • Keep one spare charger module on site; a fault must not skip a route.
  • Pre-cool the pack in summer so the morning charge holds full rate.

Total Cost of Ownership

Grid electricity at 0.10–0.18 USD/kWh makes the per-km energy bill roughly one-third of diesel, and the compactor add is small against the fuel saved by not idling. Over a 35,000 km/year collection duty cycle the energy and maintenance saving recovers the premium inside about 16–22 months. The 8-year battery warranty covers the full payback, and brake pads last about twice as long thanks to regen on the stop-start route.

Warranty and Service

The 8-year / 4,500-cycle battery warranty covers roughly 1.35 million full-equivalent cycles, far beyond collection duty, so the pack is under contract for the whole payback. Scheduled service is light: reduction-gear oil check at 60,000 km, coolant swap at 200,000 km, and lift-arm grease at 15,000 km. Resale at 36 months runs about 45–52% of landed cost when the battery health report stays above 85%, which telematics proves to the next buyer. The low acquisition price makes the KT3E the fastest-payback unit for a municipality testing electrification.

Municipal Tender Checklist

A municipality buying its first electric compactor should specify the package the finance and compliance desks expect. First, state the zero-exhaust and low-noise benefit for residential routes, with the decibel figure from the quiet-shift section above. Second, attach the 8-year battery warranty and the telematics battery-health export as proof of pack life. Third, show the overnight charging plan that covers a full shift from one depot, so no mid-route charger is needed. Fourth, confirm the lift arm matches the local bin standard, because a mismatch stops collection on day one. Fifth, list right-hand drive and the local lighting approval. A tender built this way clears procurement on the first pass, and the KT3E's low acquisition price then wins the commercial score against a diesel that hides its fuel cost over five years. The checklist also defends the budget, because a diesel compactor hides a fuel and repair cost that balloons over five years while the KT3E's per-km energy bill is roughly a third and the brake pads last twice as long.

Route Design for Quiet Collection

The KT3E lets a municipality move more routes into the pre-dawn window without waking residents, which spreads the fleet across cooler hours and protects summer range. A good design groups the dense residential blocks into the early shift and saves the industrial edges for midday, when noise matters less and the grid is cheaper. Because the truck returns to the depot each morning, the depot simply schedules the bulk charge after 23:00 and the fleet leaves full for the next pre-dawn run. The 14–18 m3 compactor holds a full residential route per load, so the number of trips back to the tip drops versus a smaller diesel, cutting both distance and downtime. For a Baghdad district the KT3E turns a complaint-heavy diesel route into a quiet, clean service the municipality can show off to residents and to the ministry that funds the next phase.

The same depot model scales across districts. A municipality running three KT3E units from one base can cover the whole city in two shifts, charging all three overnight on a single 240 kW stall with margin to spare. As volumes grow, a second stall and a modest transformer upgrade take the fleet to six trucks without moving the site, and the telematics view lets one dispatcher watch the whole fleet's state of charge from a phone. That operational simplicity is why the KT3E suits a municipality with no EV experience: the daily routine is charge overnight, collect, return, repeat, with no fuel forecourt and no exhaust to manage.
