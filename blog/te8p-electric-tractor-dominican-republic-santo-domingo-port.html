Why Drayage Loops Favor an Electric Tractor

Port drayage is the ideal electric duty cycle because trucks return to the same depot every few hours, so a single DC charger covers a whole fleet. A diesel prime mover idling in port queues burns fuel and accrues engine hours; the TE8P instead recovers energy through regenerative braking on the frequent stops at gate, weighbridge, and container stack. For a Santo Domingo operator moving 120 containers per week, that regeneration alone trims 8 to 12 percent off consumption versus a flat highway route.

The TE8P also removes the diesel particulate and NOx exposure that port communities in Santo Domingo and Caucedo have pushed regulators to reduce. Many Caribbean terminals now charge a green fee or restrict older diesel tractors during peak hours, so an electric prime mover keeps your trucks on the apron while competitors wait for a slot. That access is a hidden revenue protector that does not show up in a simple fuel spreadsheet.
