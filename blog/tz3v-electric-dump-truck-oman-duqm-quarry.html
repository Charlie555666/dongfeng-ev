Why Duqm Needs a 600 kWh Electric Dump Truck

Duqm sits on the Arabian Sea where summer air temperatures routinely exceed 45 C and equipment surfaces run far hotter under direct sun. A diesel dump truck loses efficiency in heat and burns its highest fuel rate under heavy climb and loaded haul. The TZ3V answers this with a 600 kWh CATL LFP battery pack sized for continuous haul cycles rather than short trips. LFP chemistry is chosen deliberately: it tolerates high charge and discharge temperatures better than NMC and it underpins the 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty that Dongfeng offers on the platform.

For a quarry loop of roughly 4 to 8 km per round trip with loaded climb, the 600 kWh pack sustains multiple trips per charge. Operators on similar GCC sites report that a single overnight charge plus a mid-shift top-up keeps the truck in service across a full shift without a diesel refill stop. That continuity is the single biggest reason Duqm fleets are moving off diesel.

Motor Power, Cooling and Climb Performance

The TZ3V uses a LvKong drive motor rated between 282 kW and 510 kW depending on axle configuration and gross vehicle weight. That torque band matters at Duqm because haul roads climb from the quarry face to the crusher and then to the port stockpile. Electric motors deliver full torque from zero rpm, so the loaded pull-away is smoother and faster than a diesel equivalent, with less clutch and brake wear.

Thermal management is the deciding factor in Oman. The TZ3V battery system is engineered for a 50 C cooling ceiling, which means the pack stays inside its safe operating window even when the truck is parked in sun between loads. The cooling loop prioritizes cell temperature stability during fast charging, protecting cycle life across the 4,500-cycle warranty horizon. The same loop keeps cabin and power electronics cool so performance does not sag at midday.

Duqm Charging Plan: Overnight Plus Mid-Shift Top-Up

A practical Duqm deployment uses two charging windows. The first is a slow overnight charge at the depot using available grid or on-site solar with storage. The second is a fast top-up during the operator meal break. Because the TZ3V supports battery swap in 5 to 6 minutes, fleets that cannot afford a charging gap can keep a second pack on a swap rig and exchange rather than wait. This is the preferred model for high-utilization quarries where every idle minute is lost tonnage.

Battery Swap Versus Fixed Charging at Duqm

Fleets should decide early between fixed charging and battery swap because the choice shapes the depot layout. Fixed charging is cheaper to install and suits operations with a natural overnight window. Battery swap costs more up front but removes the single biggest risk on a seven-day quarry schedule: a truck waiting on a charger while rock piles up. For Duqm, where the haul loop is short and utilization is high, many operators choose a hybrid: fixed chargers for the slow window and one swap rig for peak cover. The 5 to 6 minute swap keeps a loaded truck moving, and a second pack on the rig means the depleted pack charges off-peak.
