How Reliable Are Electric Trucks Compared with Diesel?

Electric trucks are now matching or beating diesel on the metric that matters: fleet uptime. Well-maintained electric fleets report 96-98% availability, because the electric drivetrain eliminates the components that generate most diesel breakdowns - fuel systems, turbochargers, exhaust aftertreatment, clutches and multi-gear transmissions. The TZ5Y electric 80t mining truck typifies this: a 510 kW LvKong motor with no consumable internals, a CATL LFP battery engineered for 4,500 cycles, and a maintenance schedule roughly half the length of the diesel equivalent. The failures electric trucks do have cluster in cheap, fast categories - 12V accessories, sensors, software - rather than the engine-out events that ground diesel trucks for days. Reliability skepticism was fair in 2018; the fleet data has since settled the question.

What Breaks Most Often on an Electric Truck?

The honest failure ranking surprises diesel veterans. Most frequent: 12V system items and cabin accessories - the same minor electrical faults every truck has, cheap and quick to fix. Next: wear items shared with diesel - tires, suspension bushings, brake components, though regenerative braking stretches friction brake life two to three times. Rarest by a wide margin: the high-voltage core. Battery, motor and power electronics failures are the least common category in field data, and when they occur, modular design means replacing a component rather than rebuilding a system. The TZ5Y's telematics logs every fault code with context, so patterns surface early - fleets see a sensor drifting weeks before it fails, and fix it on a scheduled day instead of a roadside one.

How Does the Warranty Protect Fleet Operators?

Warranty structure tells you what the factory really believes about reliability. The TZ5Y carries 8 years or 4,500 full cycles on the CATL LFP battery - with a guaranteed capacity floor, typically 70-80% state of health at term - plus multi-year coverage on motor, controller and charging systems. That battery term exceeds the first ownership period of most fleets and, critically, transfers to second owners, which protects resale value as well as operation. Claims run on data: the BMS continuously logs the information claims assessment needs, which shortens resolution from strip-down diagnosis to data review. A warranty this long on this component is only commercially rational if field failure rates are genuinely low - which is the strongest indirect reliability evidence available.

What Do Real Fleets Report After the First Year?

First-year fleet feedback follows a consistent pattern across export markets including South Africa. Month one brings adaptation friction - drivers learning gauges, dispatchers learning charging windows - and a spike in avoidable warnings. Months two through six show the settling: energy consumption improves 8-12% on unchanged routes as driving technique adapts, unscheduled workshop visits drop below the diesel fleet's rate, and availability stabilizes at or above 96%. By month twelve, the dominant complaint is that there are not enough of them - drivers prefer the electric trucks and bid for the shifts. The fleets that struggle are those that skipped training and parts stocking; the technology itself has ceased to be the variable.
