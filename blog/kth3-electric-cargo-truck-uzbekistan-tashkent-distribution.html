Why Tashkent Distribution Fits the EV Truck

An EV truck is the logical choice for Tashkent urban distribution because the loops are short, the stops are many, and the city is pushing clean freight through its center. The KTH3 electric cargo truck is a 4x2 box hauler with a 262 kWh CATL LFP pack and a LvKong motor up to 282 kW, sized for the 3 to 8 ton payload that grocery, parcel and spare-parts runs actually carry. For a fleet doing 120 to 180 km per day inside the city, the electric truck charges overnight and never visits a fuel station.

Uzbekistan's grid is among the cheapest in the region, which makes the energy cost of an electric box truck almost trivial next to diesel. The KTH3 turns that tariff advantage into a per-drop saving that compounds across a 20-truck fleet, and the quiet operation suits the dense residential districts Tashkent is building on its edges.

Overnight Charging at the Depot

The KTH3 charges on a 120 kW DC unit overnight, filling the 262 kWh pack in about 2.5 hours from empty, though most fleets plug in at 30 percent and need 90 minutes. At the Uzbekistan night tariff near 0.03 USD per kWh, a full charge costs under 8 USD and covers a full day of city drops.

  • Plug each truck on return so it leaves full the next morning.
  • Use a staggered timer so the depot draws below its peak contract.
  • Keep one 240 kW DC unit for any mid-day catch-up truck.
  • Add 50 kW of depot PV to cover the office and slow top-ups.
  • Log state of charge nightly to spot a weak pack early.

Because the duty is predictable, the fleet rarely needs public charging, which removes queue risk. The only design rule is to size the pack to the worst summer reefer day, and the 262 kWh unit does that with margin for most Tashkent routes.

Driver Experience and City Access

Drivers like the KTH3 because it has no gearbox, no clutch and no engine noise, which cuts fatigue across a 60-stop day. The low floor box is easy to load, and the regenerative brake holds the truck on the famous Tashkent hills without riding the pedal. City access is also smoother as clean-freight preferences spread, and an electric plate draws less scrutiny at low-emission checkpoints.

The 8-year or 4500-cycle battery warranty covers the pack through the full service life of the chassis, and Uzbekistan's flat terrain is kind to the cells, so capacity fade stays within the contract envelope. Resale value of a warranted electric box truck beats an old diesel in the same class.

Body Upfit and Local Builders

The KTH3 chassis takes a local box or curtain body with no special adapter, because the powertrain leaves a flat frame and a standard 24V body feed. Tashkent body shops already build for diesel trucks, and the only change is skipping the fuel tank and adding a small 12V converter for the box light. A reefer body wires its compressor to the pack through a rated DC unit, which is cleaner than a diesel reefer motor bolted to the front.

Buyers should spec the body to the route, not the truck: a grocery run wants a 18 m3 box with three shelves, a parcel run wants a 20 m3 curtain with a tail lift, and a drinks run wants a low 12 m3 box for weight. Because the KTH3 carries the same pack on every body, the fleet can mix bodies on one spare chassis and swap them as demand shifts through the week.

Driver Training and Safety on the Urban Route

The KTH3 is easy to drive, but Tashkent's mix of trams, carts and pedestrians rewards a short training on the electric drivetrain. Drivers learn that the truck is near-silent at low speed, so they use the warning tone in tight yards, and that regen holds the vehicle on hills without riding the brake. The daily card adds a tire and light check, and the depot keeps a written high-voltage awareness note even though the pack is sealed and the risk is low.

Because the body is local, the training also covers load securement for the specific box, which keeps the grocery and parcel loads from shifting on the famous district rounds. A half-day session per driver is enough, and the quiet cab means less fatigue across a 60-stop day, which supervisors read as fewer near-misses in the monthly log. That safety gain is part of why the EV truck suits dense city freight.

For Uzbekistan distributors, the case is short: cheap grid, short loops, many stops. The EV truck fits all three, and the KTH3 is the box hauler built to prove it on the streets of Tashkent.
