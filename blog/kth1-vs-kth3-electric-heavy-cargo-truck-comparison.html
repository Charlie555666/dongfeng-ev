KTH1 vs KTH3: Electric Heavy Cargo Truck Comparison

Choosing between the KTH1 and KTH3 chassis is one of the most common buying decisions we handle at Shaanxi Fenghan Trading for fleets that need a heavy electric truck for regional haulage. Both share the same Dongfeng cab and high-voltage architecture, yet they target different payload and duty-cycle profiles. This comparison breaks down battery capacity, axle rating, charging strategy, and five-year operating cost so a procurement manager can match the right EV truck to the route profile instead of over- or under-specifying the powertrain.

Chassis and Payload Architecture

The KTH1 is built on a 6x4 rigid platform rated for 18,000 kg gross vehicle weight, while the KTH3 uses an 8x4 configuration rated at 31,000 kg GVW. The longer wheelbase of the KTH3 supports a 9.6 metre box body, whereas the KTH1 is typically specified with a 7.2 metre cargo deck. For fleet buyers in Kazakhstan moving containerized goods or palletized FMCG, the KTH3 offers a 42 percent higher cube capacity per trip.

Battery and Charging Strategy

Both trucks use CATL LFP chemistry with an 8-year or 4500-cycle warranty, which removes the residual-value risk that worries many first-time EV truck buyers. The KTH1 runs a 262 kWh pack that reaches 80 percent in roughly 105 minutes on a 120 kW CCS2 charger. The KTH3 carries a 423 kWh pack and supports 240 kW DC fast charging, cutting the same 80 percent window to about 95 minutes despite the larger pack. Neither model offers battery swap, so depot overnight charging remains the primary energy strategy.

Overnight Depot Charging Math

For a single-shift operation running 180 km per day, the KTH1 consumes about 116 kWh daily, easily recovered in a 7-hour depot window on a 22 kW AC wallbox. The KTH3 consumes roughly 211 kWh at the same duty cycle and benefits from a 120 kW DC pedestal installed at the yard, which recovers full charge in under 3 hours. We recommend fleet buyers budget $18,000 to $35,000 per site for charging hardware depending on the number of simultaneous charge points.

Duty Cycle and Gradeability

Kazakhstan routes around Almaty and the mountain corridors to Kyrgyzstan involve sustained 6 to 9 percent grades. The KTH3's 360 kW LvKong motor holds 55 km/h on a 7 percent grade at 28,000 kg, while the KTH1 sustains 48 km/h under the same gradient at lighter load. For flat industrial park shuttle work, either chassis is over-specified, and the KTH1 becomes the cost-efficient choice.

  • Urban distribution and last-mile pallets: choose KTH1 for lower capital cost and higher daily cycles.
  • Cross-region bulk haul and container work: choose KTH3 for the 19,500 kg payload headroom.
  • Mixed fleet: pair KTH1 for city drops with KTH3 for the 200 to 300 km intercity leg.
  • Cold-climate operation: both use liquid-thermal LFP packs rated to -30 deg C, but pre-conditioning adds 6 percent consumption.

Five-Year Total Cost Comparison

At $0.09 per kWh industrial electricity and 60,000 km per year, the KTH1 costs about $8,500 annually in energy versus $14,200 for the KTH3. Maintenance runs about 35 percent lower than an equivalent diesel, saving roughly $4,200 per year on the KTH1 and $6,800 on the KTH3. Over five years the KTH3's higher payload lets a fleet remove one diesel truck from the rotation, which often offsets its higher purchase price within 38 months.

Charging Site Sizing Guidance

For a 10-truck KTH3 depot running two shifts, we recommend four 240 kW DC stalls plus two 120 kW stalls, giving 1,440 kW of simultaneous power and a 2 MVA service connection. A site of this size recovers a full 423 kWh pack in under 2 hours, so each truck cycles twice per shift without queue. The capital for chargers and civil works typically lands between $220,000 and $340,000 depending on trenching distance from the grid tie-in point, and we provide the single-line diagrams for the local electrical contractor to price accurately.

Buyers should also budget a 10 percent spare capacity on the service to absorb future fleet growth, because upgrading a substation later costs far more than oversizing it at build time. Where three-phase supply is weak, a 500 kWh buffer battery at the depot smooths the peak and can be fed by a small solar array, cutting the demand charges that otherwise dominate the electricity bill in remote oblasts where the grid is thin.
