Colombia's 2026 Import Duty Framework for Electric Trucks

Colombia applies its Arancel Nacional based on the MERCOSUR-aligned tariff lines, and electric goods vehicles fall under headings that in recent resolutions carry a reduced or zero base rate for completely built units classified as zero-emission. For a finished electric truck with a GVW above 3.5 t, the most-favoured-nation base rate on a comparable diesel is typically around 5% to 15%, while the clean-vehicle line has been pushed toward 0% to 5% under the 2022-2030 tax and transport decarbonisation schedule. The practical effect is that a 130 kWh medium cargo EV truck lands with a customs duty that is often 5 to 10 percentage points below its diesel equivalent, a gap that on a US$60,000 FOB unit is roughly US$3,000 to US$6,000 of saved duty per truck.

Importers should note the difference between finished (CBU) and semi-knocked-down (SKD) entry. A CBU electric truck clears in one line with one certificate chain. An SKD or CKD entry can lower the classified value and duty base but adds local assembly and a longer homologation clock. For most first-time Colombian buyers importing fewer than 20 units a year, CBU is simpler and faster even if the nominal duty is a point or two higher.

Homologation and Technical Regulation

Every electric truck entering Colombia must clear RETIE (Reglamento Tecnico de Instalaciones Electricas) for the high-voltage system and the vehicle-type approval under the Ministry of Transport, with data recorded in RUNT. The battery pack, the isolation monitoring, the charging inlet, and the fire-suppression behaviour all fall under RETIE review. Importers need a certificate of conformity from the manufacturer, a Spanish-language technical dossier, and test evidence for the battery thermal-runaway and insulation-resistance limits.

For Dongfeng EV trucks, the CATL LFP battery and the LvKong motor platform arrive with IEC-aligned test reports that shorten the local review. The most common delay is translation and notarisation of the dossier, not the technical merit, so budget two to four weeks of calendar time for document preparation before the unit ships.

Paperwork Checklist and Clearance Timeline

A clean 2026 import runs on a predictable document set. Missing one item is the usual cause of a unit sitting at Buenaventura or Cartagena for an extra week. The core list is below.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list in USD with FOB value and HS code.
  • Bill of lading and marine insurance certificate.
  • Certificate of conformity for the EV truck chassis and battery.
  • RETIE dossier with Spanish translation and notarisation.
  • RUNT registration data package for type approval.
  • Import declaration (DIAN) with the clean-vehicle line claimed.
  • Broker mandate and the regional incentive application.

The typical timeline is: document prep 2 to 4 weeks, ocean transit Shanghai to Buenaventura 28 to 38 days, DIAN clearance 5 to 12 working days, RETIE and RUNT finalisation 3 to 7 days, and depot delivery plus charger commissioning 1 week. Plan for a 9 to 14 week end-to-end window and you will not be surprised.

Planning depot charging for this EV truck

Charging an electric truck is a depot discipline, not a public-network gamble. A 350 kWh pack working a 260 km day typically returns with 25 to 35 percent in reserve, so overnight demand is about 210 kWh per truck. On a 120 kW DC charger the pack climbs from 20 to 80 percent in roughly 105 minutes; on 240 kW that falls to about 52 minutes, which lets two trucks share one charger on staggered shifts.

Size the site transformer for your busiest shift plus 30 percent headroom, keep cable runs under 30 metres to limit voltage drop, and meter each charger separately so energy cost per truck stays visible. In Colombia, grid connection lead times of three to nine months are common, so apply for the connection the day you sign the truck order, not the day the ship arrives.
