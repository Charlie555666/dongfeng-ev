KTH3 Electric Cargo Truck on Pakistan's Karachi-Lahore Corridor

The KTH3 electric truck is a heavy cargo platform aimed at Pakistan's Karachi-Lahore freight spine, where roughly 1,200 kilometers of motorway carries containerized and breakbulk freight between the port and the industrial heartland. Electrifying the whole leg is a staging exercise, not a single-charge problem, and this review explains how the KTH3's payload, range, and charging plan make the corridor workable segment by segment. It also covers the Pakistan import pathway for first-time EV fleet buyers.

Why the Corridor Electrifies in Segments

The Karachi-Lahore run is too long for one charge, but it decomposes into depot-anchored segments: Karachi to Hyderabad, Hyderabad to Multan, Multan to Lahore, each 250 to 350 kilometers. Fleets that re-base vehicles at segment depots, or stage fast chargers at motorway service areas, can run the corridor electrically today. For full specs, see the KTH3 electric cargo truck page.

Payload and Range Staging

The KTH3 carries 18,000 kilograms at a 31-ton GCW and returns 260 to 310 kilometers per charge loaded on motorway duty. That figure fits the Karachi-Hyderabad and Multan-Lahore segments without a mid-stop, and the Hyderabad-Multan segment needs one 75-minute top-up at a staged charger. Regenerative braking recovers useful energy on the descent from the Punjab plateau approaches, trimming the net charge each leg requires.

Fleets should treat the first corridor deployment as a two-depot operation: base trucks at Karachi and Lahore, stage one mid-route charger, and run each vehicle on its home segment until motorway charging density improves.

Charging Plan for the Corridor

The practical model is 240 kW chargers at the Karachi and Lahore fleet depots plus one mid-corridor unit at a motorway service area near Sukkur or Multan. Each charger restores 20 to 80 percent in about 75 minutes, which aligns with driver rest requirements on the long leg.

  • Base trucks at Karachi and Lahore depots with 240 kW chargers.
  • Stage one mid-corridor charger at a motorway service area.
  • Run each truck on its home segment until charging density grows.
  • Use overnight off-peak charging to cut the energy tariff.
  • Track per-segment energy to refine staging over the first season.

Warranty, Resale, and Lifecycle Confidence

The battery warranty is the foundation of the ownership case: 8 years or 4,500 cycles on the CATL LFP pack, transferable to a second owner, which means the truck can be sold or refinanced mid-life with a factory guarantee still running. No diesel powertrain offers anything comparable, and that difference shows up in residual value, where documented, warranted electric trucks are clearing stronger percentages of original price than worn diesel equivalents. Fleets that keep disciplined charging habits, staying between 20 and 90 percent state of charge in daily operation, routinely show 85 to 90 percent pack capacity after five years, and the telematics health report converts that directly into resale price.

Maintenance economics reinforce the point. The sealed LvKong motor has roughly one-third the moving parts of a diesel powertrain, with no oil, injectors, turbocharger, clutch, or exhaust aftertreatment to service, and regenerative braking cuts friction brake wear by an estimated 40 percent. The parts that remain are conventional wear items, tires, suspension bushings, filters, and body-specific consumables, all pre-stocked in the Fenghan regional parts buffer for the first year of operation.
