TE8P Electric Tractor for Veracruz Port Drayage, Mexico

The TE8P heavy electric tractor is purpose built for Veracruz port drayage, where a 510 kW EV truck moves loaded containers between the terminal gate and inland rail ramps without the noise, heat and emissions of a diesel prime mover. For Mexican fleets running hundreds of gate turns per day, this electric truck converts idle time at the gate into cheap kilowatt-hour savings and delivers a quieter, cleaner cab for the driver on long shifts.

Why Veracruz Port Drayage Favors an Electric Tractor

Veracruz handles the largest share of Mexico's container throughput on the Gulf coast, and the drayage lane from terminal to warehouse district is short, repetitive and stop-start by nature. That duty profile is exactly where a battery electric tractor wins. A diesel tractor burns fuel while queuing; an EV truck recaptures energy on every deceleration through regenerative braking. With electricity priced at roughly $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh and diesel at $0.90 to $1.20 per liter, the per-kilometer energy gap is wide enough to reshape a fleet's operating budget within the first year of service. Port authorities are also tightening idling and emissions rules inside terminal zones, which makes a zero-tailpipe tractor a compliance asset rather than a cost center for the operator.

Port Duty Cycle and Gate Turns

A typical Veracruz drayage round trip covers 30 to 70 km with a heavy loaded pull on the outbound leg and an empty return. The TE8P completes four to six gate turns per shift on a single charge, and fleets using opportunity charging during loading windows extend that to a full double shift. The 510 kW motor delivers the low-end torque needed to launch a 40-foot container load from a standstill at the gate without bogging down, keeping terminal throughput steady through the busiest hours of the day.

Charging and Battery Swap at the Terminal

Most Veracruz fleets install a DC fast charger at the depot and top the pack between shifts. The 20 to 80% window fills in about 60 to 90 minutes, which fits naturally into meal and load breaks. For terminals that want continuous operation, the swap-capable architecture supports a 5 to 6 minute battery exchange on compatible variants, eliminating charger waits during peak windows. Because the thermal system is liquid cooled, the pack stays inside its 25 to 35 °C comfort band even during back-to-back charging sessions in the Gulf's humid heat, protecting long-term pack health.

Operator Comfort and Safety

The TE8P cab is laid out for port work: low step-in height, wide visibility and a quiet drivetrain that reduces operator fatigue across long shifts. Regenerative braking also reduces wear on friction brakes, lowering maintenance downtime and improving safety near pedestrians inside the terminal. Fleet managers report fewer unscheduled service events versus comparable diesel prime movers because the electric drivetrain has far fewer moving parts and no exhaust system to corrode in the salt-air port environment that accelerates rust on conventional trucks.

Maintenance and Total Cost of Ownership

Electric drivetrains remove engine oil, fuel filters, diesel exhaust fluid and complex transmissions from the maintenance plan. For a drayage fleet running high annual kilometers, those eliminated service items add up quickly. Combined with the $0.08 to $0.20 per kWh electricity rate versus $0.90 to $1.20 per liter diesel, the TE8P typically reaches total cost parity with a diesel tractor well inside the 8-year battery warranty window. The 4,500-cycle rating means the pack is still covered even under intensive multi-shift duty, which is the normal pattern at a busy container port.
