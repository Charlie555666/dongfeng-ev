Cairo's Clean Air Case for Electric Sweepers

Cairo suffers some of the highest urban particulate levels in the region, and a large share of that load is road dust re-suspended by traffic. Mechanical sweeping with water suppression is one of the most cost-effective interventions, but a diesel sweeper adds tailpipe NOx and PM right back into the air it is meant to clean. Brush, Vacuum, and Water Systems

The KT1D uses a dual side-brush layout with a central main broom and a vacuum intake that lifts fines into a 4,000 liter sealed hopper. A 1,200 liter water tank feeds misting nozzles ahead of the brushes to keep dust down during sweeping, which is essential in Cairo's dry months. Filtration is a two-stage cyclone plus panel filter, so the expelled air meets the clean-air intent of the program rather than re-releasing particles.

Because the chassis is electric, the sweeper can run a pre-dawn shift with almost no noise, letting districts cover busy corridors before the morning rush. Operators report that a single 6 to 8 hour shift covers 40 to 60 lane-kilometers of sweeping on a full charge, depending on debris load and the number of hopper dumps required.

Range, Charging, and Depot Layout

With the tank and hopper empty the chassis returns 240 to 280 kilometers of range, and a realistic loaded sweep shift still delivers 6 to 8 hours of continuous duty before a recharge. The 120 kW DC fast charger brings the pack from 20 to 80 percent in about 90 minutes, so a depot can rotate two or three units through one charger across a 24-hour cleaning window.

  • Install a 120 kW charger at the municipal depot for overnight and break top-ups.
  • Route two sweepers per district so coverage continues during charging.
  • Schedule hopper dumps at fixed points to keep the 4,000 L bin from overflowing.
  • Use misting on the brushes to meet Cairo dust-suppression requirements.
  • Train crews on regen braking to extend range in stop-start street work.

Safety, Training, and Operator Comfort

The KT1D carries a high-voltage isolation monitor and automatic pack disconnect, and its low-noise electric drivetrain lets crews work pre-dawn shifts in dense Cairo districts without waking residents. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd. provides a one-day sweeper course covering brush pressure, hopper dump sequence, and high-voltage shutdown. Districts that train crews report roughly 20 percent fewer brush and filter replacements because operators learn to match brush downforce to debris rather than running full pressure constantly. That saving alone pays back a meaningful slice of training time within a single season.

Operator comfort matters for retention on a physically demanding job. The sealed, air-conditioned cab keeps fine dust out during Cairo's dry months, reducing respiratory exposure versus an open diesel cab. The smooth electric torque also cuts vibration fatigue on long street runs, and the simple single-pedal driving mode reduces left-foot strain on stop-start sweeping. For municipalities, lower crew turnover translates directly into steadier service coverage and lower retraining cost across the fiscal year.

The KT1D logs sweep distance, water use, and hopper dumps to onboard telematics, so a district can prove coverage to citizens and to the national clean-air program with data rather than estimates. That evidence base supports the next budget round and helps justify expanding the electric sweeper fleet beyond the pilot district into adjacent neighborhoods that currently rely on manual sweeping.
