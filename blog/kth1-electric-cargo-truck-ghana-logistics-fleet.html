KTH1 Electric Cargo Truck: Ghana Logistics Fleet Buyer's Guide

The Accra-Kumasi corridor is the busiest freight spine in Ghana, and it is where the electric truck makes its strongest case for West African logistics operators. Roughly 250 km of paved dual and single carriageway connects the port of Tema and the capital with Kumasi, the inland commercial hub, and the round trips are dense, predictable and repeatable. That duty profile - long but steady, heavy but not extreme, and anchored at both ends by depots with power - is exactly what an electric cargo truck is built for. This guide walks Ghana fleet buyers through the KTH1 electric cargo truck and its KTH3 sibling, the spec sheet that matters for the corridor, the charging plan, the import and duty picture, the driver and service story, and a full total-cost-of-ownership model you can take to your finance team.

Why the Accra-Kumasi Corridor Electrifies First

Most Ghana freight is corridor freight. A box or flatbed truck leaves Accra or Tema loaded with consumer goods, industrial inputs or agricultural produce, runs to Kumasi, reloads and returns the same day. Daily distance sits between 350 km and 600 km round trip. Diesel in Ghana is imported and cedi-priced, so every exchange-rate dip lands directly on your operating cost. Electricity from ECG is local, tariff-regulated and substantially hydro-based, which decouples your energy bill from the currency. Three structural facts make this corridor the right place to start, and they compound over a fleet's life rather than appearing once.

  • Fixed endpoints with power: depots, warehouses and forwarding yards at both ends already have three-phase connections. You do not need roadside infrastructure; you charge where the truck already parks overnight and during the midday rest.
  • Predictable cycles: routes, loads and dwell times are stable, which lets you size batteries and chargers to the real duty rather than guessing and over-buying capacity you never use.
  • Driver retention: the quiet, low-vibration cab and one-pedal driving reduce fatigue on the long haul, a real advantage in a market where skilled long-haul drivers are hard to keep and harder to replace.
  • Currency hedge: because energy is domestic and tariff-regulated, your largest variable cost stops tracking the cedi and the oil price at the same time.

KTH1 and KTH3: Specs That Matter on the Corridor

The KTH1 is the 4x2 long-haul cargo platform for the corridor. The KTH3 is its 6x2 higher-payload sibling that shares the same CATL LFP chemistry and LvKong drive system, so fleet managers can standardize maintenance across both and train mechanics on one powertrain. Both run permanent-magnet LvKong motors in the 282-510 kW band with 2200-3200 Nm of torque - enough to hold highway speed with a full load on the climb toward the Kwahu plateau and to restart cleanly on the grades after the Kumasi reload. Range under full load is 200-350 km per charge depending on pack size, and DC fast charge from 20-80 percent takes 1.5-2 hours, which slots neatly into the mandatory rest break at a midpoint depot.

Charging Strategy for Ghana Fleets

For the corridor, the lowest-risk plan is depot-to-depot overnight charging plus one midday fast-charge session. A 120-180 kW DC charger at a midpoint yard in Konongo or Ejisu covers the 20-80 percent top-up during the legal rest window, eliminating range anxiety on the return leg. ECG industrial tariffs make overnight charging roughly half the cost of diesel per kilometer, and because LFP chemistry tolerates daily 20-80 percent cycling without meaningful degradation, the 8-year / 4500-cycle warranty is realistic rather than optimistic. Operators near the coast should specify corrosion-rated enclosures for chargers; inland, dust filtering on the charge cabinet pays for itself within a season. We also recommend a simple charge-management schedule so the fleet charges during off-peak windows and the depot's existing supply is never overloaded by several trucks drawing at once.

Ghana Import, Duty and Registration

Importing a Dongfeng EV truck into Ghana runs through Tema port, where the vehicle attracts import duty, VAT, the Ghana Shippers Authority and processing fees, and the DVLA registration stack. Electric commercial vehicles currently benefit from favorable treatment versus equivalent diesel imports in several ECOWAS configurations, and our export team prepares the full CIF documentation, conformity certificate and left-hand-drive build for Ghana. Budget for the charge hardware separately, and confirm with your clearing agent whether the battery pack is assessed as part of the vehicle or as a component, because that single line item can swing landed cost by several thousand dollars. Plan the clearing timeline into your commissioning date, since a delayed battery assessment can hold a unit at port longer than the chassis alone would.

Service, Training and Uptime

An electric cargo truck is not just a different fuel; it is a simpler machine. With no diesel engine, turbo, clutch, gearbox or exhaust aftertreatment, the scheduled service list shrinks to brakes, tires, suspension, coolant loops and the high-voltage inspection. We provide Ghana operators with a documented service schedule, spare inverter and motor bearings, and remote diagnostics so a fault can often be cleared without a workshop visit. Driver training is short because one-pedal driving is intuitive, and the regen braking means foundation brakes last far longer than on a diesel unit running the same corridor.

TCO: Electric vs Diesel on the Corridor

On a 500 km daily round trip, a diesel 4x2 cargo truck burns roughly 28-34 liters per 100 km, which at Ghana diesel prices puts fuel alone near USD 0.30-0.38 per kilometer. The KTH1 at 1.0-1.3 kWh/km draws about 500-650 kWh per day; at an industrial tariff that is USD 0.06-0.10 per kilometer for energy. Even after amortizing the higher FOB price and the charger, the energy gap alone typically recovers the premium within 18-30 months on double-shift corridor operation, and maintenance savings from the simpler drivetrain widen the gap further.

Fleet Rollout and Residual Value

The practical path for a Ghana operator is a phased rollout: convert the most predictable Accra-Kumasi lanes first, validate the charging plan for 90 days, then scale as drivers and mechanics gain confidence on the electric powertrain. Because the KTH1 and KTH3 share the same LvKong drive system and CATL LFP pack architecture, the second wave of trucks needs no new training program and no separate spare-parts kit, which keeps the workshop simple as the fleet grows and protects the uptime that corridor freight depends on.

Residual-value risk, the old objection to electric trucks, is cushioned here by the 8-year / 4500-cycle battery warranty, which covers most of the first ownership period and gives financiers a defensible endpoint for lease and resale math. Insurers are increasingly comfortable underwriting LFP battery fleets, and the lower fire risk of LFP chemistry versus other lithium types is a point they weight favorably when pricing a corridor fleet. For most Ghana buyers the question is no longer whether to electrify the corridor, but how fast the depot charger capacity should grow with the truck count, and a single 180 kW unit already supports a small fleet through overnight plus midpoint charging.

Financing the transition is straightforward when the TCO model is shown end to end. We help operators present the FOB premium, the charger capex, the energy saving and the maintenance saving as one payback curve, and several development-finance lines now favor clean freight specifically because the operating-cost reduction is visible from the first month of operation. The cedi hedge embedded in domestic power is the quiet winner: as the oil price and exchange rate move, the electric fleet's energy line stays put.
