EV vs Diesel Maintenance: What Disappears

The maintenance case for electric trucks starts with subtraction. Gone are engine oil and filter changes, fuel filters, injectors, turbochargers, DPF regeneration, exhaust aftertreatment fluid, clutch wear on automated manuals and most transmission servicing. What remains is a shorter list: coolant for the battery and motor thermal loop, brake fluid, gear oil in the drive axle, cabin filters, tires, and friction brakes that last two to three times longer thanks to regenerative braking. For a fleet in Algeria, this typically cuts scheduled maintenance hours per truck by 50-60% and parts spend by 40% or more. The TZ3V concentrates its service needs into predictable, clean, workshop-friendly tasks.

Battery Care in Algerian Climate

Climate shapes battery care more than any other factor. In hot Algerian conditions, the rules are simple: use the thermal management system as designed, avoid parking at 100% charge in extreme heat for days, and favor overnight or shaded charging when ambient temperatures peak. The liquid-cooled CATL LFP pack in the TZ3V manages cell temperatures actively, so normal operation needs no heroics - just respect for the charging windows in the operator manual. Fleets that follow these basics consistently see degradation at the gentle end of the warranted curve. Cold-climate operation reverses the advice: precondition the pack while plugged in, and expect a modest winter range reduction that planning should absorb.

Charging Planning for TZ3V Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The TZ3V supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 600 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in Algeria should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
