Battery Health: The Core of Used EV Valuation

In a used electric truck, the battery report is the valuation. A 140 kWh CATL LFP pack showing 85-90% state of health after four or five years still delivers most of its original 190 km range, and LFP chemistry degrades slowly and predictably enough that the remaining life is bankable. Buyers should demand the BMS health report, charging history and cycle count, and should understand what they read: daily fast-charging history is worth a discount versus gentle overnight charging, and any gaps in the record are a red flag. The 8-year or 4,500-cycle battery warranty transfers with the truck, so a three-year-old unit still carries five years of factory coverage - a floor under value that no used diesel can offer.

Inspection Checklist Before You Buy

A used electric truck inspection covers familiar ground plus EV-specific items. The conventional checks - chassis, suspension, brakes, tires, accident repair evidence - follow standard practice. The electric layer adds: the battery health report and its consistency with the odometer; a full diagnostic scan for stored high-voltage fault codes; charging inlet and cable condition; a live fast-charge test confirming the pack accepts rated power; and verification that BMS software is current. Cosmetics matter less than records. A KT1D with complete telematics and service history is worth a premium over a shinier truck with missing files. Import Process and Documents for the Dominican Republic

Importing a used electric truck into the Dominican Republic follows the same skeleton as any used commercial vehicle import, with battery-specific additions. The file includes the purchase invoice, deregistration or export documents from the origin country, bill of lading, and the conformity paperwork the Dominican Republic requires - INDOCAL conformity where applicable. The lithium battery adds shipping documentation: the UN38.3 test summary and a dangerous goods declaration, with state of charge limited to around 30% for ocean transit. Used vehicle age or inspection rules in the Dominican Republic must be checked before purchase, not after - some markets restrict commercial vehicle imports by age. Clearance at Caucedo Port runs smoothly when the document pack is complete and pre-lodged with a licensed broker in Santo Domingo.

Charging Planning for KT1D Fleets

Charging infrastructure decisions made at order time shape fleet economics for a decade. The KT1D supports DC fast charging through a CCS2 inlet, recovering 20-80% of its 140 kWh pack in roughly an hour on a 120 kW unit, while overnight AC or lower-power DC charging covers base load at the cheapest tariff. A sound depot plan matches charger count to shift patterns rather than truck count - fleets running single shifts rarely need more than one fast charger per four to six trucks plus overnight points per bay. Grid connection capacity should be confirmed with the utility before truck delivery, because transformer upgrades carry the longest lead times in the whole project. Fleets in the Dominican Republic should also model tariff structures: time-of-use pricing rewards overnight charging discipline, and the savings fund the infrastructure faster than most buyers expect.
